HEB ISD Overview

~21,000 Students Enrolled
31 Campuses
B (88) TEA Grade 2024–25
A+ Niche Grade (3rd-party)
2 HS High School Campuses
$1.39 Tax Rate per $100 (2024)

Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD is one of DFW's most underrated school districts for a specific family profile: solid academics, genuine access to DFW Airport, and a significantly lower total housing cost than the Keller or Colleyville corridors. The district earns a TEA B (88) and a Niche A+, placing it in the top tier of Tarrant County ISDs for academic performance. For corporate transfer families landing at DFW, HEB ISD's geography makes it a practical and frequently overlooked option that deserves a real look.

Before diving into the data, there is one structural fact about HEB ISD that every family must understand: the grade configuration is not the standard Texas layout. HEB runs on a K-6, 7-9, 10-12 model. Elementary school covers kindergarten through sixth grade. Junior high covers seventh through ninth grade. High school covers tenth through twelfth grade only. If your family is coming from a district using a 6-8 middle school model, this will change how you think about campus transitions. A ninth-grader at HEB attends junior high, not high school. Some families find the extended junior high experience beneficial; others prefer the earlier high school start that most suburban Texas districts use. Understanding the model before you buy is worth the time.

The district serves roughly 21,000 students across three primary cities: Hurst, Euless, and Bedford. It also covers significant slices of Arlington, Colleyville, Fort Worth, and North Richland Hills, which creates boundary complexity worth addressing specifically. For a family relocating from outside Texas, the name "Hurst-Euless-Bedford" is less recognizable than Keller or Southlake, but the district's performance data is competitive with both.

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Need airport access as a non-negotiable: most HEB addresses are within 10 minutes of DFW Airport
  • Want solid B-level academics without the premium price of Keller or Colleyville homes
  • Are comfortable with a K-6 / 7-9 / 10-12 grade configuration
  • Value the Mid-Cities location for commutes to both Fort Worth and Dallas employment corridors
  • Have a student interested in AP, dual credit, or JROTC programs at the high school level

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • You specifically want an IB programme or a single large high school with 3,000+ students
  • The 9th-grade-in-junior-high model is a hard no for your family's preference
  • Commute geometry pulls you north toward Keller or south toward Mansfield rather than toward DFW Airport
  • You want the highest-rated academic district in Tarrant County at any price
Boundary Verification

HEB ISD serves parts of six different cities. A Hurst, Euless, or Bedford address is usually within the district, but portions of Arlington, Colleyville, Fort Worth, and North Richland Hills also fall within HEB boundaries. The reverse is also true: not every address in those three named cities falls in HEB ISD. Always verify your specific street address at hebisd.edu or through the TEA school locator before making an offer.

TEA Accountability Rating

HEB ISD earned a TEA score of 88 (B) in 2024-25, described by the district as a "strong B+" performance. This places HEB in the upper tier of Tarrant County's B-rated districts and meaningfully above the statewide median for Texas ISDs. For context, the district received an A rating from TEA in 2022, and the B in subsequent years reflects both the recalibrated TEA scoring formula and a genuine but manageable performance gap from the top rating tier.

TEA's A-F system evaluates three accountability domains. Student Achievement measures STAAR performance, graduation rates, and college/career/military readiness. School Progress looks at academic growth over time relative to comparable districts statewide. Closing the Gaps assesses equity in performance across student subgroups. For HEB ISD, the district's overall demographic profile, which is more diverse than Keller or Grapevine-Colleyville, means the Closing the Gaps domain carries more weight in the composite score.

A score of 88 translates practically to this: families who move to HEB ISD for the schools are overwhelmingly satisfied with the outcome. The district is not at the absolute top of the DFW academic performance list, but it delivers above-average results consistently. For a family who cannot or does not want to pay for Keller or Colleyville housing, HEB ISD produces comparable academic outcomes at a lower home price point.

Academics

Both of HEB ISD's high schools, L.D. Bell and Trinity, offer Advanced Placement courses and dual credit pathways. AP access at the high school level is districtwide. Dual credit partnerships allow students to earn college credit through Tarrant County College (TCC) while still enrolled in high school, a meaningful advantage for college-cost-conscious families.

HEB ISD's academic proficiency rates on STAAR assessments sit above the statewide average and are competitive with neighboring Birdville and Keller ISDs on most measures. The district's graduation rate, CCMR performance, and AP participation data are published annually at txschools.gov and the district's accountability page at hebisd.edu. For specific campus-level STAAR data, the Texas Tribune's Schools Explorer provides annual breakdowns by campus and subject.

The district's Niche A+ rating reflects strong parent and student reviews alongside solid academic outcomes. Niche grades incorporate survey data, so they can reflect community satisfaction more than raw test scores. The combination of TEA B (88) and Niche A+ suggests a district that performs well academically and maintains strong parent engagement and satisfaction, which are not always the same thing in large Texas ISDs.

Notable Campuses

HEB ISD operates two comprehensive high schools, multiple junior highs serving grades 7-9, and elementary campuses across its three-city service area. The district's K-6/7-9/10-12 structure means that "junior high" carries more scope here than in most Texas districts.

L.D. Bell High School

High School (Grades 10-12) Hurst AP & Dual Credit

The district's Hurst-side comprehensive high school, serving students in grades 10-12. Bell offers AP courses across multiple subjects and dual credit opportunities through Tarrant County College. Known for competitive athletics and a range of extracurricular programs. Families in Hurst and the northern portion of the HEB service area will typically feed to Bell.

Trinity High School

High School (Grades 10-12) Euless AP & Dual Credit

The Euless and Bedford-area comprehensive high school, also serving grades 10-12. Trinity mirrors Bell's AP and dual credit offerings and has a strong fine arts and performance culture. The school's location near DFW Airport makes it the high school campus most familiar to families relocating through that corridor. Verify your specific address to confirm which of the two high schools your child is zoned to attend.

Harwood Junior High School

Junior High (Grades 7-9) Bedford

One of HEB ISD's junior high campuses serving Bedford area students in grades 7-9. The extended junior high (which includes 9th grade) is a structural feature of HEB ISD that families coming from standard 6-8 middle school districts should understand clearly before choosing a home address. Students spend their freshman year at a junior high campus, not a high school campus.

Euless Junior High School

Junior High (Grades 7-9) Euless

Serves Euless-area students in the 7-9 grade span. Like the other junior highs, this campus bridges the elementary and high school experience across a wider age range than a traditional middle school. Extracurricular options at the junior high level are more developed than in most Texas districts because the campus holds three grade levels.

For current GreatSchools ratings and STAAR performance data on individual campuses, visit the district's school locator or GreatSchools.org.

Specialty Programs

Advanced Placement (AP)

Campuses: L.D. Bell and Trinity High Schools. Access: Open enrollment within each campus; teacher recommendation or prerequisite courses may apply for specific AP courses. Both high schools offer a broad AP catalog. Districtwide availability, not concentrated at a single campus.

Dual Credit (TCC Partnership)

Campuses: L.D. Bell and Trinity. Access: Available to eligible 10th-12th graders through Tarrant County College. Students earn simultaneous high school and college credit. Transportation to TCC campuses is the parent's responsibility unless the course is taught on-site. A meaningful cost-reduction tool for families planning for college expenses.

JROTC

Access: Available at HEB ISD high school campuses. JROTC offers leadership development, character building, and career pathway exposure for students interested in military service or related fields. Programs operate as regular elective courses during the school day.

Fine Arts

Access: Districtwide, at all campus levels. HEB ISD maintains strong fine arts programs in band, choir, orchestra, and visual arts. Both high schools have competitive performance groups with a regional reputation. Fine arts is woven into the standard curriculum from elementary through high school, not siloed as a magnet option.

Gifted & Talented (GT)

Access: Districtwide via state identification process. GT services are available at neighborhood campuses. Families relocating with a GT-identified student should contact the district's Curriculum and Instruction department to understand the scope of services available at their assigned campus and what documentation to bring at enrollment.

Career & Technical Education (CTE)

Campuses: High school level. HEB ISD CTE pathways cover health science, information technology, and business tracks. The airport-adjacent location and Fort Worth aviation industry proximity give the district a practical context for career pathways that is less common in purely residential suburban districts.

Enrollment & Transfers

New Student Registration (2025-26)

New student enrollment is managed through the HEB ISD enrollment portal at hebisd.edu. Required documents for new students typically include proof of residency within HEB ISD boundaries, student birth certificate, immunization records, and prior school records. Enrollment for the 2025-26 school year opened in spring 2025. Contact the assigned campus directly if you are enrolling mid-year.

Intra- and Inter-District Transfers (2026-27)

Transfer applications for the 2026-27 school year ran January 12 through March 13, 2026. Transfer requests are reviewed based on available space at the receiving campus. HEB ISD processes both intra-district transfers (moving between campuses within the district) and inter-district requests from outside the district. Transfers are not guaranteed. Visit the district's Transfer and School of Choice page at hebisd.edu for current-year instructions.

Grade Configuration Note for Transfer Families

If your child is completing 6th grade at their current school (typically entering middle school in most Texas districts), they will enter 7th grade at an HEB ISD junior high campus, not a traditional middle school. If they are in 9th grade at a high school in their current district, they will attend a junior high in HEB ISD instead of a high school. This is not an error. It is the district's grade structure. Confirm where your child will be placed by contacting the district enrollment office before making any assumptions.

Special Education & IEP/504 Transfers

Texas law requires HEB ISD to convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment to review, adopt, or revise an existing IEP or 504 plan. Bring all prior evaluation reports, the current IEP or 504 document, and any supplementary assessment materials. Contact HEB ISD's Special Education department before your child's first day to discuss campus-level services. Parent experience varies by campus and student need profile.

Boundary & Address Verification

HEB ISD's service area is more complex than its name suggests. While the district is named for its three primary cities, the actual boundaries extend into portions of six municipalities. This means that buying in one of the three named cities does not automatically place you in HEB ISD, and buying in one of the other cities does not automatically place you outside it.

Cities Served by HEB ISD

The district primarily serves Hurst, Euless, and Bedford in full or near-full. It also includes portions of Arlington (particularly northeast Arlington near the Euless border), portions of Colleyville (southeast Colleyville near Bedford), parts of North Richland Hills, and a small section of Fort Worth near the Hurst boundary. The Colleyville overlap is particularly relevant for home buyers: some Colleyville addresses fall in HEB ISD rather than in Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, which carries different academic performance implications.

Common Edge Cases

The Colleyville-Bedford border area is the most frequently misunderstood boundary in the district. Families searching for homes in "Colleyville" may find properties that feed to HEB ISD rather than Grapevine-Colleyville ISD. Since GCISD and HEB carry different tax rates and different academic reputations in some buyers' minds, the specific boundary line matters. Similarly, northeast Arlington near the Euless border can fall in HEB ISD, HEB ISD-adjacent Birdville ISD, or Arlington ISD depending on the precise street. A block-level difference can mean a different district entirely.

High School Attendance Zones Within HEB ISD

With two comprehensive high schools serving the entire district's 10-12 population, your address determines whether your child attends L.D. Bell (Hurst area) or Trinity (Euless/Bedford area). The zone line between the two schools runs roughly north-south through the middle of the district. Both schools offer comparable programs, so the choice of high school is less consequential here than in districts where campus quality varies significantly. That said, verify your high school zone before buying if the specific campus matters for your family.

How to Verify

Use the HEB ISD school locator at hebisd.edu or the TEA school directory at tea.texas.gov/apps/school-directory. Making an offer based on city name or ZIP code alone, without address-level verification, is the single most common school district mistake I see relocating families make.

Tax Rate & Finances

HEB ISD's 2024 certified total tax rate is $1.3875 per $100 of assessed value (M&O: $1.04, I&S: $0.3475). The I&S (Interest and Sinking) component reflects bond debt service from the district's capital improvement programs. This total rate is higher than several neighboring districts, including Keller ($1.09) and Fort Worth ISD ($1.06), which is worth building into any budget comparison. However, because home prices in HEB's service area are meaningfully lower than in Keller or Colleyville, the total annual tax bill can still be competitive.

November 2025 Tax Referendum

HEB ISD placed a Voter-Approval Tax Rate Election (VATRE) on the November 2025 ballot to allow the district to exceed the state-mandated M&O threshold. The outcome of that election may affect the M&O component of the tax rate for 2026 and beyond. Verify the current certified rate with the Tarrant County Tax Assessor before closing on any property.

What the Rate Means for a Monthly Payment

Texas homeowners receive a $100,000 homestead exemption on school district taxes. On a $375,000 home with the exemption applied, the taxable value is $275,000. At $1.3875 per $100, annual HEB ISD school taxes on that home run approximately $3,816, or roughly $318 per month. On a $450,000 home (taxable at $350,000), the figure rises to about $4,856 annually, or $405 per month. HEB home prices typically run below Keller and Colleyville, so even with a higher rate, the total annual tax bill on a comparable lifestyle choice is often similar or lower.

Rate Comparison to Neighbors

HEB ISD at $1.3875 total carries a higher rate than Keller ISD ($1.09), Fort Worth ISD ($1.06), and Birdville ISD ($1.20). Families comparing the same dollar amount of home purchase in HEB vs. Keller will pay more in school district taxes per $100 of value in HEB. The offset is that homes of similar quality are typically priced lower in the Hurst-Euless-Bedford area than in Keller or Colleyville, so the real comparison requires running the numbers on a specific set of homes rather than on rates alone.

How HEB ISD Compares

Families looking at the Mid-Cities area of Tarrant County most commonly cross-shop HEB ISD against Keller ISD (if they're willing to move north), Birdville ISD (for more affordable options to the east), and Grapevine-Colleyville ISD (for the highest-rated Tarrant County experience at a premium price).

Keller serves a different geography and is a more natural comparison for northwest Tarrant families than for Mid-Cities buyers. Birdville is the value alternative for similar student populations with a lower academic rating and lower home price expectations.

Community Feel

Hurst, Euless, and Bedford are established suburban communities with genuine neighborhood character, though they carry less of the new-construction premium-subdivision energy of Frisco or Keller. The communities are mature, which means the parks are established, the streets are tree-lined, and the PTAs are experienced. Booster clubs at both L.D. Bell and Trinity run active programs for athletics and fine arts, and competition between the two high schools in football, basketball, and band is a genuine part of local culture.

The airport proximity creates an unusually cosmopolitan character for the district's size. The Euless school population includes a meaningful proportion of airline industry families and international corporate transferees, which contributes to the demographic diversity that HEB ISD manages well. Families coming from large urban metro areas often comment that HEB ISD feels more culturally varied than comparable-size suburban districts they encountered in other parts of Texas, and that the classroom diversity is genuinely reflective of the global economy around DFW Airport.

The 7-9 junior high model creates a different kind of community dynamic at the secondary level. Parent involvement tends to follow students through elementary school and into junior high, which benefits campus cultures at the 7-9 level. The high school experience starting in 10th grade is shorter than at most Texas districts. Some families find this an advantage: the high school years feel more purposeful and less dominated by the social drama of early adolescence. Others simply prefer the freshman-in-high-school model their students came from. Both perspectives are reasonable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Hurst, Euless, or Bedford address always mean I'm in HEB ISD? ▾
For most addresses in those three cities, yes. But parts of neighboring cities (Colleyville, Arlington, North Richland Hills, Fort Worth) also fall within HEB ISD, and a small number of addresses in Hurst or Bedford may fall in an adjacent district. Always verify your specific street address at hebisd.edu or through the TEA school locator before making a purchase decision. A city name or ZIP code is not a substitute for address-level verification.
Why does HEB ISD use a K-6 / 7-9 / 10-12 grade structure instead of the standard Texas model? ▾
HEB ISD has operated a K-6, 7-9, 10-12 model for decades, and the district has maintained it because it works for the community. The extended junior high model means a 9th-grader attends a junior high campus rather than a high school. Families who prefer this structure often cite the additional year of maturation before the high school social environment. Families who dislike it typically cite wanting their student to experience four years of high school athletics, extracurriculars, and leadership opportunities. Neither preference is wrong. The practical implication: if you move to HEB ISD with a student entering 9th grade, they will spend the year at a junior high, not a high school.
What is the difference between L.D. Bell and Trinity High School? ▾
Both schools serve grades 10-12 and offer comparable AP and dual credit programs. Bell serves the Hurst area; Trinity serves Euless and Bedford. The two schools have similar academic profiles and athletic traditions, with a long-running cross-town rivalry that is part of local community culture. Assignment is based on your home address. Unlike some large districts with meaningful campus quality disparities, both HEB high schools perform at a similar level. The main reason to research the distinction is confirming which campus your specific address feeds to.
How does HEB ISD's tax rate compare to neighboring districts? ▾
HEB ISD's 2024 certified total rate is $1.3875 per $100 of assessed value, which is higher than Keller ISD ($1.09), Fort Worth ISD ($1.06), and Birdville ISD ($1.20). The I&S component of $0.3475 reflects bond debt from capital improvements. On a $375,000 home with the $100,000 Texas homestead exemption, school taxes run approximately $3,816 per year. Note: a November 2025 VATRE was placed on the ballot by HEB ISD; verify the current certified rate with Tarrant County before closing on a purchase.
Does HEB ISD serve the Colleyville area? ▾
Parts of Colleyville fall within HEB ISD, particularly in the southeast Colleyville area near Bedford. Families searching for homes in Colleyville often expect to be in Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, which has a different TEA rating and tax rate. Before making an offer on any Colleyville property, verify the school district assignment with the specific address. The district line between HEB and GCISD runs through Colleyville in a way that is not obvious from a map or real estate listing.
What happens to my child's IEP when we move to HEB ISD? ▾
Texas law requires HEB ISD to convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment to review, adopt, or revise the existing IEP or 504 plan. Bring all prior evaluation reports and your child's current IEP or 504 documentation. Contact the HEB ISD Special Education department before your child's first day to discuss what services are available at the assigned campus and what the transition process looks like. Parent experience with special education services varies by campus and by individual student need.
Is HEB ISD a good choice for families who travel frequently through DFW Airport? ▾
Yes, for a specific reason: virtually every address in the HEB ISD service area is within a 10-15 minute drive of DFW Airport. For airline employees, corporate transferees on frequent travel, or families with members who commute regionally, the airport proximity is a genuinely functional advantage that most DFW suburbs don't offer. Euless in particular borders the airport property. The practical benefit shows up in less time spent commuting to the airport for family members on work travel and in easier logistics for students doing dual enrollment at colleges accessible via regional air travel.
