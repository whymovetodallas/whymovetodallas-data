Things to Do in Burleson, TX with Kids

Burleson has quietly built some of the best youth sports infrastructure in Johnson County. The 136-acre Chisenhall Fields complex, 16-field Bartlett Soccer Complex, BRiCk Recreation Center with a free splash pad, and Old Town's live music scene all come together in a city that still feels like genuine Texas community.

What Burleson Families Actually Do Here

Burleson sits on the southern edge of the Fort Worth metro in Johnson and Tarrant counties, and it has invested deliberately in family infrastructure. The combination of Chisenhall Fields Sports Complex, Bartlett Soccer Complex, and the BRiCk Recreation Center gives youth athletes more purpose-built facilities than most similarly-sized Texas cities. Old Town Burleson adds a dining and live music district that keeps weekends interesting beyond sports. See the full Burleson relocation guide →

Elite Sports Facilities

Chisenhall Fields (136 acres, 10 baseball, 5 softball, 1 football field) and the 16-field Bartlett Soccer Complex give Burleson youth athletes purpose-built spaces that exceed what most suburban cities can offer at this scale.

BRiCk & Free Splash Pad

The BRiCk Recreation Center at Bartlett Park offers an indoor pool with water slides, a free outdoor splash pad, day camps, year-round swim lessons, and a full gymnasium. Free splash pad admission makes it one of the best family value plays in the area.

Old Town & Community Events

Old Town Burleson is a genuine historic district with live music at Station 330 and Old Texas Brewing Co., local boutiques, and community events including the BTX Honey Tour Carnival and the Hot Sounds of Summer free concert series.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Burleson maintains 20+ parks totaling 348 acres, with major anchors at Chisenhall Fields (136 acres), Bailey Lake Park (49 acres), and Bartlett Park. A concrete linear trail connects several parks. The free Splash Pad at the BRiCk is Burleson's top warm-weather family destination.

Chisenhall Fields

136-acre metropolitan park and sports complex with 10 baseball fields, 5 softball fields, 2 practice fields, 1 football field, hike and bike trails, playground, restrooms, and a covered pavilion. One of the largest youth sports facilities in Johnson County. Lit fields support evening games and tournament weekends. Ample parking for large events.

Baseball Softball Football Trails Playground Pavilion

Bailey Lake Park

49 acres built around an 8-acre lake, with an 18-hole disc golf course, concrete linear trail connecting to Chisenhall and Tinker Trail, covered pavilion, benches, and picnic tables. The lake-and-disc-golf combination is unique among Burleson parks. Flat terrain around the lake perimeter makes it accessible for strollers. Host to Burleson Nature Discovery events in June.

Lake Access Disc Golf Walking Trails Picnic Areas Pavilion

Bartlett Park

Burleson's family hub: home of the BRiCk Recreation Center, Bartlett Soccer Complex (16 fields), free Splash Pad, outdoor aquatics facility, playground, and concrete linear trail. A single park destination that covers water play, organized sport, fitness, and casual outdoor time. The splash pad runs with free admission per the official city schedule.

Splash Pad (Free) Soccer Complex Outdoor Pool Recreation Center Playground Trails

Burleson Bark Park

Dedicated off-leash dog park serving Burleson's many dog-owning families. Separate areas for large and small dogs, with water stations and shaded resting areas for the humans standing at the fence. A popular after-school stop for families with dogs and kids who need outdoor time.

Dog Park Off-Leash Water Stations

Veterans Memorial Plaza

Civic green space honoring Burleson's military service members, located near Old Town. The adjacent Mayor Vera Calvin Plaza has a small play area with a trolley feature for young children, shaded picnic areas, outdoor games, and greenspace for events. A gathering point for the Hot Sounds of Summer concert series.

Memorial Play Area Community Events Picnic Areas

Hidden Creek Sports Complex

Additional city-managed sports facility providing field capacity for youth leagues during peak tournament and league seasons. Part of Burleson's multi-complex sports infrastructure that accommodates the city's large youth athletics population without overcrowding a single site.

Sports Fields Youth Leagues Tournaments

Cedar Ridge Park

Neighborhood park with playground, open turf, and walking paths in a residential Burleson neighborhood. Stroller-friendly with low foot traffic on weekday mornings. A practical everyday outdoor option for younger children within walking distance for many Burleson households.

Playground Open Turf Walking Paths

Trail System Highlight

Burleson Concrete Linear Trail connects Chisenhall Fields Sports Complex through to Bailey Lake Park and onward to Tinker Trail, providing a car-free corridor for walking, jogging, and biking. Great for family bike rides on weekends and after-school afternoon walks with younger kids in the stroller.

Relocation Tip

Start with Bartlett Park. One visit covers the BRiCk, the free splash pad, the soccer complex, and the playground in a single location. It gives you the clearest picture of how Burleson has concentrated family amenities in one destination. The free splash pad hours are Monday 2:00 to 8:00 p.m. and Tuesday through Sunday 10:00 a.m. to 8:00 p.m., with early-morning sensory hours on Wednesday and Friday.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

The Burleson Youth Association (BYA) and Burleson Independent Soccer Association (BISA) anchor the city's youth athletics landscape, with city-run swim programs and i9 Sports rounding out options for younger children. The Chisenhall Fields and Bartlett Soccer Complex facilities put Burleson in an elite tier for dedicated youth sports infrastructure at the suburban scale. See the full Burleson guide for Burleson ISD athletic programs at the school level.

Non-competitive, skills-first leagues in soccer, flag football, basketball, and t-ball for children ages 3 and up. An accessible first-sport option before kids enter BYA or BISA.

Featured Sports Complex

Chisenhall Fields Sports Complex, 136 acres on Burleson's western side. Ten baseball fields, five softball fields, two practice fields, one football field, hike-and-bike trails, playground, and covered pavilion. Host to Burleson Youth Association leagues and regional tournaments throughout spring and fall. One of the most complete youth sports campuses in North Texas for a city of Burleson's size.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Burleson's entertainment scene is built around the BRiCk, Alley Cats, and Old Town's live music district. It's a city that hasn't over-built the commercial entertainment side, which means what's here tends to be well-maintained and genuinely community-centric.

BRiCk Recreation Center

Recreation / Aquatics · Burleson's flagship family facility at Bartlett Park. Indoor pool with water slides, vortex pool, and current river; 5-lane lap pool; outdoor pool; walking track above the gymnasium; two 1,400-square-foot meeting rooms; and a patio for events. Day camps during spring, summer, and Christmas break. The indoor aquatics section alone makes it a year-round destination.

Alley Cats Entertainment Center

Family Entertainment · Bowling, arcade games, go-karts, and laser tag under one roof. A newer family fun center in Burleson that combines the four major entertainment categories families typically need. Works for ages 5 through teen and makes a reliable birthday venue without driving to Fort Worth.

Old Town Burleson

Historic District / Entertainment · Live music at Station 330 and Old Texas Brewing Co., local boutiques, art, and restaurants in Burleson's walkable downtown. Mayor Vera Calvin Plaza has a children's play trolley and shaded picnic space. The cultural center of the city and the venue for most community events.

Pirates Cove Wet & Dry Fun

Water Park / Entertainment · State-of-the-art water park, playground, rides, and bowling in one complex. Particularly well-suited for the under-12 age group with both wet and dry attractions in the same footprint. A solid summer-day destination without the drive to a major water park.

Bailey Lake Park Nature Programs

Nature Education · Bailey Lake Park hosts Burleson Nature Discovery events, disc golf on the 18-hole course, and family nature walks around the 8-acre lake. A slower-paced outdoor option that works well for kids who need nature time away from organized sports. The disc golf course is free and open to all skill levels.

Day Trip from Burleson

Fort Worth Stockyards National Historic District (about 25 minutes), with the Stockyards Championship Rodeo, Texas Longhorn cattle drives twice daily, Western wear shops, live honky-tonk music, and a range of family-friendly dining. For families new to Texas culture, the Stockyards visit is the experience that makes relocating families say "we get it now."

Family-Friendly Dining

Old Town Burleson anchors the dining scene with scratch-made food and live music at Station 330, craft beer at Old Texas Brewing Co., and a growing restaurant district. The city also has reliable family standards along the US-174 corridor that handle post-practice dinners without ceremony.

Station 330 Best Old Town experience Scratch-made food, craft cocktails, live music Thursdays, BOGO pizza Mondays, and weekend brunch in the heart of Old Town Burleson. One of the standout restaurants in the city with a genuinely welcoming family atmosphere.
Old Texas Brewing Co. Best weekend evening out Family-owned craft brewery and barbecue in Old Town with weekend live music, Texas BBQ, and a relaxed atmosphere where kids are welcome. The patio fills up on nice evenings and creates the kind of community feeling Burleson is known for.
Rio Mambo Best Tuesday family night Tex-Mex with a contemporary atmosphere, Family Night Tuesday specials, Wine Wednesday, and weekend brunch. Fajitas, burritos, and quesadillas cover the kids' priorities while the adult menu goes broader.
Our Place Best lazy Sunday breakfast All-day breakfast American eatery with homestyle dishes and hearty lunch options. One of the more highly rated casual spots in Burleson. The kind of neighborhood breakfast place families discover by accident and return to every weekend.
Roscoe's Smokehouse Best post-game BBQ Authentic Texas BBQ with brisket, ribs, and sides in Burleson Commons. Rustic country aesthetic, smoky cooking, and portions sized for hungry athletes. A reliable post-game stop that keeps the whole car happy.
Villa Dianna Restaurant Best picky-eater friendly Italian restaurant serving classic pizza and pasta in a relaxed family-friendly setting. When the family agrees pizza is dinner but wants to sit down somewhere other than a chain, Villa Dianna is the answer.
Cracker Barrel Best all-ages comfort food Country cooking with biscuits, fried chicken, and a full kids' menu. Reliable from a 4-year-old's perspective and from a grandparent's. The rocking-chair porch is a genuine family waiting-area that keeps kids occupied without a screen.
Texas Roadhouse Best when the team wants to celebrate Hand-cut steaks, freshly baked bread, and a kids' menu with mac and cheese and corn dog bites. A consistent choice for post-tournament dinners when you need a big table and a kitchen that won't let anyone down.
Whataburger Best fast stop after practice Texas-born burger chain and a rite of passage for families moving to the Lone Star State. Open 24 hours, kid-friendly, and one of the first things families who relocated from other states mention when explaining why they love Texas.

Old Town Burleson along Main Street and Ellison Street is the dining and entertainment hub, with the US-174 and I-35W corridors handling the everyday family quick-dinner traffic. Most of the city is within a 10-minute drive of either area.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Burleson's retail landscape mixes Old Town's local boutiques and specialty shops with the everyday big-box corridor along US-174 and proximity to the Fort Worth Premium Outlets a short drive north. It's a practical retail ecosystem for families who want character shopping and practical errands in separate trips.

Old Town Burleson

Historic District

Locally owned boutiques, gift shops, specialty food, art galleries, and one-of-a-kind retail in Burleson's historic downtown. The kind of shopping that doesn't feel like shopping. Browse between bites at Station 330 or pints at Old Texas Brewing.

Best for: a Saturday afternoon with older kids or a date-night browsing run that turns into dinner without leaving the block.

US-174 / I-35W Everyday Corridor

Everyday

Target, HEB, Walmart, Lowe's, and national chain retail along the major commercial corridors. Handles weekly grocery runs, back-to-school shopping, and quick-errand days. Practical and efficient without requiring a highway trip.

Best for: weekly household runs and every practical purchase that doesn't need a special trip.

Fort Worth Premium Outlets (Nearby)

Outlet Center

About 25 to 30 minutes north in Hillsborough, with 100+ stores at outlet prices on Nike, Coach, Polo Ralph Lauren, and more. When Burleson families want a larger outlet shopping day, this is the standard destination without heading into Fort Worth proper.

Best for: seasonal shopping trips and back-to-school athletic gear runs when outlet pricing matters.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Burleson's events calendar is community-centric: the BTX Honey Tour Carnival fills Old Town in May, the Hot Sounds of Summer concert series runs every Friday evening from late May through July 4th, and city-hosted events at Bailey Lake and Bartlett Park fill out the warmer months.

May - July 4

Hot Sounds of Summer Concert Series

Free Friday evening concerts at Mayor Vera Calvin Plaza in Old Town Burleson, running from late May through Independence Day. Live music across genres, family seating, and the Old Town restaurant district right behind you for dinner before or after the show.

May

BTX Honey Tour Carnival

Annual outdoor carnival in Old Town Burleson with free parking and free admission. Live music featuring multiple bands across the weekend, carnival rides, arts and crafts vendors, novelty entertainment, and food. Held annually since 2022. Check burlesontx.com/events for the current year's dates.

June

Burleson Nature Discovery at Bailey Lake

Annual family nature program at Bailey Lake Park, introducing children to local wildlife, plant identification, and outdoor skills in a guided format. Part of the city's effort to connect families with Burleson's natural park resources beyond the sports complexes.

September

BTX Best Fest

Month-long culinary event throughout September where participating Burleson restaurants create dishes inspired by State Fair foods. Visitors collect passport stamps at 10+ locations for a chance to win prizes. A fun way for new residents to discover the full Burleson dining scene in one month.

Year-Round

BYA & BISA Youth Tournaments

Chisenhall Fields and Bartlett Soccer Complex host youth baseball, softball, football, and soccer tournaments throughout the spring and fall seasons, drawing teams from across North Texas. Tournament weekends are community events in themselves, with food vendors and strong family attendance.

December

Old Town Holiday Events

Old Town Burleson hosts seasonal holiday events through December, with festive lighting, small business shopping, and community gatherings in the historic district. Check the city calendar at burlesontx.com/events for each year's specific programming and dates.

Full calendar: Burleson official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

Burleson's indoor activity lineup centers on the BRiCk's year-round aquatics and gymnasium, Alley Cats for bowling and arcade entertainment, and the Burleson Public Library's well-regarded children's programming. For trampoline parks, nearby Cleburne and Fort Worth options fill the gap.

BRiCk Recreation Center

Type: Recreation / Indoor Aquatics · Ages: All ages
Indoor pool with water slides, vortex pool, current river, and 5-lane lap pool. Walking track above the gymnasium. Day camps during spring, summer, and Christmas break. Year-round swim lessons. The most complete family recreation facility in Burleson, open to members and day visitors.

Alley Cats Entertainment Center

Type: Bowling / Arcade / Entertainment · Ages: All ages
Bowling, arcade games, go-karts, and laser tag in one facility. Burleson's answer to the all-in-one family entertainment center. Works for birthday parties, post-game celebrations, or rainy afternoons when the parks aren't an option.

Pirates Cove Wet & Dry Fun

Type: Water Park / Indoor-Outdoor · Ages: All ages
Water park, indoor playground, rides, and bowling combined. Particularly good for the 4-to-12 age range with the mix of wet and dry activities keeping the group occupied without splitting up. Good summer-season option before the outdoor heat sets in fully.

Burleson Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: All ages
Youth robotics programs, volunteering opportunities, homework assistance, book groups, board and video game programs. The Burleson library punches above its size on children's programming. A free, climate-controlled option that keeps kids engaged beyond screen time.

House of Air Trampoline & Ninja Park (Nearby)

Type: Trampoline / Ninja Course · Ages: All ages
Nearby trampoline and ninja obstacle course park drawing Burleson families for active indoor sessions. About 20-25 minutes away. Combines trampolining with American Ninja Warrior-style obstacles for a different experience than standard trampoline parks.

Urban Air Adventure Park (Nearby)

Type: Adventure / Trampoline · Ages: All ages
Full-featured adventure park with trampolines, climbing walls, obstacle courses, and more. Available from nearby Fort Worth and Mansfield locations. Burleson families use it as the go-to for bigger-group birthday outings when the local options are booked up.

BRiCk Day Camps

Type: Day Camp · Ages: School-age
City-run day camps through the BRiCk during spring break, summer, and Christmas break. Structured programming that gives working parents a supervised, activity-filled option during school off-days without the cost of a full private day camp.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When Burleson afternoons climb past 100 degrees in July, the BRiCk's indoor pool section is the most used family destination in the city. The indoor water slides and current river give kids the water-play experience without the sun exposure of the outdoor splash pad. Check burlesontx.com/brick for current admission pricing and summer-hour schedules.
