What Saginaw Families Actually Do Here

Page last reviewed 2026-06-06. We update activities pages annually as parks, leagues, and venues change.

Quick answer: Saginaw has 7 city parks, a community aquatic center, adult and youth recreation programs, and 10+ annual events, making it a genuine family community in northwest Tarrant County where the infrastructure punches above the city's size.

Saginaw is a smaller northwest Tarrant County city where the community feel is real and the infrastructure (seven parks, a full aquatic center, city-run adult leagues, and a rotating food truck park) punches above its size. Families who move here typically find that weekly routines fill up quickly between the Switchyard, the rec center, and the farmers market. It is not Keller or Southlake in terms of scale, but it is also not a bedroom community where you have to drive 30 minutes to do something. See the full Saginaw relocation guide →

The Switchyard

Saginaw's Switchyard Food Truck Park at 752 S. Knowles Dr has become the city's living room, with rotating local food trucks, live music nights, and the backdrop for the annual Cinco de Mayo Festival and Concert Series.

Willow Creek Park

The city's anchor park covers multiple activity zones: jogging and bike paths, a 6-hole disc golf course, basketball, softball, two tennis courts, two volleyball courts, and playgrounds. Steps from The Switchyard at the southern end.

Year-Round Events

From the Easter Egg Hunt in spring through the December Holiday Parade and Drone Show, Saginaw runs more than ten community events per year, most of them free and centered in the parks and Rec Center.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Saginaw maintains seven city parks distributed across the neighborhoods. Two parks have confirmed jogging and bike paths; all parks have playgrounds and most have covered picnic facilities. The city is not trail-dense by DFW suburban standards, but Willow Creek Park provides a genuine multi-activity outdoor destination within city limits.

Willow Creek Park

101 Knowles Dr. The largest and most activity-rich park in Saginaw. Jogging and bicycle paths wind through the grounds with exercise stations along the route. Wide paved surfaces work well for strollers and bikes. Restrooms and covered picnic pavilions on site. The park also connects directly to The Switchyard Food Truck Park at its southern end, making it easy to walk trails and grab dinner in one outing.

Trails Disc Golf Basketball Tennis Volleyball Softball Playgrounds Picnic Areas

Highland Station Park

1332 High Country Trail. The second park with a paved trail system, a concrete walking path with exercise stations spaced throughout. Good shade coverage makes it a reasonable option on warm afternoons. Two playgrounds, a swing set, horseshoe pits, covered picnic area, two tennis courts, and a volleyball court round out the amenities. Restrooms on site.

Trails Exercise Stations Tennis Volleyball Horseshoes Playgrounds Picnic Areas

William Houston Park

700 Park Center Blvd. A solid park for families with soccer players, with two full-size soccer fields on site. Also includes a playground and covered picnic areas. Located close to the Joyce Spindor Community Center, making it convenient to combine an outdoor session with an indoor activity on the same trip.

Soccer Fields Playground Picnic Areas

Opal Jo Jennings Memorial Park

700 Opal Dr. A newer neighborhood park with a recently updated playground and two covered picnic areas. Good for toddlers and younger kids. Quieter crowd level than Willow Creek, which makes it a reliable choice for early-morning playdates or low-key afternoons.

Playground Picnic Areas Good for Under 5

Sagewood Park

1051 Sagewood Dr. A neighborhood green space with a playground and a covered picnic area. Best for families within walking distance. Lower-traffic than the larger parks, which keeps it relaxed on weekday afternoons.

Playground Picnic Area Neighborhood Scale

Kiwanis Park

333 W McLeroy Blvd. Home to the Saginaw Veterans' Memorial, a ceremonial platform, and a gazebo. Less of a play park and more of a community gathering and reflection space. Worth a visit with older kids to learn about the city's history and the community organizations that built it.

Veterans Memorial Gazebo Community Space

Brian Schwengler Memorial Park

816 Thompson Dr. A neighborhood park serving the eastern part of the city. Detailed amenity information is not listed on the official parks page; the city parks department (817-232-4640) can confirm current features.

Neighborhood Park

Trail System Highlight

Willow Creek Park jogging and bike path system, 101 Knowles Dr, with paved paths running throughout the park alongside exercise stations at regular intervals. Well-suited for walking with a stroller, jogging, and casual cycling. The southern end connects to The Switchyard Food Truck Park, so a Saturday morning walk can end with lunch from rotating food truck vendors.

Relocation Tip

Start your Saginaw park exploration at Willow Creek. It has the most complete set of amenities in the city, and the connection to The Switchyard means you can do the trail loop, let the kids play disc golf, and grab food from the trucks, all in one outing. Once you are settled, Highland Station is the better weekday walk because it tends to be quieter and has good shade on the concrete trail circuit.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Saginaw's city-run athletics program focuses on adult leagues, with youth programs offered through the Recreation Center. For competitive team sports at younger ages, families typically connect with Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD school athletics or leagues in neighboring Keller, Haslet, and Fort Worth.

Adult Softball (Coed)

League: City of Saginaw Recreation
Ages: Adult
Season: Spring, summer, and fall seasons
The coed league runs concurrently with the men's league. A popular option for couples and workplace teams.

Adult Volleyball

Ages: Adult

Courts at Willow Creek Park and Highland Station Park. Check saginawtx.org for registration dates as the 2026 season is finalized.

Classes held at 633 W McLeroy Blvd. Registration through the Rec Center programs office.

Youth Martial Arts (Karate)

Two skill levels offered. A structured option for kids who want a discipline-based physical activity with clear progression.

Disc Golf

Course: 6-hole course at Willow Creek Park
Ages: All ages
Season: Year-round, free
A laid-back option that works well across age groups. Kids as young as 5 or 6 can play alongside adults. Discs can be purchased at most sporting goods stores.

Recreation Center

Saginaw Recreation Center, 633 W McLeroy Blvd, the hub for youth classes, adult leagues, fitness programs, and summer camp. Hours: Mon-Thu 6am-9pm, Fri 6am-6pm, Sat 8am-3pm. Phone: 817-230-0350. For competitive youth team sports (soccer, baseball, tackle football), families connect with Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD school athletics or neighboring leagues in Keller and Fort Worth.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Saginaw's own entertainment scene is centered on community-run activities, the Switchyard, and the Aquatic Center. For movie theaters, trampoline parks, and larger regional attractions, Fort Worth and Alliance Town Center are 10 to 20 minutes away.

Saginaw Aquatic Center

Water Park (seasonal) · The city's most popular summer destination for families. Features a large water slide, leisure pool, train slide, and several spray and splash features. Summer 2026 hours: Tue-Thu 12-7PM, Fri 12-5PM, Sat 12-7PM, Sun 1-6PM (closed Mondays). Resident daily admission around $10/person. Two pavilions available for group rentals. Phone: 817-230-0350.

Saginaw Switchyard Food Truck Park

Community Hub · 752 South Knowles Dr. Rotating food trucks, outdoor seating, and a calendar of live events throughout the year. The Cinco de Mayo Festival and Saginaw Concert Series both use the Switchyard as their home base. Check @SaginawSwitchyard on Facebook for the current truck lineup and event schedule.

Saginaw Concert Series

Free Outdoor Music · A summer music series partnered with ARTSNET, held at the Switchyard or Recreation Center. Brings live performances to the community at no cost throughout the warmer months. See the season schedule at saginawtx.org.

Saginaw Public Library

Library · Children's storytimes, summer reading challenge, and programs throughout the year for all ages. Free library cards available to Saginaw residents. Visit saginawlibrarytx.org for current hours and program calendar.

Saginaw Recreation Center

Recreation Center · 633 W McLeroy Blvd. Indoor fitness classes, youth martial arts and art programs, summer day camp, and a drop-in space for all ages. The Joyce Spindor Community Center at 620 Park Center Blvd handles community gatherings and facility rentals.

Willow Creek Disc Golf Course

Outdoor Recreation · 6-hole course inside Willow Creek Park, open to all ages at no cost year-round. A rare amenity for a city of Saginaw's size. Kids as young as five or six can play a full round alongside adults, making it a reliable free activity on weekend afternoons.

Day Trip from Saginaw

Fort Worth Stockyards National Historic District (about 20 min south), a Texas icon with the nightly longhorn cattle drive down Exchange Avenue, live country music at Billy Bob's, rodeo events, and Western-themed shops and restaurants. It is genuinely unlike anything else in DFW and is the kind of outing kids remember. Go on a weekday to avoid weekend crowds.

Family-Friendly Dining

Saginaw's dining scene is centered on the Switchyard Food Truck Park, which functions as the city's most popular gathering place. Everyday dining runs along W McLeroy Blvd and US-287, where suburban chains cover weeknight needs. For a broader restaurant selection, Fort Worth (15 min south) and Alliance Town Center (10 min north) expand the options considerably.

Saginaw Switchyard Food Truck Park The Go-To Spot 752 S. Knowles Dr. Rotating local food trucks serving BBQ, tacos, specialty desserts, and rotating international concepts. Check @SaginawSwitchyard on Facebook for this week's lineup.
Saginaw Farmer's Market Food Vendors Saturday Mornings 752 S. Knowles Dr, open every 2nd and 4th Saturday, 8am-noon, March through fall. Seasonal produce, baked goods, and food vendors alongside local craft makers. A great Saturday morning outing for the whole family.

Fort Worth Stockyards District 20 Min South Texas barbecue, craft beer, and Western fare along Exchange Avenue. Riscky's Barbecue and H3 Ranch are longtime family stops in the district. Worth the drive for a weekend outing with kids.

Most of Saginaw's within-city dining is concentrated along W McLeroy Blvd and the US-287 service road. The Switchyard at 752 S. Knowles Dr is the standout local destination; check Facebook for current food truck schedules before you go.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Saginaw's in-city shopping is primarily practical retail along McLeroy Blvd. Major lifestyle and destination shopping is a short drive north to Alliance Town Center or south into Fort Worth.

Alliance Town Center

Lifestyle Center10 Min North

Fort Worth's Alliance corridor, about 10 minutes north of Saginaw, with 1.2 million square feet of open-air retail, restaurants, and entertainment anchored by major national brands. Target, Best Buy, a full movie theater, and dozens of dining options.
Best for: full Saturday family outing: shopping, lunch, and a movie all in one trip.

McLeroy Blvd Retail Corridor

Everyday EssentialsIn City

Saginaw's main commercial strip with grocery anchors, pharmacy, hardware, and everyday retailers within minutes of most neighborhoods. Covers the practical side of weekly errands without requiring a longer drive.
Best for: weekday grocery runs, pharmacy, and quick errands that don't need a full shopping center.

Sundance Square, Fort Worth

Downtown Experience25 Min South

Fort Worth's premier downtown pedestrian district with boutique shops, major retailers, restaurants, and year-round outdoor events. A bit further at 25 minutes, but worth it for a special occasion or when the family wants something genuinely different from a strip mall experience.
Best for: a special outing when you want walkable urban energy and more unique retail options.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Saginaw runs more than ten community events per year, most of them free and centered in the parks and the Switchyard. The December holiday finale and the October Train & Grain Festival are the two biggest anchors on the annual calendar.

April

Easter Egg Hunt

Held annually at Willow Creek Park, organized by the Saginaw Fire Department. Eggs are hidden throughout the park with age-separated search areas. One of the city's most reliably attended spring events.

May

Cinco de Mayo Festival

Annual celebration at The Switchyard with live music and rotating food truck vendors. One of the Switchyard's biggest nights of the year. Outdoor, family-appropriate, and well-attended by Saginaw residents.

June / July

Independence Celebration

Saginaw holds its own fireworks show rather than directing families to neighboring cities; the 2026 edition is set for June 27. Check saginawtx.org/community/saginaw_events.php for location and time as the event approaches.

Summer

Saginaw Concert Series

Free outdoor concerts partnered with ARTSNET, held at the Switchyard or Recreation Center throughout the summer. Live entertainment (check the official series page for this year's lineup).

Mar – Nov

Saginaw Farmer's Market

Every 2nd and 4th Saturday, 8am to noon, at 752 S. Knowles Dr alongside The Switchyard. Local produce, crafts, and food vendors. Held annually since the early 2010s. Free to attend; parking on site.

October

Train & Grain Festival

One of Saginaw's signature annual events, honoring the city's railroad and agricultural heritage. The 2026 edition is set for October 3. Vendors, family activities, and community participation. Details at saginawtrain-grain.com.

October

Trunk or Treat

Annual Halloween event organized for younger kids in the community. Location typically announced closer to the date on the city calendar and social media channels.

December

Holiday Parade, Drone Show & Tree Lighting

The city's largest annual event. A community parade followed by a drone light show and tree lighting ceremony. The 2026 event is scheduled for December 5. Brings out a large crowd from across the city, so arrive early for a good spot.

Full calendar: Saginaw official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

When temperatures climb above 100°F in July and August, Saginaw families have a solid mix of indoor options both in the city and within 15 to 20 minutes in Fort Worth and the Alliance area.

Saginaw Recreation Center

Type: Community Recreation · Ages: All ages
633 W McLeroy Blvd. Fitness classes, youth martial arts (Kung Fu and Karate), children's art, summer day camp, and drop-in indoor access. Mon-Thu 6am-9pm, Fri 6am-6pm, Sat 8am-3pm. Phone: 817-230-0350. Air-conditioned and accessible year-round.

Saginaw Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: All ages
Storytimes for toddlers and preschoolers, summer reading challenge with prizes, and free programs year-round. Free library cards for residents. Visit saginawlibrarytx.org for current hours, catalog, and event listings.

Saginaw Aquatic Center

Type: Community Pool / Water Park · Ages: All ages
Seasonal (summer only). Water slide, leisure pool, spray features, and train slide. Tue-Thu 12-7PM, Fri 12-5PM, Sat 12-7PM, Sun 1-6PM. Resident admission around $10/person. A full afternoon water day for a fraction of a commercial water park ticket.

Urban Air Trampoline Park (Fort Worth area)

Type: Trampoline & Adventure Park · Ages: Ages 2+
The nearest major indoor adventure park is in the greater Fort Worth area, roughly 15 to 20 minutes from Saginaw. Trampolines, foam pits, climbing walls, and toddler zones. Good for birthday parties and rainy-day energy bursts.

AMC Theatre at Alliance (10 min north)

Type: Movie Theater · Ages: All ages
A major multiplex at Alliance Town Center in Fort Worth, about 10 minutes north. The closest full-service movie theater to Saginaw. Recliner seating and standard showtimes throughout the week.

Main Event Entertainment (Fort Worth)

Type: Arcade, Bowling & Laser Tag · Ages: All ages
Bowling, laser tag, billiards, and a large arcade floor. About 15 to 20 minutes from Saginaw in the greater Fort Worth area. A reliable rainy-day or birthday party option when kids need a bigger venue than the Rec Center provides.

Joyce Spindor Community Center

Type: Community Hall · Ages: All ages
620 Park Center Blvd. Air-conditioned indoor gathering space adjacent to William Houston Park. Available for facility rentals and community events. Contact Saginaw Recreation (817-230-0350) for rental availability and programming calendar.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

Saginaw Aquatic Center is the most economical beat-the-heat option in the city at around $10/person for a full afternoon of water slides and spray features. On Mondays when the pool is closed, head to the Saginaw Recreation Center for air-conditioned classes and programs. On genuinely brutal afternoons, the Saginaw Public Library is free, cool, and has story programs that make the stop feel productive. For a bigger splash experience, Hawaiian Falls in Mansfield or NRH2O in North Richland Hills are both under 30 minutes and the closest full-scale water parks.
