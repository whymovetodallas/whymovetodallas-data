What Fairview Families Actually Do Here

Fairview is a small, affluent town in Collin County that families move to mostly for one reason: Lovejoy ISD, consistently one of the highest-performing small districts in Texas. Around that, life is low-key and low-density, with custom homes on bigger lots, creekside parks, and Fairview Town Center handling the shopping, dining, and entertainment. Allen and McKinney sit right on either side, so the big-suburb amenities are never far. See the full Fairview relocation guide →

Fairview Town Center

The retail and dining heart of town, with department stores, restaurants, an iPic dine-in theater, and free family events most of the year.

Lovejoy ISD

One of the top small school districts in Texas and the main reason most families land here, anchoring youth life from elementary through the Lovejoy Leopards.

Room to Breathe

Larger lots, a custom-home feel, and a network of parks along Sloan Creek give Fairview a calmer, more spread-out rhythm than its busier neighbors.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Fairview keeps a deep bench of small, well-kept neighborhood parks woven along Sloan Creek, tied together by roughly 12 miles of paved and natural trail. None are huge on their own, but together they give families an easy, close-to-home green space in almost every neighborhood.

Creekside Park

Fairview's first park, opened in 2005 along Sloan Creek, with a picnic pavilion, a playground with climbing equipment and swings, open greenspace, and a short paved trail. Shaded creekside setting; an easy first stop for younger kids.

Playground Creek Access Pavilion

Beaver Run Park

In the Thompson Springs neighborhood, with a wood-mulch playground, a basketball court, a large picnic pavilion, horseshoe pits, a half-mile paved trail, and a nature trail. The most amenity-packed of the town parks; good for older kids and a family walk.

Playground Basketball Nature Trail

Village Green Park

An 8-acre park with open spaces, a playground, a gazebo, and two half-court sports courts, with a trail that curves around Fairview Town Center. Handy to pair with a meal or a movie; mostly open, so go earlier in summer.

Playground Sports Courts Trails

Cypress Park

A small hidden-gem park with an artificial-turf playground, benches, picnic tables, a pavilion and grill, a water fountain, and a dog station, with a paved walkway connecting to the Roadrunner Trail. Low-traffic and shaded; nice for toddlers.

Turf Playground Picnic Dog-Friendly

Monarch Park

An easy-to-miss park in Summerhill Farms, across from Lovejoy Elementary, with an artificial-turf playground and a popular climbing rock, a large picnic pavilion, a grill, and a long greenspace for catch. Great pre- or post-school stop for Lovejoy families.

Turf Playground Climbing Rock Pavilion

The Meadows

A 3-acre park in The Villas in the Park with a turf climbing structure and swings, a three-quarter sport court, a shaded pavilion, and a walking path. The shade and turf make it a comfortable pick on warm afternoons with younger kids.

Turf Playground Sport Court Shade

Coyote Meadow

An 18-acre open-space park carrying the paved Road Runner Trail, with benches along the way and room to roam. The most open of Fairview's parks; best for a longer walk, a bike ride, or letting kids stretch their legs.

Open Space Paved Trail

Trail System Highlight

Fairview has built out roughly 12 miles of connected trail, from the Road Runner Trail through Cypress Park and Coyote Meadow to the Sloan Creek greenbelt and the long Stacy Road and Parkside paths. Great for stroller walks, first bike rides, and a quiet morning lap close to home.

Relocation Tip

Start at Beaver Run Park. It packs the most into one stop, a real playground, a basketball court, and both paved and nature trails, so you get the clearest read on what daily park life in Fairview feels like. For the full neighborhood picture, see the Fairview relocation guide.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Fairview's youth sports run mostly through the long-established Allen Sports Association, which has served Allen, Lucas, and Fairview since 1976, with school teams taking over once kids reach the Lovejoy Leopards.

Baseball & Softball

League: Allen Sports Association
Ages: T-ball through teens
Season: Spring and fall

Soccer

League: Allen Sports Association; TAG Soccer at Fairview Soccer Park
Ages: Roughly 4 through high school
Season: Fall and spring

Football & Basketball

League: Allen Sports Association
Ages: Elementary through middle school
Season: Fall football, winter basketball

Lacrosse & Ice Hockey

Program: Lovejoy Leopards (school teams)
Ages: Middle and high school
Note: Less common locally, so Lovejoy's programs are a real draw

Swimming & Diving

Where: Lovejoy Leopards swim & dive; Allen's Don Rodenbaugh Natatorium for lessons and clubs
Ages: Youth through high school
Season: Year-round indoors

School Sports (Lovejoy ISD)

Program: Lovejoy Leopards athletics
Ages: Middle and high school
Sports: Football, soccer, volleyball, basketball, baseball, softball, track, cross country, tennis, golf, wrestling, and more

Good to Know

Allen Sports Association is the backbone of recreational youth sports for Fairview families, with thousands of volunteer coaches and tens of thousands of participants across Allen, Lucas, and Fairview. Because it coordinates with Lovejoy ISD, your child can move smoothly from rec leagues into school teams.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Fairview Town Center carries most of the in-town entertainment, and Allen, McKinney, and The Colony add bigger draws within a short drive.

Fairview Town Center

Open-air center · Shopping, dining, a splash fountain kids play in, and free Family Friday and live-music events at The Park in season.

iPic Theaters

Dine-in cinema · Recliner seating and in-theater dining at Fairview Town Center, an easy date-night or treat-the-kids movie outing.

Fairview Youth Theater

Community theater · Live youth performances at Fairview Town Center, a fun outlet for stage-minded kids and an easy afternoon show.

Allen Event Center

Arena · About 10 min away, home of Allen Americans hockey plus family shows and concerts throughout the year.

Andretti Indoor Karting & Games

Karting & arcade · At Grandscape in The Colony, about 20 min away, with go-karts, an arcade, and ropes courses for older kids and teens.

Urban Air Adventure Park

Trampoline park · The nearby McKinney location adds trampolines, climbing, and obstacle courses about 10 to 15 min from Fairview.

Day Trip from Fairview

Heard Natural Science Museum & Wildlife Sanctuary (about 15 min east in McKinney), with nature trails, native wildlife, an outdoor dinosaur exhibit, and hands-on programs. One of the best all-ages outdoor learning days in Collin County.

Family-Friendly Dining

Almost all of Fairview's everyday dining sits inside Fairview Town Center, an easy walkable mix of sit-down spots and quick treats.

Gloria's Latin Cuisine — popular Tex-Mex and Salvadoran spot with a roomy patio and a kids' menu. Best weekend family dinner
AKa Hibachi & Sushi — teppanyaki tables where the chef cooks at your seat, always a kid favorite. Best dinner-and-a-show
BoomerJack's Grill & Bar — big-menu sports grill with burgers, wings, and plenty of screens. Best game-day lunch
Café Gecko Bar & Grill — bright Caribbean flavors and an easygoing room. Best try-something-new
Neon Cactus — casual taqueria for fast, fresh tacos and Tex-Mex. Best quick weeknight
Chicken Salad Chick — sandwiches, soups, and sides, light and quick with kids. Best picky-eater lunch
Revolving Kitchen — fresh, healthy prepared meals and bowls for an in-and-out bite. Best grab-and-go
K-Cup Korean — Korean comfort food for families ready to branch out. Best adventurous dinner
Cream & Sugar — ice cream and sweets, the classic after-dinner stop. Best dessert run
Churro On Top — fresh churros and treats for a quick reward. Best treat stop

Nearly all of these sit inside Fairview Town Center at Stacy Road and US 75, so dinner pairs easily with a movie, a show, or some shopping.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Fairview's marquee shopping is its own town center, with charming and upscale options just minutes away in McKinney and Allen.

Fairview Town Center

Lifestyle Center

An open-air center anchored by Dillard's, JCPenney, Macy's, Old Navy, and Academy Sports, with dining and an iPic theater. Best for: a full Saturday of shopping, a meal, and a movie in one place.

Historic Downtown McKinney

Historic Square

A walkable, award-winning square about 15 min away with boutiques, toy shops, and family-friendly cafes. Best for: a slower morning of strolling, a treat, and a real small-town feel.

Watters Creek at Montgomery Farm

Outdoor Lifestyle

An open-air Allen lifestyle center about 10 min away with creek paths, fountains, and frequent family events. Best for: shopping with little ones who need room to roam between stores.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Between the town and Fairview Town Center, the calendar stays full of free, family-first gatherings.

Free Friday-evening events at Fairview Town Center from spring into early summer, with rotating fun like jugglers, face painters, and family entertainers.

Spring – Fall

Live Music at The Park

Free Saturday-evening concerts at Fairview Town Center's outdoor stage, with a range of styles from bluegrass to rock and Americana.

Summer

Movie Nights in the Park

The town hosts outdoor movie nights and community gatherings through the warmer months, a relaxed way to meet neighbors.

Summer

Community Concerts & Picnics

Outdoor concerts and community picnics round out Fairview's summer, bringing families together at the parks and town center.

August

Back-to-School Event

An early-August community event helping families gear up for the school year, supported by local volunteers and organizations.

December

Holiday Tree Lighting & Market

A seasonal tree-lighting and holiday market with local vendors, food trucks, kids' activities, and a visit from Santa to open the season.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When summer heat or a storm settles in, Fairview families lean on the town center's theater and a tight cluster of indoor spots in neighboring Allen and McKinney.

iPic Theaters

Type: Dine-in cinema · Ages: All ages
Recliner seating and in-theater dining right at Fairview Town Center.

Allen Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: Babies through teens
The nearby Allen library runs story times and children's programs, a free rainy-day standby.

Urban Air Adventure Park

Type: Trampoline park · Ages: Toddlers to teens
Trampolines, climbing, and obstacle courses at the nearby McKinney location.

Don Rodenbaugh Natatorium

Type: Indoor pool · Ages: All ages
Allen's indoor aquatic center with lap and leisure pools, about 10 min away, for year-round swimming.

Chuck E. Cheese

Type: Indoor playground · Ages: Toddlers to tweens
The Allen location pairs arcade games and a trampoline area with pizza, a rainy-day default.

Andretti Indoor Karting & Games

Type: Karting & arcade · Ages: Older kids and teens
Go-karts, an arcade, and ropes courses at Grandscape in The Colony, about 20 min away.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

On triple-digit afternoons, Fairview families let little ones splash in the Fairview Town Center fountain, swim at Allen's Don Rodenbaugh Natatorium, or duck into iPic and the indoor spots in Allen and McKinney. Save the Sloan Creek park trails for early morning before the heat builds.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Fairview.

Fairview is a small, affluent Collin County town built around Lovejoy ISD, one of the top small districts in Texas, with a low-density, custom-home feel and creekside parks. The trade-off is that it is smaller and pricier than its neighbors, so families lean on Allen and McKinney for the larger menu of shopping, dining, and activities, both only minutes away.
  • Most recreational youth sports run through the Allen Sports Association, which has served Allen, Lucas, and Fairview since 1976 and offers baseball, softball, soccer, football, and basketball, coordinating with both Allen ISD and Lovejoy ISD. Once kids reach middle and high school, the Lovejoy Leopards field a deep slate of teams, including less common sports like lacrosse and ice hockey.

  • Fairview's city parks do not include a municipal splash pad or public pool. Kids often cool off in the interactive fountain at Fairview Town Center, and the nearest formal aquatic facilities are in Allen, including the indoor Don Rodenbaugh Natatorium and Allen's outdoor pools. Many Fairview neighborhoods also have their own community pools.

Fairview is smaller and lower-density, with Lovejoy ISD as the draw, and it leans on Allen and McKinney for the bigger amenities. Families who want a quieter, more spacious setting with elite small-district schools choose Fairview; those who want everything in town often look at Allen.

It is the quieter, more spread-out option between Allen and McKinney, trading big-suburb bustle for space, schools, and a semi-rural feel at Collin County prices.
