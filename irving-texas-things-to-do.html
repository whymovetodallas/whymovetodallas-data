What Irving Families Actually Do Here

Irving surprises families who assume it's just a stopover near the airport. The Las Colinas Urban Center is one of the most polished public-space investments in DFW, with a gondola canal, a world-class entertainment complex, and a walkable lakefront district. Beyond that core, 90 parks, 12 City recreation leagues, a 22-mile planned trail greenbelt, and a genuine petting farm at Fritz Park give families an infrastructure that rewards living here week after week. See the full Irving relocation guide.

Las Colinas Urban Center

The Mandalay Canal, Toyota Music Factory, Lake Carolyn, and Water Street development create a walkable entertainment and dining hub that's genuinely unlike anything else in DFW suburbs.

Fritz Park Petting Farm

The Animal Connection Experience at Fritz Park is a fully interactive petting zoo where kids pet, brush, and feed farm animals. A genuinely unique urban-suburb find that younger children love on repeat visits.

Campion Trail Greenbelt

A 22-mile planned greenbelt along the Elm Fork and West Fork of the Trinity River, with more than 10 miles of wide paved trail currently open to the public for biking, jogging, and stroller walks.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Irving's Parks and Recreation Department maintains more than 90 parks covering over 2,000 acres. The Campion Trail greenbelt along the Trinity River is the crown jewel, with free spray parks, family aquatic centers, and recreation centers distributed across the city's neighborhoods.

Fritz Park

A 57-acre park with basketball courts, softball fields, a disc golf course, and the standout Animal Connection Experience petting farm where kids interact with farm animals up close. Restrooms on-site. Best for ages 2 through 10 for the petting farm; all ages for the sports fields and disc golf.

Petting Farm Disc Golf Softball Fields Basketball Courts

Lee Park

A neighborhood park with a seasonal swimming pool, racquetball courts, playground, and picnic tables. The Lee Park Recreation Center adds game rooms and structured programming year-round. Good for a half-day outing with elementary-age kids. Restrooms at the rec center.

Swimming Pool Playground Racquetball Picnic Areas

Heritage Park

Located at 217 N. Main St. in Irving's Heritage District, Heritage Park is an outdoor gathering hub used for community events throughout the year including outdoor movies in September and the Holiday Extravaganza in December. A central, walkable green space in older Irving.

Event Venue Outdoor Concerts Walkable

Cimarron Park

Home to the Cimarron Family Aquatic Center (199 Red River Trail), a seasonal outdoor pool complex open summers with water features for all ages. The adjacent Cimarron Park Recreation Center adds year-round programming, courts, and fitness facilities for families.

Family Aquatic Center Recreation Center Seasonal Pool

Senter Park

The Senter Park Recreation Center at 901 S. Senter Road is undergoing a major upgrade to become the Senter Park Aquatic and Recreation Center, including a new indoor natatorium expected to open in 2026. Check irvingtx.gov for current project status and opening timeline.

New Indoor Pool (2026) Recreation Center

Lake Carolyn and Las Colinas Urban Center

The lakefront greenspace along Lake Carolyn in the Las Colinas Urban Center is a walkable public park area connecting the Mandalay Canal, restaurants, and hotel promenades. Irving hosts its annual 4th of July fireworks show here over the lake. Great for evening walks with strollers.

Lake Access Stroller-Friendly Fireworks Venue Dog Friendly

West Irving Aquatic Center

A seasonal outdoor aquatic center at 3701 Conflans Road serving west Irving families with pools and water features open summers. The city's free spray parks are separate facilities; check irvingtx.gov/splash-parks for spray park locations and current seasonal hours.

Seasonal Pool Family Aquatics Spray Parks Nearby

Trail System Highlight

Campion Trail, a 22-mile planned greenbelt along the Elm Fork and West Fork of the Trinity River, with more than 10 miles of 12-foot-wide paved concrete trail currently open. Two main segments: 6.5 miles in the Elm Fork (north) section and 4 miles in the West Fork (south) section. Great for family bike rides and weekend stroller runs.

Relocation Tip

Head to Fritz Park on a weekday morning during your first month. The petting farm is genuinely charming, parking is easy, and the disc golf course is a low-key way to explore the park while kids interact with animals. Then walk or drive to the Campion Trail north trailhead for a morning bike ride before the heat peaks. You'll cover two of Irving's best family assets in a single outing.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

The City of Irving Parks and Recreation department runs 12 seasonal leagues serving more than 5,000 participants annually, covering sports from basketball to volleyball. Irving Soccer Association is the primary club soccer outlet, and i9 Sports operates at the Irving Sports Complex for multi-sport recreational programs.

Soccer

League: Irving Soccer Association (ISA)
Ages: Ages 3U through 18U
Season: Fall (September through November) and Spring; registration typically opens months before each season. Visit irvingsoccer.org for current registration.

Basketball

League: City of Irving Parks and Recreation
Ages: Ages 7 through 12
Season: Winter; competitive and recreational leagues with a season-ending tournament. Games at Irving recreation centers.

Baseball and Softball

League: City of Irving Athletics
Ages: Various age groups
Season: Spring; games at City athletic fields. Registration through the City of Irving Parks and Recreation department.

Flag Football and Multi-Sport

League: i9 Sports Irving/Las Colinas
Ages: Ages 3 and up
Season: Spring and Fall; programs at the Irving Sports Complex covering flag football, soccer, baseball, basketball, and volleyball.

Volleyball

League: City of Irving Parks and Recreation
Ages: Various
Season: Seasonal; part of the City's 12-league calendar. Irving also has access to club volleyball programs through DFW-wide organizations for older competitive players.

City Recreation All-Sports

League: City of Irving Parks and Recreation
Ages: Various
Season: Year-round; 12 seasonal leagues spanning football, golf, gymnastics, martial arts, lacrosse, swimming, and more. See irvingtx.gov/athletics for current season registration.

Featured Sports Facility

Irving Sports Complex is the primary multi-field venue hosting i9 Sports leagues and City recreation programs. With multiple fields and courts in one location, it's the most convenient starting point for families looking to sign kids up for organized sports after relocating to Irving.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Irving's entertainment range is wider than most families expect: from a Venetian-inspired gondola canal and one of the DFW metro's largest entertainment venues to a sculpture worth stopping for and an arts center with consistent family programming throughout the year.

Toyota Music Factory

Entertainment Complex · One of DFW's largest entertainment venues with an 8,000-seat indoor-outdoor amphitheater (The Pavilion), an outdoor plaza stage, 200,000+ sq ft of restaurants and retail. Check for family-friendly shows on the calendar throughout the year.

Mandalay Canal and Gondola Adventures

Cultural Attraction · The Mandalay Canal winds through the Las Colinas Urban Center past restaurants, shops, waterfalls, and vine-covered bridges. Gondola Adventures of Las Colinas offers canal cruises, a memorable experience for families with older kids who enjoy something different from a theme park.

Irving Arts Center

Arts Center · A 10-acre complex with two fully-equipped theaters, four galleries, a sculpture garden, and 11 resident arts organizations. Rotating exhibitions, summer camps, and year-round family performances. One of the best consistent arts programming hubs in the mid-cities area.

Mustangs of Las Colinas

Public Art and Museum · Nine bronze mustang horses mid-gallop through a water fountain in Las Colinas, with an adjacent museum and exhibit explaining the sculpture's creation. Free to view outdoors. A quick, memorable stop that kids find visually striking.

Fritz Park Animal Connection Experience

Interactive Petting Farm · Kids pet, brush, and feed friendly farm animals in a fully supervised, interactive environment at Fritz Park. Open most days; check cityofirving.org for current hours and admission. A weekday morning favorite for families with toddlers and preschoolers.

West Irving Aquatic Center

Family Aquatics · The West Irving Aquatic Center at 3701 Conflans Road and the Cimarron Family Aquatic Center at 199 Red River Trail both serve families with seasonal outdoor pools. Affordable family admission, with free spray parks at separate locations across the city.

Day Trip from Irving

SEA LIFE Aquarium and Legoland Discovery Center (about 20 minutes from Irving at Grapevine Mills), with interactive LEGO building zones, 4D movies, and an immersive shark ocean tunnel at SEA LIFE. A full afternoon for ages 3 through 10. Families exploring Irving often also look at what Grapevine has to offer families given the short drive between the two cities.

Family-Friendly Dining

Irving's dining scene is anchored in Las Colinas, where 150+ restaurants line the Urban Center, Water Street, and the Toyota Music Factory district. The variety ranges from teppanyaki to Tex-Mex to handmade pizza, with a strong concentration of walkable, family-welcoming spots.

Benihana Las Colinas
Best for: Big group kids dinner
Teppanyaki chefs cook tableside with real flair, making dinner a genuine show for kids. The Las Colinas location handles large family groups well and the performance aspect keeps kids engaged through the meal.
Mattito's Tex-Mex
Best for: Neighborhood Tex-Mex weeknight
A family-welcoming Tex-Mex staple near 635 and MacArthur with generous portions, a kids-friendly menu, and the relaxed atmosphere that works for any day of the week.
Grimaldi's Pizzeria
Best for: Pre-show or post-show pizza
New York-style coal-fired pizza near the Toyota Music Factory, with a crispy thin crust that works for picky eaters and pizza purists alike. Good before or after an evening at The Pavilion.
Hugo's Invitados
Best for: Weekend dinner special occasion
Modern Mexican at the Water Street development in Las Colinas, with fresh, seasonal ingredients and a patio that works well for families on a relaxed weekend evening.
The Ranch at Las Colinas
Best for: Texas dinner with older kids
Classic Texas cuisine in an upscale-casual setting at the Marriott Las Colinas, with a range of steaks, salads, and sides that satisfy both kids who eat well and adults who want something beyond the usual.
Thirsty Lion Gastropub
Best for: Casual weekend lunch Las Colinas
Full American pub menu in Las Colinas with burgers, salads, sandwiches, and a kids' menu that covers the basics without fuss. A dependable stop when you want something everyone will eat.
Strikz Entertainment
Best for: Dinner plus activity Friday nights
Handmade pizzas, loaded nachos, burgers, and salads alongside bowling lanes and a game room. An all-in-one Friday night option when you want food and activity without two separate stops.
Pinstack Irving
Best for: Full evening out with school-age kids
Lane-side food and beverage service at a modern bowling venue that also has laser tag, bumper cars, and a rock climbing wall. The kind of place where dinner blends into 2-3 hours of activity.

Most of these restaurants concentrate in the Las Colinas Urban Center and along the MacArthur/635 corridor, making it easy to combine dinner with a walk along the Mandalay Canal or a stop at the Mustangs sculpture.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Irving's retail is concentrated in Las Colinas and the Toyota Music Factory district, where dining and entertainment integrate with shopping so naturally that a Saturday outing rarely has a clear beginning or end.

Toyota Music Factory District

Entertainment Hub

Over 200,000 square feet of restaurants, bars, and retail space surrounding an outdoor plaza and an 8,000-seat amphitheater. Walkable and lively, with something happening most weekends throughout the year.

Best for: A full Saturday that mixes browsing, dining, and catching whatever show or event is happening at The Pavilion.

Water Street at Las Colinas

Lifestyle District

A newer mixed-use development in the Las Colinas Urban Center with upscale dining, boutique retail, and walkable streets near the Mandalay Canal. A polished, low-key option for families who want quality over volume.

Best for: A relaxed weekend afternoon of browsing and dining without navigating a large mall or parking structure.

Irving Mall

Regional Mall

Irving's traditional enclosed regional mall at 3880 Irving Mall Dr., with anchor stores, a food court, and a range of standard national retailers. A practical option for everyday shopping needs and back-to-school runs.

Best for: Everyday errands, back-to-school shopping, or quick weekend retail needs without the Las Colinas premium pricing.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Irving's event calendar runs across both its older Heritage District neighborhoods and its newer Las Colinas Urban Center, giving families a mix of civic traditions and polished entertainment-district events throughout the year.

January 1

Day 1 DFW

A family-centered, all-inclusive New Year's Day event held at the Irving Convention Center. Held annually to give families a welcoming, community-focused start to the year. Check the Irving Convention Center calendar for this year's programming details.

February

Frost Fest

A free family winter fun event at Levy Event Plaza, held annually in February. Activities, entertainment, and a community gathering that gives Irving families a mid-winter reason to get outside. Check irvingtx.gov/frost-fest for this year's date and details.

Spring

CanalFest

An annual celebration along the Mandalay Canal, with cultural dances, a local art exhibit, live music, and food vendors. Held annually in spring, this is one of Irving's signature community festivals with a genuine multicultural character.

Annual

Irving-Las Colinas Jazz and Art Festival

Held annually at the Irving Arts Center, this two-day festival brings together jazz performances and visual arts exhibitions. Families attend for outdoor music on the Arts Center grounds. Check irvingartscenter.com for each year's lineup.

July 4th

4th of July at Lake Carolyn

Irving hosts a free two-day Independence Day celebration with fireworks over Lake Carolyn in the Las Colinas Urban Center. One of the best free family fireworks events in the DFW mid-cities area, with the lakefront setting adding to the atmosphere.

September

Heritage Park Movie Nights

Outdoor movies under the stars at Heritage Park on Friday nights throughout September. Bring blankets and lawn chairs. Food vendors on-site or bring your own from nearby restaurants. A low-key, community-focused family tradition.

December

Holiday Extravaganza

A parade through the Irving Heritage District followed by a tree lighting ceremony at Irving City Hall. Held annually each December, this is Irving's signature holiday family event, drawing residents from across the city to the older Heritage District neighborhood.

Indoor Activities for Kids

Irving has strong indoor options spread across its entertainment district and neighborhoods. When summer temperatures climb past 100 degrees, families rotate between trampoline parks, bowling and laser tag, the Irving Arts Center, and the public library system.

Pinstack Irving

Type: Bowling and Entertainment · Ages: All ages
Modern bowling with lane-side food service, plus laser tag, bumper cars, and a rock climbing wall all under one roof. A complete evening-out destination for families with school-age kids who want more than just bowling.

Chuck E. Cheese Irving

Type: Family Entertainment · Ages: Best for ages 3-10
The Adventure Zone indoor playground with trampolines, superhero-style obstacles, and slides for kids under 56". Plus 50+ arcade games and fresh pizza. A reliable weekday afternoon option during summer break.

Strikz Entertainment

Type: Bowling and Dining · Ages: All ages
Bowling lanes combined with a full food menu: handmade pizzas, nachos, burgers, and salads. A step up from the typical bowling alley experience and a good option for family birthday parties or Friday evening outings.

Irving Arts Center

Type: Arts and Theater · Ages: All ages
Two theaters, four galleries, and a sculpture garden on a 10-acre campus. Year-round family-oriented performances, rotating gallery exhibitions, summer arts camps, and free indoor spaces to explore. A genuine community arts anchor.

Irving Public Library System

Type: Public Library · Ages: All ages
Multiple branch locations across Irving, with children's story times, reading programs, and summer reading clubs. Free, air-conditioned, and consistently well-programmed. The Central Library branch is the largest and most fully-stocked.

Heritage Aquatic Center (All-Season Indoor)

Type: Indoor Pool · Ages: All ages
Heritage Aquatic Center at 200 S. Jefferson St. operates year-round as an all-season indoor facility, unlike Irving's seasonal outdoor aquatic centers. Lap swim, lessons, and open swim available. Check irvingtx.gov/heritage-aquatic-center for current schedule and fees.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

Irving's best indoor summer rotation: mornings at the Heritage Aquatic Center (open year-round, indoor) before peak heat, early afternoons at the Irving Public Library or Irving Arts Center (both free and air-conditioned), and evening energy burns at Pinstack or Strikz when temperatures finally drop. For toddlers and preschoolers, the free city spray parks open around Memorial Day weekend each year. Check irvingtx.gov/splash-parks for current seasonal hours and locations before heading out.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Irving.

  • Irving works well for families who value access: DFW Airport minutes away, downtown Dallas within 20 minutes, and Las Colinas giving you a polished live-work-play environment without the Dallas price tag. The city has 90+ parks, a 22-mile planned trail greenbelt, 12 seasonal recreation leagues, the Irving Arts Center, and genuinely good public amenities. The housing is more affordable than Coppell or Southlake, the commute access is better than almost any DFW suburb, and Irving ISD has been investing in its schools. It's a solid family city that rewards families who look past the airport association.
  • Irving Soccer Association (ISA) is the primary youth soccer organization, serving ages 3U through 18U with fall and spring seasons. The City of Irving Parks and Recreation department runs 12 seasonal leagues serving more than 5,000 participants annually, covering basketball (ages 7-12), baseball, softball, volleyball, football, and more. i9 Sports operates at the Irving Sports Complex offering flag football, soccer, baseball, and basketball for ages 3 and up with spring and fall seasons. The city's full athletics program also covers gymnastics, martial arts, lacrosse, swimming, and golf. Visit irvingtx.gov/athletics for current registration and program details.
  • Irving's indoor options are solid. Pinstack covers bowling, laser tag, bumper cars, and a rock climbing wall under one roof. The Irving Arts Center has galleries and theater performances throughout the year. Heritage Aquatic Center is a year-round indoor pool that stays operational regardless of weather. Chuck E. Cheese has an indoor playground for younger kids. The Irving Public Library system has multiple branches with children's story times and activities. For a longer outing, the 20-minute drive to Legoland Discovery Center or SEA LIFE Aquarium at Grapevine Mills is a reliable backup when the weather closes outdoor options.
  • Irving operates free spray parks open to the public that are separate from its paid aquatic centers. The city's seasonal outdoor aquatic centers include the Cimarron Family Aquatic Center (199 Red River Trail) and West Irving Aquatic Center (3701 Conflans Road), both open summers. The Heritage Aquatic Center (200 S. Jefferson St.) operates year-round indoors. For the free spray park locations and current seasonal open dates, check irvingtx.gov/splash-parks directly, as the city updates this page with each season's schedule. Spray parks generally open around Memorial Day weekend per city announcements.
  • Irving is known for Las Colinas, one of the most thoughtfully designed urban-center districts in the DFW metro, with the Mandalay Canal, Mustangs sculpture, Toyota Music Factory, and Lake Carolyn all within walking distance. Beyond that, the Fritz Park Animal Connection petting farm is a genuinely unique amenity for families with young children. Campion Trail offers a 22-mile planned greenbelt along the Trinity River. And Irving's location, central to the whole DFW metroplex with airport access, appeals to corporate-transferee families who want to be near everything without committing to one directional corridor.
  • Coppell and Irving share a border and a lot of families compare the two seriously. Coppell is smaller, more uniformly suburban, and known for its highly-rated school district; it has a tighter community feel, good parks, and a walkable Old Town area. Irving is larger, more diverse in its neighborhoods, and gives you Las Colinas with genuine urban-energy entertainment alongside quieter traditional suburbs. Irving ISD is a larger district; Coppell ISD is smaller and more consistently high-performing. Families who prioritize school reputation often lean Coppell; families who prioritize location flexibility, commute access, and entertainment variety often choose Irving.
