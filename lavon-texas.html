Getting Around

Where Is Lavon, Texas? Location & Commutes

Lavon sits in eastern Collin County, about 35 miles northeast of downtown Dallas. It borders Wylie to the west and south, Nevada to the north, and Lake Lavon to the northwest — a 17,000+ acre Corps of Engineers reservoir that is one of the most significant natural assets in all of northeast DFW. The city is positioned on TX-78, which connects southwest to Wylie and the George Bush Turnpike and northeast toward Greenville. TX-205 also connects north to Princeton and south toward Rockwall County.

Education

Schools in Lavon — Community ISD

Lavon is served by Community Independent School District (Community ISD) — a small Collin County district headquartered in Nevada, TX, that also serves Lavon, Josephine, and surrounding rural communities. The district earns a Niche B overall rating in 2025, with solid marks for teachers and a diverse, growing student body that reflects the area's rapid population growth.

The district's small size means students are known by name — class sizes are smaller than you'd find in Allen ISD or Frisco ISD, which appeals to many families relocating from suburban districts that feel oversized. The district has been adding capacity rapidly to keep pace with Lavon's growth.

Where to Live

Lavon Neighborhoods for Families

Lavon is a small, fast-growing city with a mix of established older sections and large master-planned new construction communities that have defined the city's growth surge since 2015. Most of what relocating families will consider falls into the new construction category.

Search Current Lavon Listings →

Grand Heritage

Lavon's signature master-planned community — large lots, resort-style amenity center with pools, trails, and fishing. Primarily new construction from $370K–$520K. HOA-managed with strong community culture. One of the best-regarded family communities in eastern Collin County.

Traditions of Lavon

Another large master-planned community with new and recent construction from $360K–$490K. Features community pools, playgrounds, and walkable streetscapes. Active HOA. Popular with young families due to pricing and amenities.

Lake Breeze Estates

Smaller established community with proximity to Lake Lavon access. Mix of older and newer homes from $320K–$440K. Quieter, less HOA-intensive feel. Good for families who want lake proximity without full master-planned community pricing.

Elevon

One of the newest planned communities (launched ~2020), still actively under construction by multiple builders. Mix of home sizes from $350K–$550K+. Modern amenity center, pool, trails. Expect active construction activity for several more years.

Lavon's older core sections (pre-2010 construction) are modest and priced well below the new construction communities — typically $240K–$330K. These can offer value for families not focused on master-planned amenities, but the school feeder patterns and neighborhood condition should be verified by address.

Financial Picture

Cost of Living in Lavon, Texas

Lavon is priced like a Collin County suburb — not as affordable as south Dallas County, but significantly more accessible than Frisco, Prosper, or Southlake. The combination of a growing inventory of new construction homes in the $360K–$500K range and a competitive property tax rate (for Collin County) makes it an appealing value proposition for families priced out of better-known suburbs.

Property Taxes in Lavon

The combined property tax rate in Lavon runs approximately $2.00–$2.05 per $100 of assessed value. On the ~$385K median home, that's roughly $7,700–$7,900/year before exemptions. Breakdown: Community ISD ~$1.2346, City of Lavon ~$0.5575, Collin County ~$0.1720. Apply for your homestead exemption immediately after closing — the $100,000 school district homestead exemption significantly reduces the ISD portion of your bill. Source: Texas Comptroller 2024 certified rates.

Lavon vs. California & New York

Texas has no state income tax. A family earning $200,000 in California typically saves $15,000–$18,000 per year in state income taxes by moving to Texas. Lavon's median home (~$385K) would be a starter condo in most Bay Area markets and mid-tier in greater New York. When families calculate their total annual position — income tax savings minus higher property taxes — most coastal families come out $20,000–$30,000 ahead per year in Lavon, even before accounting for the price difference in housing equity.

Day-to-Day Life

Lifestyle in Lavon, Texas

Lavon has a small-town, community-first character that its residents genuinely value. The city does not have a dense retail or restaurant scene of its own — daily shopping and dining happen primarily in nearby Wylie (~10 min) or Rockwall (~20 min). What Lavon does have is exceptional outdoor access, a tight-knit community feel, and new construction homes with amenity centers that bring the resort lifestyle to your neighborhood.

Small-Town Community

Lavon has annual community events, a locally-engaged city government, and a growing network of neighborhood-level connections through master-planned HOA communities. You'll know your neighbors.

New Construction Quality

The majority of Lavon's growth is in master-planned communities with new construction from major national builders — modern floor plans, energy efficiency, and amenity centers with pools, trails, and fitness facilities.

Outdoor-Centered Living

Lake Lavon defines the lifestyle — boating, fishing, kayaking, hiking, and wildlife viewing are part of everyday life. The lake has multiple public access points and Corps of Engineers parks within minutes of any Lavon address.

Remote Work Ready

New construction communities have fiber and cable broadband. Low traffic, quiet streets, and home offices that are actually big enough. Lavon is an ideal landing spot for WFH families seeking space without the premium North Dallas address.

Tight-Knit HOA Communities

Grand Heritage, Traditions of Lavon, and Elevon all have active HOA boards, community pools, social events, and strong neighbor networks. Families who want to feel connected quickly find these master-planned communities make it easy.

Collin County Quality of Life

Lavon sits in Collin County — one of the best-governed county jurisdictions in Texas. Strong infrastructure investment, excellent emergency services, low county tax rate, and long-range planning for the growth corridor.

Shopping & Dining Near Lavon

Lavon proper has minimal commercial development — expect a handful of local restaurants, a gas station, and basic services. For groceries and everyday retail, Wylie (10–15 min on TX-78) is the primary destination, with HEB, Kroger, and Target. Rockwall (20 min via TX-205) offers The Harbor mixed-use development with waterfront dining, retail, and a cinema. Families who prioritize walkability to retail should look at Wylie or Rockwall instead — Lavon's value proposition is space, affordability, and lake access, not urban convenience.

Parks & Recreation

Outdoor Recreation in & Around Lavon

Lake Lavon — The Crown Jewel

Lake Lavon is a 17,077-acre Corps of Engineers reservoir that wraps around the western and northern edges of the city. It's one of the largest lakes in northeast DFW and offers boating, fishing (largemouth bass, catfish, crappie), kayaking, swimming beaches, hiking trails, and wildlife viewing. Multiple parks provide public access: East Fork Park, Collin Park, Pebble Beach Park, and Lavonia Park among others. For families who love outdoor life, having a lake of this scale within a 5–10 minute drive is genuinely exceptional.

City Parks & Community Amenities

Lavon City Park anchors the city's public park system with playgrounds, sports fields, and open space. Master-planned communities add private amenity centers (pools, fitness, playgrounds, trails) that are often the primary recreation hub for their residents. The combination of public lake access and private community amenities gives Lavon families more outdoor infrastructure than most small Texas cities of its size.

Walkability & Getting Around

Lavon is car-dependent for all errands and commuting — a Walk Score of roughly 5/100. However, within newer master-planned communities like Grand Heritage, internal trail systems allow walking and biking within the neighborhood. The city is working on connectivity improvements as it grows. For daily errands, expect to drive to Wylie or Rockwall.

Recreation Center & Facilities

Lavon does not currently have a dedicated city recreation center, but the master-planned community amenity centers (Grand Heritage, Traditions of Lavon, Elevon) provide fitness facilities, pools, and event spaces for HOA members. The City of Wylie Recreation Center (10 min, TX-78) offers the nearest full-service municipal recreation center with indoor courts, fitness equipment, and youth programming.

Youth Sports & Activities

Community ISD supports athletics and extracurricular activities at scale for the district size. Lavon's master-planned HOA communities have organized youth programming. The Wylie and Rockwall recreation departments are within 15–20 minutes and offer extensive youth sports leagues for families wanting more program variety.

Safety

Is Lavon, Texas Safe?

Lavon earns an A safety grade from Niche — one of the strongest safety profiles in this guide. Violent crime is very low (~1.2 per 1,000 residents, well below the national average of ~4.0), and property crime is minimal for a city of its size. The small-town character and strong homeownership rates create a low-crime environment that families consistently cite as a top reason they chose Lavon.

The Honest Take

Pros & Cons of Living in Lavon, Texas

Pros

  • Lake Lavon access — 17,000+ acres of boating, fishing, and recreation literally next door. A rare lifestyle asset at this price point.
  • Exceptional safety — Niche A safety grade, violent crime well below national average. One of the safest communities in northeast DFW.
  • Collin County quality — Strong county-level governance, infrastructure investment, and school district trajectory in Community ISD.
  • New construction value — Large lots, modern floor plans, builder incentives, and amenity-rich communities from $360K–$500K.
  • Affordability vs. peers — Meaningfully less expensive than Wylie, Rockwall, or Heath while sharing the Collin County/eastern DFW lifestyle.
  • Remote work friendly — Space, quiet, fiber internet in newer communities, and low traffic make WFH life genuinely comfortable.

Cons

  • Long commute — Downtown Dallas is 40–50 minutes. Daily commuters must use the George Bush Turnpike (toll costs add up).
  • No DART access — Fully car-dependent. No commuter rail or light rail alternative planned for this area.
  • Minimal local retail — No walkable town center or substantial grocery/restaurant scene. All shopping requires a drive to Wylie or Rockwall.
  • Active construction zone — Fast growth means ongoing construction activity throughout multiple neighborhoods. Road expansions and builder noise are part of the picture for several more years.
  • Smaller ISD — Community ISD is improving but smaller than Wylie ISD or Rockwall ISD in program breadth. Families with specialized needs (gifted programs, performing arts, athletics) may find more at neighboring ISDs.
  • Limited established character — As a fast-growing small city, Lavon is still building its identity. Less established than Wylie, Rockwall, or Allen in terms of amenities, community events, and local business ecosystem.
