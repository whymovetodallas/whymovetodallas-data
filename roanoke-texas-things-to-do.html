What Roanoke Families Actually Do Here

Roanoke is a small city in northwest Denton County with an outsized reputation for two things: a park system built for active families, and a restaurant row that earned an official state designation. For families relocating to the Northwest ISD corridor, Roanoke sits in a sweet spot between the wide-open feel of a small town and easy access to everything the larger DFW metro offers. Families who love Roanoke often also check out Keller's family activities just to the south. See the full Roanoke relocation guide →

Unique Dining Capital

The Texas House of Representatives officially named Roanoke the Unique Dining Capital of Texas. Over 60 restaurants line Oak Street and the surrounding blocks, covering everything from Southern comfort to authentic Italian and Thai.

Parks and Splash Pads

Nine city parks are spread across Roanoke, including two with splash pads open May through September, a community pool with slides, a skate park, a fishing pier, and lighted walking trails throughout.

Year-Round Events

From biweekly Evenings on Oak Street concerts to the July 3rd fireworks festival and October's Celebrate Roanoke, the city keeps the calendar full of free events for families throughout the year.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Roanoke maintains 9 city parks with lighted walking trails totaling more than 2 miles of paths, two splash pads, a community pool, and a dedicated skate park, all within a city of about 10,000 people.

Cannon Parkway Park

The park most families head to first. It has a splash pad (open May 1 through Sept. 30, 9 AM to 8 PM), basketball and tennis courts, sand volleyball, a 0.34-mile walking trail, and the full Roanoke Skate Park. Pavilion with shade and restrooms make it stroller-friendly for all ages.

Splash Pad Skate Park Tennis Trails Pavilion

Parks of Roanoke

The city's other splash pad park, with the same May 1 through Sept. 30 schedule, plus a lighted 0.34-mile trail, basketball, a large open field, and shaded playground equipment. Swings and a compact layout make it ideal for younger kids on weekday afternoons.

Splash Pad Playground Basketball Lighted Trail

Roanoke Community Park

The city's hub for organized sports and fishing. It has a softball field, a Little League baseball diamond, a fishing pier on a small pond, a shaded pavilion, restrooms, and a 0.51-mile walking trail that loops around the pond. Flat and paved trail is excellent for strollers.

Fishing Pier Baseball Softball Pond Trail Restrooms

FairPlay Park

Home to the Roanoke Community Pool, which features slides and water play features. The park also has softball and baseball fields, a soccer field, playground equipment, and multiple trail loop options from 0.18 to 0.44 miles. Restrooms are on-site.

Community Pool Soccer Field Playground Trails

Briarwyck Park

A quiet neighborhood park near Marshall Creek with playground equipment, picnic tables, benches, and a walking trail. Lower traffic on weekday mornings makes it ideal for toddlers and younger kids who prefer uncrowded play spaces.

Playground Picnic Tables Walking Trail

Country Acres Park

A compact neighborhood park with basketball, playground equipment, swings, and a pavilion with picnic tables. Good quick-stop park after school or on evenings when kids just need to burn some energy without a long drive.

Basketball Playground Swings Pavilion

Linear Park

A dedicated 0.37-mile lighted walking trail along Howe Road with benches along the route. Best suited for evening walks and light jogging. The lighting makes it usable year-round after work hours, which is a practical detail parents with busy schedules appreciate.

Lighted Trail Benches

Fairway Ranch Park

Tucked into the Fairway Ranch neighborhood with a playground, shaded pavilion, and picnic tables. The suburban setting keeps crowds light, making it a reliable weekday option for parents with young children who need a low-key outdoor break.

Playground Pavilion Picnic Tables

Trail System Highlight

Roanoke Community Park Pond Trail, a 0.51-mile loop wrapping around the park's fishing pond, is the longest single trail in the city's park system. Great for morning walks with a stroller and easy fishing outings with younger kids.

Relocation Tip

Start with Cannon Parkway Park on your first visit. It gives you the clearest picture of what Roanoke offers: splash pad, skate park, tennis and volleyball courts, and a shaded pavilion all in one location. It's the park that convinces most relocating families this city is built for active kids.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Roanoke Parks and Recreation runs several youth leagues directly out of the Recreation Center on Roanoke Road, with games also held at NISD facilities. The city's soccer complex on Roanoke Road hosts independent youth leagues throughout the season.

Basketball

League: Roanoke Parks and Recreation
Ages: Kindergarten through 6th grade (boys and girls)
Season: Winter
Games are played at the Roanoke Recreation Center (501 Roanoke Rd) and various NISD facilities. Players in 3rd grade and up are drafted after a coach-look session. Contact: 817-837-9937.

Volleyball

League: Roanoke Parks and Recreation
Ages: 8 and under, 10 and under, 12 and under (girls)
Season: Spring and Fall
Two divisions available: a team division for experienced players who supply their own coach, and a recreational individual-signup division with jerseys provided and volunteer coaches needed.

Softball

League: Roanoke Parks and Recreation (ASA sanctioned)
Ages: 6 and under (T-ball) through 12 and under
Season: Spring (March through May) and Fall (September through November)
Games played at Community Park and FairPlay Park. Multiple age divisions including coach-pitch 8U and competitive 10U and 12U brackets.

Soccer

League: Youth leagues at Roanoke Soccer Complex
Ages: Varies by organization
Season: Spring and Fall
The Roanoke Soccer Complex at 505 Roanoke Rd hosts youth soccer leagues organized through independent youth sports associations. Contact Roanoke Parks and Recreation at 817-837-9937 for current season information.

Skateboarding

Facility: Roanoke Skate Park at Cannon Parkway Park
Ages: All ages, free and open daily
Year-round
A full skate park with a pool-style bowl with pool coping, a 7-stair hubba, front and backside hook ledges, a mini ramp with extension, and a slappy/manual pad feature. Helmets strongly recommended.

Featured Sports Facility

Roanoke Recreation Center at 501 Roanoke Rd is a 38,000-square-foot facility with a full weight room, gym space, and programming for all ages. Youth sports registrations are processed here in person. Open Monday through Thursday 5 AM to 9 PM and Friday 7 AM to 4 PM. Phone: 817-837-9930.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Roanoke keeps its own entertainment close to home with a skate park, community pool, and history museum, while placing families within 20 minutes of major regional attractions in Grapevine and Keller.

Roanoke Skate Park

Skate Park · Located at Cannon Parkway Park, this full-featured park has a pool-style bowl with pool coping and tiles, a 7-stair with hubba ledge and handrail, multiple ramps, and a mini ramp with extension. Free and open to all ages year-round. Draws a regular crowd on weekend afternoons.

Roanoke Community Pool

Aquatics · Located at FairPlay Park, the community pool has water slides and play features for kids of various ages. A seasonal option for families who want a swimming destination close to home without driving to a larger regional waterpark.

Roanoke Visitor Center and Museum

Local History Museum · At 114 N. Oak Street, this free museum showcases local and regional artifacts. Open Wednesday, Thursday, and Saturday noon to 6 PM; Friday noon to 8 PM. Good rainy-day stop for kids curious about the area's history, paired easily with a walk down Oak Street for lunch.

Oak Street Dining & Strolling

Walkable Dining District · The heart of Roanoke is a walkable stretch where families can browse boutique shops, grab dessert at Oak St. Pie Co., and spend an entire afternoon working through the lineup of unique restaurants. The Evenings on Oak Street concert series adds live music April through June.

Day Trip from Roanoke

SEA LIFE Grapevine Aquarium (about 20 minutes southeast), featuring over 35 ocean species in walk-through tunnels and touch pools, is the most popular full-day family outing from Roanoke. The Legoland Discovery Center in Grapevine is in the same complex, making it easy to combine both into one trip for families with kids ages 3 to 10.

Family-Friendly Dining

Roanoke holds an official state designation as the Unique Dining Capital of Texas, with over 60 restaurants concentrated along Oak Street and the surrounding blocks. The range runs from legendary fried chicken to scratch-made Italian pasta to organic Thai, all within walking distance of each other.

Babe's Chicken Dinner House
Best family weekend dinner
A Roanoke institution since 1993. Fried chicken and chicken-fried steak served family style with homemade mashed potatoes, biscuits, and creamy gravy. Kids eat here confidently. Expect a wait on weekends.
The Classic Cafe
Best date night with a sitter
New American cuisine in a charming cottage with an on-site garden. Menu changes seasonally. Open since 1993 and still the neighborhood's go-to special-occasion spot.
Inzo Italian Kitchen
Best picky-eater friendly
Established in 2010 by native Italian chef Daniele Puelo. House-made pasta, extensive wine list, indoor and outdoor seating. Kids who like pasta are right at home here.
Craft and Vine Taproom and Eatery
Best weekend brunch
Farm-to-fork kitchen with 72 craft beers, gourmet sandwiches, salads, and Sunday brunch. The third-floor patio has good views of downtown. More relaxed for older kids.
Thai Monkey
Best quick weeknight dinner
Authentic Thai with organic vegetables and locally-sourced ingredients delivered daily. Pad Thai and curry dishes travel well for families who prefer takeout during the week.
Oak St. Pie Co.
Best post-dinner dessert stop
Scratch-made pies, pastries, and fudge at 110 N. Oak St. Open since 2006. A mandatory stop on any Oak Street afternoon. Kids head straight for the fudge counter.
Oak and Main
Best with toddlers
Wood-fired grill with a family- and pet-friendly outdoor patio. Booths and outdoor tables work well for parents with younger kids. More casual than The Classic Cafe, friendlier for unpredictable toddler behavior.
Oak St. Food Hall
Best when the family can't agree
A chef-driven food hall with multiple concepts under one roof. Useful when you have multiple kids with different opinions about dinner, or when you want to sample several things on one visit.
3 Vino
Best for parents after bedtime
Wine bar on Main Street with an extensive collection of local, domestic, and international varietals paired with charcuterie boards. More of a parents-only evening destination.

Most of these are concentrated along Oak Street and the surrounding blocks in downtown Roanoke, making it easy to walk between restaurants and shops in an afternoon.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Roanoke's shopping is split between a walkable downtown district built for browsing and a practical Highway 114 corridor strip for everyday household needs.

Downtown Oak Street

Walkable District

The city's signature commercial strip, where over 60 restaurants sit alongside boutique shops, a pie bakery, and the Visitor Center Museum. This is Roanoke's community living room. You can spend a full Saturday afternoon here without running out of things to do.

Best for: a full Saturday family outing with dining, dessert, and light browsing all walkable.

Roanoke Crossing

Everyday Retail

At the intersection of Highway 114 and Highway 377, this major retail strip includes a Walmart Supercenter, Home Depot, Ross, Marshalls, Kohl's, PetSmart, and Dollar Tree. Everything a new family needs to stock a house after a move, all within a few minutes of home.

Best for: weekly errands and post-move household setup without navigating a large mall.

Alliance Town Center

Lifestyle Center

About 12 minutes east in Fort Worth, this premium open-air center has Target, Whole Foods, dozens of dining options, and a full entertainment lineup. Larger family shopping trips that call for more options than Roanoke Crossing can handle typically land here.

Best for: bigger back-to-school shopping runs and evenings that pair dinner with a movie or walk.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Roanoke keeps most of its signature events free for families, with most happening on or near Oak Street. The city's events department runs a full calendar from February through December.

February

Valentine's Dance

A family-friendly evening dance event hosted by Roanoke Parks and Recreation. A lower-key event that draws families looking for something beyond a restaurant dinner in February.

March

Fishing in the Park

A morning fishing event held at Roanoke Community Park, typically from 9 AM to noon. Targeted at younger kids and families new to fishing. A great first outdoor activity for the spring season.

March / April

Eggapalooza

An Easter egg hunt held at the Roanoke Soccer Complex with age-group divisions so younger kids aren't competing against older ones. One of the most popular family events of the spring season.

Apr–Jun

Evenings on Oak Street

A free biweekly outdoor concert series at Austin Street Plaza running from April through June. Local artists perform every other Thursday evening from 6:30 to 8 PM. Families bring chairs and kids run around the plaza area.

May

Taste and Tunes on Oak Street

A food and music event featuring live entertainment, food trucks, food competitions, and vendors along Oak Street. Held annually since 2017 in various formats; check the city events page for this year's lineup and schedule.

July

July 3rd All American Fireworks & Festival

Roanoke's signature summer event, held the evening of July 3rd with live entertainment, food, vendors, family activities, and a fireworks display. Check the city events page for this year's entertainment lineup and set times.

October

Celebrate Roanoke

The city's signature fall festival, held annually since 1998. Western entertainment, live music, food, artisan vendors, children's activities, a talent competition, reenactments, a motorcycle rodeo, and street dances in downtown Roanoke.

October

Trick or Treat on Oak Street

A Halloween event on Oak Street where participating businesses hand out candy to costumed kids. A safe, walkable alternative to neighborhood trick-or-treating and a good reason to explore the downtown corridor with younger children.

November / December

Hometown Holiday

Roanoke's holiday season event with seasonal decorations, community gatherings, and family activities in the downtown area. Followed by Breakfast with Santa in December for younger children.

Full calendar: Roanoke official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

When July heat hits 100 degrees, Roanoke families cycle through a reliable indoor rotation that covers everything from the city's own rec center to trampoline parks and bowling in nearby communities.

Roanoke Recreation Center

Type: Gym and fitness · Ages: All ages
A 38,000-square-foot facility at 501 Roanoke Rd with a weight room, gym space, and youth programming. Open weekday mornings as early as 5 AM. The air conditioning alone makes it a go-to in July and August when outdoor activities are impractical before 7 PM.

Roanoke Visitor Center and Museum

Type: Local history museum · Ages: All ages
Free admission at 114 N. Oak St. Open Wednesday, Thursday, and Saturday noon to 6 PM; Friday noon to 8 PM. Combines well with a walk down Oak Street. A genuinely useful rainy-day stop that costs nothing and gives kids a sense of where they live.

Urban Air Trampoline and Adventure Park

Type: Trampoline park · Ages: All ages
About 10 minutes south in Keller. Trampolines, climbing walls, an adventure obstacle course, a warrior course, and a dedicated toddler area. A full-afternoon option for kids ages 3 through 14 that wears everyone out by dinner.

Brunswick Zone

Type: Bowling and arcade · Ages: All ages
About 15 minutes away in Watauga with 40 bowling lanes, billiard tables, and arcade games. Cosmic bowling on weekend evenings. A reliable backup when the parks are too hot and you need something with a specific activity structure for kids of different ages.

SEA LIFE Grapevine Aquarium

Type: Aquarium · Ages: All ages
About 20 minutes southeast. Walk-through ocean tunnels, touch pools, and over 35 species on display. Fully air-conditioned and typically 2 to 3 hours for families with young kids. Tickets are timed-entry, so buying online in advance is standard practice for summer visits.

Northwest ISD Library Programs

Type: Library · Ages: All ages
Northwest ISD school libraries and the Denton County library branches serving the area run summer reading programs, story times, and STEM events throughout the school year. Free and accessible, with programming specifically designed for families with school-age children.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When July temperatures push past 100 degrees, Roanoke families rely on a three-stop indoor rotation: the Recreation Center in the early morning before the heat peaks, the Visitor Center and Museum on Oak Street for a free midday activity, and Urban Air in Keller for a full afternoon burn. It covers morning, midday, and afternoon without repeating anything or spending a lot.
