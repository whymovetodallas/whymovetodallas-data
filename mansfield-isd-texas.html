Mansfield ISD Overview

~35,000 Students Enrolled
47 Campuses
B (88) TEA Grade 2024–25
A Niche Grade (3rd-party)
5 Traditional High Schools
$1.15 Tax Rate per $100 (2024)

Mansfield ISD offers something most DFW suburbs of its size do not: genuine program variety at the high school level. Five traditional 9-12 high schools, a dedicated Career and Technology Academy, and an Early College High School give a 35,000-student district the breadth of academic pathways you would expect from a much larger system. Add in two standalone STEM Academies and a Two-Way Dual Language option at the elementary level, and the district has built one of the more comprehensive choice-program menus in Tarrant County.

The district earns a TEA B (88) and a Niche A. For a district of its size serving a demographically diverse student population, the B (88) score reflects above-average academic performance and a strong 95% graduation rate. The honest context: Mansfield is not Southlake Carroll or Highland Park in terms of academic prestige. It is a district that performs well consistently, offers multiple high school and specialty pathway options, and serves a large geographic footprint that includes both the Mansfield city core and a significant portion of South Arlington.

That South Arlington inclusion is the single most important geographic fact about Mansfield ISD for home buyers. A meaningful slice of South Arlington falls within Mansfield ISD rather than Arlington ISD. This creates real opportunity for buyers who want Mansfield ISD academics but prefer the city of Arlington's infrastructure, restaurant options, and access to DFW Airport. It also creates real confusion for buyers who assume "Arlington address" automatically means "Arlington ISD."

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Want five high school options and a large district's program variety at a suburban price point
  • Have a student interested in STEM Academy, Career and Technology pathways, or Early College coursework
  • Want a Two-Way Dual Language option beginning in kindergarten
  • Are buying in the Mansfield city core or South Arlington and want consistent, above-average academics
  • Value a 95% district graduation rate as a meaningful outcome signal

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • Your priority is a small district where a single campus defines the community identity
  • You want a TEA A rating at any cost and will choose housing accordingly
  • Your location geometry pulls you toward Midlothian, Crowley, or Arlington ISD rather than the Mansfield core
  • You are specifically shopping for the IB programme, which Mansfield ISD does not offer
South Arlington Boundary Alert

Not every South Arlington address falls in Mansfield ISD, and not every Mansfield ISD address is in the city of Mansfield. The district covers portions of South Arlington, Kennedale, and reaches into other communities near its growing perimeter. Always verify your specific street address through the Mansfield ISD school locator at mansfieldisd.org or the TEA school directory before making any purchase decision.

TEA Accountability Rating

Mansfield ISD earned a TEA score of 88 (B) in 2024-25. This places the district in the upper tier of Tarrant County's B-rated districts, tied with HEB ISD on numeric score and significantly above the statewide median for Texas ISDs. The B reflects a district that is performing above average across its student population, not one that is falling short of potential.

TEA's accountability framework scores districts across three domains. Student Achievement captures STAAR performance, graduation rates, and college/career/military readiness outcomes. At 95%, Mansfield's graduation rate is one of the stronger signals in its accountability data. School Progress measures academic growth relative to comparable districts statewide. Closing the Gaps evaluates equity across student subgroups including economically disadvantaged, English learner, and special education populations.

For a district of Mansfield's size (35,000+ students) and demographic profile, an 88 is a meaningful accomplishment. Scaling academic performance across five high schools, multiple choice programs, and a diverse geographic service area is genuinely harder than doing so in a small, uniform suburban district. The 88 score across that complexity is evidence of operational consistency. For campus-level detail, the district's accountability page at mansfieldisd.org and txschools.gov publish individual campus scores, which will vary meaningfully across 47 campuses.

Academics

All five traditional high schools in Mansfield ISD offer Advanced Placement courses and dual credit pathways. AP access is districtwide. Dual credit opportunities are available through Tarrant County College (TCC), allowing students to earn simultaneous college credit while completing high school requirements. The Early College High School takes this further: students in the Early College program can complete an associate degree while finishing high school, making it the most accelerated academic pathway in the district.

Mansfield's graduation rate of 95% (2024-25) places the district in the top tier among large Texas ISDs. The CCMR (College, Career, or Military Ready) graduation indicator, which captures students who met an additional readiness standard beyond simply receiving a diploma, is available by school year at txschools.gov. Individual campus STAAR data is available at the Texas Tribune's Schools Explorer.

The Niche A rating reflects the combination of academic performance data and strong parent and student satisfaction scores. For a district with five high schools and 47 total campuses, a consistent A from Niche signals that the overall experience across those campuses is positive, not just at the most selective or highest-performing campus. Campus-level Niche and GreatSchools ratings vary; the district average masks meaningful individual variation that families should research for their specific assigned campus.

Notable Campuses

Mansfield ISD's seven high school campuses (five traditional, one career-focused, one early college) give the district a breadth of secondary school options unusual for a district this size. Which campus your student attends is determined by your home address for traditional schools, and by application for the specialty campuses.

Mansfield High School

High School (Grades 9-12) Mansfield AP & Dual Credit

The original and flagship campus in the district, serving the Mansfield city core. Offers AP coursework and dual credit through TCC. Strong athletics and fine arts tradition anchored in the historic heart of the district.

Mansfield Summit High School

High School (Grades 9-12) Mansfield AP & Dual Credit

One of the district's newer comprehensive high schools. Serves students in the growing northern portion of the Mansfield ISD service area. Consistent AP offerings and an active extracurricular culture. Verify the attendance zone for your specific address.

Mansfield Timberview High School

High School (Grades 9-12) Arlington / Mansfield area AP & Dual Credit

Serves students in portions of South Arlington that fall within Mansfield ISD. For families buying in that corridor, Timberview is often the assigned campus. AP and dual credit available. The campus reflects the district's geographic bridge between Mansfield city and its Arlington service area.

Mansfield Lake Ridge High School

High School (Grades 9-12) Mansfield AP & Dual Credit

Located in the southern and growing portion of Mansfield's service area. Lake Ridge serves newer residential developments near the southern Mansfield boundary. Offers a full AP program and dual credit access like all district high schools.

Mansfield Legacy High School

High School (Grades 9-12) Mansfield AP & Dual Credit

The district's fifth traditional high school, serving its area of the Mansfield ISD boundary. Like the other four comprehensive campuses, Legacy offers AP courses and dual credit opportunities. Newer physical plant than the original Mansfield High campus.

Career & Technology Academy (CTECH)

High School (Grades 9-12) Application Required CTE Pathways

A dedicated high school campus for career-focused students, not merely a program within a traditional high school. CTECH offers structured CTE pathways in fields including health science, information technology, and business. This level of investment in a standalone career-focused campus is uncommon even among large Texas ISDs. Application required; space is not guaranteed.

Early College High School

High School (Grades 9-12) Application Required Associate Degree Pathway

Students in the Early College program can earn a college associate degree while completing high school graduation requirements. This is the district's most academically accelerated pathway and the most significant college cost-reduction option available in Mansfield ISD. Not a dual credit add-on but a fully structured program. Application required; speak with Student Services about eligibility criteria and the application window.

Specialty Programs

STEM Academy (Grades 6-12)

Access: Application required; transfer application deadline is February 1. Two standalone STEM Academy campuses serve middle and high school students. The STEM program provides immersive, hands-on science, technology, engineering, and math instruction with an emphasis on critical thinking and project-based learning. Space is limited and based on availability. Transportation is the parent's responsibility unless provided at your campus. Do not use STEM Academy access as a home purchase assumption without confirming seat availability for the relevant school year.

Two-Way Dual Language (Spanish/English)

Campuses: D.P. Morris Elementary, Erma Nash Elementary, Charlotte Anderson Preparatory Academy. Access: Application required; parents must attend an in-person information meeting before submitting a Kindergarten application. The program is a 50% Spanish / 50% English instructional model across all core subjects. Transportation is provided based on campus assignment. Families must commit to keeping their child in the program through the elementary years. The Charlotte Anderson Preparatory Academy adds an AVID (Advancement Via Individual Determination) focus to the Dual Language model.

Advanced Placement (AP)

Campuses: All five traditional high schools, plus Early College. Districtwide AP access means a student at any of the five traditional high schools has the ability to take AP coursework. Teacher recommendation or prerequisite coursework may apply to specific courses. For families with high-achieving students, confirming the AP catalog breadth at the specific assigned campus is more useful than the districtwide average.

Dual Credit (TCC Partnership)

Access: Available at all traditional high schools through Tarrant County College. Eligible students earn simultaneous high school and college credit. The Early College High School takes dual credit further into a full associate degree pathway. Transportation to TCC campuses for traditional dual credit is the parent's responsibility unless courses are taught on-site. A useful first step for college-cost-conscious families regardless of campus assignment.

Fine Arts

Access: Districtwide, all levels. Mansfield ISD maintains active band, choir, orchestra, and visual arts programs across elementary, middle, and high school campuses. All five high schools have competitive fine arts programs with regional participation. Fine arts is integrated into the standard curriculum at all grade levels, not siloed as a magnet or specialty option.

Gifted & Talented (GT)

Access: Districtwide via state identification process. GT services are available at neighborhood campuses. Families relocating with a GT-identified student should contact the Mansfield ISD Curriculum and Instruction department for documentation requirements and to understand the scope of services at the specific assigned campus. Identification is a state process; carry all prior evaluation documents from the home state.

Enrollment & Transfers

New Student Registration (2025-26)

New student enrollment is managed through the Mansfield ISD Student Central portal at mansfieldisd.org/student-central/enrollment-registration. Required documents typically include proof of residency within Mansfield ISD boundaries, the student's birth certificate, current immunization records, and prior school records. Contact your assigned campus directly for mid-year enrollment.

Out-of-District Transfers (Destination Mansfield)

Mansfield ISD accepts out-of-district transfer requests through its "Destination Mansfield" program. Transfers are granted based on available space at the receiving campus and are not guaranteed. Visit the district's transfer page at mansfieldisd.org/student-central/destination-mansfield for current-year application windows and requirements. Intra-district transfers (to a campus other than your assigned school within the district) follow a separate process through Student Services.

Specialty Campus Applications

The STEM Academy, Career and Technology Academy, and Early College High School all require separate applications. The STEM Academy transfer application deadline is February 1 for the following school year. For CTECH and Early College, contact Mansfield ISD Student Services for current application windows and eligibility requirements. Admission to all specialty campuses is based on available space and is not guaranteed. Do not select a home based on proximity to a specialty campus without confirming that a seat is available for your child's school year and grade level.

Special Education & IEP/504 Transfers

Texas law requires Mansfield ISD to convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment to review, adopt, or revise an existing IEP or 504 plan. Bring all prior evaluation reports, the current IEP or 504 document, and any supplementary assessment documentation. Contact the Mansfield ISD Special Education department before your child's first day to discuss campus-level services and the transition process. Parent experience with special education services varies across 47 campuses; verify specific services for your child's need profile directly with the district.

Boundary & Address Verification

Mansfield ISD's service area is larger and more geographically complex than its name suggests. The district extends well beyond the city of Mansfield and includes portions of South Arlington, parts of Kennedale, and other communities near the district's growing edges. Understanding the boundary is particularly important for buyers in two areas: South Arlington and the fast-growing new development corridors in South Mansfield near the Johnson County line.

South Arlington: The Most Misunderstood Boundary in the District

South Arlington is the area where most buyer confusion occurs. A significant portion of South Arlington, particularly the area south of a roughly east-west line through the mid-city, falls within Mansfield ISD rather than Arlington ISD. For buyers, this matters because the two districts have different TEA ratings, different high school campus options, and different tax rates. A buyer assuming "Arlington address = Arlington ISD" will miss Mansfield ISD homes in South Arlington that may be a better fit for their priorities.

The reverse is also possible: a buyer specifically seeking Mansfield ISD may choose a South Arlington address that actually falls in Arlington ISD. The boundary does not follow obvious streets or landmarks consistently. Address-level verification is mandatory, not optional.

Kennedale and the Southern Growth Corridors

Some addresses in or near Kennedale fall within Mansfield ISD, while others fall in Kennedale ISD or Arlington ISD. New construction in the southern growth corridors of the district can be especially ambiguous, as development is outpacing neighborhood awareness of school district boundaries. If you are considering new construction near the southern edges of the Mansfield ISD footprint, verify the school assignment directly with the builder and then confirm it independently at the Mansfield ISD school locator before signing.

High School Attendance Zones

With five traditional high schools and two specialty campuses, the attendance zone question at the secondary level is more complex in Mansfield ISD than in most Tarrant County districts. Your address determines which of the five traditional high schools you are zoned for. If you have a strong preference for a specific campus (or if campus-level TEA scores matter to your decision), verify the zone before you buy. Campus ratings vary within the district, and the zone boundary between Legacy and Mansfield High, or between Timberview and Summit, can be a single street apart in some neighborhoods.

How to Verify

Use the Mansfield ISD school locator at mansfieldisd.org or the TEA school directory at tea.texas.gov. For high school zone confirmation, contact Mansfield ISD Student Services directly at (817) 299-6300.

Tax Rate & Finances

Mansfield ISD's 2024 certified total tax rate is $1.1469 per $100 of assessed value (M&O: $0.7869, I&S: $0.36). The board adopted this rate in August 2024, representing a slight decrease from the prior year's $1.1492. The I&S rate of $0.36 has held steady for four consecutive years, reflecting stable bond debt service from the district's capital program. The district has consistently indicated that the current M&O rate is at or near the statutory floor, meaning further reductions would require legislative changes to the school funding formula.

What the Rate Means for a Monthly Payment

Texas homeowners receive a $100,000 homestead exemption on school district taxes. On a $400,000 home with the exemption applied, the taxable value is $300,000. At $1.1469 per $100, annual Mansfield ISD school taxes on that home run approximately $3,441, or about $287 per month. On a $475,000 home (taxable at $375,000), the figure rises to about $4,301 annually, or $358 per month. Mansfield home prices in the city core tend to be slightly above mid-market for Tarrant County, so the blended monthly payment for a Mansfield ISD home buyer will vary significantly by where within the district's footprint you are buying.

Rate Comparison to Neighbors

Mansfield ISD's $1.1469 total rate sits in the mid-range for Tarrant County. It is higher than Keller ISD ($1.09) and Fort Worth ISD ($1.06), comparable to Birdville ISD ($1.20) in overall territory, and lower than HEB ISD ($1.3875). For buyers comparing Mansfield ISD against Arlington ISD, the rate difference is a factor worth building into a budget model given that South Arlington addresses could fall in either district.

How Mansfield ISD Compares

Families considering Mansfield ISD most often cross-shop it against Arlington ISD (for South Arlington addresses), Midlothian ISD (for families near the south growth corridor), and Crowley ISD (for the northwest perimeter of Mansfield's service area). Each district serves a different buyer profile.

Families who want the highest possible academic rating at any price often look past Mansfield to Midlothian ISD, accepting a smaller district and a longer drive to DFW metro employment. Families who want the program variety of a large district and are comfortable with the B rating find Mansfield a strong fit. Arlington ISD is the relevant comparison for South Arlington buyers: those addresses could fall in either district, and the choice between them should be made with full academic, geographic, and tax data in hand.

Community Feel

Mansfield is an established suburban city with genuine local identity: a historic downtown, strong booster club culture across five high schools, and a growth trajectory that has brought new development to its southern and eastern edges without entirely replacing the small-town feel of the original community. The five-high-school structure creates natural cross-town rivalries in athletics and fine arts that anchor a lot of community engagement. Friday night football between Mansfield, Summit, Timberview, Lake Ridge, and Legacy is a genuine community event in the city.

The district's South Arlington service area has a different character than the Mansfield core. Families living in South Arlington within Mansfield ISD are geographically closer to Six Flags, AT&T Stadium, and the Arlington entertainment corridor than to the Mansfield city center. Their students attend campuses like Mansfield Timberview, but the neighborhood feel is urban-suburban rather than small-town suburban. This geographic diversity within the district means the "Mansfield ISD experience" is not uniform across its footprint. Where you live within the district matters as much as the district average.

PTAs and booster clubs are active across the district's campuses. Parent involvement at the choice program campuses, including STEM Academy and Early College, tends to run particularly high given the application and commitment requirements those programs carry. Families who engage with those programs generally report a tight-knit experience that resembles a small private school environment more than a large public district.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a South Arlington address mean I'm in Arlington ISD or Mansfield ISD? ▾
It depends entirely on your specific street address. South Arlington is split between Arlington ISD and Mansfield ISD by a boundary that does not follow obvious streets or landmarks consistently. A significant portion of South Arlington south of the mid-city falls within Mansfield ISD. Assuming "Arlington address = Arlington ISD" is the most common mistake buyers in this area make. Verify your specific address at mansfieldisd.org or through the TEA school directory before making an offer.
How do Mansfield ISD's five high schools differ from each other? ▾
All five traditional high schools offer AP coursework and dual credit through Tarrant County College. Each campus has its own athletics and fine arts programs and competes in district rivalries that are a major part of community culture. Campus-level TEA and Niche ratings vary, so if a specific campus's performance profile matters to your decision, research the assigned campus directly at txschools.gov or Niche rather than relying on the district average. Your address determines which of the five campuses you are zoned for; verify the zone at mansfieldisd.org before buying.
How do I get my child into the Mansfield ISD STEM Academy? ▾
The STEM Academy is an application-based program. Transfer applications for the STEM Academy have a February 1 deadline for the following school year. Admission is based on available space and is not guaranteed. Transportation to the STEM Academy campus is the parent's responsibility unless a bus route serves your area. Do not select a home assuming your child will have a STEM Academy seat without confirming availability directly with Mansfield ISD Student Services for the specific year and grade level you need.
What is the Early College High School and who is it for? ▾
Mansfield ISD's Early College High School is a structured program that allows students to earn a full college associate degree while completing their high school diploma requirements. This is not the same as dual credit, where students take individual college courses alongside high school classes. The Early College program is a complete, structured pathway built around a degree plan. It is best suited to students with strong academic readiness who are motivated to begin college-level coursework as early as 9th grade. Application is required; contact Mansfield ISD Student Services for current eligibility criteria and application windows.
What is the Two-Way Dual Language program and when does it start? ▾
Mansfield ISD's Two-Way Dual Language program begins in Kindergarten and follows a 50% Spanish / 50% English instructional model across all core subjects through the elementary years. It is offered at D.P. Morris Elementary, Erma Nash Elementary, and Charlotte Anderson Preparatory Academy (which also integrates AVID). Parents must attend an in-person information meeting before submitting a Kindergarten application. The program requires a family commitment to keep the child enrolled through the elementary grades. Transportation is provided based on campus assignment.
What is Mansfield ISD's tax rate and how does it compare to nearby districts? ▾
Mansfield ISD's 2024 certified total tax rate is $1.1469 per $100 of assessed value (M&O: $0.7869, I&S: $0.36). The board adopted this rate in August 2024, a slight decrease from the prior year. With Texas's $100,000 homestead exemption, annual school taxes on a $400,000 home run approximately $3,441. The rate is higher than Keller ISD ($1.09) and Fort Worth ISD ($1.06), and lower than HEB ISD ($1.3875). Always verify the current certified rate with the Tarrant County Tax Assessor before closing.
What happens to my child's IEP when we move to Mansfield ISD? ▾
Texas law requires Mansfield ISD to convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment to review, adopt, or revise the existing IEP or 504 plan. Bring all prior evaluation reports and your child's current IEP or 504 document. Contact the Mansfield ISD Special Education department before your child's first day to discuss what services are available at the assigned campus. Parent experience varies across 47 campuses and by individual student need profile. Verify specific services for your child's profile directly with the district.
