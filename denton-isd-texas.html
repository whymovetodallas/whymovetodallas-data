District Overview

Denton ISD is one of the fastest-growing large school districts in Texas, serving roughly 33,200 students across 46 campuses in the northern DFW county of Denton. If you are a relocating parent weighing this district, the decision usually comes down to one question: does a big, diverse, college-town district with four distinct high schools fit your family better than a smaller, higher-rated neighbor? This page is built to help you answer that honestly, including the tradeoffs.

The district earned an overall B in the 2024-25 TEA accountability ratings, up a full letter grade from its 2023-24 rating, and holds a third-party Niche grade of A-. Denton itself is a genuine college town, home to the University of North Texas and Texas Woman's University, and that academic culture shapes the district's strong fine arts, dual language, and career and technical education programs. The honest tradeoff: with 46 campuses and rapid growth, your child's actual experience depends heavily on their specific campus, not the district average, and several fast-growing communities feed schools that are still building their enrollment and culture.

Denton ISD serves the city of Denton plus Corinth, Hickory Creek, Shady Shores, Cross Roads, and a ring of newer master-planned communities. Its enrollment has climbed more than 20% over the past decade, which means new campuses, shifting boundaries, and a district that looks different today than it did even five years ago.

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Want a large district with four comprehensive high schools and the program breadth that scale allows
  • Value a diverse student body and a college-town culture tied to UNT and TWU
  • Are drawn to newer master-planned communities like Savannah, Providence Village, Union Park, or Sandbrock Ranch
  • Want strong fine arts, dual language, and hands-on CTE pathways
  • Prefer a lower tax rate than several fast-growth Denton County peers

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • You want a district holding a consistent A rating and are not comfortable with a recent C-to-B trajectory
  • You prefer a small district where your child moves through school with the same tight cohort
  • You want certainty that boundaries will not shift, in a fast-growth district new campuses can redraw zones
  • Your target community has a Denton or Aubrey mailing address but you have not verified which ISD it feeds
Boundary Verification

A Denton mailing address does not guarantee a Denton ISD school, and many communities with Aubrey, Cross Roads, or Little Elm addresses are actually zoned to Denton ISD. Always verify your specific address with the Denton ISD attendance boundary lookup and the TEA School District Locator before making an offer.

TEA Accountability Rating

The Texas Education Agency assigns every district an annual A to F accountability rating built from three domains. In the 2024-25 ratings, Denton ISD earned an overall B, improving a full letter grade from the C it received in 2023-24. According to the Denton Record-Chronicle's reporting on the state release, the district's domain scores were:

  • Student Achievement: 78 (a C), measuring STAAR results, graduation, and college and career readiness
  • School Progress: 74 (a C), measuring year-over-year academic growth
  • Closing the Gaps: 83 (a B), measuring performance equity across student groups

What this means for a relocating family: an overall B places Denton ISD solidly in the "strong but not top-tier on paper" tier. The biggest gain came in Closing the Gaps, which is meaningful in a district this diverse, it suggests improvement is reaching students across groups, not just the top performers. The flip side is that Student Achievement and School Progress still sit in the C range districtwide, so if raw STAAR proficiency is your top filter, you will want to look campus by campus rather than trust the district letter alone. Every campus rating is published at txschools.gov, and TEA ratings can lag current campus reality by a year, so pair them with a recent campus visit.

Graduation Rate

Denton ISD reports a four-year graduation rate of about 97.1%, well above the national average. For a district serving more than 33,000 students, a rate this high signals a strong districtwide graduation pipeline, though graduation rate alone does not tell you how rigorous a given campus is.

Advanced Placement & Dual Credit

All four comprehensive high schools, Denton, Guyer, Braswell, and Ryan, offer Advanced Placement courses, with AP participation strongest at Guyer. The district partners with the University of North Texas and North Central Texas College for dual credit, letting eligible students earn college hours before they graduate, a practical benefit of a genuine college town. Denton ISD does not currently operate an International Baccalaureate (IB) program, so families specifically seeking IB should look to districts like Coppell, Frisco, or Grapevine-Colleyville.

College & Career Readiness (CCMR)

TEA tracks College, Career, and Military Readiness for every graduating class. Denton ISD's CCMR indicators include AP exam performance, dual credit completion, and industry certifications earned through its CTE pathways. Because the district feeds a strong local higher-education corridor, dual credit is one of its more accessible readiness routes.

STAAR Proficiency

Districtwide STAAR proficiency (students at "Approaches Grade Level" or above) is published by campus at txschools.gov. Given the C-range Student Achievement domain, proficiency varies meaningfully across the district's 46 campuses, which is exactly why boundary verification and campus-level review matter more here than in a small, uniform district.

The Four High Schools

Denton ISD's four comprehensive high schools each have a distinct geography and feel. Because your home address determines which one your child attends, this is often the single most important campus decision for a relocating family. Enrollment figures below reflect the most recent state data.

Ray Braswell High School

Grades 9-12~2,740 students

The district's largest and newest comprehensive high school, in the fast-growing far-northern communities near Aubrey and Savannah. Newer facilities and a young campus culture, popular with families buying in master-planned neighborhoods.

Guyer High School

Grades 9-12~2,560 students

Located in south Denton, Guyer has the district's highest AP participation and a strong athletics tradition. Often the campus academically ambitious families ask about first.

Denton High School

Grades 9-12~2,070 students

The historic central-Denton campus, recently rebuilt. Deep community roots, close to the UNT and TWU corridor, and a diverse student body that reflects the city itself.

Ryan High School

Grades 9-12~2,100 students

A central and west Denton campus that posted strong 2024-25 accountability growth, including a jump to an A in the Closing the Gaps domain. A campus on an upward trajectory.

Denton ISD also operates The LaGrone Academy (its career and technical center), Fred Moore High School, and other specialized campuses. Elementary and middle school assignments are set by address, verify current zoning at dentonisd.org before you buy.

Specialty Programs

Denton ISD's program strengths reflect its college-town setting. For each program below, the practical question for a home search is which campus offers it, since access is often tied to your attendance zone or an application.

Advanced Technology Complex (CTE)

The LaGrone Academy and the district's Advanced Technology Complex house career and technical pathways such as engineering, biomedical science, culinary arts, and digital media. Several strands are application-based and draw students from across the district, so confirm eligibility and transportation with the district, not just your zoned campus.

Dual Language

Denton ISD offers Spanish dual language programming at designated elementary campuses. Because it is campus-limited rather than districtwide, families targeting dual language should confirm their address feeds a program campus before making an offer.

Fine Arts

Band, orchestra, choir, theatre, and visual arts are a genuine district signature, reinforced by the two universities in town. Fine arts programming runs across all secondary campuses and regularly advances in UIL competition.

Gifted & Talented

GT identification uses a multi-criteria process, with services beginning in elementary and continuing through advanced high school coursework. Available at every campus level, though the depth of acceleration can vary by campus.

Dual Credit (UNT & NCTC)

Partnerships with the University of North Texas and North Central Texas College let eligible juniors and seniors earn college credit while in high school, one of the more accessible readiness pathways in a college town.

Special Education & Support

Denton ISD provides a full continuum of services for eligible students ages 3 to 21 under IDEA, including inclusion support, specialized settings, dyslexia intervention, and 504 accommodations. Fit varies by campus and student need, contact the Special Education office directly for a sensitive placement.

Enrollment & Transfers

Denton ISD registers new students online and in person. For the current 2025-26 school year, the general steps are:

  • Verify residency documents: proof of residence (lease or closing documents plus a current utility bill), the student's birth certificate, immunization records, and a parent or guardian ID.
  • Register online through the district's enrollment portal at dentonisd.org/enroll, then complete verification at the campus or district enrollment center.
  • Intra-district transfers: Denton ISD allows in-district transfer requests within an annual window and subject to campus capacity, which is limited at fast-growing schools. A transfer is not guaranteed.
  • Inter-district transfers: students living outside Denton ISD may apply to attend, subject to approval and space, confirm current policy with the district.
  • IEP or 504 transfers: Texas law requires the district to either adopt an existing IEP or convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior documentation and contact Special Services early.

Because capacity is the binding constraint at popular campuses, families relocating on a school-driven timeline should buy or lease inside their target attendance zone rather than counting on a transfer.

Boundary Verification (Read Before You Offer)

Denton County school boundaries are some of the trickiest in DFW, and Denton ISD sits in the middle of that complexity. Several fast-growing master-planned communities with Aubrey, Cross Roads, or Little Elm mailing addresses, including Savannah, Providence Village, Paloma Creek, Union Park, and Sandbrock Ranch, are actually zoned to Denton ISD, not to the district their mailing address might suggest. The reverse is also true: parts of the greater Denton area feed neighboring districts.

Two additional cautions specific to a fast-growth district: new campuses open regularly, and when they do, attendance zones can be redrawn, so a community's current high school assignment may change as the district builds out. And within a single neighborhood, elementary, middle, and high school zones do not always line up the way common-sense geography suggests.

How to verify: use the Denton ISD attendance boundary lookup at dentonisd.org and cross-check with the TEA School District Locator. Making an offer without verifying your specific address can mean your child attends a different school than you planned, and boundary maps do not always match common-sense geography. If a builder tells you a community's zoned school, ask whether that assignment is expected to change when the next campus opens.

Taxes & District Finances

Denton ISD's total certified property tax rate was approximately $1.16 per $100 of assessed value for the 2024 tax year, after the district lowered its rate for five consecutive years. In November 2025, voters approved a five-cent maintenance-and-operations increase, which raised the rate to roughly $1.21 per $100 for the 2025 tax year.

What that means for a monthly payment: on a $450,000 home, a rate near $1.16 works out to roughly $435 per month in school taxes before exemptions, and the five-cent increase adds about $19 per month. Texas homeowners can claim the $100,000 school-district homestead exemption on their primary residence, which meaningfully lowers the taxable value, so your actual bill will be lower than the sticker math suggests. Always run the numbers on your specific home value and confirm current rates with the Denton County Appraisal District.

For context against nearby districts families cross-shop, Denton ISD's rate sits below Little Elm ISD (about $1.23) and is in the same neighborhood as several other Denton County districts. A lower rate on a lower-priced home is one of the reasons budget-conscious families shortlist Denton ISD over pricier neighbors.

What It Feels Like to Be a Family Here

Denton ISD carries the personality of its namesake city: a real college town with an independent, arts-forward streak, anchored by two universities. That translates into serious fine arts programs, a genuinely diverse student body, and a community that shows up for Friday-night football and UIL band finals alike. For families coming from more homogeneous suburbs, the diversity of Denton ISD is often cited by local parents as a feature they came to value.

The honest tradeoffs are the ones that come with size and growth. Parents in the northern master-planned communities describe brand-new schools that are still forming their traditions, while families near central Denton get established campuses with decades of history but older facilities in some cases. Commutes vary widely across a district this geographically large, and boundary changes are a real possibility as new campuses open. None of that is a knock, it is simply the texture of a big, growing district, and it is why matching your family to the right campus matters more here than the district letter grade.

On safety, every family weighs it differently, and it is not something I rank. I encourage you to review third-party resources such as NeighborhoodScout, your local police department's public crime map, or City-Data, and evaluate specific areas directly so you can make your own call.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Denton address guarantee enrollment in Denton ISD? ▾
No. A Denton mailing address does not guarantee a Denton ISD school, and just as importantly, many communities with Aubrey, Cross Roads, or Little Elm addresses (Savannah, Providence Village, Paloma Creek, Union Park, Sandbrock Ranch) are actually zoned to Denton ISD. Always verify your exact address with the Denton ISD boundary lookup and the TEA School District Locator before you make an offer.
What is the difference between Denton ISD's four high schools? ▾
Braswell is the largest and newest, serving the fast-growing far-northern communities. Guyer, in south Denton, has the district's highest AP participation and a strong athletics program. Denton High is the historic central campus, recently rebuilt, near the university corridor. Ryan, in central and west Denton, showed notable accountability growth in 2024-25. Your home address determines which one your child attends, so confirm the zone before buying.
Why did Denton ISD's rating change from a C to a B? ▾
In the 2024-25 TEA accountability ratings, Denton ISD improved to an overall B from a C the year before. The biggest gain was in the Closing the Gaps domain (an 83, a B), which measures performance equity across student groups. Student Achievement (78) and School Progress (74) remained in the C range districtwide, so it is worth reviewing individual campus ratings at txschools.gov rather than relying on the district letter alone.
Denton ISD has a B from TEA but an A- from Niche. Why the difference? ▾
The two systems measure different things. TEA's A to F rating is a state accountability score built mainly from STAAR results, growth, and readiness data. Niche's grade blends academics with survey data, demographics, and other factors, and tends to reward diversity and breadth. A district can land a solid B with TEA and an A- with Niche when its strengths (program variety, diversity, graduation rate) are things Niche weights heavily.
How do transfers work in Denton ISD? ▾
Denton ISD accepts in-district transfer requests within an annual window, subject to campus capacity, which is tight at fast-growing schools, so a transfer is never guaranteed. Students living outside the district may apply for an inter-district transfer, also subject to space and approval. If your move is driven by a specific campus, the safest route is to live inside that attendance zone rather than rely on a transfer.
How does Denton ISD's tax rate compare to nearby districts? ▾
Denton ISD's certified rate was about $1.16 per $100 for the 2024 tax year, rising to roughly $1.21 after voters approved a five-cent M&O increase in November 2025. That is below Little Elm ISD (about $1.23) and in the same range as several Denton County peers such as Argyle, Northwest, and Lewisville (all near $1.19). Remember to apply the $100,000 Texas homestead exemption when you estimate your actual bill.
Does Denton ISD offer IB or dual credit? ▾
Denton ISD does not currently operate an International Baccalaureate (IB) program. It does offer robust dual credit through partnerships with the University of North Texas and North Central Texas College, letting eligible high schoolers earn college hours before graduation. Families specifically seeking IB should look at Coppell, Frisco, or Grapevine-Colleyville ISD.
