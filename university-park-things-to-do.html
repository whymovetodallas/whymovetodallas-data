What University Park Families Actually Do Here

University Park is the residential half of the Park Cities, an affluent enclave wrapped entirely by Dallas. Family life here is unusually walkable: 13 city parks strung along Turtle Creek, the Holmes Aquatic Center, Snider Plaza's century-old shops, and the SMU campus with its museums are all within a few minutes of one another. It's one of the rare DFW addresses where kids bike to the pool and parents walk to dinner. See the full University Park relocation guide →

A Walkable Grid of Parks

Thirteen city parks, the Holmes pool, and tree-lined sidewalks make most of daily life reachable on foot or by bike.

SMU at the Doorstep

The Meadows Museum, the George W. Bush Center, and Mustangs game days sit right on the edge of the neighborhood.

Snider Plaza & the Village

A 1927 shopping plaza and the landmark Highland Park Village put shops, dining, and seasonal events steps away.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

University Park maintains 13 city parks across just 3.7 square miles, most of them threaded along the five-mile-long Turtle Creek green corridor. For a small city, the park density is remarkable, and the playgrounds and pool are the social center of summer.

Curtis Park

The summer hub: two lighted tennis courts, a baseball diamond and soccer field, half-mile walking paths, the Turtle Creek ponds, and a 2023 shaded playground, plus the Holmes Aquatic Center. Restrooms and shade on site. Best as your first stop as a new resident.

Aquatic Center Tennis Playground

Holmes Aquatic Center

Inside Curtis Park at 3501 Lovers Lane: a 50-meter pool, a giant water slide, diving boards, and a zero-depth playground that distributes water to more than 25 spray and splash features for little ones. Lifeguards, a snack bar, and summer Dive-In Movies. Best for all ages on a hot day.

Splash Pad Pool Water Slide

Caruth Park

At 6.8 acres on Hillcrest Avenue, the city's largest children's play area: dual playgrounds under massive solar shades, a stocked fishing pond that hosts the Children's Fishing Derby, and wide open lawns. Plenty of shade and benches. Best for families with toddlers and grade-schoolers.

Playground Fishing Shade

Centennial Park (formerly Goar Park)

Open since 1947, with jogging and walking trails that feed into the tree-lined sidewalks, two practice soccer fields, a rentable gazebo, and a stretch of beautifully landscaped Turtle Creek. The post-parade hub for the July 4th celebration. Best for runs and weekend soccer.

Trails Soccer Fields Gazebo

Williams Park

A relaxed neighborhood green that hosts the spring Movie in the Park, with open lawn and play space. Easy parking and a low-key feel. Best for younger kids and casual evenings.

Open Lawn Events Playground

Germany Park

A quieter residential park with open green space and play areas, tucked into a tree-lined neighborhood. Best for a quick stop close to home with the little ones.

Playground Open Space

Elena's Children's Park

An intimate park at Boedeker and Hanover honoring children in the community, with a quiet, tree-lined setting and a meaningful holiday tree lighting each December. Best for a calm, reflective walk.

Quiet Tree-Lined Community

Trail System Highlight

The Turtle Creek green corridor runs five miles through University Park, and Centennial Park's jogging trails feed directly into the tree-lined sidewalks that make the whole city walkable. Great for stroller walks, morning runs, and bike rides to the pool.

Relocation Tip

Start at Curtis Park. It's the social center of University Park in summer, with the Holmes Aquatic Center, tennis courts, and the newest playground all in one place. Then walk or drive five minutes to Caruth Park for the city's biggest shaded play area and the fishing pond.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Organized youth sports in the Park Cities center on the Moody Family YMCA, which opened in 2016 right between University Park and Highland Park. It runs the bulk of the rec leagues, while Highland Park ISD handles middle and high school athletics and SMU offers summer camps on campus.

Soccer

League: Park Cities YMCA / Moody Family YMCA (fields at the YMCA, Centennial Park, and Curtis Park)
Ages: 3 and up
Season: Spring & fall

Basketball

League: Moody Family YMCA
Ages: Preschool (3 to 5) and Youth (Kinder to 6th grade)
Season: Summer & winter

Volleyball

League: Moody Family YMCA (YMCA of Metropolitan Dallas)
Ages: Beginners through teens, age-grouped
Season: Seasonal, drill-and-game format

Baseball & Tee Ball

League: Moody Family YMCA, with games at the Curtis Park diamond
Ages: 4 to 12
Season: Spring

Swimming

League: Moody Family YMCA (25-yard lap and instructional pools) and the Holmes Aquatic Center
Ages: All
Season: Year-round at the YMCA, summer at Holmes

Tennis

League: Curtis Park's two lighted courts, plus Park Cities and SMU programs
Ages: All
Season: Year-round lessons and open play

Featured Sports Complex

The Moody Family YMCA (Park Cities) is a 58,500-square-foot facility that opened in 2016 with a full gymnasium, a 25-yard lap pool, an instructional pool, group fitness studios, and outdoor soccer fields. It's the hub of organized youth sports for University Park and Highland Park families.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Few DFW neighborhoods put this much culture within walking or a five-minute drive. The SMU campus alone gives families two world-class museums.

George W. Bush Presidential Center

Museum · On the SMU campus, with interactive exhibits, a full-size Oval Office replica, and rotating special exhibitions families can spend a half-day exploring.

SMU Meadows Museum

Art Museum · One of the largest collections of Spanish art outside Spain, nicknamed the Prado on the Prairie, with free family programming throughout the year.

SMU Mustangs Athletics

College Sports · Walkable game days at Gerald J. Ford Stadium for football and Moody Coliseum for basketball, a fun, affordable family outing in the fall and winter.

NorthPark Center

Shopping & Art · About 5 minutes away, a top-rated mall with a museum-quality art collection and a children's play garden, great any time of year.

Klyde Warren Park

Urban Park · About 12 minutes south, a deck park over the freeway with food trucks, a children's area, and free programming, paired easily with the nearby Arts District.

Day Trip from University Park

The Perot Museum of Nature and Science (about 12 minutes south) packs five floors of hands-on science exhibits, from dinosaurs to an indoor earthquake simulator, a reliable favorite for a hot or rainy day.

Family-Friendly Dining

Snider Plaza is the everyday dining heart of University Park, with Highland Park Village adding a cluster of higher-end favorites a few minutes south.

Kuby's Sausage House Best old-school breakfast
A Snider Plaza German institution since 1961, house-made sausages and a diner-style breakfast.
Bubba's Cooks Country Best after-practice dinner
Fried chicken and cheese fries at Hillcrest and Lovers, a longtime Park Cities kid favorite.
Food From Galilee Best quick lunch
Affordable gyros, shawarma, and falafel in the middle of Snider Plaza.
CAVA Best fast-casual
Build-your-own Mediterranean bowls at 6800 Snider Plaza, easy for picky eaters.
El Molino del Rey Best family Mexican
A Mexico City-inspired restaurant in the heart of Snider Plaza.
Mi Cocina Best Tex-Mex
The Highland Park Village mainstay, a dependable Park Cities Tex-Mex favorite.
Patrizio Best patio dinner
Italian dining with a family-friendly patio in Highland Park Village.
Cafe Pacific Best date night
A Highland Park Village seafood favorite since 1980, for a grown-up evening out.

Most of these sit in Snider Plaza at Hillcrest and Lovers Lane, with Highland Park Village a few minutes south near Preston Road and Mockingbird.

Shopping & Retail Districts

University Park is bracketed by two historic shopping districts and a few minutes from one of the country's best malls.

Snider Plaza

Historic District

Open since 1927 next to SMU, with more than 100 independently owned shops and restaurants, from children's stores to boutiques.
Best for: a walkable afternoon of browsing, lunch, and the holiday tree lighting.

Highland Park Village

Luxury Landmark

A short drive south, the first self-contained shopping center in America, a Spanish-style national landmark with luxury brands, dining, and family events.
Best for: a special outing, window-shopping, and seasonal events.

Preston Center

Everyday

A compact district just north with grocery, services, restaurants, and boutiques.
Best for: quick errands and casual meals close to home.

NorthPark Center

Mall & Art

About 5 minutes away, one of the country's top malls, with a museum-grade art collection and a children's play garden.
Best for: a full rainy-day outing of shopping, dining, and art.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

University Park's Parks and Recreation Department runs a packed calendar of free, family-first events, most of them in the city's parks and at Snider Plaza.

Feb & June

Children's Fishing Derby

For ages 6 to 12 at the Caruth Park pond, which the city stocks beforehand. Awards go to the biggest and smallest fish. Bring your own pole and bait.

Typically March

Eggstravaganza

A spring egg hunt with inflatables, carnival games, music, and a visit from the Easter Bunny, recently held at Caruth Park.

Typically May

Movie in the Park

A free family movie under the stars in Williams Park each spring, with food trucks on site.

Typically July

Park Cities 4th of July Parade

A decades-long tradition sponsored by the Park Cities Rotary Club, winding through Highland Park and University Park, with bounce houses, a petting zoo, and face painting afterward at Centennial Park.

Summer

Dive-In Movies

Free Friday-night movies shown poolside at the Holmes Aquatic Center, with the snack bar open and lifeguards on duty.

Typically October

Eggtober

The Parks Department fills a dozen Halloween eggs per child with allergy-friendly candy and hides them in your front yard, a uniquely University Park tradition.

Typically November

Arbor Day Celebration

Held annually since 1982, the city plants a tree with help from Highland Park ISD elementary students, part of the Trees for Town program.

Nov & Dec

Holiday Tree Lightings

The main tree lighting returns to Snider Plaza with live carols and dances, followed by a quieter remembrance tree lighting at Elena's Children's Park.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When Dallas summers hit triple digits, University Park has an unusually rich set of indoor options, several of them within walking distance.

George W. Bush Presidential Museum

Type: Museum · Ages: All
Interactive exhibits and an Oval Office replica on the SMU campus, an easy half-day even in the heat.

SMU Meadows Museum

Type: Art Museum · Ages: All
Spanish masters and free family programming, air-conditioned and minutes from home.

University Park Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: All
Children's story times and reading programs at the city's own public library.

Moody Family YMCA

Type: Rec Center · Ages: All
Indoor lap and instructional pools, a full gym, and open play right between UP and Highland Park.

NorthPark Center

Type: Mall & Art · Ages: All
Indoor shopping, dining, museum-grade art, and a children's garden about 5 minutes away.

Perot Museum of Nature and Science

Type: Science Museum · Ages: 3+
Five floors of hands-on exhibits about 12 minutes south in downtown Dallas.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When Dallas summers hit triple digits, University Park families split time between the Holmes Aquatic Center's zero-depth playground and the air-conditioned galleries at the Meadows Museum and the Bush Center, both on the SMU campus minutes from home. The Friday-night Dive-In Movies at Holmes are the local summer ritual.
