District Overview

Little Elm ISD is a small, growing district on the shores of Lewisville Lake in southern Denton County, serving roughly 7,700 students across about 12 campuses, anchored by a single comprehensive high school. If you are relocating and drawn to Little Elm for its lakefront living and lower prices than Frisco or Southlake, this page is meant to give you the honest picture, because the school district is a real factor families should weigh carefully before buying, and because not every Little Elm address is even served by Little Elm ISD.

Here is the tradeoff stated plainly in the first breath: Little Elm ISD has earned a C from TEA for three consecutive years, and its proficiency numbers trail most Denton County peers. That is not a trend I can spin, and I would rather you hear it from me than discover it after closing. At the same time, the district offers a genuinely tight-knit, single-high-school community, real affordability, and a lakeside lifestyle that larger, higher-rated districts nearby cannot match on price. Whether that trade is right for your family depends entirely on your priorities.

Little Elm ISD serves the western and central parts of the town of Little Elm plus Hackberry, Lakewood Village, Oak Point, and slices of neighboring communities. Because the town of Little Elm is split across three different school districts, verifying your exact address is more important here than almost anywhere else in DFW.

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Want lakeside living near Lewisville Lake at a lower price point than Frisco, Prosper, or Southlake
  • Like the idea of one comprehensive high school where the whole district's teens grow up together
  • Value a small, close-knit community feel over big-district program breadth
  • Have verified their specific address is inside a Little Elm ISD attendance zone

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • You want a district with a consistent A or B TEA rating, Little Elm ISD has three straight C years
  • You need broad specialty menus like IB, multiple magnet high schools, or a deep AP catalog
  • You are drawn to the Little Elm name but have not confirmed which of three ISDs your street feeds
  • You are sensitive to a higher debt-service tax component tied to building for growth
Boundary Verification

This is the single most important step for a Little Elm buyer. The town of Little Elm is split among three districts: Little Elm ISD, Frisco ISD (the eastern side near the Frisco border), and Denton ISD (some northern communities). A home in Frisco ISD on the Little Elm side of the line is a very different school experience than one zoned to Little Elm ISD. Verify your exact address with the Little Elm ISD boundary lookup and the TEA School District Locator before you make an offer.

TEA Accountability Rating

The Texas Education Agency assigns every district an annual A to F accountability rating built from three domains: Student Achievement, School Progress, and Closing the Gaps. In the 2024-25 ratings, Little Elm ISD earned an overall C with a score of 79. That marks the third consecutive year the district has landed in the C range, holding a score in the 70s across the most recent rating cycles.

What this means for a relocating family: a score of 79 sits just one point below the 80 threshold for a B, so the district is close to the next tier but has not crossed it, and has not for three years running. A stable C is not a failing district, most campuses meet standard, but it does mean Little Elm ISD trails the A and B ratings common among nearby Denton and Collin County districts. If academic rating is at the top of your list, this is the number to sit with honestly. If you are weighing it against price and lifestyle, it is one factor among several.

Campus-level ratings, which can differ from the district average, are published at txschools.gov. Because Little Elm ISD has just one comprehensive high school, the high school's rating carries a lot of weight in how families experience the district. TEA ratings can also lag current campus reality by a year, so pair the score with a recent campus visit.

Graduation Rate

Little Elm High School reports a four-year graduation rate of about 89% (per third-party sources including SchoolDigger, most recent published year), close to the Texas state average of roughly 90%. Solid, but not the standout numbers you will see at higher-rated neighbors.

STAAR Proficiency & Readiness

Districtwide STAAR proficiency at Little Elm ISD trails most Denton County peers, which is the main driver of the district's C accountability rating. Proficiency data by subject and campus is published at txschools.gov. For a relocating family, the practical read is this: the average academic profile here is closer to the state middle than to the top-performing DFW suburbs, so if you have a student who thrives on a competitive, high-achieving cohort, look closely at the specific campus and its programs.

Advanced Placement & Dual Credit

Little Elm High School offers Advanced Placement courses and dual credit through partnerships with area colleges, letting students earn college hours before graduation. The AP and dual-credit catalog is more limited than what a large multi-campus district can offer simply because everything runs through one high school, which is a real consideration for families whose student wants a very wide slate of advanced courses.

Specialty Programs

As a small district, Little Elm ISD offers a solid core of programs rather than the sprawling specialty menu of a large neighbor. Here is an honest look at what is available and how access works.

Career & Technical Education (CTE)

CTE pathways run through Little Elm High School and cover fields such as business, health science, and technology, with opportunities to earn industry certifications. Because everything is on one campus, the pathway list is more focused than a multi-high-school district, confirm which strands are offered in a given year.

Dual Credit

Eligible juniors and seniors can earn college credit through the district's dual-credit partnerships with area colleges while still in high school. This is one of the more accessible college-readiness routes in the district.

STEM (Prestwick Academy)

The district's STEM emphasis is anchored at Prestwick STEM Academy at the elementary level, a distinguishing option for families who want an early science and technology focus. Confirm eligibility and any enrollment steps with the district.

Fine Arts & Athletics

Band, choir, theatre, visual arts, and a full UIL athletics program run through the single high school. A one-high-school district can mean deeper hometown identity in these activities, since there is no crosstown rival within the district.

Gifted & Talented

Little Elm ISD identifies gifted students through a multi-criteria process, with services beginning in elementary. Depth of acceleration is more limited than a large district simply due to scale, so ask about specifics for your child's grade.

Special Education & Support

The district provides services for eligible students ages 3 to 21 under IDEA, including inclusion support, specialized settings, dyslexia intervention, and 504 accommodations. Because the district is small, contact the Special Services office directly to discuss fit for a student with specific needs.

Enrollment & Transfers

Little Elm ISD registers new students online and at the district's enrollment center. For the current 2025-26 school year, the general steps are:

  • Verify residency documents: proof of residence (lease or closing documents plus a current utility bill), the student's birth certificate, immunization records, and a parent or guardian ID.
  • Register online through the district portal at littleelmisd.net, then complete verification with the district.
  • Intra-district transfers: with essentially one high school, in-district transfer options are naturally limited, confirm current policy for the elementary and middle levels.
  • Inter-district transfers: students living outside Little Elm ISD may apply to attend, subject to space and approval.
  • IEP or 504 transfers: Texas law requires the district to either adopt an existing IEP or convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior documentation and contact Special Services early.

The most important enrollment step for a Little Elm buyer happens before registration: confirming your address is actually in Little Elm ISD and not Frisco ISD or Denton ISD.

The Little Elm Boundary Trap (Read Before You Offer)

This is the section that matters most on this page. The town of Little Elm is served by three different school districts: Little Elm ISD, Frisco ISD, and Denton ISD. That makes it one of the highest boundary-verification burdens anywhere in DFW, and it regularly catches buyers who assume a Little Elm address means Little Elm schools.

The pattern in broad strokes: the western and central parts of town are generally Little Elm ISD, the eastern side near the Frisco border feeds Frisco ISD, and some northern communities are zoned to Denton ISD. Because those lines follow specific streets and development boundaries rather than tidy geography, you cannot eyeball it from a map. A home marketed as being in "Little Elm" can carry a Frisco ISD or Denton ISD assignment, which changes the school experience, the ratings, and the resale story.

How to verify: use the Little Elm ISD attendance boundary tool at littleelmisd.net and cross-check the exact address with the TEA School District Locator. Making an offer without verifying your specific address can mean your child attends a different district than you planned, and boundary maps do not always match common-sense geography. If schools are driving your move, confirm the district and the specific campus in writing before you go under contract.

Taxes & District Finances

Little Elm ISD adopted a total property tax rate of about $1.23 per $100 of assessed value for 2024-25 ($0.7552 for maintenance and operations plus $0.47 for debt service). The relatively high debt-service, or I&S, portion reflects the bonds the district has issued to build schools for a growing community, a common pattern in fast-developing Denton County.

What that means for a monthly payment: on a $400,000 home, a rate near $1.23 works out to roughly $410 per month in school taxes before exemptions. Texas homeowners can claim the $100,000 school-district homestead exemption on a primary residence, which lowers the taxable value and your actual bill, so the real number will be lower than the sticker math. Confirm current rates and your assessed value with the Denton County Appraisal District.

For comparison, Little Elm ISD's rate sits above Denton ISD (about $1.16) and well above Frisco ISD (about $1.03). A family choosing Little Elm for affordability should factor the tax rate into the total monthly cost, a lower home price paired with a higher rate can narrow the gap versus a pricier home in a lower-rate district.

What It Feels Like to Be a Family Here

Little Elm's identity is built around the water. With Lewisville Lake wrapping much of the town, the lifestyle draw is real: parks, a beach, and lakeside neighborhoods that cost less than comparable homes in Frisco or Southlake. For a school district built around a single high school, that translates into strong hometown spirit, everyone's kids end up at the same Friday-night games, and the community tends to feel connected rather than sprawling.

The honest tradeoffs are the ones this whole page keeps returning to. The district's three straight C ratings and below-peer proficiency are real, and administration scores on third-party sites like Niche have been among the district's weaker marks. The area's affordability draws young and growing families, which is a genuine plus for community energy, but it also means the district is stretching to build and staff for growth, reflected in that higher debt-service tax rate. Whether the lifestyle and price offset the rating is a personal call, and I am happy to walk through it with your specific family in mind.

On safety, every family weighs it differently, and it is not something I rank. I encourage you to review third-party resources such as NeighborhoodScout, your local police department's public crime map, or City-Data, and evaluate specific areas directly so you can make your own call.

Why does Little Elm ISD have only one high school? ▾
Little Elm ISD is a small district of about 7,700 students, and much of the residential growth in and around the town actually feeds Frisco ISD and Denton ISD rather than Little Elm ISD. As a result, all Little Elm ISD high schoolers attend Little Elm High School together. That creates a tight-knit, one-team community, but it also concentrates the district's AP, CTE, and extracurricular offerings on a single campus, which is worth weighing if your student wants a very wide slate of advanced courses.

Is Little Elm ISD a good fit for a student who needs special education services? ▾
Little Elm ISD provides special education and related services for eligible students ages 3 to 21 under IDEA, including inclusion support, specialized settings, dyslexia intervention, and 504 accommodations. If your student has an existing IEP, Texas law requires the district to adopt it or convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Because the district is small, program fit varies by campus and student need, so contact the Special Services office directly to discuss your child's specific situation before you commit.
Why do some Little Elm homes feed Frisco ISD? ▾
The eastern edge of the town of Little Elm sits against the Frisco border, and district lines there follow specific streets and development boundaries drawn before or during the area's rapid growth. Homes on the Frisco side of that line are zoned to Frisco ISD even though they carry a Little Elm mailing address. This matters because Frisco ISD and Little Elm ISD have different ratings, programs, and price points, so a "Little Elm" home can be a very different school decision depending on which side of the line it sits.
