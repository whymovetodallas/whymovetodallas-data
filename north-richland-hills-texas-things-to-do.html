What North Richland Hills Families Actually Do Here

North Richland Hills is the anchor city of the Northeast Tarrant family corridor, a compact city with 800-plus acres of parkland, the city-owned NRH2O Family Water Park, 30 miles of hike and bike trails, and the NRH Centre recreation facility that draws members from Keller, Hurst, and Haltom City alike. It punches well above its size for outdoor and recreational infrastructure. See the full North Richland Hills relocation guide.

NRH2O: City-Owned Water Park

The city of North Richland Hills owns and operates NRH2O Family Water Park: water slides, a wave pool, a lazy river, and splash areas. This gives residents access to a full water park at a price well below commercial alternatives.

30 Miles of Hike and Bike Trails

Thirty miles of off-road and paved trails connect NRH's parks and neighborhoods. Fossil Creek Park adds dedicated mountain bike singletrack and a new pump track in a riparian forest setting. Rare for a city this close to Fort Worth.

Recreation Built for Families

The 86,000-square-foot NRH Centre pairs an indoor aquatics complex, a suspended walking track, and a full gymnasium with BAYFA soccer and RYA baseball leagues, giving kids year-round structured activity without leaving the city.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

North Richland Hills maintains 34 parks across more than 800 acres of parkland. The trail network spans 30 miles of off-road and paved paths, with emergency location markers every quarter mile. It is a practical detail that shows the city takes its trail system seriously.

Fossil Creek Park

A 58.5-acre community park with multi-use athletic fields, soft-surface mountain bike singletrack trails through a riparian forest preserve, and a new Bicycle Playground and Pump Track opened in 2025. One of the few places in Northeast Tarrant County where kids can ride technical MTB trails. Restrooms on site, ample parking.

Mountain Biking Pump Track Athletic Fields Nature Trails

Green Valley Park

Home field for BAYFA soccer leagues and a primary venue for youth sports in NRH. Multi-purpose turf and grass fields, open space for pickup play, and shade trees along the perimeter. Good stroller access on the paved paths. Popular with families on weekend mornings during soccer season.

Youth Soccer Athletic Fields Stroller-Friendly Open Space

Walker's Creek Park

A popular neighborhood park with playground equipment, open lawn, and creek-adjacent paths. Shaded sections make it usable through the Texas spring and fall. Good for under-10 kids who want space to run without the formal structure of a sports complex.

Playground Creek Access Picnic Areas

Richfield Park

Neighborhood park with ball fields, a playground, and picnic pavilions available for rental. Clean restrooms and good shade coverage for a park of its size. Convenient for families in the central and south NRH neighborhoods looking for a quick after-dinner outing.

Ball Fields Playground Pavilion Rental

Dick Faram Park

Well-maintained neighborhood park with open fields, a playground, and picnic areas. Popular with families in the northern NRH neighborhoods. The park stays quieter than the city's larger facilities, which makes it a good choice for younger kids who find the bigger parks overwhelming.

Playground Open Fields Picnic Areas

Tommy & Sue Brown Park

A neighborhood park honoring community contributors. Playground, open space, and basketball courts. Walking paths connect to the broader trail network. Good all-ages park for families who want a quick outdoors stop without committing to a full destination park.

Basketball Playground Trail Access

Trail System Highlight

NRH's 30-mile trail network includes paved hike and bike paths connecting parks and neighborhoods citywide, plus dedicated off-road singletrack at Fossil Creek Park. The city posts green and white circle markers every quarter mile on all off-road trails with location codes for emergency dispatch. It is a practical safety feature that parents new to the trail system appreciate. The network connects into regional trails toward Keller and Hurst as well.

Relocation Tip

Bring the bikes to Fossil Creek Park on your first full weekend in NRH. The mountain bike pump track and playground mean kids of different ages can spread out in the same space. Then walk the riparian forest trail. It's the fastest way to understand what makes NRH's park system different from the typical manicured DFW suburb park.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

North Richland Hills youth sports are organized through two primary volunteer associations: BAYFA for soccer and RYA for baseball, basketball, football, and softball, along with programs at the NRH Centre. The city coordinates these associations through its Parks and Recreation department, which lists contact information and league details on the official site.

Soccer

League: Birdville Area Youth Futbol Alliance (BAYFA)
Ages: Ages 4 through 18
Season: Spring and Fall
BAYFA serves NRH, Haltom City, Watauga, and Richland Hills. Games are played at Green Valley Community Park. Part of the North Texas Soccer association. Good recreational structure across all age divisions.

Baseball & Softball

League: Richland Youth Association (RYA)
Ages: Youth divisions by age group
Season: Spring and Fall
RYA is the primary organization for baseball and softball in NRH. Multiple divisions across age groups. Community volunteer-run with a long history in the Richland Hills and NRH corridor.

Football

League: Richland Youth Association (RYA)
Ages: Youth tackle and flag divisions
Season: Fall
RYA runs both tackle and flag football programs. The fall football calendar runs alongside soccer and baseball, so check for schedule conflicts if kids want to play multiple sports in the same season.

Basketball

League: Richland Youth Association (RYA) / NRH Centre
Ages: Youth divisions
Season: Winter
RYA runs youth basketball, and the NRH Centre's double gymnasium hosts additional leagues and drop-in play throughout the year. The Centre's courts are glass-enclosed and well-maintained.

Cheerleading

League: Richland Youth Association (RYA)
Ages: Youth divisions
Season: Fall (alongside football)
RYA includes a cheerleading program that runs in coordination with the football season. Good option for kids who want structured cheer without committing to a year-round competitive all-star program.

Golf

Facility: Iron Horse Golf Course (6200 Skylark Cir)
Ages: Junior programs available
Season: Year-round
Iron Horse is a Dick Phelps-designed championship daily-fee course with a strong local following. Junior golf programming runs through the facility. One of the better-maintained mid-tier courses in Northeast Tarrant County.

Featured Sports Facility

NRH Centre at 6000 Hawk Ave is North Richland Hills's centerpiece recreation facility. The 86,000-square-foot building opened in 2012 and includes a double gymnasium, indoor aquatics with a water slide and rock climbing wall, a suspended walking track, and a full fitness center. Leagues, classes, and drop-in programs run year-round. Members from neighboring Keller, Hurst, and Watauga make up a significant share of the membership. It is a reliable indicator of quality.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

NRH's own entertainment assets are strong for a city its size, and its location in Northeast Tarrant puts families within 20 minutes of Grapevine, Fort Worth's cultural corridor, and the Mid-Cities entertainment belt.

NRH2O Family Water Park

Full water park · City-owned and operated, NRH2O at 9001 Boulevard 26 runs water slides, a wave pool, a lazy river, and splash areas from late spring through summer. Friday Night Late Night sessions in June and July extend hours. Season passes make it a weekday-afternoon regular for NRH families.

Iron Horse Golf Course

Golf course and junior programs · The Dick Phelps-designed championship course at 6200 Skylark Circle is among the best daily-fee courses in Northeast Tarrant. Junior programs run through the facility, and the clubhouse dining is a family-friendly option on its own for weekend lunches.

NRH Centre Aquatics

Indoor aquatics complex · The NRH Centre's indoor pool features a water slide, lap lanes, a resistance channel, and a rock climbing wall beside the pool. Year-round programming includes swim lessons, water aerobics, and open swim. A practical alternative to NRH2O when the weather doesn't cooperate.

North Richland Hills Public Library

Public library · Located adjacent to the NRH Centre at 6000 Hawk Ave, the library runs children's programming, story times, summer reading challenges, and family events throughout the year. The shared campus with the Centre makes it easy to combine a workout for parents with library time for kids.

Fossil Creek Park MTB Trails

Mountain biking and cycling · The Bicycle Playground and Pump Track at Fossil Creek Park opened in 2025 and quickly became a destination for families with biking kids. The soft-surface singletrack through the riparian forest is approachable for intermediate riders while still being interesting for more experienced cyclists.

Day Trip from North Richland Hills

SEA LIFE Grapevine Aquarium is about 15 minutes west and is the best under-8 day trip from NRH, especially on hot days. Fort Worth Zoo is about 20 minutes south and remains one of the top-ranked zoos in the country. Both are season-pass-worthy for NRH families who want repeat-visit destinations within easy range.

Family-Friendly Dining

North Richland Hills has a solid casual dining scene concentrated along Rufe Snow Drive, Davis Boulevard, and the Boulevard 26 corridor. The mix runs from local diners to steakhouses to kid-focused spots, with most of the best options within a 10-minute drive of anywhere in the city.

Sweetie Pies Ribeyes
Best family steakhouse
A local institution in the NRH area known for generous ribeye portions and a genuinely casual atmosphere. The kind of place where bringing a six-year-old in a soccer uniform is completely normal. Loud enough that no one notices a little chaos at the table.
Boulevard Cafe
Best weekend breakfast
A classic diner that regulars describe as exactly what you want from a great neighborhood breakfast spot. Eggs, pancakes, and coffee that arrives fast. The kind of Saturday morning anchor that new NRH families discover in their first month and return to for years.
Sickies Garage Burgers & Brews
Best after-sports burger
Garage-themed burger bar with a creative menu and a rowdy, fun atmosphere that kids love. Dozens of burger variations alongside a full bar for parents. Good after a Saturday tournament when everyone is tired and hungry and nobody wants to make decisions.
Bosses Pizza
Best quick pizza night
Local pizza spot with a following in the NRH community. New-York-style pies in a casual setting. Delivery options make it the default choice for busy weeknights when practice ran long and nobody wants to cook or park anywhere.
Daddy Jack's
Best seafood for adventurous kids
New England-style lobster and chowder house with a menu that goes beyond what most suburban DFW restaurants offer. Reviewers specifically note it's easy to order for kids. A good choice when the adults want a real meal and the kids can order chicken without drama.
Horizon 76
Best laid-back family dinner
Casual American dining with a neighborhood-restaurant feel. The menu covers enough ground to satisfy different ages at the same table without everyone having to negotiate. Consistent and reliable, which matters more than it sounds after a long week.
Chuck E. Cheese
Best for under-7 birthday parties
The classic kid-entertainment dining formula: pizza, token arcade games, and a setup that makes birthday party logistics easy. Not sophisticated, but reliably successful with the under-7 crowd when you need somewhere the kids control the itinerary.
Pappasito's Cantina
Best celebratory TexMex
The NRH-area Pappasito's brings the full Tex-Mex experience: tableside guacamole, fajitas that arrive sizzling, and a family atmosphere that handles both toddlers and grandparents in the same booth. Good for milestone dinners when you want to impress relatives visiting from out of state.
Texas Roadhouse
Best picky-eater friendly
The free peanuts, loud music, and consistently crowd-pleasing menu make Texas Roadhouse the low-risk choice when you're feeding multiple kids with different preferences. The bread alone usually buys 20 minutes of patience. Expect a wait on weekends.

Most of these are along the Rufe Snow Drive, Davis Boulevard, and Boulevard 26 corridors, a 10-minute stretch that covers the majority of NRH's sit-down dining options.

Shopping & Retail Districts

NRH is practical shopping territory. The North Hills Mall site is now city hall, so the big-box and strip-center corridors along Rufe Snow and Davis handle everyday needs. For a larger shopping outing, North East Mall in Hurst is 10 minutes south.

Rufe Snow Drive Corridor

Everyday Retail

NRH's primary commercial spine running north-south through the city. Grocery anchors, pharmacy, home goods, and a solid run of casual dining make this the default stop for most weekly errands. Everything a family needs without a mall parking lot.

Best for: weekly grocery and errand runs: predictable, fast, and close to most NRH neighborhoods.

North East Mall (Hurst)

Regional Mall

About 10 minutes south in Hurst, North East Mall gives NRH families access to a full department store anchor lineup and the full range of national retailers. The food court works for post-shopping family lunches. Back-to-school shopping, prom dress hunting, and shoe hauls all happen here.

Best for: back-to-school season and any shopping trip where variety matters more than speed.

Boulevard 26 Retail Area

Mixed-Use Corridor

The Boulevard 26 corridor near NRH2O and City Hall mixes retail, dining, and city facilities. Home to NRH2O, the convention center, and several dining options. A good axis for combining entertainment and shopping in one outing when families are already on that side of the city.

Best for: combining an NRH2O visit with shopping and dinner in a single Saturday loop.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

NRH's city events calendar runs year-round with a mix of family-focused programming, outdoor concerts, and seasonal celebrations. Most are city-produced, which means they're well-organized and free or low-cost.

February

NRH Daddy Daughter Dance

An annual Valentine-season event pairing dads, grandpas, and uncles with daughters and granddaughters for dancing, games, and activities. Held in themed attire each year. Kids talk about it for months afterward.

Spring

NRH Art Fest

An annual outdoor arts and culture event held at the NRH Centre Plaza area. Live music, local artists, and family-friendly activities make this one of the best spring community gatherings in Northeast Tarrant County.

Jun–Jul

NRH Centre Plaza Concerts

The NRH Centre Plaza hosts outdoor concerts through the summer months, with live entertainment (check the city's events calendar for this year's lineup). Free admission for most performances. Families bring lawn chairs and dinner.

July

Red, White & YOU!

NRH's Fourth of July celebration features fireworks, live entertainment (check the official events calendar for this year's lineup), and family activities. The city-run event draws NRH families and visitors from neighboring cities to the main gathering space.

Jun–Aug

NRH2O Friday Night Late Nights

NRH2O extends its hours on Friday evenings in June and July, with special music and an after-hours atmosphere. Families who hold season passes use this as their regular Friday night activity through the hottest stretch of summer.

Fall

Halloween Community Events

NRH Parks and Recreation hosts Halloween-themed family events each October, including activities for younger kids in a safe, organized environment. Check the city calendar for specific dates and venues each year.

December

Christmas in the Park

Held annually at NRH2O Family Water Park and City Hall, transforming both sites into a winter wonderland of lights and holiday activities. One of the most-anticipated family events in the city's year, held annually since the late 1990s.

Full calendar: North Richland Hills official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

NRH's own indoor options are anchored by the NRH Centre, and the Mid-Cities location puts families within 15 minutes of the entertainment belt along Highway 121 and the Hurst-Bedford area.

NRH Centre Indoor Aquatics

Type: Recreation Center · Ages: All ages
Indoor pool with a water slide, lap lanes, a resistance channel, and a rock climbing wall beside the deck. Year-round swim lessons and open swim sessions. NRH Centre membership is the most cost-effective way to access this facility regularly.

North Richland Hills Public Library

Type: Public Library · Ages: All ages
Co-located with the NRH Centre at 6000 Hawk Ave. Children's story times, summer reading challenge, maker-space programming, and after-school activities. Free. The campus layout means parents can work out while kids are at a library program.

Urban Air Trampoline Park

Type: Adventure Park · Ages: 2 and up
The nearest Urban Air to NRH serves as the default trampoline park for the Northeast Tarrant corridor. Full adventure park with warrior courses, foam pits, climbing walls, and a toddler zone. Good 2-hour energy burn for restless kids on rainy days.

Dave & Buster's (Hurst)

Type: Entertainment Complex · Ages: 7 and up
About 10 minutes south in the Hurst-Bedford area. Arcade games, billiards, and a full restaurant. The rewards card system makes repeat visits efficient. Best for kids old enough to navigate the arcade independently while parents decompress at a table.

NRH Centre Gymnasium

Type: Indoor Sports · Ages: All ages
The glass-enclosed double gymnasium at NRH Centre hosts league basketball, volleyball, and drop-in play. One of the best-equipped indoor court spaces in the Birdville ISD corridor. NRH Centre membership gives access to open gym sessions outside of league hours.

Chuck E. Cheese

Type: Entertainment Dining · Ages: 2 through 10
The NRH-area Chuck E. Cheese is a reliable birthday party venue for the under-8 set. Arcade games, pizza, and a kid-controlled environment. Not a destination for parents, but the format works consistently for birthday parties when you need a contained venue.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

NRH families have two dependable hot-weather moves: an early-morning NRH2O session (gates open at 10 a.m. on most days, and the concrete around the water areas is still cool for the first hour) or an afternoon at the NRH Centre's indoor aquatics pool, which runs year-round and doesn't require a specific season or time window. The Centre pool is the better choice when you need a shorter, more predictable outing without NRH2O's full-day commitment.
