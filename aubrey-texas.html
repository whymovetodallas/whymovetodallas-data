Getting Your Bearings

Where Is Aubrey, Texas?

Aubrey sits in northern Denton County, about 40 miles north of Downtown Dallas along the US-380 corridor. It straddles the stretch between Denton to the west and McKinney to the east, giving residents good access to both the Denton employment cluster (University of North Texas, TWU, medical) and the Frisco tech corridor (T-Mobile, Liberty Mutual, The Star) via US-380.

The city proper is small, roughly 8,500 residents, but the broader Aubrey community is substantially larger once you include the master-planned communities in the 76227 zip code. The Dallas North Tollway and I-35E are the primary routes south toward Dallas and the mid-corridor employers. US-380 is the main east-west spine connecting Aubrey to Denton, Frisco, and McKinney.

Education

Schools in Aubrey, Texas

The most important thing to know about Aubrey schools: The 76227 zip code spans two separate school districts. Your specific property address, not your neighborhood name, determines which ISD your children attend. One street can be the difference between Aubrey ISD (TEA C) and Denton ISD (TEA B). Verify by address before making an offer.

Aubrey ISD

Serves Aubrey proper, northern Providence Village, and portions of the 76227 zip. TEA rating: C. Niche overall grade: B. Roughly 3,995 students across 3 elementary schools, 1 middle school, and Aubrey High School. Aubrey HS posts a 94.9% four-year graduation rate. A workable fit for families who prioritize value and community character over district ranking.

Denton ISD

Serves Sandbrock Ranch, Paloma Creek, southern Providence Village, ArrowBrooke, and southern 76227 portions. TEA rating: B. Niche grade: B+. Most new construction communities in southern Aubrey feed Braswell High School. Stronger academic outcomes and more resources make Denton ISD the preferred choice when school ranking is a deciding factor.

Family Life

What Life Actually Looks Like in Aubrey

Youth Sports Culture

The Aubrey Area Youth Sports Association has operated since 1992 and runs baseball, basketball, soccer, softball, volleyball, and cheerleading leagues. The master-planned communities also run their own internal programs and clinics. Spring and fall seasons are well-organized with multiple fields and gym facilities across the area.

Lake Ray Roberts Access

Lake Ray Roberts State Park is less than 25 minutes from most Aubrey neighborhoods. At 29,000 acres it's one of North Texas's premier outdoor destinations: camping, fishing, boating, equestrian trails, and hiking. No other comparable DFW suburb puts you this close to that much open water. It's the lifestyle wildcard that genuinely sets Aubrey apart.

Shopping & Dining

Aubrey proper has limited local options, a few casual restaurants and a smaller HEB footprint, but a full HEB is close. For variety, Frisco is 20 minutes east (Stonebriar Mall, Legacy West) and Denton is 20 minutes west (Golden Triangle Mall, Square area). Residents plan for a drive; it's part of the Aubrey trade-off conversation.

Community Events

Each master-planned community runs its own events calendar: pool parties, seasonal festivals, food trucks, holiday parades. Providence Village and Sandbrock Ranch both have dedicated lifestyle staff. The city of Aubrey hosts farmers market events and a well-attended Fourth of July celebration with a genuine small-town feel that most newer DFW suburbs can't replicate.

New Construction Advantage

With 22 active builder communities and a buyer's market (6 months supply), Aubrey is one of the few DFW areas where you can still negotiate on new construction. Builder incentives, rate buydowns, and closing cost credits are real options here. For families who want a move-in-ready home with a warranty and modern floor plans, Aubrey is the strongest value play in North DFW right now.

Community Feel

Aubrey has something harder to quantify: a genuine rural-meets-suburban character. People wave on streets, kids ride bikes to the amenity center, and neighbors actually know each other's names. It's not manufactured like some newer DFW suburbs. Families who relocated from actual small towns or who want their kids to grow up with that texture consistently say Aubrey delivers it. The master-planned communities add the amenities; the town provides the soul.

The honest trade-off: Aubrey is car-dependent from start to finish. There's no popping out to grab coffee on foot, no walkable downtown, no transit. Every errand, school pickup, and weekend activity involves driving. For families relocating from walkable urban neighborhoods, this is a real adjustment. The master-planned communities compensate with internal walkability, but the surrounding infrastructure is suburban Texas, full stop.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Aubrey has 5 mapped parks within city limits. The master-planned communities add substantial private trail networks. Lake Ray Roberts State Park provides a major outdoor anchor less than 25 minutes away.

Matthews Park

Downtown Aubrey's central park, renovated in 2024 with new playground equipment, enhanced landscaping, and a community gathering area. The go-to spot for the city's seasonal events and farmers market gatherings.

Leslie Park

Features basketball and pickleball courts, a soccer field, and a playground. Popular with school-age kids and adults. The pickleball courts have become a neighborhood gathering point since the 2024 renovation.

Providence Village Lake

The 25-acre community lake at Providence Village includes fishing access, walking paths around the shoreline, and a community waterpark. Open to Providence Village residents; one of the strongest lifestyle amenities in the Aubrey area.

Sandbrock Ranch Trails

Internal trail network connecting neighborhoods, dog parks, and the resort amenity center. The equestrian character of the community extends to horse-friendly paths and landscaping. 2,400 acres provides genuine open space within the community perimeter.

Lake Ray Roberts State Park

The outdoor anchor of the entire area. 29,000 acres of lake, camping, fishing, boating, equestrian trails, and hiking, less than 25 minutes from most Aubrey neighborhoods. No other growing DFW suburb puts you this close to this much outdoor access.

ArrowBrooke Fishing Ponds

Stocked catch-and-release community ponds inside ArrowBrooke, open to residents. A genuinely popular amenity for families with kids who want something to do outdoors on a Tuesday afternoon without driving anywhere. Small but consistently used.

Honest Assessment

Pros & Cons of Living in Aubrey

I won't sell you on Aubrey. Here's the real picture so you can decide if it's right for your family.

What Aubrey Does Well

  • Exceptional value. $350K buys a 2,400-3,000 sq ft home here. The same money gets you half that in Frisco or Prosper. For families who need space, this is the strongest value proposition in North DFW right now.
  • New construction inventory. Multiple active builder communities mean you can customize, get builder incentives (rate buydowns, closing cost help, upgrades), and move in quickly without bidding wars.
  • Lake Ray Roberts proximity. A 29,000-acre lake with camping, hiking, fishing, and equestrian trails, less than 25 minutes away. No comparable suburb in DFW has this.
  • Master-planned community amenities. Paloma Creek, Sandbrock Ranch, ArrowBrooke, and Providence Village deliver resort-style pools, trails, and fitness facilities included in HOA fees.
  • Room to breathe. Bigger lots, less density, genuine rural character at the edges. If your family needs space to spread out, Aubrey delivers it.
  • Buyer's market conditions. Six months of supply and 48 days on market means you can negotiate. That's rare in DFW right now.
  • No state income tax. Significant savings for high earners relocating from California, New York, or Illinois.

What to Weigh Carefully

  • Dual ISD split, address-by-address verification required. The 76227 zip code spans both Aubrey ISD (TEA C) and Denton ISD (B). The difference is significant for families with school-age kids. One street can change the district. Verify every address before you fall in love with a house.
  • Aubrey ISD rating. If you land in Aubrey ISD, the district carries a TEA C rating, the weakest academic rating in this area. Some families are fine with it; others want a B or A ISD regardless of price.
  • MUD/PID fees in new communities. Sandbrock Ranch, Paloma Creek, and parts of Providence Village carry special district fees adding $1,750-$5,250/year indefinitely. These don't go away. Model the full tax picture before buying.
  • Car-dependent, all of it. Walk Score of 41. No transit. Two cars required. Every errand, every activity, every school pickup involves driving.
  • Limited local retail and dining. The options in Aubrey proper are thin. HEB is close, but variety means driving 15-25 minutes to Little Elm, Denton, or Frisco. Not a dealbreaker, but plan for it.
  • US-380 congestion is growing. The corridor is developing fast and the road hasn't kept up. Peak-hour driving on US-380 is increasingly slow. Infrastructure improvements are in progress but not complete.
  • City services still maturing. Aubrey proper is a small city. Roads, municipal services, and emergency response infrastructure are not at the level of Frisco or Prosper. The master-planned communities compensate somewhat, but it's worth understanding.

For a household earning $200,000, the combined effect of lower housing costs and no state income tax often amounts to $60,000-$80,000 per year in additional purchasing power or savings capacity.

Aubrey is one of the best new construction markets in DFW right now. With 22 active communities, 6 months of supply, and 48 days on market, buyers have genuine negotiating power. Builder incentives including rate buydowns and closing cost credits are real and available. Before signing, verify the school district assignment for the specific lot, understand the full MUD/PID tax picture, and bring independent representation. The builder's sales agent works for the builder; a buyer's Realtor costs you nothing extra.

Aubrey's main advantage is price: roughly $150,000-$200,000 less than a comparable home in Prosper. Prosper (A-rated ISD) and Celina (B-rated Celina ISD) offer stronger school districts than Aubrey ISD, though southern Aubrey communities in Denton ISD are more competitive. Little Elm is closer to Frisco and the lake, with similar pricing but less master-planned community depth. For remote workers or hybrid employees who prioritize space and value over commute proximity, Aubrey is the strongest choice in this group.

Lake Ray Roberts State Park is the standout, a 29,000-acre lake with camping, fishing, equestrian trails, and boating, less than 25 minutes away. Within the communities, Paloma Creek, Sandbrock Ranch, ArrowBrooke, and Providence Village all have internal trail networks, pools, and green space. City parks include Matthews Park (renovated 2024) and Leslie Park with basketball and pickleball courts. The Aubrey Area Youth Sports Association runs leagues for most major youth sports.
