What Cleburne Families Actually Do Here

Page last reviewed 2026-06-05. We update activities pages annually as parks, leagues, and venues change.

Quick answer: Cleburne has 13 parks across 565 city-managed acres, three dedicated youth sports leagues at one of the region's largest multi-sport complexes, and 7+ annual community events, making it one of the stronger family-activity options in Johnson County for parents relocating to the Dallas-Fort Worth area.

Cleburne is Johnson County's largest city, and its family amenities punch well above their weight class. The 87-acre John Warren Sports Complex serves as the hub for three separate youth sports organizations, drawing tournament play from across North Texas. Thirteen city parks spread across 565 managed acres give kids room to run every season, while a vibrant historic downtown adds live theater, local museums, and community events to the mix. John Warren Sports Complex

An 87-acre multi-sport facility with 7 baseball/softball fields, 20 soccer fields, and 2 football fields. It serves three dedicated youth sports organizations and regularly hosts regional tournament play.

Splash Station & Hulen Park

Hulen Park is home to Splash Station, the city's aquatic facility with a zero-depth entry pool, two waterslides, a lazy river, and a diving board. Open late May through Labor Day at $6 per person.

Downtown Arts & Culture

The Plaza Theatre Company and the Layland Museum give Cleburne a cultural anchor most cities this size simply don't have. Add a historic downtown with annual events from spring through December and you have a city that consistently surprises relocating families.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Cleburne manages 13 parks across approximately 565 acres of green space, ranging from the 87-acre sports complex to shaded neighborhood parks well suited for everyday play.

Hulen Park

Cleburne's most visited park and home to Splash Station. Mature shade trees cover the picnic pavilions, and the aquatic facility draws families from across Johnson County in summer. Restrooms are well-maintained and paths are stroller-accessible throughout.

Aquatics Picnic Pavilions Restrooms Shade

John Warren Sports Complex

At 87.81 acres, this is Cleburne's primary athletics destination. Seven baseball and softball fields, 20 soccer fields, and 2 football fields accommodate three youth leagues simultaneously. Ample parking and restrooms throughout the complex.

Baseball Soccer Football Restrooms

Nolan River Park

A riverside park along the Nolan River near the historic downtown. The paved path along the riverbank is stroller-accessible and suited to casual walking; fishing access is available along the bank. A solid option for families wanting a scenic afternoon outing close to downtown dining.

Trails Fishing Stroller-Friendly Picnic Areas

Library Park

A shaded downtown park adjacent to the public library, with playground equipment under mature trees. Well-positioned for a park-and-library combination trip with younger kids; benches and picnic tables make it easy for parents to sit while kids play.

Playground Shade Picnic Tables

Henderson Park

A neighborhood park with playground equipment and open lawn areas well-suited for informal games. Less crowded than Hulen on weekend mornings, making it a good option for families with toddlers who need more space to roam without competition for equipment.

Playground Open Lawn Picnic Areas

Marsteller Park

Open green space with athletic courts, playground equipment, and picnic facilities. The courts see regular use from older kids and teens; the playground side works well for elementary-age children. Typically quieter on weekday afternoons.

Playground Athletic Courts Open Space

Crockett Park

A mid-size neighborhood park with picnic shelters, playground equipment, and open fields suited for informal games. Picnic shelters can be reserved through the Parks & Recreation department, making this a solid option for smaller birthday gatherings.

Playground Picnic Shelter Open Fields

Trail System Highlight

Nolan River Trail System runs along the Nolan River through downtown Cleburne, connecting city parks with a paved path that passes through some of the city's most scenic terrain. Great for walking and running with a jogging stroller and recreational cycling on lower-traffic segments.

Relocation Tip

Start at Hulen Park on your first weekend visit. Splash Station gives you an immediate read on how Cleburne families actually spend their summers, and the shaded picnic pavilions are a relaxed way to meet neighbors. If you have kids in organized sports, drive through John Warren Sports Complex while you're in town to get a feel for the scale of the program before registration opens.

This is where the bulk of Cleburne youth league play takes place. The complex also hosts regional tournament events that bring travel teams from across North Texas, giving local athletes regular exposure to competitive play beyond the city's league schedule.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Cleburne's entertainment scene is anchored by its historic downtown, which has a genuine arts presence and several attractions that give families real options beyond the parks and sports complex.

Plaza Theatre Company

Community Theater · Cleburne's resident theater company stages productions year-round in the restored Plaza Theatre on Caddo Street in downtown. Kid-appropriate shows are part of the regular schedule, and the setting itself, a restored historic downtown theater, gives the experience a distinct character you don't find at a suburban multiplex.

Layland Museum

History Museum · Free admission and well-curated exhibits covering Johnson County's history from early settlement through the 20th century. The exhibits are accessible for school-age kids; it's a solid rainy-day option and a genuinely interesting introduction to the region's past for families new to Cleburne.

Chisholm Trail Outdoor Museum

Outdoor Historical Museum · An open-air museum along the old Chisholm Trail featuring historical structures and artifacts that put Texas ranching heritage in tangible context. History-minded kids genuinely connect with the format; it's a local gem that doesn't require a long drive or an admission fee to appreciate.

Splash Station

Aquatics Park · The city-operated aquatic facility at Hulen Park features a zero-depth entry pool (ideal for toddlers), two waterslides, a lazy river, and a diving board. Open late May through early September. Daily admission is $6 per person for ages 3 and up; children under 2 enter free; ages 50 and up pay $3.

Mainstay Farm

Farm Experience · A working farm near Cleburne offering seasonal hands-on activities for families. Mainstay Farm gives kids a distinctly different outdoor experience from a typical city park visit and is especially popular with families who want the kind of activity you can't replicate in a more urban suburb.

Lake Pat Cleburne

Reservoir Recreation · The city's primary reservoir offers fishing access and non-motorized boating for families with older kids who fish or kayak. It's a low-key destination that Cleburne residents treat as a regular weekend option rather than a special-occasion trip.

Day Trip from Cleburne

Cleburne State Park (approximately 15 minutes southwest of downtown), one of the more underrated state parks in North Texas. Over 500 acres of cedar breaks and the Cleburne State Park Lake offer swimming, fishing, paddling, and camping. The proximity to the city makes it a genuinely easy half-day outing that families here use on a regular rotation, not just as a special trip.

Family-Friendly Dining

Cleburne's dining scene is anchored by its historic downtown, where local institutions line Caddo Street and Henderson Street within easy walking distance of each other. The mix leans heavily toward local restaurants over national chains, which is part of what gives the city its character.

H Tea O Best after-activity refreshment A Texas-born tea and refreshment brand with locations in Cleburne. Popular with families and teens after practices, park visits, and summer afternoons at Splash Station.
Downtown Cleburne Dining District Best weekend outing anchor Several local restaurants, coffee shops, and bakeries cluster along Caddo Street within walking distance of the Plaza Theatre and Layland Museum. Worth a morning or evening stroll with the full family.

Most of these are concentrated near Caddo Street and Henderson Street in historic downtown Cleburne, where local restaurants are walkable from the theater and museum district.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Cleburne handles everyday retail needs efficiently, with a historic downtown for weekend browsing and practical options for weekly errands, with no need to drive 40 minutes to Fort Worth for most household staples.

Historic Downtown Cleburne

Historic District

The downtown core along Caddo Street features antique shops, boutiques, and local eateries spread across several well-preserved blocks. The area hosts annual events and is genuinely walkable. Good for a Saturday morning outing when you want a destination with character rather than another strip mall.

Best for: Weekend browsing with school-age kids; antique hunting is a draw for parents who enjoy the format.

Nolan River Mall

Traditional Mall

Cleburne's enclosed regional mall with a mix of chain retailers and locally-owned shops. The covered format is genuinely useful during Texas summer heat and provides the primary regional retail anchor for Johnson County families who need more than what a strip center offers.

Best for: Back-to-school shopping and covered retail on hot days when outdoor centers aren't practical.

Northland Shopping Center

Everyday Retail

An open-air center anchored by major chains with grocery, pharmacy, and general merchandise options. Handles most weekly household errands in one stop without requiring a trip out of the city.

Best for: Weekly groceries and household errands when you need a full-service stop close to home.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Cleburne's community calendar draws from Johnson County heritage, the arts, and seasonal celebrations. Most events center on or near the historic downtown, and the city's railroad history provides a running thread through several of them.

April

SpringFest

An annual spring celebration bringing live entertainment, vendor booths, food, and activities to the downtown area. One of Cleburne's primary community gatherings of the year, held annually since the early 2000s.

April & September

Antique & Arts Road Show

Held twice annually, this market draws antique dealers and artisans to Cleburne for a weekend event popular with families and collectors. The September edition coincides with the fall arts season in downtown.

July

4th of July Celebration

The city's Independence Day celebration includes live entertainment, food, and fireworks. A well-attended annual tradition for Cleburne families. Check the city events calendar for the current year's location and schedule.

September

Wine & Art Festival

A fall festival combining local and regional wines with visual arts and artisan vendors in a walkable downtown setting. Draws a mix of families and adults looking for a relaxed Saturday outing before the holiday season ramp-up.

November

Pioneer Days

A heritage festival celebrating Cleburne's frontier history with period demonstrations, vendors, and programming tied to the city's Chisholm Trail heritage. A genuinely distinctive event that makes Cleburne's history tangible for kids and adults alike.

December

Lighted Christmas Parade

Cleburne's annual holiday parade brings decorated floats, marching bands, and community groups through downtown. One of Johnson County's signature winter traditions, drawing crowds from across the region.

December

Whistle Stop Christmas

A Victorian-era holiday celebration in downtown Cleburne that draws on the city's railroad history. Caroling, local shopping, and seasonal activities make this one of the more distinctive Christmas events in the DFW area's smaller cities.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When July temperatures in Cleburne push past 100°F, these are the options Cleburne families rely on to keep kids occupied without spending the afternoon outside.

Johnson County Library, Cleburne Branch

Type: Public Library · Ages: All ages
A well-stocked children's section with storytime programs, a summer reading challenge, and computer access. Free admission, air conditioning, and a reliable schedule of programming make this a go-to for families during peak heat weeks.

Layland Museum

Type: History Museum · Ages: 5+
Free admission and climate-controlled galleries covering Johnson County history. The exhibits are organized in a way that works for kids who have a history class backdrop to connect with. A genuinely good option when you need two hours of low-cost indoor time.

Plaza Theatre Company

Type: Live Theater · Ages: 5+
Indoor productions run year-round in the restored downtown theater. Kid-appropriate shows are scheduled alongside adult fare. Checking the current season schedule is worth doing when you first move to Cleburne, as productions often book out.

Grumps Burgers (Arcade)

Type: Diner with Arcade · Ages: All ages
The arcade component gives this burger spot a functional indoor activity built in. Works well as a combined meal-and-activity stop, particularly for elementary-age kids who have been cooped up indoors and need something to do with their hands.

Splash Station (Indoor Programs)

Type: Recreation / Aquatics · Ages: All ages
The city's Parks & Recreation department offers swim lessons and indoor programming through the Splash Station facility on a seasonal basis. Check the city's recreation calendar for current session availability.

Burleson & Fort Worth (Short Drive)

Type: Regional Amenities · Ages: All ages
Trampoline parks, bowling alleys, and larger entertainment venues are available 30–45 minutes north in Burleson and Fort Worth. Cleburne families regularly make the drive for options not available locally, particularly during extended summer school breaks.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

Cleburne families with young kids typically split summer days: morning at Splash Station before the heat peaks (aim to arrive at opening), then the Johnson County Library or home for the 1–4pm window when the heat is most intense, then back outdoors for evening walks or park time after 6:30pm when temperatures drop noticeably. The library's summer reading program is well-run and genuinely keeps kids occupied through the middle weeks of July when novelty runs thin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Cleburne.

  • Cleburne has the infrastructure families are looking for at a significantly lower price point than most DFW suburbs. Cleburne ISD operates dedicated school facilities, the John Warren Sports Complex handles major youth sports for three separate leagues, and the historic downtown gives the city a character that many newer suburban developments simply lack. Johnson County's cost of living is genuinely lower than the suburban belt closer to Fort Worth, which makes the 40–50 minute commute to the city reasonable for many families who want more home for their dollar and a place where they actually know their neighbors.
  • The three primary youth sports organizations are the Cleburne Soccer Association (ages 4U through 16U, with recreational and competitive tracks), the Cleburne Baseball and Softball Association (youth through high school age, spring and fall), and the Cleburne Football League (fall season). All three play at John Warren Sports Complex. The city's Parks & Recreation department also offers swim lessons and recreational programming at Splash Station. For sports not covered locally, Burleson and Crowley offer additional options within a 20–30 minute drive.
  • Several good indoor options exist. The Johnson County Library in Cleburne has a strong children's section with programming throughout the year. The Layland Museum offers free admission and covered exhibits that work well for curious school-age kids. The Plaza Theatre Company runs indoor productions on a regular schedule. For a broader set of options, Fort Worth and its museums are approximately 40–45 minutes north, making a rainy-day road trip to the Kimbell or the Fort Worth Museum of Science and History a reasonable choice when local options have been cycled through.
  • Splash Station at Hulen Park is Cleburne's primary aquatic destination. It features a zero-depth entry pool well-suited to toddlers, two waterslides, a lazy river, and a diving board. The facility operates from late May through early September. Daily admission is $6 per person for ages 3 and up, free for children under 2, and $3 for ages 50 and over. Check the City of Cleburne Parks & Recreation website for the current season's hours before your visit, as they vary by day of the week.
  • Both cities appeal to families looking for more affordable entry into the DFW area south of Fort Worth. Burleson sits closer to Fort Worth with shorter commutes and a more developed suburban infrastructure, which typically means higher home prices. Cleburne offers the lowest typical home prices of the two, genuine small-town character centered on its historic downtown, and the John Warren Sports Complex, the largest dedicated youth sports facility in the immediate corridor. Families who prioritize commute time tend toward Burleson; families who want the most home for their dollar in a place with a distinct identity often land in Cleburne.
  • Cleburne is best known as the county seat of Johnson County and as a small city that has preserved its historic downtown while maintaining active community institutions. The city's railroad heritage is central to its identity; the Chisholm Trail Outdoor Museum and Whistle Stop Christmas both draw on this history directly. For families, Cleburne is increasingly known for the John Warren Sports Complex, which draws youth tournament play from across North Texas and gives local kids access to competitive sports infrastructure well above what you'd typically find in a city this size.
