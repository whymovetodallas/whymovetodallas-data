Anna is one of Collin County's fastest-growing cities, and the city has built out parks, youth sports leagues, and a brand-new community library that give families genuine roots from day one.

Quick answer: Anna has 9+ parks, 4 youth sports leagues (soccer, baseball, softball, basketball), and 5+ annual city events. That makes it a genuinely solid family option in Collin County for parents relocating to the Dallas-Fort Worth area, especially those who want a small-town feel with new development all around them.

What Anna Families Actually Do Here

Anna sits at the northern edge of the Collin County growth corridor, and the outdoor infrastructure has kept pace with the population boom. You get a city that still feels unhurried on weekends, with Slayter Creek Park as a genuine community anchor, a new library the whole town rallied to build, and a downtown that still carries its railroad-town character. See the full Anna relocation guide.

Slayter Creek Park Anchor

The city's flagship park handles nearly everything: a splash pad (open late May through summer), 18-hole disc golf, four lighted ball fields, sports courts, and the trail network that is expanding north-south through the city.

City-Run Youth Sports

The City of Anna runs fall soccer (ages 4U-12U) and spring baseball, softball, and T-ball (ages 4U-14U) directly through Parks & Recreation, keeping registration local and costs reasonable for newer families.

A Real Community Library

The Anna Community Library was voter-approved and purpose-built as a neighborhood gathering space. Children's programming, craft events, and storytime sessions give younger kids something structured to do year-round.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Anna maintains 9 named parks managed by the Community Services Department, ranging from the sprawling Slayter Creek complex to smaller neighborhood parks scattered through newer subdivisions. The city's trail network is actively expanding and will eventually connect residential areas north to south via a continuous pedestrian corridor.

Slayter Creek Park

Anna's largest and most-used park, located at 425 W. Rosamond Pkwy. Home to four lighted ball fields (two baseball, two softball), an 18-hole disc golf course, horseshoe pits, sports courts, walking trails, and the Slayter Creek Splash Pad. Restrooms on-site. Stroller-friendly paths throughout. The best first stop for any family new to Anna.

Splash Pad Disc Golf Ball Fields Sports Courts Trails Restrooms

Natural Springs Park

Located at 1201 W. White St. behind Brookshire's, this is Anna's most natural-feeling park. Walking trails wind past a man-made pond with a fishing pier. A playground, covered gazebo (reservable), and open picnic areas round it out. The Paw Park has separate big-dog and small-dog off-leash areas with shade structures and agility equipment. Restrooms on-site.

Fishing Pier Dog Park Playground Walking Trails Picnic Areas Restrooms

Sherley Heritage Park

At the corner of Sherley Ave. and 4th St., this is Anna's most distinctive park. The community-built, train-themed playground sits beside a restored 1885 train depot and a Mogul Locomotive. A large pavilion is available for reservation. Great for ages 3-9 who love trains and for families wanting to connect with Anna's railroad history.

Train-Themed Playground Historic Depot Pavilion Restrooms

Bryant Park

A 5-acre park on the north side of CR 371 near Bryant Elementary. Currently receiving capital improvement upgrades. Good open green space in the northern residential area, convenient for families in that part of Anna. Improvements are in progress per the city's Parks CIP plan.

Open Green Space Development Planned

Baldwin Park

One of several neighborhood-scale parks spread across Anna's residential developments. Part of the city's strategy to keep green space within walking distance as new subdivisions come online. Check annatexas.gov/266 for current amenities and any active improvement projects.

Neighborhood Park

Geer Park & Johnson Park

Two additional neighborhood parks rounding out Anna's distributed green-space network. Together with Henry Clay "Yank" Washington Park and John Flavel Greer Park, they reflect the city's commitment to having parkland within reach of every neighborhood rather than concentrated in one location.

Neighborhood Parks Multiple Locations

Trail System Highlight

Slayter Creek Trails currently run 4,400 feet (0.83 miles) from Slayter Creek Park to Hackberry Drive, with distinctive "Coyote Crossing" track markings along the southern section. This is the first segment of a larger trail planned to run the full north-south length of the city, connecting neighborhoods and parks as Anna grows. Good for walking and jogging with a stroller.

Relocation Tip

Start at Slayter Creek Park on a weekday morning. The splash pad is open Tuesday through Sunday starting at 9 a.m. (the 2026 season opened May 21), the disc golf course is rarely crowded mid-week, and the ball fields give you a good sense of how organized youth sports are run in Anna. It tells you more about the community in 90 minutes than any neighborhood tour will.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

The City of Anna runs its youth athletics programs directly through the Community Services Department, which keeps costs and registration local. Slayter Creek Park's four lighted ball fields handle most of the action on weekends, and the multi-purpose fields host fall soccer. Basketball runs through a partnership with PSA McKinney.

Fall Co-ed Youth Soccer

League: City of Anna Parks & Recreation
Ages: 4U through 12U (ages 3-12, co-ed divisions)
Season: September through November
Where games are played: Slayter Creek Park multi-purpose fields, Saturdays. All-Anna city league format.

Spring Youth Baseball & T-Ball

League: City of Anna / Princeton League
Ages: 4U T-ball through 14U baseball
Season: March through May
Where games are played: Slayter Creek Park (4U-6U all-Anna); 8U-14U play the Princeton League with teams from Melissa, Princeton, Blue Ridge, and Farmersville, alternating home and away on Saturdays.

Spring Youth Softball

League: City of Anna / Princeton League
Ages: 8U through 14U (girls softball divisions)
Season: March through May
Where games are played: Slayter Creek Park ball fields (Saturdays), with 8U-14U girls divisions playing Princeton League competition on the same schedule as baseball.

Winter Youth Basketball

League: PSA McKinney (provided through City of Anna)
Ages: Multiple youth age divisions
Season: Winter (December through February)
Registration: Check psaplano.org for enrollment windows. Games played at Anna-area facilities.

Adult Softball

League: City of Anna Parks & Recreation
Ages: Adult
Season: Seasonal; check annatexas.gov for current registration
Where games are played: Slayter Creek Park lighted softball fields. Good for parents who want to keep playing alongside their kids' leagues.

Featured Sports Facility

Slayter Creek Park Athletic Complex is the heart of organized youth sports in Anna. It includes four lighted ball fields (two dedicated baseball, two softball), multi-purpose grass fields for fall soccer, and the newly expanded sports courts area with basketball and tennis/pickleball courts. All Saturday-morning youth leagues run from here, which means the parking lot is busy but the fields are well-maintained and the atmosphere is exactly the kind of community-on-the-sidelines scene most families are looking for when they relocate.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Anna is a smaller city, so the entertainment options are local and community-focused rather than large-venue. That said, the city has genuine character in its historic downtown, and McKinney's much-broader entertainment scene is about 15 minutes down US 75.

Sherley Heritage Park & Anna Depot

Local history museum · The restored 1885 train depot at Sherley Heritage Park is recognized as the oldest surviving railroad station in Texas. For younger kids, the community-built train-themed playground next to the depot makes history hands-on rather than hands-off. Free to visit; central to old town Anna. About 2 min from downtown Anna.

Slayter Creek Disc Golf Course

Outdoor sports attraction · A free 18-hole disc golf course at Slayter Creek Park, open to the public year-round. Laid out with varying difficulty levels, so it works for both first-timers and experienced players. Good low-cost Saturday activity when the ball fields are occupied. Kids 6 and up tend to enjoy it. About 5 min from most Anna neighborhoods.

Gar Hole

Bar & grill with live music · At 106 Houston St., Gar Hole has earned a reputation as the most kid-welcoming live music venue in the area. Regulars call it "the most kid-friendly biker bar in America," which tells you the vibe: casual, local, with real food (burgers, brisket, frito pie) and a music program that draws families on weekends as much as any dedicated entertainment venue would.

Anna Community Library

Community library and events venue · At 111 W. 5th St., this voter-approved library was built with families in mind from the start. Beyond books, it runs regular children's programming, craft workshops, and seasonal events. The events calendar (annatexas.libcal.com) is worth bookmarking. Free admission; open to all Anna residents.

Slayter Creek Splash Pad

Water play attraction · Free splash pad at Slayter Creek Park, open Tuesday through Sunday from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. (closed Mondays for maintenance). The 2026 season opened May 21. Ground-level water features with no drowning risk make it appropriate for toddlers through early elementary. Check the city's weather hotline (469-218-9696) before heading out on stormy days.

Day Trip from Anna

McKinney's Historic Downtown Square (about 15 min south on US 75), the most walkable town square in the DFW area, with boutique shopping, local restaurants, a farmers market, and the Heard Natural Science Museum and Wildlife Sanctuary just off the square. Families who live in Anna make this a regular weekend stop for the variety it adds without the drive into Dallas proper.

Family-Friendly Dining

Anna's restaurant scene has grown fast alongside the city itself. You get a mix of local originals with loyal regulars and national chains along the US 75 corridor for the nights when everyone just needs something fast and familiar.

Kalamaki Greek Eatery
Best first date after sports
121 W. 4th St. Greek street food: gyros, loaded gyro fries, dolmas, loukoumades. One of the highest-rated spots in town (4.8 stars, 270+ reviews) and the kind of local gem that makes a city feel more interesting than its size suggests.
K Bite
Best picky-eater flex
2010 W. White St. Korean-American fusion with bulgogi burgers, catfish baskets, and bulgogi cheese fries that kids who "only eat normal food" will absolutely eat. Guy Fieri-caliber burgers at a strip-mall price. Top-rated spot with 4.9 stars.
Nanda's Tex Mex
Best after-practice dinner
810 W. White St. Local Tex-Mex standby with fajitas, brisket tacos, chimichangas, and margaritas for the parents. Warm service from a staff that gets to know regulars quickly. Two Anna locations between both Tex-Mex spots.
Sunview Cafe
Best weekend brunch
2010 W. White St. Breakfast and brunch spot with chicken fried steak, migas, cinnamon French toast, and avocado toast on the same menu. Efficient, friendly staff. The place you end up going to every Saturday morning once you discover it.
Mama Mia's Pizzeria
Best Friday night pickup
203 W. 4th St. Local pizza with a loyal following in the Anna/Melissa corridor. Large portions, generous toppings, and fresh dough have made this the go-to pizza spot for families who want something better than a chain without driving to McKinney.
Crow's Country Cafe
Best Southern comfort lunch
113 W. 4th St. Old-school American Southern with steak fingers, fried okra, squash casserole, peach cobbler, and a Sunday buffet. Big portions, unpretentious atmosphere. The kind of place that reminds you Anna was a small Texas town long before the subdivisions arrived.
Texas Roadhouse
Best with a group of 8+
201 S. Central Expy. Hand-cut steaks, fall-off-the-bone ribs, and those cinnamon rolls. The default choice when relatives visit, grandparents are in town, or a youth sports team needs to feed 15 people at once.
Gar Hole
Best with live music
106 Houston St. Burgers, brisket, tacos, and frito pie with a live music program on weekends. Defies expectations at every turn. It is far more family-oriented than the name implies, and local families pack it on weekends. A genuine Anna original.
Fuzzy's Taco Shop
Best quick Tex-Mex
409 S. Central Expy. Fast-casual Tex-Mex with shredded brisket tacos, nachos, and queso. Reliable and consistent. The spot for a quick lunch on a Saturday between sports games when you do not want to wait.
Chili's Grill & Bar
Best chain backup
211 S. Central Expy. The familiar fallback when everyone is tired and nobody can agree. Free chips and salsa, the 3 for Me deal, and a kids menu that covers the basics. Open late most nights.

Most of the local originals are clustered along W. White Street and the old 4th Street corridor near downtown Anna; national chains line S. Central Expressway (US 75) near the S. Powell Pkwy intersection.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Anna's retail is practical and growing. The US 75 corridor handles everyday needs well, and McKinney's retail and specialty shopping is about 15 minutes south for anything more specific.

S. Central Expressway Corridor

Everyday

The stretch of US 75 through Anna has the full national chain lineup for groceries, pharmacy, and household goods. Walmart Supercenter, Brookshire's, CVS, and the usual fast-casual food corridor are all here. Quick in and out without navigating a larger development.

Best for: weekly groceries, pharmacy runs, and every errand you need to knock out between activities on a Saturday morning.

W. White Street Local Shops

Local Retail

The W. White St. and downtown Anna corridor has a growing mix of local businesses, including restaurants, a coffee shop (Grace Place), and small retail. The strip-center at 2010 W. White St. houses several local dining options and is a community gathering point on weekends.

Best for: supporting local businesses, coffee before a park visit, and finding the spots that give Anna its own personality rather than just another suburb identity.

McKinney & Melissa for More

Nearby Retail Hub

For specialty shopping, boutiques, home goods, or a real shopping day, McKinney's Historic Square and Craig Ranch area are 12-15 minutes south. Melissa (just south of Anna on US 75) adds a growing commercial corridor that Anna residents use for Target, Home Depot, and the larger national retail options.

Best for: any purchase that is not available in Anna itself, or a full Saturday family outing that includes shopping, dining, and entertainment in one direction.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

The City of Anna Community Services Department produces a tight calendar of city-wide events that get real community turnout. The lineup is small-city in the best sense: low barrier, high participation, and free for most events.

Spring (typically April)

Easter Eggstravaganza

The city's spring family event, held annually with an egg hunt, children's activities, and community vendors. One of the most attended Parks events of the year. Free to attend; held at Slayter Creek Park. Check the official events page for current year dates (check official events page for this year's schedule).

Spring

Touch-a-Truck

A perennial favorite for younger children: fire trucks, construction equipment, city vehicles, and more parked for kids to climb in, honk horns, and explore up close. Held annually in spring. Free event, typically at a city park or municipal campus location.

June

Juneteenth Celebration

Anna's annual Juneteenth event, held each June with community programming, live entertainment, and family activities. The 2026 edition is scheduled for June 13 at the city's event grounds. Free and open to all Anna residents and neighbors.

Late June / Early July

Boots and Booms

Anna's signature Independence Day celebration, held annually in late June or early July. Food trucks, kids' activities, a military flyover, live music with multiple acts, and a fireworks show capping the evening at approximately 9:30 p.m. Evening events begin at 5 p.m. Check the official events page for each year's performers and exact date (check official events page for this year's lineup).

December

12 Days of Anna

The city's holiday season programming, held annually in December. Typically includes a tree-lighting ceremony, community events across multiple venues, and activities spread across the holiday period. One of the most anticipated city events of the year for families with young children.

Full calendar: Anna official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

Anna's indoor scene is smaller than its larger neighbors, but the community library is a genuine standout, and McKinney's indoor entertainment options are close enough that they function as Anna's extended backyard for summer months and rainy days.

Anna Community Library

Type: Public library with children's programming · Ages: All ages, programs targeted at birth through teen
Located at 111 W. 5th St. on the municipal campus. Storytime sessions, craft workshops, summer reading programs, and rotating events are on a published calendar at annatexas.libcal.com. The facility was purpose-built for community connection, not just book storage. Free.

Slayter Creek Sports Courts

Type: Outdoor courts (covered/lighted) · Ages: All ages
Basketball and tennis/pickleball courts at Slayter Creek Park. While technically outdoor, the lighted courts extend usability into evenings and the courts see year-round play during the mild Texas fall, winter, and spring. Good for rainy-season alternatives when other facilities are full.

Altitude Trampoline Park (McKinney)

Type: Indoor trampoline park · Ages: Ages 2 and up
About 20 minutes south in McKinney. Wall-to-wall trampolines, foam pits, dodgeball courts, and a dedicated toddler area. The most popular rainy-day solution for Anna families with kids in the 4-12 range who need to burn energy indoors. Birthday party packages available.

Regal McKinney or Cinemark (McKinney)

Type: Movie theater · Ages: All ages
McKinney has multiple cinemas about 15 minutes south on US 75. Standard and IMAX screenings, plus summer discount matinees that Anna families use heavily during July and August when outdoor play is uncomfortable during peak afternoon heat.

Everything Ice Cream

Type: Local ice cream shop · Ages: All ages
810 W. White St. Sno-cones, hand-scooped ice cream, waffle bowls, and novelty items like Pop-Tart ice cream sandwiches. A local-owned spot that Anna families have adopted as the post-game reward of choice for every youth sports Saturday. Air-conditioned and cheerful.

Grace Place Coffee

Type: Local coffee shop and community hub · Ages: All ages
505 N. Powell Pkwy. A well-reviewed local coffee shop with a welcoming atmosphere. Blended mochas and cinnamon rolls are the go-to orders. Works as an indoor hangout for parents while kids do homework or read, and as a low-key playdate spot for school-age kids in the afternoons.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When it is 100 degrees in July, Anna families split the day: morning at Slayter Creek Splash Pad before 11 a.m. (the sweet spot before it gets packed), then lunch at Sunview Cafe or K Bite, then the Anna Community Library programming or a movie in McKinney during peak afternoon heat. That sequence gets you outdoors, fed, and cooled down without anyone melting by 2 p.m.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Anna, TX.

  • Anna is a good choice for families who want a small-town atmosphere on the northern edge of the DFW growth corridor. The city has invested in parks infrastructure at a pace that has kept up with the population boom. Slayter Creek Park is a genuine civic anchor, and the new community library reflects serious intent to build community, not just housing. The school district (Anna ISD) is smaller, which many families prefer for the individual attention it tends to provide. The tradeoff is fewer local entertainment options compared to McKinney or Frisco, but those cities are a 15-30 minute drive south on US 75.
  • The City of Anna Parks & Recreation runs three youth sports programs directly: Fall Co-ed Soccer (ages 4U-12U, September through November), Spring Youth Baseball and T-Ball (ages 4U-14U, March through May), and Spring Youth Softball (ages 8U-14U, March through May). Winter Youth Basketball is provided through PSA McKinney. Fall soccer and spring baseball/softball games are held at Slayter Creek Park. Registration opens seasonally through the city's website at annatexas.gov/1494/Athletics.
  • For in-Anna options: the Anna Community Library at 111 W. 5th St. runs regular children's programming and is worth checking for scheduled events at annatexas.libcal.com. Everything Ice Cream on W. White St. is a local favorite for younger kids. For more variety, McKinney is 15 minutes south and has trampoline parks, movie theaters, indoor climbing, and a broad entertainment corridor that Anna families use regularly when the weather does not cooperate.
  • Slayter Creek Park (425 W. Rosamond Pkwy.) is the only park in Anna with a splash pad. The 2026 season opened May 21. The splash pad is open Tuesday through Sunday from 9 a.m. to 8 p.m. and is closed every Monday for maintenance. Entry is free. The city maintains a weather hotline (469-218-9696) and a real-time lightning detection status page for days when weather is a question. None of Anna's other parks currently have splash pads.
  • McKinney has significantly more family entertainment infrastructure: more parks, more youth sports leagues, a broader dining and shopping scene, indoor entertainment venues, and more community events. Anna trades quantity for a quieter, more small-town feel where youth sports leagues are smaller and the community events draw a higher percentage of the population. Families who want to be at the edge of development (with lower home prices than McKinney) and value the small-city character often prefer Anna specifically because it is not yet McKinney-scale. McKinney's full activity scene is accessible in 15 minutes.
  • Anna is known for being one of the last Collin County cities where you can still get into a new construction home at a price that feels like a real deal relative to more established suburbs. The family lifestyle is defined by the community parks (especially Slayter Creek), youth sports on Saturday mornings, and city events that draw genuine participation from a small enough population that you see the same families repeatedly. The Anna Depot at Sherley Heritage Park gives it a historical anchor that most newer suburbs do not have. Most residents accept that for big entertainment they drive south and find it a reasonable tradeoff for the land, the quiet, and the community feel.
