District Overview

Waxahachie ISD serves the historic city of Waxahachie in Ellis County, about 30 miles south of downtown Dallas, enrolling roughly 11,000 students across 17 campuses, including three high schools. If you are relocating and drawn to Waxahachie for its historic character, affordability, and easy I-35E access, this page is meant to help you read the district honestly, because Waxahachie ISD carries one of the most talked-about rating splits in the DFW area.

Here is the tension stated plainly in the first breath: TEA gives Waxahachie ISD a C (a score of 76 in 2025), while Niche gives the district an A-. Those are not typos, and they are not contradictory once you understand what each measures. TEA's grade is built largely from standardized test proficiency; Niche's grade leans heavily on community reviews, self-reported data, and factors like culture and extracurriculars that parents rate highly here. The honest read sits between the two, and this page will walk through both so you can decide which matters more for your family.

Waxahachie is a genuinely charming historic Texas city with well-regarded fine arts programs, a strong career and technical education slate, and a small-city school culture that parents consistently praise. It also posts objective proficiency numbers that land it a C from the state. For a family with some flexibility on the academic ceiling who values character, community, and affordability, Waxahachie ISD is a legitimate choice worth a close look.

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Are drawn to historic small-city character and more affordable real estate south of Dallas
  • Value strong fine arts and a deep career and technical education (CTE) slate
  • Are interested in an early-college pathway (Waxahachie Global High School)
  • Weigh community culture and parent satisfaction alongside test-based ratings
  • Have verified their specific address is inside a Waxahachie ISD attendance zone

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • You cannot compromise on a TEA rating, Waxahachie ISD sits at a C (76)
  • You want the shortest possible commute to Dallas or Fort Worth job centers
  • You are optimizing purely for standardized proficiency scores over community fit
  • You need a large-district specialty menu like IB or multiple magnet pathways

Verify your exact address with the Waxahachie ISD boundary lookup and the TEA School District Locator before you make an offer.

TEA Rating vs Niche Grade: The Honest Explanation

This is the section families come to this page for. In the 2025 TEA accountability ratings, Waxahachie ISD earned an overall C with a score of 76, built from three domains: Student Achievement, School Progress, and Closing the Gaps. On Niche, the same district holds an A- and ranks near the top of Ellis County. So which is right? Both, because they measure different things.

What TEA measures: the A to F rating is driven mostly by standardized STAAR proficiency and growth. It is an objective, test-based snapshot, and it does not weigh how much families like the schools. Under the state's tougher redesigned STAAR standards introduced in 2024, many Texas districts saw scores fall, and Waxahachie's proficiency lands it in the C range.

What Niche measures: the Niche grade blends some of the same academic data with heavy weighting on community reviews, parent and student surveys, culture, diversity, and extracurricular strength. Waxahachie's well-loved fine arts, small-city feel, and engaged community lift the Niche score above what test data alone would produce.

The practical takeaway for a relocating parent: if your priority is measurable academic proficiency, weight the TEA C heavily and dig into the specific campus your address feeds at txschools.gov. If community culture, fine arts, and parent satisfaction carry equal weight for your family, the Niche A- reflects something real that the test score misses. Neither number is the whole story on its own.

Graduation Rate

Waxahachie High School reports a four-year graduation rate of about 94% (per third-party sources including Niche and U.S. News, most recent published year), above the Texas state average of roughly 90%. A strong graduation rate paired with a C accountability score is part of what creates the split picture here: students are finishing, while proficiency on state tests is where the district trails top DFW performers.

STAAR Proficiency

Districtwide STAAR proficiency is the main driver of Waxahachie ISD's C rating, and it is worth pulling by subject and campus at txschools.gov rather than relying on the district average. For context on the student body: about 48% of Waxahachie ISD students qualify as economically disadvantaged (per state data), a factual data point families can weigh as they interpret proficiency figures and campus comparisons.

Advanced Academics & Dual Credit

Waxahachie ISD offers Advanced Placement courses and dual credit, and its Waxahachie Global High School provides an early-college pathway where students can earn college credit or an associate-level head start while in high school. For a motivated student, that early-college route is one of the district's most distinctive academic offerings, so ask about application timing and eligibility.

Specialty Programs

Waxahachie ISD's program strength is a real part of its appeal and a big reason its Niche grade runs ahead of its test-based rating. Here is an honest look at what is available and how access works.

Career & Technical Education (CTE)

One of the district's signature strengths. Waxahachie High School offers hands-on CTE pathways with industry certifications in fields such as health science, welding, construction, culinary arts, cosmetology, and computer science. Confirm which strands run in a given year, since offerings rotate.

Early College (Global High School)

Waxahachie Global High School lets students earn college credit while in high school, a distinctive route for academically motivated teens. Access typically involves an application and selection process, so plan around the timeline if this is your goal.

Fine Arts

Band, choir, theatre, and visual arts are well-regarded and often cited by parents as a district highlight. Fine arts run through the comprehensive high school and its feeder campuses, contributing to the strong community culture reflected in the Niche score.

Gifted & Talented

Waxahachie ISD identifies gifted students through a multi-criteria process, with services beginning in elementary. As with any district, delivery varies by campus, so ask about specifics for your child's grade level.

Athletics

A full UIL athletics program anchors the comprehensive high school and is a strong part of the town's Friday-night culture. For many families, the athletics and fine arts community is a meaningful draw independent of the test-based rating.

Special Education & Support

The district provides services for eligible students ages 3 to 21 under IDEA, including inclusion support, specialized settings, dyslexia intervention, and 504 accommodations. Contact the Special Services office directly to discuss fit for a student with specific needs.

Enrollment & Transfers

Waxahachie ISD registers new students online and at the district's enrollment office. For the current 2025-26 school year, the general steps are:

  • Verify residency documents: proof of residence (lease or closing documents plus a current utility bill), the student's birth certificate, immunization records, and a parent or guardian ID.
  • Register online through the district portal at wisd.org, then complete verification with the district.
  • Early-college application: Waxahachie Global High School is a separate application pathway, so confirm the timeline and criteria if that campus is your goal.
  • Intra-district transfers: confirm current policy for moving between campuses within the district, subject to space.
  • Inter-district transfers: students living outside Waxahachie ISD may apply to attend, subject to space and approval.
  • IEP or 504 transfers: Texas law requires the district to either adopt an existing IEP or convene an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior documentation and contact Special Services early.

Before registration, the most important step for a Waxahachie buyer is confirming your address is actually in Waxahachie ISD and not one of the neighboring Ellis County districts.

The Waxahachie Boundary Check (Read Before You Offer)

Ellis County is a patchwork of school districts, and a Waxahachie mailing address does not automatically mean Waxahachie ISD. The city core is served by Waxahachie ISD, but homes on the rural edges and in newer developments around the city can fall into Midlothian ISD, Red Oak ISD, Ennis ISD, Ferris ISD, or Maypearl ISD instead.

Why it matters: those neighboring districts carry different ratings, tax rates, and programs. Midlothian ISD, for example, posts a TEA B (85), so a home just across the line can mean a different accountability rating than the Waxahachie ISD C. Because district lines here follow specific roads and development boundaries rather than tidy city limits, you cannot eyeball it from a map.

How to verify: use the Waxahachie ISD attendance boundary tool at wisd.org, cross-check the exact address with the TEA School District Locator, and confirm the district shown on the Ellis County appraisal district property record. Making an offer without verifying your specific address can mean your child attends a different district than you planned, and boundary maps do not always match common-sense geography. If schools are driving your move, confirm the district and the specific campus in writing before you go under contract.

Taxes & District Finances

Waxahachie ISD adopted a total property tax rate of about $1.17 per $100 of assessed value for 2025 (roughly $0.62 for maintenance and operations plus $0.55 for debt service), unchanged from the prior year. The sizable debt-service, or I&S, portion reflects bonds the district has passed to build and renovate schools, including a 2023 bond program, a common pattern in growing Ellis County.

What that means for a monthly payment: on a $350,000 home, a rate near $1.17 works out to roughly $340 per month in school taxes before exemptions. Texas homeowners can claim the $100,000 school-district homestead exemption on a primary residence, which lowers the taxable value and your actual bill, so the real number will be lower than the sticker math. Confirm current rates and your assessed value with the Ellis County appraisal district.

For comparison, Waxahachie ISD's roughly $1.17 rate sits above Midlothian ISD (about $1.08) and near several other Ellis County districts. A family choosing Waxahachie for affordability should factor the tax rate into the total monthly cost, because a lower home price paired with a higher rate can narrow the gap versus a home in a lower-rate district.

What It Feels Like to Be a Family Here

Waxahachie's identity is built on its history and its downtown. The 1895 Ellis County Courthouse, the brick streets, and the preserved Victorian homes give the city a character that few DFW suburbs can match, and that sense of place carries into the schools. Parents frequently describe the district as tight-knit and community-driven, with strong fine arts and a Friday-night football culture that anchors the town. That community warmth is a big part of why the Niche grade runs ahead of the test-based rating.

The honest tradeoffs are the ones this whole page keeps returning to. The TEA C (76) is a real, objective marker that the district trails top DFW performers on standardized proficiency, and about 48% of students qualify as economically disadvantaged per state data, context worth understanding as you compare campuses. At the same time, the graduation rate is strong, the CTE and early-college options are genuine assets, and families who value character and community consistently rate the district highly. Whether that balance works for your family is a personal call, and I am happy to walk through it with your specific priorities in mind.

On safety, every family weighs it differently, and it is not something I rank. I encourage you to review third-party resources such as NeighborhoodScout, your local police department's public crime map, or City-Data, and evaluate specific areas directly so you can make your own call.
