Things to Do in Rowlett, TX with Kids

Rowlett wraps around Lake Ray Hubbard and builds its family identity around that water: kayaking, a 6.4-mile paddling trail, a city waterpark, and 600+ acres of parks that feel genuinely unhurried compared to DFW's bigger suburbs.

What Rowlett Families Actually Do Here

Rowlett is one of the few DFW suburbs with genuine lakefront identity. Lake Ray Hubbard defines how the city feels: water access for paddlers and anglers, parks that follow the shoreline, and a pace of life that families moving from denser metros consistently describe as one of Rowlett's best attributes. The city maintains 600+ acres of parkland, a full youth sports network through Garland ISD-area programs, and the Rowlett Community Centre as an anchor for indoor recreation year-round. Lake Ray Hubbard Access

Rowlett wraps around one of DFW's largest recreational lakes. The 6.4-mile Paddle Point Creek Paddling Trail, kayak rentals, and lakefront parks give families waterfront access that suburban DFW rarely offers this close to the city.

Uncrowded Parks

With 600+ acres of parks and a population a fraction of the size of its DFW neighbors, Rowlett parks tend to be less crowded than Frisco or McKinney equivalents on weekends. That's a real quality-of-life difference for families who use parks regularly.

Garland ISD Youth Sports

Rowlett's youth sports network runs through the city's Parks and Recreation department with basketball, baseball, volleyball, soccer, and hockey, plus the Rowlett Youth Soccer Association for competitive play.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Rowlett maintains more than 600 acres of parkland, organized around Lake Ray Hubbard and Rowlett Creek. Paddle Point Park offers unique lake paddling access with kayak rentals. The Rowlett Creek Preserve adjacent to Garland adds 16+ miles of soft-surface trails for hikers and mountain bikers. The Wet Zone Waterpark sits adjacent to Pecan Grove Park for combined water and park outings.

Paddle Point Park

The gateway to the Paddle Point Creek Paddling Trail, a 6.4-mile loop winding through coves and shaded shorelines on Lake Ray Hubbard. Kayak rentals and instruction available on-site (contact Kayak Instruction, Inc. at 972-629-4794). The trail passes through areas with great blue herons, bald eagles, and other waterside wildlife. Floating dock, restrooms, and parking on-site. One of the genuinely distinctive outdoor experiences in the DFW east corridor.

Paddling Trail Kayak Rentals Lake Access Wildlife Viewing

Rowlett Community Park

The city's main athletic park at 8500 St. Andrews Dr, home to the Youth Baseball League fields and youth soccer fields for younger age groups (U4-U8). Playgrounds, open fields, and picnic areas round out a park that most Rowlett families visit regularly during sports seasons. Restrooms and parking available.

Baseball Fields Soccer Fields Playground Picnic Areas

Pecan Grove Park

A 27-acre park adjacent to the Wet Zone Waterpark, with soccer fields, playground equipment, picnic areas, and hiking paths. The combination of the park and the waterpark next door makes this the most practical summer destination in Rowlett: bring a picnic, let younger kids use the playground while older ones hit the waterpark slides.

Soccer Fields Playground Hiking Paths Adjacent to Wet Zone

Rowlett Creek Preserve

A 94-acre preserve adjacent to the Garland city boundary with 16+ miles of soft-surface trails through wooded and grassland terrain. Relatively flat and good for mountain bikers of varying skill levels. The trail network makes this one of the better urban off-road biking venues on the east side of Dallas. Hours: 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. Paved parking lot with picnic pavilion on-site.

Mountain Biking Soft-Surface Trails Wooded Terrain Free Access

Lake Ray Hubbard Shoreline

Rowlett's most defining geographic feature: a large reservoir shared with Garland, Rockwall, and Fate that provides fishing, boating, paddling, and waterfront park access across the city. Families moving from coastal regions often cite Lake Ray Hubbard as a meaningful substitute for water access they left behind. Sunrise and sunset views from shoreline parks are genuinely impressive.

Fishing Boating Waterfront Access Scenic Views

Wet Zone Waterpark

Rowlett's city waterpark at 5304 Main St, with slides, a lazy river, and a splash play area. The park underwent major improvements during 2025 and reopened with a Grand Reopening Celebration in June 2026. A practical summer option for families who want waterpark access without the drive to a regional park. Check wetzonewaterpark.com for current 2026 hours and admission pricing.

Water Slides Lazy River Splash Area Admission Fee

Waterview Park

A neighborhood park in the Waterview community with open lawn, playground equipment, and walking paths. The Waterview master-planned community is one of Rowlett's best-known residential areas; the park reflects the planning standards of the neighborhood and tends to be well-maintained and popular with families on weekday afternoons.

Playground Walking Paths Open Lawn

City Park Network

Rowlett maintains multiple neighborhood parks beyond the larger anchors, with playgrounds, disc golf, tennis courts, fishing ponds, and picnic pavilions spread throughout the city. Use the City Parks Listing at rowletttx.gov/751/Current-Parks-Listing to find the park nearest your neighborhood before you settle on one.

Disc Golf Tennis Courts Fishing Ponds Multiple Locations

Trail System Highlight

Paddle Point Creek Paddling Trail, a 6.4-mile loop on Lake Ray Hubbard through Paddle Point Park, is the most distinctive outdoor experience in Rowlett and one of the few urban paddling trails in the Dallas metro. Kayak rentals are available at the park. Beginners complete the loop in 2 to 3 hours; the flat water is appropriate for families with kids old enough to paddle or ride along. Great blue herons and bald eagles are commonly spotted along the trail.

Relocation Tip

Start at Paddle Point Park. Rent a kayak, do a portion of the lake trail, and let the kids watch for bald eagles. Then drive five minutes to Pecan Grove Park for a picnic. This combination of lake and park, free and easy, captures exactly what makes Rowlett's outdoor identity different from most DFW suburbs.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Rowlett's youth sports are run through the city's Parks and Recreation department and through independent associations. All city leagues use the Rowlett Community Centre (5300 Main Street) or Community Park (8500 St. Andrews) for games and practices. Registration is available online at webtrac.rowletttx.gov or in person at the Community Centre.

Soccer

League: Rowlett Youth Soccer Association (RYSA)
Ages: U4 through U18
Season: Fall and spring
U4 through U8 play at Rowlett Community Park. U9 and above play interleague with Wylie and surrounding cities for broader competition. Visit rowlettsoccer.org for registration.

Baseball

League: City of Rowlett Parks and Recreation
Ages: Youth divisions
Season: Spring and fall
All games and practices at Community Park fields (8500 St. Andrews). Both spring and fall seasons available. Register at webtrac.rowletttx.gov or call (972) 412-6170.

Basketball

League: City of Rowlett Parks and Recreation
Ages: 4 to 13
Season: Summer and winter
Both summer and winter leagues available. All games and practices at the Rowlett Community Centre (5300 Main Street). A good introductory league for younger kids new to organized basketball.

Volleyball

League: City of Rowlett Parks and Recreation
Ages: Girls youth divisions
Season: Spring and fall
Girls youth volleyball available through the city. Games at the Rowlett Community Centre. Register at webtrac.rowletttx.gov. Spring and fall seasons run concurrent with the school calendar.

Hockey

League: Rowlett Hockey League
Ages: Youth divisions
Season: Varies
One of the few DFW suburbs with an organized youth hockey program. Visit the league site for current age divisions and registration information. Parks amenities include in-line hockey facilities.

Multi-Sport (i9 Sports)

League: i9 Sports Garland-Rowlett-Richardson
Ages: 3 to 17
Season: Year-round by session
Non-competitive recreational leagues covering soccer, basketball, flag football, baseball, and more. A low-pressure option for younger children or those new to organized sports who aren't ready for the city leagues.

Featured Sports Facility

Rowlett Community Centre at 5300 Main Street is the hub of youth athletics in the city. Indoor gyms, multi-use courts, an indoor walking track, and fitness equipment make it a year-round anchor for families who want structured recreation. Walk-in registration for sports leagues is available Monday through Friday 6 a.m. to 9 p.m., Saturday 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., and Sunday noon to 6 p.m.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Rowlett's family entertainment combines city-owned recreation with private venues and the lake access that defines the area's lifestyle. For larger attractions, the drive to nearby Rockwall or Garland adds options without a long trip.

Rowlett Wet Zone Waterpark

Rowlett's city waterpark at 5304 Main St, with slides, a lazy river, and a splash area. The park underwent major improvements during 2025 and celebrated a Grand Reopening in June 2026. A well-priced option for regular summer visits without the travel to Hurricane Harbor or Denton Water Works. Check wetzonewaterpark.com for current hours and pricing.

Shenaniganz Entertainment Center

A Rowlett-area family entertainment center with bowling, laser tag, and escape rooms. Covers multiple activity categories in one visit, which makes it a practical option for mixed-age birthday parties or a rainy-day outing when you need something to hold the group's attention for two or three hours.

Rowlett Bowlarama

A 26-lane bowling facility with open and league bowling, glow bowling events, a pro shop, bar and grill, arcade area, and pool tables. One of the better standalone bowling centers in the east Dallas corridor. Glow bowling events on weekend evenings are popular with school-age kids.

Putt-Putt Fun Center

Mini-golf, batting cages, and an arcade. An affordable family outing that doesn't require much planning. Mini-golf works well for families with a wide age range since even younger kids can participate without needing special skills or fitness. Good for a quick 90-minute outing after school or on a weekday evening.

Rowlett Community Centre

Rowlett's state-of-the-art recreation center at 5300 Main Street, with a fitness room, indoor walking track, full gyms, multi-use courts, a game room, and banquet rooms. Open to residents and non-residents with a day pass. A reliable anchor for indoor family activity year-round, particularly on hot summer afternoons.

Rowlett City Library

A community library with year-round children's programming, summer reading challenges, and regular events for all ages. The library system is part of the Dallas County Libraries network, giving Rowlett residents access to a broader collection and shared programs. A free, consistent option for families who make library visits a regular habit.

Day Trip from Rowlett

The Harbor at Rockwall (about 10 minutes east), a waterfront lifestyle center on Lake Ray Hubbard's east shore with lakefront dining, live music at the Harbor Amphitheater (free summer concerts), and a marina walkway. Families from Rowlett treat it as an extension of their own waterfront; the drive is short and the atmosphere is notably more relaxed than entertainment districts to the west.

Family-Friendly Dining

Rowlett's dining scene skews toward local and neighborhood-friendly, with a mix of independent restaurants and reliable chains concentrated along Main Street and near the Rowlett/Garland border. For a fuller waterfront dining experience, The Harbor in Rockwall is 10 minutes east.

Hat Creek Burger Company
Best smash burger run
Texas-grown burger chain with an outdoor, family-friendly setup. Smash burgers, hand-cut fries, and a kid-tolerant atmosphere. Strong local following among Rowlett families.
Napoli's Italian Restaurant
Best family Italian night
A Rowlett institution since 1999 with NYC-style hand-tossed pizza and Italian classics. Relaxed family-style atmosphere; the kind of restaurant a neighborhood keeps coming back to for years.
Dalrock Diner
Best breakfast run
Old-school American diner with hearty breakfast, brunch, lunch, and dinner staples. Friendly service and wallet-friendly prices. A local favorite for Saturday morning family breakfasts before park outings.
Sabaidee Lao
Best flavor adventure with kids
Savory, tangy, and aromatic Thai and Lao-inspired dishes in a welcoming restaurant. A good introduction for families who want their kids to explore flavors beyond burgers and pizza.
Raising Cane's
Best quick chicken run
Texas-born chicken finger chain with a simple, dependable menu. Fast, clean, and reliably good. The default post-sports-practice dinner choice for many Rowlett families who don't have time to cook.
Fuzzy's Taco Shop
Best cheap taco night
Baja-style tacos at casual prices in a loud, kid-tolerant atmosphere. A solid weeknight option when the family is hungry and nobody wants to plan.
Rudy's Country Store & Bar-B-Q
Best Texas BBQ night
Cafeteria-style Texas BBQ with brisket, sausage, and creamed corn. Kids pick their own plate; parents pick theirs. A reliable, unpretentious Texas experience for families relocating from out of state.
Chili's Bar & Grill
Best fallback family dinner
A dependable American chain with a kids' menu, full bar for parents, and the kind of atmosphere that handles a loud table without drama. Good for nights when everyone just wants something familiar after a long day.
Chicken Express
Best Texas-style quick dinner
A Texas institution serving fried chicken, tenders, and Southern sides. Kids love it, parents know exactly what they're getting, and the price point makes it easy. Common post-game stop in Rowlett.
Cracker Barrel Old Country Store
Best country breakfast outing
Full country breakfast and dinner menu in a relaxed, family-friendly environment. The rocking chairs and old-country store add a novelty factor kids enjoy. A Rowlett-area location makes it accessible for weekend morning outings.

Most of Rowlett's dining options cluster along Main Street and the Dalrock Road corridor. For a waterfront dining experience, The Harbor at Rockwall is about 10 minutes east with lakefront restaurants and a waterside walkway.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Rowlett's retail is concentrated in everyday-practical centers rather than lifestyle destinations. For larger shopping trips, the drive to Garland, Mesquite, or Rockwall adds options within 15 to 20 minutes.

Rowlett Crossing & Dalrock Corridor

Everyday Retail

The Main Street and Dalrock Road corridors hold Rowlett's core everyday retail: Target, Home Depot, grocery stores, and the mix of casual dining and service businesses that families need weekly. Not destination shopping, but highly functional for new residents building their routine.

Best for: weekly groceries, hardware runs, and routine errands without leaving Rowlett.

The Harbor at Rockwall (Nearby)

Lifestyle & Waterfront

About 10 minutes east, Rockwall's waterfront lifestyle center combines boutique shopping, lakefront dining, and an amphitheater. More of a destination than a shopping trip, but Rowlett families treat it as their go-to for a Saturday evening out with atmosphere. The marina walkway and lake views add to the experience.

Best for: a Saturday evening outing combining waterfront dinner, shopping, and a casual walk along the lake.

Town East Mall (Mesquite, Nearby)

Enclosed Mall

About 15 minutes west in Mesquite, Town East Mall is the closest full enclosed mall to Rowlett with major anchors, a food court, and a range of retail categories. A practical option for full-day back-to-school shopping trips or when you need a wider anchor-store selection than Rowlett's everyday corridors offer.

Best for: full-day shopping trips requiring major anchors and department stores, about 15 minutes from central Rowlett.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Rowlett's Special Events Division creates festivals, concerts, markets, and cultural celebrations throughout the year. The city's calendar reflects its community-first identity: most events are low-cost or free, and they consistently draw from the full population of the city rather than specific neighborhoods.

June

Wet Zone Waterpark Season Opening

The Rowlett Wet Zone reopened with a Grand Reopening Celebration in June 2026 following major improvements during the 2025 closure. The opening of the waterpark each summer marks the unofficial start of the outdoor season for Rowlett families. Check wetzonewaterpark.com for seasonal programming and events.

May through August

Free Summer Concerts at The Harbor

The Harbor Amphitheater in Rockwall (10 minutes from Rowlett) hosts free outdoor concerts through the summer season. Live entertainment (check the Harbor's official site for this year's lineup), waterfront setting, and no admission charge make this a standard Rowlett-area family outing from Memorial Day through Labor Day.

Summer

Rowlett Summer Concerts

The city hosts outdoor summer concerts as part of the Parks and Recreation Special Events calendar. Free or low-cost, family-friendly evenings with live music and community gathering. Check rowletttx.gov/calendar.aspx for current dates and venues as the schedule is set annually.

Fall

Rowlett Fall Festival

An annual fall community gathering with food vendors, family activities, entertainment, and the seasonal transition from summer to fall programming. A good early introduction to Rowlett's community character for families who move in during summer. Check the city's Special Events page for current details.

Spring

Rowlett Youth Sports Seasons

Spring marks the opening of soccer, baseball, and volleyball seasons at Rowlett Community Park and the Community Centre. The parks and fields fill with families from March through May. For new residents, attending a youth game or practice at Community Park is a quick way to meet neighbors and understand the city's family culture.

Year-Round

Paddle Point Paddling & Kayak Events

Kayak instruction and group paddling outings run through the season at Paddle Point Park. The 6.4-mile Lake Ray Hubbard paddling trail is accessible year-round, with autumn and spring offering the most comfortable conditions. Kayak Instruction, Inc. runs organized events and lessons; contact via Paddle Point Park at (972) 629-4794.

December

Rowlett Holiday Events

The city's Special Events Division organizes winter holiday programming including community gatherings, lights displays, and seasonal activities around Downtown Rowlett. Check the official city calendar at rowletttx.gov/calendar.aspx closer to the holiday season for confirmed dates and venues.

Spring

Downtown Rowlett Market Events

Downtown Rowlett hosts periodic market events, pop-ups, and cultural celebrations organized by the Special Events Division and the Rowlett Chamber of Commerce. These events support local businesses and give families a reason to explore the downtown area. Check the Rowlett Chamber calendar at cca.rowlettchamber.com for upcoming events.

Full calendar: Rowlett official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

When Texas summer heat hits and outdoor options become less appealing, Rowlett's indoor roster covers the main categories families need. The Community Centre is the anchor; the entertainment venues add variety.

Rowlett Community Centre

Type: Recreation Center · Ages: All ages
Indoor gyms, multi-use courts, indoor walking track, fitness room, and a game room at 5300 Main Street. The city's primary indoor recreation facility; day passes available for non-members. Open Monday through Friday 6 a.m. to 9 p.m., Saturday 8 a.m. to 9 p.m., Sunday noon to 6 p.m.

Rowlett Bowlarama

Type: Bowling · Ages: All ages
26 lanes with open bowling, league bowling, and glow bowling events. Bar and grill on-site for dinner while you bowl. Glow bowling nights are a popular weekend option for school-age kids. Call ahead for open lanes on weekend evenings.

Shenaniganz Entertainment Center

Type: Bowling, Laser Tag, Escape Rooms · Ages: All ages
Multiple activity categories under one roof: bowling, laser tag, and escape rooms. A good extended-visit option for birthday parties or mixed-age groups who want more than one type of activity in an evening.

Putt-Putt Fun Center

Type: Mini-Golf, Batting Cages, Arcade · Ages: All ages
An affordable multi-activity option that handles a range of ages without anyone getting bored too fast. Mini-golf is one of the more family-inclusive activity formats since younger kids can participate alongside older ones without skill gaps creating friction.

Rowlett Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: All ages
Part of the Dallas County Libraries network, with year-round children's programming, story times, summer reading challenges, and family events. A free, consistent anchor for families who make regular library visits part of their routine. Check rowletttx.gov for current branch hours and programming schedule.

Nearby: Rockwall Entertainment

Type: Multiple venues · Ages: All ages
Rockwall, about 10 minutes east, adds additional indoor options including bowling and entertainment venues near The Harbor. Rowlett families routinely cross into Rockwall for entertainment options, making the two cities practically one activity zone for east Dallas families.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When Rowlett temperatures push past 100 degrees in July and August, families rotate through four main options: the Wet Zone Waterpark for a full water day, Paddle Point Park's shaded lake trail for a morning kayak before it gets too hot, the air-conditioned Rowlett Community Centre for gym time or open court, or the drive to Rockwall's Harbor for an evening on the waterfront when the temperature drops. The lake and the Community Centre together handle most of what families need through a long Texas summer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Rowlett.

  • Rowlett is a strong choice for families who prioritize waterfront access, lower density, and a genuine community feel alongside practical DFW commute positioning. The city sits on Lake Ray Hubbard's western shore, which gives it a natural amenity that most Dallas suburbs can't match. Garland ISD serves Rowlett's schools. The city's parks, youth sports leagues, and the Wet Zone Waterpark provide solid family infrastructure. Families moving from lakefront or coastal environments often cite Rowlett as the DFW suburb that came closest to their previous quality of life. It's quieter than Frisco or McKinney and offers better home value per square foot for the lake-access lifestyle it delivers.
  • Rowlett's city-run youth sports include basketball (ages 4 to 13, summer and winter seasons), baseball (spring and fall seasons at Community Park), and girls volleyball (spring and fall). The Rowlett Youth Soccer Association (RYSA) is the independent soccer organization; U4 through U8 play at Community Park while U9 and above play interleague with Wylie and surrounding cities. The Rowlett Hockey League handles youth hockey through an independent association. i9 Sports covers the Garland-Rowlett-Richardson area for recreational multi-sport leagues for ages 3 to 17. All city leagues register through webtrac.rowletttx.gov or in person at the Community Centre (5300 Main Street).
  • Several good options. The Rowlett Community Centre handles indoor gym time, open courts, and the walking track with a day pass. Shenaniganz Entertainment Center covers bowling, laser tag, and escape rooms in one visit. Rowlett Bowlarama offers 26 lanes plus a restaurant for a full evening. Putt-Putt Fun Center handles mixed-age groups with mini-golf, batting cages, and arcade games. The Rowlett Public Library offers free children's programming year-round. For a broader set of options, the drive to Rockwall or Garland (15 to 20 minutes) opens additional indoor entertainment venues.
  • Rowlett's water activities center on Lake Ray Hubbard. Paddle Point Park offers a 6.4-mile paddling trail on the lake with kayak rentals and instruction available from Kayak Instruction, Inc. (972-629-4794). The Rowlett Wet Zone Waterpark at 5304 Main St provides water slides, a lazy river, and a splash area with seasonal operation. The lake itself supports boating and fishing from various access points. For families who want ocean-adjacent water recreation, Lake Ray Hubbard is the closest DFW equivalent: large, open, and home to diverse birdlife including bald eagles and great blue herons along the Paddle Point trail.
  • Rowlett and Rockwall share Lake Ray Hubbard but have different characters. Rockwall is on the east shore of the lake, has a more polished downtown core with The Harbor waterfront development, and sits in Rockwall County with Rockwall ISD. Rowlett is on the west shore, is in Dallas County, is served by Garland ISD, and offers more home-value per dollar than Rockwall at comparable proximity to the lake. Families who prioritize the established waterfront dining and entertainment scene at The Harbor often prefer Rockwall; families who prioritize value and lake access without paying Rockwall premiums tend to find Rowlett compelling. Both cities are close enough that Rowlett families use The Harbor regularly, making the distinction less about what's available and more about home cost and school district preference.
  • Rowlett is known for three things families consistently value: Lake Ray Hubbard access (the 6.4-mile Paddle Point paddling trail is genuinely unique in DFW), the Rowlett Wet Zone Waterpark (a city-owned facility that underwent major improvements and reopened in 2026), and a pace of life that feels genuinely different from the high-growth northern suburbs. The city's 600+ acres of parks, youth sports network through Parks and Recreation, and the Community Centre anchor family life outside of school. For families relocating from waterfront markets, Rowlett often surprises with how much lake-adjacent lifestyle it delivers at a price point well below what similar access costs in markets like Austin or the Houston waterfront suburbs.
