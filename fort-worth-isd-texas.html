Fort Worth ISD Overview

~70,000 Students Enrolled
140+ Campuses
C (73) TEA Grade 2024–25
B− Niche Grade (3rd-party)
83% Graduation Rate
$1.06 Tax Rate per $100 (2024)

Fort Worth ISD is Tarrant County's largest school district and the urban core of the western DFW metroplex, serving roughly 70,000 students across more than 140 campuses in the City of Fort Worth. If you're relocating to the Fort Worth side of DFW and wondering whether this is the district for your family, the honest answer depends almost entirely on one question: are you pursuing a specific magnet or specialty program, or are you choosing a district based on overall academic performance? Those are two very different conversations.

Most families relocating to Tarrant County with general academic priorities will land in Keller, Grapevine-Colleyville, Carroll, Mansfield, or Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD rather than Fort Worth ISD. The district's TEA C rating (73 in 2024-25) reflects the reality of serving a large urban population, with approximately 85% of students qualifying as economically disadvantaged. The district-wide score does not represent the ceiling for every campus within it. Fort Worth ISD's 125 rated schools in 2024-25 include 17 rated A and 28 rated B alongside campuses with lower scores. The variance between campuses is among the widest of any DFW district.

Where Fort Worth ISD deserves serious attention is its specialized programming. The IB programme at Paschal High School has a long track record as a college-prep pipeline. The PASCAL fine arts magnet at Wyatt High School is one of the most respected performing arts programs in Texas. The district's CTE pathways in aviation, healthcare, and culinary reflect Fort Worth's actual industry landscape in ways suburban districts rarely replicate. For a family whose student is serious about a specific program, Fort Worth ISD's magnet and Gold Seal options change the calculation.

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Have a student targeting the IB programme at Paschal or the PASCAL fine arts magnet at Wyatt
  • Are interested in CTE pathways in aviation, healthcare, or culinary arts not available in suburban districts
  • Are already living in Fort Worth and not looking to relocate to an outer suburb
  • Want a large, diverse student environment with varied extracurricular and program options
  • Understand that campus selection, not just district assignment, is the decision that matters most here

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • Your primary criterion is overall district-level academic performance ratings
  • You want neighborhood-school consistency without relying on magnet program access
  • Commute geometry favors a suburban Tarrant County address near Keller, Colleyville, or Southlake
  • You're relocating from a high-performing suburban district and want a similar default neighborhood-school experience
Boundary Verification

Not every Fort Worth mailing address falls within Fort Worth ISD. Parts of the City of Fort Worth are served by Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD, Keller ISD, Crowley ISD, or White Settlement ISD depending on exact location. Always verify your specific address at enrollment.fwisd.org before making an offer. A Fort Worth ZIP code alone is not confirmation of district assignment.

TEA Accountability Rating

Fort Worth ISD earned a C (73) from the Texas Education Agency in 2024-25, continuing a genuine improvement trend: the district scored a D (65) in 2022-23, improved to C (70) in 2023-24, and reached C (73) in 2024-25. That three-year trajectory matters for families considering the district's direction, not just its current standing.

TEA A-F ratings assess districts on three domains. Student Achievement measures current academic performance across STAAR exams, graduation rates, and college/career readiness indicators. School Progress measures how much students grew academically year over year, regardless of starting point, and how the district performs relative to similar districts statewide. Closing the Gaps assesses how well the district serves students with disabilities, English learners, and students from lower-income households.

For a large urban district serving roughly 85% economically disadvantaged students, a C in 2024-25 represents genuine progress. The improvement in the Closing the Gaps domain is particularly meaningful for families whose students receive special education or English-language-learner services. That said, what matters most for a relocating family is not the district average but the specific campus their child will attend. The same district that has 17 A-rated schools also has campuses rated D and F. Identifying the right campus or program within Fort Worth ISD is a more useful exercise than evaluating the district number alone.

TEA vs. Niche: What Each Measures

TEA's A-F grades are calculated from STAAR performance, growth, and equity metrics using a state-defined formula. Niche.com's B- grade for Fort Worth ISD incorporates parent reviews, college data, and survey factors alongside academic metrics. The two systems measure different things and often diverge for large urban districts. Neither tells the full story of an individual campus experience.

Academics

Fort Worth ISD's 2022-23 graduation rate was 83%, below the Texas statewide average of roughly 90% (TEA PEIMS). The gap reflects both the district's student demographics and the reality that a portion of students in large urban districts transfer, age out, or exit before graduation in ways smaller suburban districts do not experience at the same rate. The individual high school graduation rates vary significantly: Paschal High School and the district's magnet campuses typically perform well above the district average.

STAAR proficiency rates across Fort Worth ISD show wide campus-to-campus variation. The district's overall reading and math proficiency percentages trail statewide averages, with the strongest performance concentrated at magnet campuses and A-rated neighborhood schools. Families should research the specific campus they are zoned to or applying to rather than relying on district averages. Texas Tribune's Schools Explorer (linked in Sources) shows campus-level STAAR data by year.

Advanced Placement access varies by campus. Paschal High School has historically led the district in AP enrollment and AP Scholar recognition. The Gold Seal Programs of Choice at each high school create college-and-career pathways that go beyond the standard curriculum but function differently from the traditional AP/IB model. College, Career, and Military Readiness (CCMR) data from TEA provides the most reliable indicator of how each campus prepares students for life after high school. For the most current campus-level STAAR, AP, and CCMR data, visit txschools.gov.

Notable Campuses

Fort Worth ISD operates 13 high school campuses plus several specialized schools. The quality range is wide. Below are the campuses most relevant for families evaluating specialty programs or high-performing options within the district.

R.L. Paschal High School

High School ~2,200 students IB Programme

The district's flagship campus and one of Fort Worth's oldest continuously operating high schools. Paschal leads Fort Worth ISD in AP enrollment and hosts the district's International Baccalaureate programme. Families targeting IB in the Fort Worth area typically center their home search around campus proximity and FWISD attendance zones that feed Paschal.

O.D. Wyatt High School

High School PASCAL Fine Arts Magnet

Home to the PASCAL (Performing Arts and Communication Technologies) Gold Seal Program, widely regarded as one of the strongest performing arts magnets in North Texas. PASCAL draws students from across the district by application. Students in theatre, visual art, music, dance, and film pursue dedicated studio and performance coursework alongside the standard curriculum.

Trimble Technical High School

High School CTE Focus

Fort Worth's dedicated career and technical education campus, with programs in healthcare technology, aviation maintenance, culinary arts, cosmetology, and construction trades. Trimble Tech students often leave with industry certifications alongside their diplomas. For families with students who are career-pathway oriented, this campus offers options not available in most DFW suburban districts.

South Hills High School

High School Southwest Fort Worth

Serves the southwest Fort Worth area with a Health Science Technology Gold Seal pathway. Rated above the district average in recent TEA cycles, South Hills is among the stronger neighborhood high schools in the district for families zoned to the southwest quadrant of the city.

Carter-Riverside High School

High School Near Southside / River Oaks

Serves the Near Southside and River District neighborhoods with a Business and Finance Gold Seal pathway. A midsize campus with a close-knit reputation. Families buying near the Medical District or the Cultural District who remain within Fort Worth ISD will typically feed to Carter-Riverside.

Diamond Hill-Jarvis High School

High School Dual Language

Features a Spanish/English Dual Language pathway at the high school level, one of the few in DFW. For families seeking bilingual continuity from elementary through high school, Diamond Hill-Jarvis is a meaningful option worth researching with the district directly.

For ratings on all Fort Worth ISD campuses, the Fort Worth Report's accountability coverage (linked in Sources) provides campus-by-campus TEA scores updated annually.

Specialty Programs

Fort Worth ISD's specialty programs are among the most diverse in DFW for a single district. The caveat: program access is not automatic. Every program below requires an application, and most draw from across the district or the region. Families should identify the program first, then verify which campus houses it and what the address and transportation implications are before choosing a home.

International Baccalaureate (IB)

Campus: Paschal High School. Access: Application required; open to all Fort Worth ISD residents. The IB Diploma Programme at Paschal is one of the district's most established college-prep pipelines. Families specifically pursuing IB should confirm they are zoned to or can transfer to Paschal before finalizing a home purchase.

PASCAL Fine Arts (Gold Seal)

Campus: O.D. Wyatt High School. Access: Application and audition/portfolio required; open to all FWISD residents. Disciplines include theatre, visual arts, music, dance, and film/media. Transportation is the parent's responsibility for students outside the Wyatt attendance zone.

Career & Technical Education (CTE)

Campuses: Trimble Technical HS (aviation, healthcare, culinary, construction); CTE pathways also exist at most other high schools. Access: Industry certifications available. Trimble Tech operates as an open-enrollment magnet within the district. One of the few DFW districts with an FAA-approved aviation maintenance program.

Gold Seal Programs of Choice

Campuses: All 13 FWISD high schools. Access: Application-based; lottery for oversubscribed programs. Each high school operates a themed Gold Seal pathway (health science, business, engineering, arts, etc.) that provides applied coursework alongside the standard curriculum. Pathways vary by campus.

Two-Way Dual Language

Campuses: Select elementary schools and Diamond Hill-Jarvis HS. Access: Enrollment at the designated campus. Fort Worth ISD runs one of the more developed dual-language pipelines in Tarrant County, offering Spanish/English instruction from elementary through high school at continuity campuses. Verify current campus list with the district directly.

Gifted & Talented (GT)

Access: Districtwide; assessed via state identification process. GT services are available at neighborhood campuses across the district. Quality and depth of GT programming varies by campus. The district's magnet high schools typically serve GT-identified students through advanced coursework rather than separate programming.

Home Search Strategy for Program Seekers

If your child is targeting a specific magnet program, start with the program campus, then work backward to which neighborhoods feed it or allow transfers to it. Buying in an FWISD attendance zone that sends students to a lower-performing neighborhood school, then assuming a magnet transfer will be granted, is not a sound strategy. Verify transfer availability and waitlist status for the 2025-26 school year before making an offer.

Enrollment & Transfers

New Student Registration (2025-26)

Fort Worth ISD registration is handled online through the district's enrollment portal at enrollment.fwisd.org. Required documentation for new students typically includes proof of residency (lease or utility bill with the Fort Worth ISD address), student birth certificate, immunization records, and prior school records. Enrollment windows open in spring for the following school year. Contact the assigned campus directly for campus-specific enrollment support.

Open Transfers (2025-26)

For incoming 6th-12th graders, the Open Transfer Application window for 2025-26 ran March 1-25, 2025. Transfers use a lottery system similar to the Gold Seal application process. Parents may select up to three campus preferences. Certain campuses designated as near-capacity may not be available as first-choice transfers. Transportation for transfer students is the parent's responsibility unless the student qualifies under specific program transportation rules. Intra-district elementary transfers follow a separate review process.

Inter-District Transfers

Families seeking to transfer into Fort Worth ISD from outside the district, or out of Fort Worth ISD into an adjacent district, should contact the receiving district directly. Inter-district transfers are granted at each district's discretion and are not guaranteed.

Special Education & IEP Transfers

Texas law requires that a receiving district convene an ARD (Admissions, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of a student's enrollment to review and adopt or revise the student's IEP or 504 plan. Families relocating with a child receiving services should bring all prior evaluation reports, current IEP or 504 documents, and any supplementary materials. Contact Fort Worth ISD's Special Services office at (817) 814-2000 before finalizing enrollment to discuss services available at your assigned campus. Parent experience with special education services varies by campus and by student need.

Address Verification

Proof of a specific Fort Worth address within FWISD boundaries is required at enrollment. A utility bill, lease agreement, or mortgage statement in the parent or guardian's name is typically required. The district verifies residency and may conduct home visits if questions arise about address legitimacy.

Boundary & Address Verification

Fort Worth ISD serves the core of the City of Fort Worth, but the city's boundaries extend into territory served by other districts. Families buying in the Fort Worth area must verify their specific street address, not just ZIP code or city name, before assuming Fort Worth ISD assignment.

Which Districts Also Serve Fort Worth Addresses

The northwest quadrant of the City of Fort Worth, including areas near Lake Worth and Saginaw, falls within Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD and parts of Keller ISD. The south Fort Worth area near Crowley Road and south of Loop 820 falls in Crowley ISD for most addresses. The far west side of the city near NAS Fort Worth is largely White Settlement ISD territory. Benbrook area addresses may fall in Fort Worth ISD or Benbrook-area districts depending on exact location. None of these distinctions follow ZIP code lines.

High School Attendance Zones Within FWISD

Even within confirmed Fort Worth ISD boundaries, your address determines which of the 13 high schools your child is zoned to attend. The zone boundaries inside the district are not intuitive. A block-level difference can send one student to Paschal and a neighbor to a different campus with a very different academic profile. Use the district's school locator at enrollment.fwisd.org to verify both district assignment and campus assignment for your specific address.

New Construction Communities

Active master-planned development in southwest and west Fort Worth sits near the Crowley ISD and EMS ISD boundary lines. New construction communities marketed as "Fort Worth" may feed to a different ISD than buyers expect. Confirm district assignment with both the builder and the district's enrollment office before signing a contract on new construction.

How to Verify Your Address

Use Fort Worth ISD's online enrollment portal at enrollment.fwisd.org or the Texas Education Agency school locator at tea.texas.gov/apps/school-directory. Making an offer without verifying your specific address can mean your child attends a school you did not plan for, and boundary maps do not always match common-sense geography in a city as large and irregularly shaped as Fort Worth.

Tax Rate & Finances

Fort Worth ISD's 2024 certified total tax rate is $1.0624 per $100 of assessed value, the highest rate the district can levy without voter approval. For context, this is actually lower than most of the suburban Tarrant County districts families typically cross-shop: Keller ISD runs $1.09, Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD runs $1.25, and Arlington ISD runs $1.17. Fort Worth ISD's rate reflecting the district's access to more commercial and mixed-use property within the city limits helps offset the residential rate compared to bedroom-community suburbs.

What the Rate Means for a Monthly Payment

Texas homeowners receive a $100,000 homestead exemption on school district taxes. On a $400,000 home with a $100,000 exemption, the taxable value is $300,000. At $1.06 per $100, annual Fort Worth ISD school taxes on that home run approximately $3,187, or roughly $266 per month added to a mortgage payment. On a $500,000 home (taxable at $400,000), the figure rises to about $4,250 annually, or $354 per month.

Comparison to Neighboring Districts

Fort Worth ISD's $1.06 rate compares favorably on paper to Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ($1.25), Arlington ($1.17), and Keller ($1.09). Families sometimes assume urban districts carry higher tax rates than suburban ones; for FWISD, the reverse is often true. That said, tax rate alone is not a reason to choose a district. School quality, program fit, and campus assignment remain more consequential factors than a fraction of a percentage point difference in tax rate.

Budget & Per-Pupil Expenditure

Fort Worth ISD's 2024-25 adopted budget projected approximately $844 million in expenditures against an estimated $826 million in revenue from local, state, and federal sources. The per-pupil expenditure is publicly available through TEA's PEIMS Financial Summary at txschools.gov. As a large urban district, FWISD receives proportionally more state equalization funding per student than many wealthier suburban districts, reflecting the state funding formula's design.

Families relocating to the Fort Worth area most commonly cross-shop Fort Worth ISD against Keller ISD, Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD, and Arlington ISD. Each serves a different geography and family profile.

Families who want a consistent, above-average academic experience without navigating campus selection or magnet applications will generally prefer Keller ISD or Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD over Fort Worth ISD. Those already living in Fort Worth who don't want to relocate to a suburb, or who have a student whose specific program interest aligns with an FWISD magnet, will find the district's program diversity genuinely useful. Arlington ISD serves a similar urban scale to FWISD but with a different geography serving the Mid-Cities and east Tarrant County. None of these districts is uniformly "better," they serve different household priorities and geographic situations.

Community Feel

Fort Worth ISD covers an enormous geographic range, so the community feel varies considerably by neighborhood and campus. The Near Southside, Cultural District, and Westside neighborhoods fed by Paschal and Carter-Riverside have an active, engaged parent community with strong booster programs, particularly around athletics and the arts. Families in these neighborhoods often describe a big-city school culture with genuine neighborhood school attachment, where PTA involvement and campus fundraising are meaningful at individual campuses even within a large district.

The magnet campuses, particularly PASCAL at Wyatt, have tight-knit program communities that transcend neighborhood boundaries. Students and families who are there specifically for the arts program develop strong bonds within that cohort regardless of where they live in the city. This creates a dynamic where the "community" of a Fort Worth ISD family can be program-based rather than geography-based, which is different from the way suburban districts work.

The district's size means youth sports and extracurriculars operate on a large scale. Football rivalries between Fort Worth ISD high schools have long histories and strong fan cultures. The performing arts community around PASCAL and Paschal's IB program connects Fort Worth ISD students to citywide cultural institutions in ways that smaller suburban districts don't replicate. Parents who have relocated from large urban districts like Houston ISD, Dallas ISD, or Chicago Public Schools tend to adapt to Fort Worth ISD's dynamic more easily than those coming from small suburban districts where all campuses operate at a similar level.

The practical reality from families on Reddit and Fort Worth-area parenting forums: campus selection matters enormously within this district, and families who research before buying are consistently happier with their experience than those who assumed district averages would represent their child's campus. That's consistent advice across virtually every large urban Texas ISD, and Fort Worth is no exception.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Fort Worth mailing address mean I'm in Fort Worth ISD? ▾
Not necessarily. The City of Fort Worth spans multiple school district boundaries. Parts of the city's northwest quadrant fall in Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD or Keller ISD. Areas near Crowley Road and south Loop 820 are typically Crowley ISD. The far west side near NAS Fort Worth is largely White Settlement ISD. A Fort Worth address or ZIP code alone is not confirmation of Fort Worth ISD assignment. Always verify your specific street address at enrollment.fwisd.org before making a purchase decision.
How different are Fort Worth ISD's 13 high schools from each other? ▾
Significantly. Fort Worth ISD's high school ratings in 2024-25 ranged from A to F at different campuses. Paschal High School leads in AP and IB, South Hills performs above district average, while other campuses score lower. Each high school has a Gold Seal specialty program pathway, but the overall campus environment, academic performance, and extracurricular culture vary widely. Identifying which high school your address feeds to, and researching that specific campus, is more useful than evaluating the district as a whole. Campus TEA scores are available at txschools.gov.
How do the magnet and Gold Seal programs work for students outside the campus zone? ▾
Gold Seal Programs are open to all Fort Worth ISD students by application. Oversubscribed programs use a lottery system. Application windows for major programs like PASCAL (Wyatt) and the IB programme (Paschal) typically open in January or February for the following school year. Transportation to a magnet program outside your attendance zone is the parent's responsibility unless the district specifies otherwise. Acceptance is not guaranteed. Families who are specifically relocating to access a magnet program should not assume acceptance before confirming with the district's School Choice office at (817) 814-1920.
What does the TEA C rating actually mean for day-to-day school experience? ▾
The district-level TEA C (73) is an average across more than 140 campuses serving very different student populations and neighborhoods. It means the average FWISD campus performs in the C range, not that every campus does. Seventeen of the district's 125 rated campuses earned A in 2024-25. The C rating reflects the academic challenge of serving a large urban population where approximately 85% of students are economically disadvantaged. For a family whose student will attend a specific A or B-rated campus, or who is enrolled in a program like IB at Paschal, the district average has limited bearing on that child's daily school experience.
How does Fort Worth ISD's tax rate compare to nearby suburban districts? ▾
Fort Worth ISD's 2024 certified rate of $1.06 per $100 of assessed value is lower than most Tarrant County suburban alternatives: Keller ISD runs $1.09, Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD $1.25, and Arlington ISD $1.17. On a $400,000 home (with Texas's $100,000 homestead exemption, so taxed at $300,000), FWISD school taxes run approximately $3,187 per year. EMS ISD on the same home would run about $3,750 per year. The rate difference amounts to roughly $47 per month on that example home. Rate alone is not a reason to choose a district, but Fort Worth ISD does not carry the premium tax rate that some families expect from a large urban district.
What happens to my child's IEP or 504 plan when we move to Fort Worth ISD? ▾
Texas law requires Fort Worth ISD to convene an ARD (Admissions, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of your child's enrollment to review, adopt, or revise the existing IEP or 504 plan. Bring all prior evaluation reports, the current IEP or 504 document, and any supporting assessment materials. Contact FWISD's Special Services department before your child's first day to discuss what services are available at your assigned campus. Parent experience with special education services varies by campus and student profile. For fit-sensitive placements, contacting the district directly before finalizing your address decision is time well spent.
Is Fort Worth ISD at risk of a state takeover? ▾
As of 2024-25, Fort Worth ISD has been on TEA's watch list given prior low ratings, but the district's three-year improvement trajectory (D in 2022-23 to C in 2023-24 to C/73 in 2024-25) and the significant reduction in F-rated campuses have reduced immediate takeover concern. TEA intervention typically triggers after a district receives multiple consecutive low ratings. Families should monitor annual TEA releases for the most current status. The district's accountability page at fwisd.org publishes current ratings and improvement plans.
