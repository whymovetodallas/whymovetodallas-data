Hurst packs more than 20 parks, two aquatic centers, and four dedicated youth sports organizations into 22 square miles, all anchored by the HEB community spirit that makes this mid-cities gem feel like a small town inside the metroplex.

What Hurst Families Actually Do Here

Hurst is a compact mid-cities community where park density is genuinely impressive for its size, youth sports organizations serve families from across the HEB area, and community events draw neighbors out year-round. Families relocating here are often surprised by how much Hurst delivers without the sticker price of neighboring Southlake or Colleyville. See the full Hurst relocation guide →

20+ Parks in 22 Sq Mi

Hurst maintains one of the highest park-to-land ratios in Tarrant County, from 50-acre Chisholm Park to quiet neighborhood wildlife sanctuaries. Two seasonal aquatic centers add to the outdoor options every summer.

H-E-B Youth Sports Hub

Four dedicated youth sports associations serve Hurst, Euless, and Bedford families together, covering soccer, baseball and softball, basketball, and football. The 42-acre Hurst Athletic Complex handles most competitive play.

Year-Round Community Events

Stars and Stripes fireworks on July 3rd, the free Masterworks Concert Series at Heritage Village Plaza, and Hurst Fest in the fall keep the community calendar active from spring through the holidays.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Hurst maintains more than 20 parks totaling several hundred acres across the city. Chisholm Park (50 acres), Hurst Community Park (44 acres), the Hurst Athletic Complex (42 acres), and Rickel Park (29 acres with 1.5 miles of trails) anchor the major greenspace corridors, while a dozen smaller neighborhood parks put green space within walking distance of most homes.

Chisholm Park

The largest park in Hurst at 50 acres, located at 2200 Norwood Drive. Home to the Chisholm Aquatics Center (lazy river, water slides, children's pool, toddler area), fishing opportunities, and multiple youth sports fields. Picnic shelters provide solid shade; restrooms and concessions on-site. Good for a full family day without leaving the park.

Aquatics Fishing Youth Athletics Picnic Shelters Restrooms

Hurst Community Park

A 44-acre community anchor at 601 Precinct Line Road and the site of the city's annual Stars and Stripes fireworks celebration on July 3rd. Wide-open fields for athletic practices and informal play, with paths throughout. Stroller-friendly terrain; good parking for community events.

Events Venue Open Fields Stroller-Friendly Playground

Rickel Park

A 29-acre passive park at 1001 Bluebonnet with 1.5 miles of walking and jogging trails winding past a stream. Two pavilions, 8 picnic areas, and generous tree cover make it the most serene spot in the city. Calm and shaded; particularly good for families with toddlers who want trails without crowds.

Walking Trails Stream Pavilions Picnic Areas Shaded

Hurst Athletic Complex

A 42-acre complex at 2104 Precinct Line Road, fully renovated in 2003, and home to the city's adult softball leagues as well as multiple youth sports associations. Stadium-quality softball fields with lighting for evening games; concessions on-site. Busy on weekends during spring and fall seasons.

Softball Fields Youth Sports Concessions Night Lighting

Central Park

A 17.5-acre hub park in South Hurst at the 700 block of Mary Drive, home to the Hurst Recreation Center, Central Aquatics Center, and the Hurst Tennis and Pickleball Center. Everything families need for active programming is in one location. Established in 1955 and still the recreational heart of the city.

Rec Center Aquatics Tennis/Pickleball

Redbud Park

A 7.2-acre neighborhood park at 525 Redbud Drive with a playground, soccer field, and a 0.33-mile jogging trail. Good for elementary-age kids; best visited on weekday mornings before summer heat builds. Fitness stations along the trail make it popular with parents who want to exercise while kids play.

Playground Soccer Field Jogging Trail Fitness Course

Mayfair Linear Park

An 8.3-acre natural corridor at 1725 Norwood running along Valley View Creek between L.D. Bell High School and Harwood Road. Wide-open natural space along both sides of the creek makes it popular for informal nature walks and creek exploration with kids. A quiet alternative to the athletic parks.

Creek Corridor Nature Walks Wildlife

Wan-Ka-Kani Park

A 4.1-acre wildlife sanctuary at 748 Shadylane in Southwest Hurst, established in 1976. Named from a word meaning "to be outdoors," it serves as a bird and wildlife refuge. Peaceful and low-traffic; a good option for families with nature-curious young children who want something other than a playground.

Wildlife Sanctuary Bird Watching Passive Park

Trail System Highlight

Rickel Park Trail System, 1.5 miles winding through 29 acres with a stream and natural canopy. Great for morning walks with a stroller and weekend jogging before the heat sets in. The most complete trail experience inside Hurst city limits.

Relocation Tip

Start your Hurst park exploration at Rickel Park on Bluebonnet Drive. It has the best combination of trail mileage, shade, and a natural stream, and it tends to be quieter than the main athletic parks. Once you have visited, try Chisholm Park on a warm evening to watch youth sports under the lights and get a feel for the community's athletic culture.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Hurst sits at the center of the HEB area, where four independent youth sports associations serve families from Hurst, Euless, and Bedford together. The 42-acre Hurst Athletic Complex at 2104 Precinct Line Road handles most competitive play, while the Hurst Tennis and Pickleball Center adds individual-sport programming for kids and adults at Central Park.

Featured Sports Complex

Hurst Athletic Complex, 42 acres at 2104 Precinct Line Road, fully renovated and reopened in 2003. Home to the city's adult softball leagues (men's, women's, co-ed, and church divisions) and multiple youth sports associations. Stadium-quality lighted fields handle evening games through the fall season.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Hurst sits just off Loop 820 and HWY 121/183, which puts a solid range of family entertainment within a short drive while keeping North East Mall and its Cinemark 18 right in town. Most options are within 10-15 minutes of any address in the city.

Rebounderz Hurst

Trampoline Park · Arena-style trampolines, a large foam pit, basketball slam dunkers, a dedicated toddler jumping zone, video arcade, and cafe. Handles birthday parties well and works for a wide age range in a single visit.

Putt-Putt Fun Center

Family Entertainment Center · A 25,000-square-foot complex with two miniature golf courses, go-karts, a laser maze, mini bowling, batting cages, bumper boats, and a restaurant. Good for ages 5 and up; plan on 3-4 hours.

Cinemark 18 at North East Mall

Movie Theater · 18 screens including an XD auditorium with digital projection and RealD 3D, inside North East Mall at HWY 121/183 and Loop 820. An easy weeknight option that pairs naturally with dinner on the mall corridor.

WhirlyBall & LaserWhirld Mid-Cities

Laser Tag / Sports Entertainment · Laser tag plus whirlyball, a mix of lacrosse and bumper-car basketball requiring at least six players. Best for birthday parties and youth team outings; older kids 7+ get the most out of it.

Chisholm Aquatics Center

Waterpark / Aquatic Facility · Lazy river, multiple water slides, a children's activity pool, and a toddler area at 2200 Norwood Drive. Open seasonally from late May through early September; check swimhurst.com for the current year's schedule and daily admission rates.

Heritage Village Plaza

Outdoor Performance Venue · A 1.2-acre plaza at 837 W. Pipeline Road adjacent to Hurst Community Park, featuring a concert stage and the Souder Pavilion. Home to the free Masterworks Concert Series, with performances ranging from orchestra and ballet to country music and family theater throughout the year.

Day Trip from Hurst

SEA LIFE Grapevine Aquarium and Legoland Discovery Center (about 15 minutes northeast), both at Grapevine Mills, make a natural full-day outing for families with kids ages 3-10. The proximity to Loop 820 makes getting there quick from anywhere in Hurst.

Family-Friendly Dining

Hurst has a diverse dining scene spread across the Precinct Line Road corridor and the North East Mall area at HWY 121/183. Families generally have no trouble finding something quick on a Tuesday or something more special on a Saturday without leaving the city.

Abuelo's Mexican Restaurant
Best festive family dinner
Upscale Mexican with handmade tortillas, a deep salsa bar, and generous portions. Known for queso, fajitas, and a warm atmosphere that handles big family tables without a fuss.
Bacon's Bistro and Cafe
Best weekend brunch
A neighborhood favorite with high marks for French toast and eggs Benedict. Great Saturday morning stop before a morning at Chisholm Park or a youth soccer game.
Italianni's
Best sit-down Italian
A family-operated Italian restaurant on Precinct Line Road open since 2002. Known for Italian wedding soup, Salmon Oscar, and a dining room that works well for groups.
BJ's Restaurant & Brewhouse
Best after-mall dinner
American favorites, handcrafted pizzas, and a solid kids menu inside the North East Mall dining corridor. Big enough to handle a group; consistent quality.
Catfish and Company
Best Southern comfort food
Fried catfish, hush puppies, and classic Texas sides from a no-frills kitchen. A local staple for casual family dinners that won't stretch the budget.
Red Robin Gourmet Burgers
Best for picky eaters
Bottomless steak fries, a wide burger menu, and staff who genuinely welcome families. The reliable fallback when someone in the group only wants a burger.
Rosa's Cafe & Tortilla Factory
Best quick Tex-Mex
Fast-casual Tex-Mex with freshly made tortillas, efficient ordering, and affordable prices. A reliable weeknight choice after youth sports practice.
Carrabba's Italian Grill
Best special occasion Italian
Sit-down Italian with consistent quality and a lively room inside the North East Mall dining corridor. Good for family dinners that feel slightly more intentional.
Soulman's Bar-b-que
Best BBQ with kids
Pulled pork, brisket, and ribs from a Texas chain with a loyal Hurst following. Kids gravitate to the Texas toast and the no-fuss ordering line.
Trailer Birds
Best quick lunch after sports
Nashville hot chicken sandwiches and loaded tater tots at 1858 Precinct Line. Fast, affordable, and popular with youth sports families grabbing lunch between Saturday games.

Most of these are concentrated near Precinct Line Road between Pipeline Road and Airport Freeway, and the North East Mall dining corridor at HWY 121/183 and Loop 820.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Hurst offers both a major regional mall and a practical everyday retail corridor. Most household needs are handled within city limits without a drive to a neighboring suburb.

North East Mall

Regional Mall

A Simon Property with 140+ stores at HWY 121/183 and Loop 820, anchored by Dillard's, Macy's, and JCPenney. The dining corridor includes BJ's Restaurant, Carrabba's, and multiple quick-serve options, with Cinemark 18 for a movie after dinner.

Best for: a full Saturday outing where you need department stores, a movie, and dinner in one stop.

Precinct Line Road Corridor

Everyday Retail

The primary north-south retail spine running through Hurst, with grocery anchors, national chains, local restaurants, and service businesses. Everything from weekly groceries to quick-errand stops is along this corridor.

Best for: routine weekly errands and grabbing dinner on the way home from school pickup.

Airport Freeway / HWY 183 Corridor

Mixed Retail

The east-west commercial artery connecting Hurst to Bedford, Euless, and Irving, with big-box retailers, home goods stores, and automotive services. Good coverage for large-format shopping without leaving the immediate HEB area.

Best for: home improvement runs and big-box errands that need a larger footprint.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Hurst runs a full community events calendar anchored by an outdoor fireworks show, a year-round performing arts series, and a fall community festival. Most signature events are free and held in city parks.

July (July 3)

Stars and Stripes

The city's signature summer celebration at Hurst Community Park, held annually. Live entertainment (check the city events page for this year's lineup), food trucks, a Kidzone with games, and a fireworks show close out the evening. Free shuttles typically run from North East Mall. One of the most attended community events in Tarrant County mid-cities.

Year-Round

Masterworks Concert Series

A free performing arts series at Heritage Village Plaza and the Souder Pavilion, funded by the city's Hotel/Motel Tax Fund in partnership with Arts Council Northeast. Performances span orchestra, ballet, country music, marionettes, and family theater. A low-key way to introduce kids to live performance without a ticket price. Check the city calendar for dates.

Typically September/October

Hurst Fest

A free community festival held annually in the fall at Hurst Community Park. Family games and activities, food trucks, local vendors, and live entertainment make it a popular first fall outing for families new to the area. Check the city events calendar for the confirmed date each year.

Late May / Early June

Summer Reading Challenge Opening Party

The Hurst Public Library kicks off its Summer Reading Challenge with an opening party featuring live performances and family activities. The Summer Reading Challenge itself runs through the summer for early childhood, kids, teens, and adults. Free to participate; prizes available at each level.

July

Park and Recreation Month

Hurst Parks and Recreation celebrates Park and Recreation Month throughout July with special programming, free activities, and community events across the city's parks. A good time for new families to explore park amenities through guided programming before the school year starts.

Year-Round

Hurst Public Library Events

The Hurst Public Library at 901 Precinct Line Road maintains a full programming calendar with weekly storytimes, craft events, teen programs, brown bag book clubs, and seasonal themed activities. Free to all; check the library calendar at hursttx.gov for the current schedule.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When July and August temperatures climb past 100 degrees, Hurst has solid air-conditioned options within a few miles of home. The city's indoor recreation programming runs year-round, not just when it's hot.

Rebounderz Hurst

Type: Trampoline Park · Ages: All ages; toddler zone for under-5
Arena trampolines, foam pit, basketball slam dunkers, toddler zone, arcade, and cafe. Popular for birthday parties and rain-day visits. Good for a mixed-age group in a single outing.

Putt-Putt Fun Center

Type: Family Entertainment Center · Ages: Ages 5 and up
Two miniature golf courses, go-karts, laser maze, batting cages, bumper boats, mini bowling, and a restaurant in a 25,000-square-foot facility. Plan on 3-4 hours when outdoor play is not an option.

WhirlyBall & LaserWhirld Mid-Cities

Type: Laser Tag / Whirlyball · Ages: Ages 7 and up
Laser tag and whirlyball (bumper cars meet lacrosse). Ideal for birthday parties and youth team outings; minimum player count applies for whirlyball. Good for a group of older kids who have outgrown trampoline parks.

Cinemark 18 at North East Mall

Type: Movie Theater · Ages: All ages
18 screens with XD format available; first-run releases and stadium seating. Combines naturally with the North East Mall dining corridor for an easy family evening. Matinee pricing keeps weekday visits affordable.

Hurst Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: All ages; dedicated children's and teen areas
Weekly storytimes for toddlers and preschoolers, Summer Reading Challenge (June-July), craft events, teen programs, and adult book clubs. At 901 Precinct Line Road inside Library Park. Free to all with a library card.

Hurst Recreation Center

Type: Recreation Center · Ages: All ages
Fitness center, indoor gymnasium (home to MCBBA basketball leagues), recreation classes for all ages, personal training, and summer camps. At 700 Mary Drive in Central Park. Resident memberships available at reduced rates.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When the heat peaks in mid-July and August, Hurst families rotate between three reliable options: Chisholm Aquatics Center's lazy river (open through early September per the current season schedule at swimhurst.com), indoor recreation classes at Hurst Recreation Center, and Cinemark 18 matinees at North East Mall. All three are within 10 minutes of any address in Hurst, and the Recreation Center's summer camps give kids a structured program if both parents are working.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Hurst.

  • Hurst works well for families who want an established, well-served mid-cities community without the price premium of Colleyville or Southlake. The city maintains more than 20 parks, two seasonal aquatic centers, and four youth sports organizations serving the full HEB area. Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD provides solid academics across a large campus network. Families typically describe Hurst as a place where neighbors know each other and the infrastructure for active family life is already in place from the time kids are in diapers.
  • Hurst is served by four dedicated youth sports associations: Hurst United Soccer Association (recreational U4 and older, academy U5-U10, competitive U11-U19), Tri-Cities Baseball and Softball (ages 3-14, boys and girls), Mid-Cities Basketball Association (grades 1-8), and Mid-Cities Peewee Football and Cheerleading for the HEB area. N Zone Sports North Fort Worth offers additional options for ages 3-14 in soccer, flag football, basketball, and t-ball. The Hurst Tennis and Pickleball Center at Central Park runs USTA youth leagues year-round, and the city also maintains adult softball and basketball leagues at the Hurst Athletic Complex and Recreation Center.
  • Yes, Hurst has several solid indoor options. Rebounderz covers trampoline play and arcade games, Putt-Putt Fun Center combines go-karts, a laser maze, batting cages, and mini bowling under one roof, and the Cinemark 18 at North East Mall handles films for all ages. WhirlyBall and LaserWhirld Mid-Cities works well for older kids (7 and up) with a group large enough for whirlyball. Hurst Public Library runs programming year-round, and the Hurst Recreation Center has a full slate of drop-in classes and open gym time when the weather keeps families indoors.
  • Hurst operates two city-run aquatic centers rather than traditional park splash pads. Chisholm Aquatics Center at 2200 Norwood Drive features a lazy river, multiple water slides, a children's activity pool, and a toddler area. Central Aquatics Center at 715 Mary Drive has a splash pad, a leisure pool with zero-depth entry, and swim lessons for ages 3 and up. Both facilities open seasonally in late May. Visit swimhurst.com for the current year's opening dates, hours, and daily admission rates, as the schedule is updated annually.
  • Hurst and Bedford share Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD and the same network of youth sports associations, so families in either city draw from the same leagues and programs. The Hurst Athletic Complex serves the full HEB area for competitive play. Hurst has Chisholm Aquatics Center with a lazy river and full waterpark amenities as its primary seasonal pool. North East Mall sits just inside the Hurst city line and functions as a community hub for both cities. Families choosing between the two usually make their decision based on a specific neighborhood, school campus assignment, or home price rather than meaningful differences in family amenities, since both cities are well-served.
  • Hurst is known as a compact, highly livable mid-cities community with exceptional park density and a strong sense of neighborhood identity. The Stars and Stripes fireworks celebration draws thousands each July 3rd, the Masterworks Concert Series keeps community life active year-round at Heritage Village Plaza, and the Hurst Athletic Complex is one of the more active recreational venues in Tarrant County. Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD creates a shared community that extends across three cities. Families who move to Hurst for its value relative to neighboring suburbs often discover a level of community infrastructure and engagement that keeps them longer than they originally planned.
