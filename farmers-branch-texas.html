Getting Your Bearings

Where Is Farmers Branch, Texas?

Farmers Branch sits 15 miles northwest of downtown Dallas, wedged inside the Mixmaster interchange where I-35E and I-635 (LBJ Freeway) converge. That central position is its biggest asset: you're 15 minutes from both DFW International Airport and Dallas Love Field, 20 minutes from Las Colinas and the Irving employment corridor, and roughly 25 minutes from downtown Dallas on a good traffic day. The city is entirely within Dallas County, and its corporate and industrial base along the I-35E corridor gives it a broader tax base than most bedroom suburbs — one reason it can sustain 30+ award-winning parks on a population of ~40,000.

Major access routes include I-35E (north-south spine toward downtown and Denton), I-635 LBJ Freeway (east-west across the northern arc of the metro), and the Dallas North Tollway (available via a short hop to SH 183). DART's Green Line also runs through Farmers Branch, connecting to downtown Dallas, Deep Ellum, and the Galleria area — making it one of the few DFW suburbs where you can genuinely commute into the city without driving. SH 183/Airport Freeway provides a non-toll alternative west toward DFW Airport.

Education

Carrollton-Farmers Branch ISD — Schools Deep Dive

Homes in Farmers Branch are zoned to Carrollton-Farmers Branch ISD (CFBISD), one of the larger ISDs in Dallas County with roughly 26,000 students across ~50 campuses. The district earned a TEA B (82/100) rating in 2025 — its highest score in several years — and a Niche overall grade of B+. Notably, all five CFBISD high schools received an A or B rating in 2025 for the first time, a significant milestone. The district's student body is highly diverse (Hispanic, White, Asian, and Black students all represented), which mirrors Farmers Branch's community demographics. For families relocating from California or the Midwest, CFBISD compares favorably to most mid-tier suburban ISDs they're leaving behind.

Family Life

What Life Actually Looks Like in Farmers Branch

Active Sports Scene

Farmers Branch Recreation offers youth leagues for soccer, baseball, softball, basketball, and tennis through city facilities. CFBISD athletics are competitive at all five high schools. Travel leagues and club programs have easy access to the broader DFW sports infrastructure within 20 minutes.

30+ Award-Winning Parks

The "City in a Park" nickname is earned. Farmers Branch maintains over 30 parks across its 12 square miles — a per-capita ratio that outpaces cities three times its size. The 104-acre John F. Burke Nature Preserve anchors the park system with wetlands, woodlands, and multi-use trails.

Dining & Shopping Access

Mercer Crossing Towne Center brings retail and dining close to home. The Galleria Dallas is 10 minutes away, and Addison's restaurant corridor — one of the most concentrated dining strips in DFW — is 15 minutes east. You won't have the hyper-local restaurant scene of Southlake or Frisco, but proximity to everything else makes up for it.

Annual Events & Community Life

Farmers Branch hosts the annual Arbor Days & Earth Fair, Fourth of July celebrations at Farmers Branch Historical Park, and seasonal Farmers Market events. The Historical Park itself — a working 19th-century farm — is a genuine community gathering point and an unusual amenity for a city this size.

The Rose Garden & Historical Park

The Farmers Branch Rose Gardens feature nearly 400 rose varieties across four distinct themed gardens — the largest public rose display in North Texas. Combined with the adjacent Historical Park (authentic 1800s farmstead), these are signature landmarks that draw families from across the metro on weekends.

Diverse, Welcoming Community

Farmers Branch is one of the more ethnically diverse cities in the northwest Dallas corridor — Hispanic, White, and Asian populations all significant. Nextdoor residents consistently rate the city as clean, dog-friendly, family-friendly, and peaceful. Families relocating from coastal metros often note they feel at home faster here than in more homogeneous suburbs further north.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Farmers Branch maintains one of the highest park-per-capita ratios in Dallas County — 30+ parks across a city of just 40,000 residents. The system ranges from neighborhood pocket parks to the 104-acre Burke Nature Preserve. Combined with the Elm Fork of the Trinity River trail access and the city's recreation center, outdoor families find more here than the city's modest population would suggest.

John F. Burke Nature Preserve

104 acres of wetlands, upland forest, and walking trails — the city's crown jewel. Rare for a suburb this close to downtown Dallas. Families use it for weekend hikes, nature education, and bird watching. Access is off Valley View Lane.

Farmers Branch Rose Gardens

Nearly 400 varieties of roses across four distinct gardens. One of the most extensive public rose displays in North Texas. Peak bloom is April–May and October–November. A legitimate weekend destination for families with young children.

Rawhide Park & Sports Complex

Multi-field sports park with softball and soccer facilities, playground equipment, and ample parking. Home to many of the city's youth sports leagues. Located off Valley View Lane near the Burke Preserve.

Farmers Branch Historical Park

A working 1800s farmstead with authentic historical structures, interpretive programs, and community events. One of the most unique public parks in the Dallas metro — free to visit and actively programmed for school groups and families year-round.

Mercer Park & Mercer Crossing Trail

Newer green space near the Mercer Crossing development, with trail connectivity, open lawn areas, and proximity to the retail corridor. Popular with the newer residential community and dog owners.

Brookhaven Trail Corridor

Multi-use trail connecting Farmers Branch parks to the broader Dallas trail network. Links to the Elm Fork Greenbelt system and connects cyclists and runners between city parks. A practical amenity for active families seeking car-free recreation routes.

New Construction

New Homes in Farmers Branch

Farmers Branch is a fully established suburb — the kind of place where the neighborhoods are set, the trees are mature, and the community feel is already there when you move in. There's little vacant land left for large-scale new residential development, so most families buying here are focused on the resale market. That's not a disadvantage: Farmers Branch has strong inventory across a range of price points, lot sizes, and neighborhood styles, and resale homes often come with finished landscaping, custom touches, and HOA communities that are already thriving. If you're weighing Farmers Branch against a suburb with active new construction, the honest trade-off is this — you're giving up the ability to choose your finishes and get a builder warranty, and gaining a suburb where the character and school patterns are well-established and predictable. For many relocating families, that's exactly what they're looking for. Pros & Cons of Living in Farmers Branch

I won't sell you on Farmers Branch. I'll give you the real picture so you can decide if it's right for your family.

✓ What Farmers Branch Does Well

  • Unbeatable Northwest Dallas Location — 15 minutes to DFW Airport, 15 minutes to Love Field, 25 minutes to downtown Dallas. Nothing in DFW at this price point has better access to all three.
  • DART Green Line Access — Two light rail stations connect you to downtown Dallas, the Galleria area, and beyond. One of the very few DFW suburbs where you have a genuine car-free commute option.
  • 30+ Award-Winning Parks — The "City in a Park" reputation is real. Burke Nature Preserve, Rose Gardens, Historical Park, and dozens of neighborhood parks give this city a per-capita park ratio that rivals suburbs three times its size.
  • CFBISD B+ Schools — A legitimate B+ Niche grade and TEA B (82) rating, with all five high schools earning A or B ratings in 2025. Not Southlake, but solid — and a fraction of Southlake's home prices.
  • Price Point Below Comparable Suburbs — At a median of ~$429K, Farmers Branch runs $50–$100K below what you'd pay in Addison, North Dallas, or Coppell for similar square footage and location quality.
  • No state income tax — Meaningful savings, especially for high earners from CA, NY, or IL.
  • Diverse, Inclusive Community — Hispanic, White, and Asian residents all well-represented. Families from coastal metros often feel welcome more quickly here than in more homogeneous suburbs further north.

— What to Weigh Carefully

  • Feeder Pattern Complexity — Farmers Branch addresses feed into multiple CFBISD high schools, and the zoning boundaries are not intuitive. Always verify your specific address before committing to a neighborhood.
  • Mixed Commercial Corridors — The I-35E and I-635 corridors through the city are dense with logistics, warehousing, and commercial uses. Neighborhoods near those corridors deal with truck traffic and industrial noise.
  • Modest Local Dining Scene — The city's own restaurant options are limited for its size. You'll drive to Addison or Carrollton for a strong dining experience most nights.
  • Owner Occupancy Rate — At ~55% owner-occupied, Farmers Branch has a higher rental share than most family-oriented DFW suburbs, which affects neighborhood stability in some areas.
  • Car-dependent — Outside of the DART station corridors, a personal vehicle is essential. Despite light rail access, most of the city requires driving for daily errands.

Renting for 3–6 months before buying is always smart for out-of-state relocating families, and Farmers Branch is no exception. The city has a relatively active rental market (about 45% rentals citywide), so finding a short-term rental in your target neighborhood is feasible. Renting first lets you experience the DART commute, get to know the neighborhood feel, and verify ISD attendance zones before locking in a purchase. In a market where days on market average ~47 days, you won't be penalized for taking a bit more time to decide.

Farmers Branch earns a B safety grade from Niche and is generally safe for a city of its size and location adjacent to a major metro. Violent crime runs approximately 2.8 per 1,000 residents — below the national average of ~4.0. Property crime is approximately 18 per 1,000 — below the Texas average of 22.4. Commercial corridors near I-35E and I-635 see higher property crime activity, but established residential neighborhoods are quiet and well-maintained. The Farmers Branch Police Department is well-staffed relative to the population it serves.

Yes — Farmers Branch has two DART Green Line stations (Farmers Branch Station and Bachman Station), which is exceptional for a DFW suburb of this size and price point. The Green Line connects to downtown Dallas (West End) in about 30 minutes, with service to Deep Ellum, Baylor Medical Center area, and the Galleria corridor. For commuters whose office is on or near the Green Line, this is a genuine daily alternative to driving. That said, most daily errands still require a car — the transit system serves corridors, not neighborhoods.

In Farmers Branch itself, Mary Kay Cosmetics is the largest anchor employer (corporate HQ, ~3,500 employees), with Cheddar's Restaurant chain HQ also based here. The I-35E corridor hosts significant logistics and warehousing operations. Within 20 minutes, the Las Colinas/Irving employment corridor hosts major employers including Celanese, Michaels Companies, Kimberly-Clark, Fluor, McKesson, Nokia, and Ericsson. The Addison corridor (15 min east) adds financial services and tech firms. DFW Airport is 15 minutes away — relevant for airport-adjacent industries and frequent business travelers.

Traffic is typical for a northwest Dallas suburb — manageable off-peak, congested during rush hours. The I-35E and I-635 interchange (the "Mixmaster") is one of the more complex interchanges in North Texas and can add 10–20 minutes during peak commute periods. The practical reality: plan commutes for 7–9am and 4:30–6:30pm to take 30–45 minutes to downtown Dallas or Las Colinas. Outside those windows, the drive times in this guide (15–25 minutes to most destinations) are accurate. The DART Green Line is a real pressure-release valve for downtown-bound commuters who want to skip I-35E entirely.
