Things to Do in Richardson, TX with Kids

Richardson pairs one of DFW's strongest school districts with a nature preserve, two celebrated art festivals, and a walkable arts district, the kind of city where families who care about both academics and quality of life end up staying much longer than they planned.

What Richardson Families Actually Do Here

Richardson is the city that serious RISD families choose and then quietly become evangelical about. The school district is one of the metro's strongest. The parks include a 100-acre nature preserve. The Cottonwood Art Festival draws 200,000 visitors twice a year. And the CityLine development brought a walkable mixed-use district that gives the city a modern center it didn't have a decade ago. See the full Richardson relocation guide.

Cottonwood Art Festival

Held annually since 1969 at Cottonwood Park, the Cottonwood Art Festival runs twice yearly (May and October) with 190-plus artist booths, live entertainment, ArtStop kids' activities, and a craft beer garden. One of the most visited outdoor art festivals in Texas.

Spring Creek Nature Preserve

A 100-plus-acre hardwood forest preserve with a 4.5-mile loop trail along Spring Creek. The canopy coverage and creek-side habitat make it one of the best birding and trail-running spots in the inner Dallas suburbs. Located just south of CityLine.

Wildflower! Music Festival

An award-winning three-day arts and music festival held every May at Galatyn Park Urban Center. Six stages of live entertainment, interactive art, family programming, and a signature competition format that draws national acts alongside Texas regional performers.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Richardson maintains more than 40 parks across the city. Two city parks have confirmed splash playgrounds (Glenville Park and Heights Park, both from the city's official parks page). The trail network connects through parks, creek corridors, and the CityLine development, with the Spring Creek Nature Area anchoring the longest continuous trail experience in the city.

Spring Creek Nature Area

More than 100 acres of hardwood forest in the heart of the Dallas suburbs. The 4.5-mile loop trail winds through a densely wooded corridor along Spring Creek with enough canopy to stay cool on moderate spring mornings. The nature preserve connects north to Galatyn Woodland Preserve. Popular with birders, trail runners, and families who want something that actually feels like nature. Good for ages 6 and up on the main loop.

Nature Trails Birding Hardwood Forest Creek Access

Galatyn Woodland Preserve

A multi-use trail park with a boardwalk for bird-watching, wetland ponds, an animal sculpture playground, horseshoe pits, and picnic areas. Trail links connect south to Spring Creek and north toward Galatyn Park Plaza. The boardwalk makes this stroller-accessible and a good first trail experience for younger kids. Restrooms on site.

Boardwalk Wetland Ponds Stroller-Friendly Bird-Watching

Glenville Park

A neighborhood park with a seasonal splash playground (confirmed on the city's official parks page), swimming pool, picnic and playground equipment, shelter, one baseball field, and two athletic fields. Good all-ages park for a summer afternoon. Check the city's parks page at cor.net for current splash playground season dates and hours.

Splash Playground Swimming Pool Baseball Field Playground

Heights Park

The larger of the two city parks with confirmed splash playgrounds. Heights Park also has a recreation center, swimming pool, two lighted baseball fields, two lighted tennis courts, athletic fields, two shelters, and public art on site. A true community hub park that covers most recreational needs in one location. Good for all ages.

Splash Playground Recreation Center Tennis Courts Swimming Pool

Duck Creek Linear Park

A creek-corridor linear park with a multi-use trail, a plaza with playground equipment, picnic facilities, and ponds. The trail links to Owens Trail, extending the continuous trail network. Paved sections are stroller-friendly throughout. Good for families who want a flat, accessible trail with amenities at both ends.

Multi-Use Trail Playground Ponds Stroller-Friendly

Cottonwood Park

The city's most well-known park and the home of the Cottonwood Art Festival each May and October. Outside festival season it functions as a standard community park with open lawn, picnic facilities, and parking. In the days leading up to festival weekends, the park transforms into a 190-booth outdoor gallery that draws families from across DFW.

Festivals Open Lawn Picnic Areas

Galatyn Park Urban Center

The outdoor gathering space adjacent to CityLine and the Eisemann Center. Home to the Wildflower! Music Festival each May. The urban park format with paved plazas and shade structures gives it a different feel from Richardson's creek-corridor parks. Easy DART rail access makes it the city's most transit-friendly public space.

Trail System Highlight

Spring Creek Nature Area is Richardson's signature outdoor experience. The 4.5-mile loop through 100-plus acres of hardwood forest is the closest thing to a genuine nature hike you'll find in the inner Dallas suburbs. The preserve connects north via trail to Galatyn Woodland Preserve, creating a continuous wooded greenway that feels longer than it is. Best on weekday mornings when trail traffic is light and the birding is good.

Relocation Tip

On your first Richardson weekend, walk the Spring Creek Nature Area trail in the morning and then drive to CityLine for lunch. That two-hour loop captures what most Richardson families love about the city: access to actual nature followed by walkable urban amenities, all within a few miles of each other. It's a combination that most DFW suburbs can't replicate.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Richardson's youth sports scene is anchored by the Richardson Soccer Association, which has been organizing youth soccer in North Texas for more than 50 years, and the City of Richardson's Parks and Recreation athletics programs covering baseball, softball, basketball, and more. The Spring Valley Sports Association serves residents in the east Richardson and adjacent areas.

Soccer

League: Richardson Soccer Association (RSA)
Ages: U4 through U19
Season: Fall and Spring
More than 50 years of youth soccer in Richardson and North Texas. Players are assigned to teams based on birth year and school or neighborhood. Age groups span U4-U8 through U15+ with different divisions. One of the most established recreational soccer programs in Dallas County.

Baseball & Softball

League: City of Richardson Parks & Recreation Athletics
Ages: Youth divisions by age group
Season: Spring and Fall
City-run baseball and softball programs through the Parks and Recreation Athletics department. Contact Athletics@cor.gov or 972-744-7892 for current registration information and season schedules. Games played at city park ball fields including Glenville and Heights parks.

Multi-Sport (East Richardson)

League: Spring Valley Sports Association (SVAA)
Ages: Youth divisions
Season: Year-round by sport
The Spring Valley Sports Association serves youth athletes in the east Richardson and adjacent communities. Covers multiple sports with a volunteer-run structure. A good option for families in the eastern Richardson zip codes who want a local league alternative.

Basketball

League: City of Richardson Parks & Recreation Athletics
Ages: Youth divisions
Season: Winter
City-organized youth basketball through Parks and Recreation. Indoor gymnasium play at city recreation centers. Check the Richardson Recreation Guide at cor.net for current season registration dates and gym locations.

Tennis

Facility: Heights Park (lighted courts), city recreation centers
Ages: All ages, junior programs available
Season: Year-round
Heights Park has two lighted tennis courts available for public use. The city recreation department runs junior tennis clinics and lessons through its programming calendar. RISD high schools also run competitive tennis programs that feed from strong local junior training.

City Recreation Day Camps

Program: City of Richardson Summer Camps
Ages: School-age children
Season: Summer
Richardson Parks and Recreation runs structured summer day camps for school-age kids. Programs cover sports, arts, and outdoor activities. A popular choice for working parents who need structured programming and can't commit to a full-season league.

Featured Sports Resource

Richardson ISD Athletics offers competitive programs at both junior high and high school levels across all major sports. For families relocating, RISD's athletics infrastructure is notable: the district's competitive culture and the depth of its programs give student-athletes real pathways from recreational youth leagues into high school competition. Richardson High School and other RISD campuses are known for strong programs in football, soccer, tennis, and cross country.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Richardson's entertainment profile skews toward the arts and outdoors, anchored by the Eisemann Center and the two annual festivals. The CityLine development added a walkable mixed-use layer, and the city's position on the DART Red Line puts Uptown Dallas and Deep Ellum within 25 minutes for older kids and teens.

Eisemann Center for the Performing Arts

Performing arts venue · A state-of-the-art performance hall adjacent to CityLine with an eclectic lineup of comedy, music, theater, and dance productions throughout the year. The Hill Performance Hall format makes sight lines good from almost every seat. A practical place to introduce kids to live performing arts without the parking drama of a downtown Dallas venue.

CityLine Richardson

Mixed-use development · The CityLine development at US 75 and Renner Road brought walkable retail, dining, and office space to central Richardson. Restaurants, shops, outdoor space, and DART station access make it the city's most active weekday and weekend hub. Good for families who want urban-feel amenities without going into Dallas proper.

Richardson Mercantile

Antique and vintage marketplace · More than 200 vendors in a large indoor space near downtown Richardson. For families, it's a treasure-hunt-style outing, kids who like vintage toys, records, and odd finds get genuinely engaged. Good rainy Saturday destination for families who don't need screens to have a good time.

Richardson Public Library

Public library system · Children's story times, summer reading programs, maker-space access, and year-round family events. Richardson's library system is well-funded and consistently runs more programming than families from other cities expect. Free, and one of the most-used civic resources in the city.

Interurban Railway Museum

Local history museum · The Interurban Railway Museum tells the story of the Texas interurban railroad system with antique rail cars and historical artifacts from the era when electric railways connected DFW cities. Niche but genuinely interesting for kids who like trains and transportation history. Located in historic downtown Richardson.

Day Trip from Richardson

Perot Museum of Nature and Science in downtown Dallas is about 20 minutes south, the top family museum day trip in the metro, with multiple floors covering dinosaurs, space, energy, and Texas geology. For a closer option, SEA LIFE Grapevine Aquarium is about 30 minutes west and works well for under-8 kids on a weekday when crowds are lighter.

Family-Friendly Dining

Richardson's dining scene reflects its demographic: a highly educated, internationally diverse population that demands more than strip-center chain restaurants. The result is a genuine mix of Mediterranean, Japanese, Texas BBQ, and casual American that keeps families with adventurous eaters very well fed.

Babe's Chicken Dinner House
Best family-style comfort dinner
Southern comfort food in big shared bowls: fried chicken, mashed potatoes, biscuits, and corn. The family-style format means no one gets left out and the table stays full. A Texas institution that works for ages 4 to 84.
Spring Creek Barbeque
Best classic Texas BBQ
Serving meat and sides in a simple, family-friendly atmosphere since 1980. Brisket, ribs, smoked sausage, and sides on trays. No pretense, no reservations, no wait for quality. The format handles kids without drama.
Pappadeaux Seafood Kitchen
Best celebratory family dinner
New Orleans-style seafood and steaks in generous portions. The Pappadeaux format works well for family celebrations: the menu is wide enough for picky eaters and impressive enough for out-of-town guests. Expect a wait on Friday and Saturday evenings.
Ali Baba Mediterranean Grill
Best weekday lunch with kids
A Richardson institution since 1989, with a large lunch buffet of Lebanese and Mediterranean grilled cuisine and meze. Kids who have never tried hummus, falafel, or shawarma tend to discover their preferences quickly here. Relaxed atmosphere, consistent quality.
Dos Arroyo's
Best TexMex for the whole family
Traditional Mexican fare including homemade tamales and sauces in a modern, rustic setting. Formerly Frankie's Mexican Cuisine, with a long neighborhood following. The kids menu covers the basics while the adult options go deeper into traditional preparation.
Moriya Shokudo
Best Japanese for adventurous kids
A Japanese restaurant with authentic shokudo-style dishes. Richardson's tech-industry demographics support genuine Japanese dining options that go beyond sushi. For families whose kids are willing to try ramen, donburi, or udon in a real setting, this is the one to try.
Rodeo Goat
Best elevated burger night
Craft burger bar with inventive combinations and a lively atmosphere. Good for families who want better-than-chains without the formality. Kids can build simple burgers while parents work through the more creative menu. The fries are worth the visit alone.
Fanah Cafe and Pastry
Best weekend brunch spot
A cafe and pastry shop with a loyal following for its brunch menu and baked goods. The kind of place where you go for 45 minutes and stay for two hours. Kids who appreciate pastries quickly become fans. A strong Saturday morning option before a Spring Creek trail walk.
Anaya's Seafood Scratch Kitchen
Best scratch-kitchen seafood
Scratch-kitchen seafood with a focus on fresh preparation. A good choice for families who want to eat well without going into Dallas. The menu leans toward Gulf Coast and Latin seafood preparations that reflect Richardson's diverse dining culture.

The CityLine area at US 75 and Renner Road has added a walkable restaurant cluster with several fast-casual and sit-down options. The older dining corridor along Arapaho Road and Belt Line has the longest-established neighborhood restaurants.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Richardson does everyday retail efficiently and has a distinctive vintage and antique scene, with easy freeway access to NorthPark Center for when the family needs a bigger shopping day.

CityLine DFW

Mixed-Use District

The CityLine development combines retail, dining, and office space in a walkable format near the DART Red Line station. Restaurants, specialty shops, and outdoor seating create the mixed-use atmosphere that Richardson's older strip corridors lack. Growing tenant mix with new additions annually.

Best for: lunch followed by a walkable afternoon with older kids or teens who can navigate independently.

Richardson Mercantile

Antique Marketplace

More than 200 vendors selling antiques, vintage items, collectibles, and eclectic goods in a large indoor space. For families, it's a treasure-hunt experience where kids often stumble into vintage toys, board games, and records that spark genuine excitement.

Best for: rainy Saturday browsing with kids who have some patience and a sense of curiosity.

NorthPark Center (nearby)

Regional Mall

About 15 minutes south on US 75, NorthPark is one of the premier shopping destinations in Texas. Luxury and mid-tier anchors alongside a genuine museum-quality art collection displayed throughout the mall. The layout and art make it a legitimately pleasant family outing even for families who don't love malls.

Best for: back-to-school shopping, a full-day family outing, or when visiting relatives want a quintessential Dallas retail experience.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Richardson runs a strong events calendar for a city its size. The Cottonwood Art Festival and Wildflower! Music Festival are the anchors, but the city's Parks and Recreation department fills out the calendar with community events throughout the year.

May

Cottonwood Art Festival (Spring)

Held annually since 1969 at Cottonwood Park. More than 190 juried artist booths, live entertainment on two stages, ArtStop kids' activities, a craft beer garden, and food trucks. One of the most attended outdoor art festivals in Texas. The spring edition typically runs the first weekend of May.

May

Wildflower! Arts & Music Festival

A three-day award-winning arts and music festival at Galatyn Park, typically the third weekend in May. Six stages of live entertainment (check the city's events calendar for this year's lineup), interactive art, family-friendly programming, and a signature competition. One of the larger outdoor music events in North Texas.

July

Fourth of July Celebration

Richardson celebrates Independence Day with fireworks and family activities. Check the city events calendar at cor.net for the current year's venue, timing, and programming details, as the format has shifted over the years.

October

Cottonwood Art Festival (Fall)

The fall edition of the Cottonwood Art Festival runs the first weekend of October with the same format as spring: 190-plus artists, two stages, kids' activities, food, and beer garden. The cooler October weather makes the fall edition the more pleasant of the two for spending a full day at the park.

Year-round

Eisemann Center Performances

The Eisemann Center runs performances year-round including family-appropriate shows, theater, comedy, and music. Check the Eisemann Center schedule independently from the city events calendar, as programming is managed separately with its own ticket system.

Fall/Spring

RSA Soccer Seasons

The Richardson Soccer Association's fall and spring seasons bring youth games to parks across the city. For families with kids in RSA, the Saturday morning game-day culture at Richardson parks is a significant part of the city's community rhythm from September through November and February through May.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When July temperatures hit triple digits, Richardson families use the city's recreation centers and library system as daily anchors, with trampoline parks and entertainment centers in the surrounding Mid-Cities area for weekend energy-burn sessions.

Heights Recreation Center

Type: City Recreation Center · Ages: All ages
Located at Heights Park, the Heights Recreation Center runs youth and family programming including sports classes, dance, swim lessons, and arts. The co-location with the park's tennis courts, pool, and athletic fields makes it a one-stop facility for active families. Check the Recreation Guide at cor.net for current programming.

Richardson Public Library

Type: Public Library · Ages: All ages
One of the most-used civic resources in the city. Children's story times, summer reading challenge, maker-space access, and year-round family programming. Free. The library system consistently runs more events than families expect from a suburban library. Check the online calendar weekly.

Eisemann Center

Type: Performing Arts Venue · Ages: 6 and up (varies by show)
Family-appropriate performances run throughout the year at the Eisemann Center. Shows range from Broadway-style touring productions to local theater and children's programming. A good year-round indoor destination that introduces kids to live performance in a comfortable suburban venue.

Urban Air Trampoline Park

Type: Adventure Park · Ages: 2 and up
The nearest Urban Air serves Richardson families for rainy-day and summer heat energy burns. Full trampoline adventure park with warrior course, climbing walls, foam pits, and a toddler zone. Socks required. A reliable two-hour activity when outdoor options are closed off by weather or temperature.

Perot Museum of Nature and Science

Type: Museum · Ages: All ages
About 20 minutes south on US 75 in downtown Dallas. Five floors of interactive exhibits covering dinosaurs, energy, space, Texas geology, and human biology. One of the top family museums in the state. The Perot is worth annual membership for Richardson families who visit three or more times a year.

Richardson Mercantile

Type: Antique Marketplace · Ages: All ages
More than 200 vendors in a large climate-controlled indoor space. For families, it works as an exploratory rainy-day activity where kids discover vintage toys and oddities they weren't expecting. The scale of the marketplace means a visit takes longer than anticipated, which is usually a good sign.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

Richardson families handle summer heat with a two-track system: morning trail runs at Spring Creek Nature Area before 8 a.m. while the forest canopy still holds some cool air, then air-conditioned afternoons at the library, Heights Recreation Center pool, or a Perot Museum day trip. The splash playgrounds at Glenville and Heights parks work for under-8 kids during the first two hours of the day before the concrete heats up. Check cor.net for current splash playground hours before loading up the car.
