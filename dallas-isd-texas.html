Dallas ISD: Complete Family Guide

What relocating families need to know about Dallas Independent School District: the magnet system, TEA ratings, wide campus variation, enrollment, and how to verify boundaries before you buy.

Dallas Independent School District is the second-largest school district in Texas and the seventeenth-largest in the United States, serving roughly 139,000 students across more than 230 campuses in the city of Dallas. That scale is the single most important thing a relocating family needs to understand: a district this large is not one experience, it is hundreds of very different ones. The honest tradeoff is right up front. The district-wide letter grade, currently a TEA B, is an average that hides an enormous range from nationally ranked magnet schools to campuses that are still working hard to climb. If you take one thing from this page, take this: research the specific campus your address is zoned to, never the district average.

This page is written for families relocating to DFW who are weighing whether to live inside the city of Dallas rather than a suburb, and for high-achieving students who might compete for one of Dallas ISD's magnet seats. Dallas ISD's magnet network is genuinely exceptional and, unlike almost anything in the suburbs, it is open to families across the entire district regardless of where in the city they live. For the right student, that access can matter more than any suburb ISD's average rating.

The district is governed by a nine-member elected board of trustees and employs more than 22,000 staff. After nine straight years of declining enrollment, Dallas ISD saw enrollment rise again in the 2024-25 school year, exceeding its own projections. Its central administration is located at 9400 North Central Expressway, Dallas, TX 75231.

Best Fit For Families Who…

  • Have an academically driven student who can compete for a magnet seat and want specialized programming no single suburb can match
  • Want an urban lifestyle in the city of Dallas and are willing to research their specific neighborhood campus carefully
  • Value district choice options like dual language, Montessori, IB, and single-gender academies
  • Are relocating to an established Dallas neighborhood already zoned to a campus you have verified individually

May Not Be the Best Fit If…

  • Your priority is consistent, predictable performance at every campus (suburban districts deliver that more uniformly)
  • You want to choose a home by district reputation alone, without vetting the assigned campus
  • You need a guaranteed seat in a top program without going through a competitive application
  • A short, simple commute to one assigned neighborhood school is essential (magnet campuses draw students from across the city)
⚠ Boundary Verification

A "Dallas, TX" mailing address does not guarantee Dallas ISD enrollment. Several neighborhoods and enclaves inside the city are served by other districts, including Highland Park ISD (Highland Park and University Park), Richardson ISD (parts of Lake Highlands and far North Dallas), and Plano ISD (portions of far North Dallas). Always verify your specific address at the Dallas ISD boundary locator and the TEA School District Locator before making an offer.

TEA Accountability Rating

The Texas Education Agency assigns every district and campus an annual A to F accountability rating built on three domains. For the 2024-25 school year, Dallas ISD earned a TEA B rating (score: 83 out of 100), the district's second consecutive year of improvement after a C (79) in 2023-24 and a C (76) in 2022-23.

What the three domains measure:

  • Student Achievement: STAAR results, graduation rates, and college, career, and military readiness indicators
  • School Progress: year-over-year academic growth compared to similar campuses
  • Closing the Gaps: performance equity across student groups
Why the District Letter Can Mislead

The single district grade is an average across more than 230 campuses. In the 2024-25 ratings, of the district's 233 rated schools, 61 earned an A, 107 earned a B, 49 earned a C, 14 earned a D, and 2 earned an F. That spread is the whole story: a relocating family who reads only "Dallas ISD is a B" learns almost nothing about the school their child would actually attend. Pull the individual campus rating for your specific address at txschools.gov before you decide.

A practical way to read this: the difference between an A campus (90 or above) and a C campus (70 to 79) inside the same district can be larger than the difference between two entirely different suburbs. In Dallas ISD, that variation is the norm rather than the exception, which is exactly why campus-level research matters more here than in a small, uniform district.

Graduation Rate

Dallas ISD's four-year graduation rate was approximately 81 percent for the Class of 2023 (TEA four-year longitudinal rate), which trails most of the high-performing suburban districts in this guide. This is one of the honest tradeoffs of a large urban district: aggregate outcomes sit below the suburban average even as individual magnet and neighborhood campuses post some of the strongest numbers in the state. The figure that matters for your family is the graduation and college-readiness rate at the specific campus your child would attend, not the district composite.

Magnet Outcomes vs. District Average

At the top of the range, Dallas ISD magnet campuses are nationally competitive. The School for the Talented and Gifted has repeatedly been ranked the number one public high school in Texas by U.S. News & World Report, and the School of Science and Engineering is a National Blue Ribbon campus. Those results are real, but they reflect selective-admission programs, not the typical campus experience, and it would be misleading to read them as representative of the district as a whole. Treat the magnets as their own tier when you evaluate outcomes.

Advanced Coursework & Dual Credit

Dallas ISD offers Advanced Placement across its comprehensive high schools, International Baccalaureate at designated campuses, and dual credit through Dallas College that lets eligible students earn college hours at no cost while in high school. Career and Technical Education pathways span health science, information technology, business, and skilled trades, with industry certifications available alongside the diploma. Availability varies by campus, so confirm which programs are offered at your zoned school or targeted magnet.

The Magnet System: Dallas ISD's Crown Jewel

If there is one reason a relocating family with a high-achieving student should look seriously at Dallas ISD, it is the magnet network. These are competitive-admission schools that draw students from across the city, and several rank among the best public high schools in Texas and the nation. The heart of the system is the Yvonne A. Ewell Townview Center in East Oak Cliff, a single building that houses six independent magnet high schools, each with its own admission process and focus.

School for the Talented and Gifted (TAG) at Townview

Grades 9-12Competitive Admission

Repeatedly ranked the number one public high school in Texas and among the top in the nation by U.S. News & World Report, and a National Blue Ribbon School (2022). Admission is based on grades, test scores, and an application, with in-district students served first.

School of Science and Engineering (SEM) at Townview

Grades 9-12STEM Magnet

A National Blue Ribbon STEM magnet that regularly appears among the country's top high schools. Admission uses onsite English and math exams plus an interview with a logic and reasoning component, so preparation and eligibility matter more than proximity.

Booker T. Washington High School for the Performing and Visual Arts

Grades 9-12Arts, Audition-Based

The district's arts magnet, located in the Dallas Arts District (a separate campus from Townview). Admission is by audition or portfolio and is highly selective: roughly 900 applicants compete for about 220 freshman seats each year.

Marvin E. Robinson School of Business and Management at Townview

Grades 9-12Business Magnet

One of the six Townview magnets, focused on business, finance, and management, with student-run enterprises on campus. Like the other magnets, it admits qualified students from across the district rather than by home attendance zone.

The Townview Center also houses three more competitive magnets under the same roof: the School of Health Professions, the Rosie M. Collins Sorrells School of Education and Social Services (a Blue Ribbon campus), and the School of Government and Law (named for Judge Barefoot Sanders). Beyond Townview and Booker T. Washington, Dallas ISD operates more than 30 magnet and choice schools across every grade level. Dallas ISD also runs comprehensive neighborhood high schools whose quality varies campus by campus, which is why your zoned-campus research is as important as the magnet options.

Specialty Programs & School Choice

The Exception to Address-Based Assignment

In most Texas districts, your home address determines your school. Dallas ISD's magnet and choice schools are the exception: they admit qualified students from across the district regardless of home attendance zone. A family living anywhere inside Dallas ISD boundaries can apply city-wide. All qualified in-district (Dallas ISD resident) applicants are served before any out-of-district student is considered, so residency inside the district is a meaningful advantage.

Magnet & Choice Application

Applications run through Smart Choice (SchoolMint), typically opening November 1 and closing January 31 for the following school year. Magnets set GPA and test-score eligibility to qualify. Offers and waitlist status are emailed in late February, with families confirming by mid-March. Home-search takeaway: eligibility and the application window matter more than living near a specific magnet.

Talented and Gifted / GT

Beyond the standalone TAG magnet, Dallas ISD provides gifted and talented services district-wide, identified through a multi-criteria process. Families new from another state should bring prior GT documentation for review.

International Baccalaureate & Dual Language

IB and dual language pathways are offered at designated campuses rather than district-wide. If a specific pathway is a priority, confirm the exact campus that offers it and whether access is by zone, choice application, or both, before you choose a home.

Dual Credit (Dallas College)

Through the district's partnership with Dallas College, eligible juniors and seniors can earn college credit at no cost while completing high school. Availability and course lists vary by campus.

Career & Technical Education

CTE pathways include health science, information technology, business, and skilled trades, with industry certifications available. Some pathways and the P-TECH early-college model are campus-specific and may involve an application.

Special Education & 504

Dallas ISD provides the full continuum of special education services under IDEA. Families transferring an existing IEP should contact the district promptly: Texas law requires the district to adopt the current IEP or hold an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment. For Section 504 accommodations, contact the campus 504 coordinator.

Enrollment: New Families

How to Enroll

New student enrollment is completed through Dallas ISD's centralized enrollment system at dallasisd.org. For general enrollment questions, the district line is (972) 925-5560. Typical requirements for the 2025-26 school year include:

  • Proof of residency: lease agreement or deed and a current utility bill
  • Birth certificate for first-time Texas enrollees
  • Immunization records meeting Texas requirements
  • Previous school records: transcript, IEP or 504 plan if applicable, and test scores
  • Proof of guardianship if applicable

Magnet & Choice Timeline

Neighborhood enrollment happens year-round, but magnet and choice schools follow the separate Smart Choice application calendar (roughly November 1 to January 31 for the next school year). If a magnet seat is your goal, plan your move and application timing around that window, because mid-year magnet entry depends entirely on seat availability.

Transfers

Intra-district transfers between neighborhood campuses depend on capacity and are not guaranteed. Choice and magnet admission is handled through the application process above rather than a standard transfer request. Out-of-district students may be considered for magnets only after all qualified in-district applicants have been placed.

Students With IEPs or 504 Plans

If your child has an existing IEP or 504 plan from another district or state, contact Dallas ISD special education services as soon as you enroll. Texas law requires the district to either adopt the current IEP or convene an ARD meeting within 30 days. Bring all prior evaluation and eligibility documentation to the enrollment appointment.

Boundary Verification

Dallas ISD serves most, but not all, of the city of Dallas, plus portions of several neighboring communities. Because the district shares the city with other independent districts, a Dallas mailing address is one of the least reliable predictors of which schools a home is zoned to anywhere in DFW.

  • Highland Park and University Park are served by Highland Park ISD, not Dallas ISD, even though they sit within the Dallas footprint.
  • Parts of Lake Highlands and far North Dallas are zoned to Richardson ISD.
  • Portions of far North Dallas fall within Plano ISD.
  • Neighborhoods near the city's edges can be zoned to districts such as Carrollton-Farmers Branch, Garland, or others.

Two layers of verification matter here. First, confirm which district the home is in at all, using the TEA School District Locator and the Dallas ISD boundary tool. Second, if the home is in Dallas ISD, confirm the specific elementary, middle, and high school it feeds, because those assignments do not always follow common-sense geography and can differ from a neighbor a few blocks away.

Making an offer without verifying your exact address can mean your child attends a very different school than you assumed, which in a district with this much campus-to-campus variation is a costly surprise. Verify before you buy, every time.

District Finances

Tax Rate

Dallas ISD's total school tax rate is approximately $1.10 per $100 of assessed value (Texas Comptroller and Dallas County, 2024 certified), among the lower school tax rates in the DFW area. The district approved a small rate reduction for tax year 2025. The rate combines the Maintenance and Operations (M&O) rate that funds daily operations and the Interest and Sinking (I&S) rate that services bond debt.

To translate that into a payment: on a home assessed at $400,000, the Dallas ISD school tax is roughly $4,400 per year before exemptions. Texas homeowners with a primary-residence homestead exemption receive a $100,000 reduction in assessed value for school taxes, which lowers the taxable value to $300,000 and the annual school levy to roughly $3,300. County, city, and community-college taxes are billed separately and vary by location.

Per-Pupil Spending

Per-pupil expenditure is reported publicly through TEA PEIMS financial data and the federal NCES Common Core of Data. As a large urban district, Dallas ISD receives the state Basic Allotment per student plus weighted funding for bilingual, special education, and low-income students. Current figures are available at txschools.gov.

How Dallas ISD Compares

Relocating families rarely evaluate one district in isolation. Families considering living inside the city of Dallas usually weigh it against the higher-rated districts that share or border the city. Here is how Dallas ISD stacks up against three of the most common cross-shops, framed around the factors that actually drive the decision.

Who tends to prefer each: families set on predictable, uniform performance and a small-district feel lean toward Highland Park or Coppell. Families who want the diversity, price, and location of the inner city while keeping strong options often land in Richardson or in a carefully vetted Dallas ISD neighborhood. And families with a student who can win a magnet seat frequently find that Dallas ISD's specialized programs outperform anything a suburb can offer for that particular child. The right answer depends on your student and your address, not on the district letter alone.

What It Feels Like to Be a Family Here

Dallas ISD is the urban core of the entire DFW education landscape, and family life inside it looks different depending on the campus and neighborhood. In the magnet world, the culture is intense and college-focused: students commute from across the city to Townview or Booker T. Washington, and the peer group is drawn together by shared academic or artistic ambition rather than by ZIP code. In neighborhood schools, the experience is more locally rooted, and parent involvement, program depth, and campus culture vary widely from one school to the next.

A few district-specific realities are worth naming. This is the second-largest district in Texas and the seventeenth-largest in the country, so the range of programs is vast but so is the variation in outcomes. The Townview Center is unusual nationally, six competitive magnet high schools operating under a single roof. And after nine consecutive years of enrollment decline, the district grew again in 2024-25, a sign that some families are choosing back into the city. Parents on local forums consistently give the same advice, which mirrors ours: love the magnets, but do your homework on the exact neighborhood campus before you commit.

For a relocating family, the practical mindset is different here than in a small suburban district. Instead of trusting a district reputation, you are choosing a specific campus or competing for a specific program, and doing the research that makes that choice a good one. When it is a fit, whether through a strong neighborhood school or a magnet seat, Dallas ISD offers something the suburbs cannot: the culture and opportunity of the city itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a "Dallas, TX" address guarantee enrollment in Dallas ISD? ▾
No. A Dallas mailing address is one of the least reliable school-zone predictors in DFW. Highland Park and University Park are served by Highland Park ISD, parts of Lake Highlands and far North Dallas by Richardson ISD, and portions of far North Dallas by Plano ISD, all within the city footprint. Verify your specific address with the TEA School District Locator and the Dallas ISD boundary tool before making an offer.
What makes the Dallas ISD magnet schools special, and how does my child get in? ▾
Dallas ISD runs more than 30 magnet and choice schools. The best known are the six competitive magnets inside the Yvonne A. Ewell Townview Center, including the School for the Talented and Gifted (repeatedly ranked number one public high school in Texas by U.S. News) and the School of Science and Engineering (a National Blue Ribbon STEM campus), plus the audition-based Booker T. Washington High School for the Performing and Visual Arts in the Arts District. Admission is competitive and varies by school: grades and test scores, onsite exams and interviews, or auditions and portfolios. Applications run through Smart Choice, typically November 1 to January 31 for the following year.
The district is rated B, so is every campus about the same? ▾
No, and this is the most important thing to understand about Dallas ISD. The district letter is an average across more than 230 campuses. In the 2024-25 ratings, of 233 rated schools, 61 earned an A, 107 a B, 49 a C, 14 a D, and 2 an F. The range is enormous, so the district grade tells you very little about the specific school your child would attend. Always pull the individual campus rating for your exact address at txschools.gov.
Can we apply to a magnet if we don't live near it? ▾
Yes. Magnet and choice schools are the exception to address-based assignment: they admit qualified students from across the district regardless of home attendance zone, so where you live inside Dallas ISD matters less than eligibility and the application. One advantage does come with residency: all qualified in-district (Dallas ISD resident) applicants are placed before any out-of-district student is considered.
How does Dallas ISD handle an IEP or 504 plan transferring from another district or state? ▾
Texas law requires Dallas ISD to either adopt your child's existing IEP or convene an ARD (Admissions, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior IEP, evaluation, and eligibility documentation to the enrollment appointment and contact special education services promptly. For Section 504 accommodations, work with the campus 504 coordinator. Because service delivery can vary by campus, discuss your child's specific needs directly with the assigned school.
How does Dallas ISD's tax rate compare to nearby districts? ▾
Dallas ISD's school tax rate is approximately $1.10 per $100 of assessed value (2024 certified), which is on the lower end for the area. For comparison, Highland Park ISD is around $0.87, Coppell ISD around $1.00, and Richardson ISD around $1.17. On a $400,000 home, Dallas ISD's school tax is roughly $4,400 per year before the $100,000 homestead exemption, and about $3,300 after. County, city, and college taxes are billed separately.
How does Dallas ISD compare to Highland Park, Richardson, or Coppell ISD? ▾
Highland Park ISD (about 7,000 students, TEA A) and Coppell ISD (about 10,500, TEA A) are small and uniformly high-performing, which appeals to families who want predictability. Richardson ISD (about 38,000, TEA B) is a larger, diverse inner-ring district with strong IB and Lake Highlands options. Dallas ISD is by far the largest and the most variable: it offers magnet programs no suburb can match alongside neighborhood campuses that range widely. The best fit depends on your student and your specific address, not on the district grade alone.
