Texas has no state income tax, but it funds public services — including schools — largely through property taxes, which means rates are higher than in many other states. In DFW suburbs, you'll typically see effective property tax rates between 1.8% and 2.8% of assessed value, depending on the city and school district. On a $500,000 home, that's roughly $9,000 to $14,000 per year. The good news: Texas offers a Homestead Exemption that reduces your taxable value, and for most families relocating from California or the Northeast, the elimination of state income tax more than offsets the higher property taxes. Kristen walks through this math with every client during the consultation.

School district boundaries in DFW can be surprisingly complex — in some areas, one street is the dividing line between two completely different ISDs, and the boundaries don't always match city limits. The most reliable way is to use the Texas Education Agency's attendance boundary lookup tool or each district's individual enrollment page. For any home you're seriously considering, Kristen verifies the school assignment directly — not just the district, but the specific elementary, middle, and high school feed — because feeds matter as much as the district rating, especially when your kids are in different age groups.
