What Makes Moving to Dallas–Fort Worth Different

DFW is one of the fastest-growing metros in the country — and it works differently than most places families move from. Here's what surprises people most.

The Metro Is Massive

DFW covers roughly 9,000 square miles. The difference between Southlake and Celina is not just price — it's commute time, school district, home age, and community feel. A suburb shortlist matters more here than in most metros.

City Limits ≠ School District

You can buy a home inside Frisco city limits and be zoned to a different ISD. School district boundaries do not match city boundaries, and sometimes a single street is the dividing line between two completely different districts.

Property Taxes Vary by Suburb — and by Street

Texas funds schools through property taxes, not state income tax. Effective rates in DFW range from about 1.8% to 2.8% depending on the city, ISD, and special taxing districts. On a $500K home, that gap is roughly $5,000 per year.

Commute Corridors Drive the Suburb Decision

If you're commuting to Plano or Legacy West, the right suburbs are different than if you're headed to Fort Worth or the medical district. Kristen factors your actual office address — not just "DFW" — into the shortlist from the start.

New Construction May Include MUDs, PIDs, or HOA Fees

Many new-construction communities in outer-ring suburbs are financed through Municipal Utility Districts (MUDs) or Public Improvement Districts (PIDs), which add an extra layer to your property tax bill. Kristen explains these before you fall in love with a floor plan.

Your Budget Goes Further — With Context

Families from California and the Northeast are often pleasantly surprised by what their budget buys in DFW — but price per square foot varies significantly across suburbs. Kristen shows you what your budget actually gets in each suburb, not just the sticker price.

A Municipal Utility District (MUD) is a special taxing district used in Texas to fund water, sewer, and drainage infrastructure in new-construction areas. A Public Improvement District (PID) functions similarly but typically funds amenities like parks, trails, and landscaping in a specific community. Both add an additional layer on top of your base property tax rate. In some outer-ring suburbs like Celina, Prosper, and Northlake, MUD or PID rates can add 0.5% to 1.0% or more to your effective property tax rate — meaning a home with a 2.4% base rate might have a total effective rate closer to 3.0% to 3.4%. Kristen discloses these before you fall in love with a floor plan, because they have a material impact on your true monthly payment.
