What Westlake Families Actually Do Here

Page last reviewed 2026-06-06. We update activities pages annually as parks, leagues, and venues change.

Quick answer: Westlake has 3+ private neighborhood preserves, access to Bob Jones Nature Center (738 acres, 5 minutes away), and school options that include Westlake Academy's K–12 IB program and Carroll ISD, making it one of the most school-centered family communities in Tarrant County for parents who prioritize education and a quiet estate lifestyle over traditional suburban amenities.

Westlake operates on a different premise than every other suburb in this guide. With roughly 1,500 residents and no city-run parks department, commercial restaurant district, or recreation center inside town limits, the family appeal here is built on two things: school options and neighborhood character. Carroll ISD ranks first in Texas, and Westlake Academy offers a rare public K–12 International Baccalaureate program that families move specifically to access. For parks, sports leagues, recreation, dining, and shopping, Westlake residents draw on Southlake and Keller, both within ten minutes. See the full Westlake relocation guide →

Westlake Academy K–12 IB

One of the only public, tuition-free K–12 International Baccalaureate schools in Texas. School life through PYP, MYP, and Diploma programs is the organizing center of Westlake's family community. Athletics, performing arts, and academic events fill the calendar from September through May.

Private Neighborhood Preserves

Terra Bella's 22.6-acre nature preserve with hike-and-bike trails, Glenwyck Farms' wooded running trail and natural ponds, and Circle T Ranch's 2,500-acre open landscape between corporate campuses give residents outdoor access within their own neighborhoods, no drive required.

Southlake & Keller Within Minutes

Bob Jones Nature Center, Bear Creek Park, Southlake Town Square's 130+ shops and restaurants, and the Southlake YMCA are all within ten minutes. The broader Northeast Tarrant activity ecosystem functions as Westlake's extended backyard for everything from weekend hikes to youth soccer registration.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Westlake has no public parks department and no city-owned park system. What it does have are private neighborhood preserves with genuine trail character, a 2,500-acre open ranchland buffer, and immediate access to outstanding public parks in Southlake and Keller. For the families who live here, the natural environment inside the neighborhoods is a meaningful daily amenity, and Bob Jones Nature Center in Southlake (five minutes away, free, 738 acres) is where most serious hiking happens.

Terra Bella Nature Preserve

A 22.6-acre nature preserve within the Terra Bella neighborhood, one of Westlake's smallest and most private communities with 28 lots set across 54.7 acres. The preserve features hike-and-bike trails through wooded terrain and serves as the neighborhood's shared green space. Residents here describe it as a daily-use amenity that makes the neighborhood feel more like a ranch than a subdivision. Access is private to Terra Bella residents (Carroll ISD zone, homes $3M–$7M+).

Hike & Bike Trails Nature Preserve Private to Residents Carroll ISD Zone

Glenwyck Farms Trail & Ponds

An 82-lot wooded community with a private running trail and natural ponds woven through the neighborhood. Glenwyck Farms is one of Westlake's more established gated neighborhoods, and the trail system is a genuine outdoor amenity on par with what smaller cities build as public parks. Residents in Carroll ISD zone use this daily for morning runs and evening walks. Private to Glenwyck Farms residents (homes $2M–$5M+).

Running Trail Natural Ponds Wooded Setting Private to Residents

Circle T Ranch Open Landscape

A 2,500-acre open ranchland that separates the Fidelity Investments and Charles Schwab campuses in Westlake, maintained as a working ranch complete with longhorn cattle. The landscape is not a public park but is visible from surrounding roads and gives Westlake its distinctive open, Texas ranch character. It is one of the reasons Westlake feels unlike any other corporate suburb in DFW despite having three of the largest campuses in the Metroplex inside its borders.

Open Ranchland Texas Character Longhorn Cattle Not Public Access

Bob Jones Nature Center, Southlake

738-acre nature preserve in adjacent Southlake, approximately 5 minutes from Westlake. Features more than 4 miles of forested trails for hiking and mountain biking, a nature education center, and creek access. Open daily, free of charge. This is where most Westlake families go for outdoor recreation that requires actual trail mileage. Good shade coverage and stroller-accessible entry paths. The nature center hosts guided hikes and environmental education programs year-round.

4+ Miles of Trails Mountain Biking Nature Education Free Admission 5 Min Drive

Southlake Bicentennial Park

A 9-acre park in Southlake, 5 minutes from Westlake, with playgrounds, open green space, and covered picnic areas. This is also the site of the Saturday Southlake Farmers Market, which runs seasonally with local produce, food vendors, and family-scale outdoor programming. A practical destination for morning outings when the neighborhood preserves feel too familiar.

Playgrounds Farmers Market Picnic Areas 5 Min Drive

Bear Creek Park, Keller

A full-service community park in Keller, approximately 8 minutes from Westlake. Features athletic fields, a splash pad, paved walking trails, playgrounds, and covered pavilions. This is the nearest park with a splash pad for families with young children, and it functions as a strong public park alternative for Westlake families in the Keller ISD zone (Vaquero neighborhood). Restrooms, shade trees, and ample parking.

Splash Pad Athletic Fields Walking Trails Playground 8 Min Drive

Trophy Club Park & Lake Grapevine

Approximately 15 minutes from Westlake, Trophy Club Park sits at the edge of Lake Grapevine and offers shoreline access, kayak and canoe launch points, fishing, and open green space. Lake Grapevine is also where the Grapevine Sailing Club and Grapevine Windsurfing Association operate. A solid half-day outing for families with older kids who want water access without driving to a commercial venue.

Lake Access Kayaking Fishing Sailing 15 Min Drive

Trail System Highlight

Bob Jones Nature Center & Preserve, Southlake (5 min from Westlake). 738 acres, 4+ miles of forested hiking and mountain biking trails, a nature education facility, and creek access, all free and open to the public year-round. This is where Westlake families go for genuine trail mileage. The entry area is stroller-accessible, the woods provide shade even in summer, and the trail network is varied enough to keep older kids interested on a second or third visit.

Relocation Tip

When visiting Westlake for the first time, drive through Terra Bella and Glenwyck Farms to see what the neighborhood preserves actually look like, then head five minutes east to Bob Jones Nature Center. Between the two you get a complete picture of how Westlake families experience the outdoors: private, wooded, and estate-scale within the neighborhood; a serious nature reserve immediately next door. Bear Creek Park in Keller is the right first stop for families with young children who specifically want a splash pad.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Westlake has no city-run recreation department and no youth sports leagues of its own. Sports access runs through three channels: Westlake Academy's school athletics program (grades 6–12), whichever ISD serves the specific neighborhood (Carroll ISD eastern, Keller ISD western), and the adjacent cities' leagues, primarily through the Southlake YMCA and Keller Soccer Association. For most families, the Southlake YMCA at five minutes away is the practical hub for youth programming across all ages.

Westlake Academy Athletics

Program: Westlake Academy School Athletics
Ages: Grades 6–12 (Middle and High School)
Season: Year-round by sport
Westlake Academy competes in UIL athletics across soccer, cross country, basketball, volleyball, tennis, and swimming. For families whose children attend the Academy, school sports serve as the primary athletic community from middle school onward.

Carroll ISD Dragon Athletics

Program: Carroll ISD School Athletics (Eastern Westlake)
Ages: Elementary through high school
Season: Year-round by sport
Families in eastern Westlake (Glenwyck Farms, Terra Bella, Paigebrooke) feed into Carroll ISD's nationally ranked athletics program. Dragon football, swim and dive, tennis, lacrosse, and basketball are all part of one of the most competitive UIL programs in Texas.

Keller ISD Athletics

Program: Keller ISD School Athletics (Vaquero / Western Westlake)
Ages: Elementary through high school
Season: Year-round by sport
Families in the Vaquero neighborhood and western Westlake parcels feed into Keller ISD. Keller ISD runs a strong athletics program across football, baseball, softball, soccer, track, and swimming, with middle and high school competition at the district and regional level.

Southlake YMCA Youth Programs

Program: YMCA of Metropolitan Fort Worth, Southlake Branch
Ages: All ages, infant through adult
Season: Year-round
Approximately 5 minutes from Westlake. Family membership runs approximately $80–$100/month. Most Westlake families use the YMCA as their primary organized recreation facility for youth swim lessons, youth sports, fitness classes, and after-school programming.

Keller Soccer Association

Association: Keller Soccer Association (KSA)
Ages: Recreational and competitive, ages 3–18
Season: Spring and fall
Approximately 10 minutes from Westlake. KSA offers recreational and select-level soccer across age divisions. Westlake families in the Keller ISD zone (Vaquero) often register here for recreational youth soccer at the younger ages before transitioning to school athletics.

Vaquero Club Equestrian

Program: Vaquero Club Private Member Amenity
Ages: All ages (members and their families)
Season: Year-round
The Vaquero Club, Westlake's most exclusive private club community (298 homes, Tom Fazio golf course), includes equestrian facilities for members. Riding instruction and boarding available to members. This is a rare amenity in a DFW suburb and draws families with a specific interest in equestrian sport.

Junior Golf, Trophy Club

Program: Trophy Club Country Club Junior Golf
Ages: Youth golfers
Season: Spring through fall
Approximately 10 minutes from Westlake. Trophy Club Country Club offers junior golf instruction and youth programming. Lake Grapevine is also nearby for kayaking and sailing for older kids (15 min), providing water-based sports access without club membership.

The Southlake YMCA

The Southlake YMCA branch (5 min from Westlake, ~$80–$100/month family membership) functions as the practical recreation center for most Westlake families. Indoor and outdoor pools, group fitness, youth sports leagues, swim lessons, summer camps, and after-school care are all available here. Westlake has no public recreation center of its own, so the YMCA is where the community converges for organized programming outside of school athletics.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Entertainment in and around Westlake runs through Southlake Town Square five minutes east, where a Regal theater, dozens of restaurants, and a walkable lifestyle district serve as the practical town center for Westlake families. Grapevine's Opryland-style mixed-use and Lake Grapevine are 15 minutes away. The city itself has no commercial entertainment, which is exactly as designed for a community of 1,500 people who chose Westlake specifically for its quiet.

Southlake Town Square

Lifestyle Center  ·  5 min from Westlake. A 130-acre walkable mixed-use development with 130+ shops, dozens of restaurants, a Whole Foods, a Regal movie theater, and regular outdoor programming including the Saturday Farmers Market. This functions as Westlake's town center for most practical purposes, from daily groceries to date night to full-day family outings. Completely walkable within the square and family-friendly at all hours.

Bob Jones Nature Center

Nature Education  ·  5 min from Westlake. Beyond trails, the Bob Jones Nature Center runs interpretive programs, school field trips, guided hikes, and environmental education for all ages. A calm, low-cost family outing option that younger children find genuinely absorbing. Check the Southlake Parks website for current program schedules and seasonal events.

SEA LIFE Grapevine

Indoor Aquarium  ·  15 min from Westlake. An interactive walk-through ocean aquarium inside Grapevine Mills mall, with a tunnel tank, touch pools, and themed zones covering ocean ecosystems. Best for children ages 3–12 who love marine life. Plan for 60–90 minutes. Tickets run around $20–$25 per person; season passes available.

Legoland Discovery Center

Interactive Play  ·  15 min from Westlake, inside Grapevine Mills. Lego-themed indoor attraction with building zones, a 4D cinema, and a ride, designed specifically for children ages 3–10. A reliable full rainy-day or extreme-heat outing that burns energy and sparks creativity. Best visited on weekday mornings to avoid weekend crowds.

Regal Southlake Town Square

Movie Theater  ·  5 min from Westlake, inside Southlake Town Square. A full-service Regal multiplex with premium large-format screens and reserved seating. The walkable location inside Town Square means you can do dinner at one of the nearby restaurants before or after the movie without moving the car, which Westlake families appreciate on weekend evenings.

Day Trip from Westlake

Historic Downtown Grapevine & Lake Grapevine (15 min east). Grapevine's Main Street historic district has antique shops, local restaurants, the Grapevine Vintage Railroad, and seasonal events including the Christmas Capital of Texas programming in December. Pair it with a stop at Shirley Creek Marina on Lake Grapevine for kayak rentals and shoreline access. A genuinely complete family day without touching a highway.

Family-Friendly Dining

There is no grocery store, coffee shop, or restaurant cluster inside Westlake's city limits. The one sit-down option in town is the restaurant at the Marriott Solana hotel. Virtually all dining for Westlake families happens at Southlake Town Square, five minutes east on Hwy 114, where a Whole Foods anchors one of the most complete dining-and-shopping corridors in the Metroplex. Keller's dining corridor is ten minutes away for everyday alternatives. This is the honest reality of Westlake's food landscape, and most residents find they adapt quickly to keeping Southlake as their default dining stop.

Marriott Solana Bar & Grill Only In-Town Sit-Down Option The hotel restaurant at 1301 Solana Blvd, the only full-service sit-down dining inside Westlake's city limits. Serves breakfast, lunch, and dinner to hotel guests and local residents. Convenient for working parents who want a table close to home without driving to Southlake.
Whole Foods Market, Southlake 5 Min — Daily Groceries & Prepared Foods The anchor tenant of Southlake Town Square and the primary grocery for most Westlake families. The prepared foods section handles many quick weeknight dinners. A 5-minute drive that most Westlake families make multiple times a week.
Truluck's Seafood & Steak 5 Min — Best Date Night or Special Occasion Southlake Town Square's upscale seafood and steak anchor, known for stone crab claws and tableside service. The kind of dinner spot Westlake families use for celebrations and client dinners. Kids over 10 appreciate it; more of an adult-focused experience for younger children.
Whiskey Cake Kitchen & Bar 5 Min — Best Outdoor Patio with Kids A farm-to-table concept at Southlake Town Square with a large covered outdoor patio. More relaxed than Truluck's and very popular with families for weekend lunch and casual dinner. The menu has strong options for picky eaters and adventurous ones alike.
Jasper's Backyard Cuisine 5 Min — Best Weekend Brunch Outing Upscale American at Southlake Town Square, known for wood-smoked dishes and an inviting patio. A favorite for Westlake weekend brunch when families want something more than chain fare but less formal than a steakhouse. The outdoor setting makes it comfortable with children.
Southlake Town Square Dining (Full Corridor) 5 Min — Dozens of Options Beyond the anchors, Town Square has Japanese, Italian, Mexican, burger, sushi, pizza, and quick-casual concepts across the development. Most Westlake families rotate through 4–6 regular spots based on the night and who is eating. Check the Town Square website for the current tenant mix.
Keller Dining Corridor 10 Min — Everyday Alternative Keller's dining corridor along Keller Pkwy and the Keller Town Center mixed-use development offers everyday restaurants, casual chains, and some local independent spots. A practical alternative when Southlake Town Square is backed up on Friday nights or Saturday afternoons.
Historic Grapevine Main Street 15 Min — Best Weekend Outing Grapevine's historic Main Street has a genuine small-town character with local restaurants, craft beer, and wine tasting rooms alongside the Grapevine Vintage Railroad depot. A 15-minute drive that feels like a real destination rather than a shopping center.

Westlake has no internal restaurant district. Southlake Town Square (5 min east on Hwy 114) is the practical dining hub for all Westlake families. Keller (10 min) handles everyday errands and casual dining. Grapevine (15 min) is the destination when you want something with more character than a lifestyle center.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Westlake's commercial retail consists of one CVS pharmacy (recently opened at Entrada on Hwy 114) and a hotel. Everything else requires a short drive. Southlake Town Square at five minutes handles groceries, clothing, home goods, and dining in one trip. Grapevine Mills at fifteen minutes is the nearest major outlet and big-box destination.

Southlake Town Square

Lifestyle Center  ·  5 Min

130 acres, 130+ shops, Whole Foods, Apple, and a Regal theater alongside dozens of dining options. The walkable urban-grid layout means you can park once and spend the full day. This is Westlake's effective commercial town center and where most household shopping happens. An easy weekend outing for the whole family.

Best for: Full Saturday family outing — shopping, brunch, movie, and a walk through the square without moving the car.

Entrada at Westlake

Mixed-Use  ·  In Westlake

A Catalonian-village-inspired mixed-use development on Hwy 114 inside Westlake. CVS and a Primrose School (private childcare) are open; the Plaza Mayor central square and additional retail are under active development. When complete, Entrada is intended to serve as Westlake's first true walkable commercial district. Currently a CVS and construction site, with more to come.

Best for: Quick pharmacy run today; watch for restaurant and retail openings over the next 1–2 years.

Keller Town Center

Everyday Retail  ·  10 Min

Keller's mixed-use town center along Keller Pkwy with everyday retailers, casual dining, a gym, and services. Less expansive than Town Square but more practical for quick errands on the west side. Target, Home Depot, and big-box grocery are all within Keller's retail corridor.

Best for: Weeknight errands and everyday shopping without the Town Square crowds.

Grapevine Mills

Outlet Mall  ·  15 Min

A major enclosed mall and outlet destination in Grapevine with 180+ stores, SEA LIFE Aquarium, Legoland Discovery Center, and a full food court. Worth the 15-minute drive for back-to-school shopping, outlet pricing on brands, and entertainment in one trip. Easy DFW Airport access adjacent to the mall makes it a practical stop on travel days.

Best for: Full-day family shopping trip, or pairing retail with SEA LIFE or Legoland for a complete outing.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Westlake's own event calendar is appropriately small-town in scale, centered on the town government, Westlake Academy, and the Arbor Day tradition that earned the city its Tree City USA designation. Southlake's events calendar (5 min away) is one of the best in Northeast Tarrant County and functions as the extended community calendar most Westlake families follow.

Spring

Westlake Arbor Day Celebration

Westlake holds its annual Arbor Day tree-planting event as part of its ongoing Tree City USA designation from the Arbor Day Foundation. A community-scale gathering centered on environmental stewardship, typically held in early spring. Check westlaketx.gov for the current year's date.

Spring

Westlake Academy Annual Gala

The Westlake Academy Foundation's annual fundraising event, held annually since the Academy's founding. The gala is the school community's signature social event and raises funds for Academy programs, enrichment activities, and capital projects. One of the anchor events on the Academy social calendar each year.

Spring

Southlake Art in the Square

Held annually since 2000, Art in the Square is one of North Texas's largest outdoor arts festivals, taking place at Southlake Town Square 5 minutes from Westlake. Juried fine art, live music, local food vendors, and a children's area make it a full-day family event. Typically held the last weekend of April, with live entertainment programming (check the current year's lineup at southlake.gov).

Summer

Southlake Starlight Concert Series

Free outdoor summer concerts at Southlake Town Square, 5 minutes from Westlake, held annually each summer. Families bring blankets and lawn chairs to Southlake Town Square's open-air lawn. A relaxed, community-oriented event with live entertainment (check southlake.gov for this year's lineup and schedule).

Fall

Westlake Academy IB Events

Westlake Academy's fall athletics season, performing arts productions, and IB academic events run from September through November. For families inside the Academy community, the school's calendar functions as the primary social and events calendar, with competitions, recitals, and community nights drawing the full parent community.

December

Grapevine Christmas Capital Events

Grapevine's "Christmas Capital of Texas" programming (15 min from Westlake) includes the North Pole Express train ride, the North Pole Village at the Gaylord Texan, and Historic Downtown Grapevine holiday activities running throughout December. One of the most elaborate holiday event calendars in the Metroplex and a Westlake family staple each year.

For Westlake town events, check westlaketx.gov. For the broader community calendar Westlake families rely on, see southlake.gov/events.

Indoor Activities for Kids

Westlake has no public recreation center or indoor pool of its own. Indoor options for families run through the Southlake YMCA five minutes away, Carroll Senior High's aquatic center seven minutes away, and a set of attraction-grade indoor venues in Grapevine fifteen minutes out. When North Texas summers push past 100 degrees in July and August, Westlake families rotate between these options as their primary indoor circuit.

Southlake YMCA

5 min from Westlake. The primary indoor recreation hub for Westlake families. Indoor and outdoor pools, group fitness studios, youth sports leagues, swim lessons, summer camps, and after-school programming. Family membership approximately $80–$100/month. Available to Westlake residents without restriction. This is the closest thing Westlake has to a community recreation center.

One of the strongest high school aquatic facilities in North Texas, with 50-meter competition pool and dive well.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When July brings 100+°F temperatures to North Texas, Westlake families rotate through a short circuit: the Southlake YMCA pool (5 minutes, fastest option for afternoon swim sessions), SEA LIFE or Legoland at Grapevine Mills (15 minutes, full-day indoor outing that genuinely wears kids out), and Regal Southlake for a midday movie when everyone just needs air conditioning and popcorn. Keller Pointe's lap pool is worth the 10-minute drive on weekday mornings when the YMCA is crowded with summer camp groups.

Westlake, TX Family Life: Frequently Asked Questions

Real questions from families considering Westlake, answered honestly.

  • Yes, for the right family. Westlake is genuinely one of the most unusual suburbs in DFW: a small enclave of roughly 1,500 people with three major corporate campuses, one of the only public K–12 IB schools in Texas, and neighborhood communities with estate-level character. It is not a good fit for families who want walkable dining, a city-run park system, youth recreation leagues, or a neighborhood feel in the traditional suburban sense. It is an excellent fit for families who prioritize school access above almost everything else, value privacy and low-density living, and are comfortable using Southlake and Keller as their practical town centers for shopping, restaurants, and organized activities.

  • Westlake is served by three ISDs depending on the specific neighborhood, plus Westlake Academy as a standalone charter option. Eastern Westlake neighborhoods (Glenwyck Farms, Terra Bella, Paigebrooke, Aspen Lane) feed into Carroll ISD, which is consistently ranked the top school district in Texas by TEA. Western Westlake and the Vaquero neighborhood feed into Keller ISD. A small northern portion feeds into Northwest ISD. Westlake Academy is the town's own public K–12 International Baccalaureate charter school, free to Westlake residents, with enrollment by lottery. It earns a 10/10 on GreatSchools, an A+ on Niche, and an average ACT score of 30. For most families who move to Westlake specifically for schools, Carroll ISD and Westlake Academy are the two options they research most carefully.

  • Westlake has no city-run recreation department or youth sports leagues of its own. Sports access for Westlake children runs through school athletics (Westlake Academy grades 6–12, or Carroll ISD / Keller ISD depending on neighborhood), and through adjacent cities. The Southlake YMCA (5 min, approximately $80–$100/month family membership) is the primary hub most Westlake families use for organized youth programming including youth swim, youth leagues, and after-school sports. Keller Soccer Association (10 min) handles youth soccer for the Vaquero-area families. The Vaquero Club offers private equestrian programming for members. For competitive youth athletics at the school level, Carroll ISD's program is one of the most competitive in Texas.

  • Westlake has no public parks department and no city-owned parks. What it has are private neighborhood preserves within gated communities (Terra Bella's 22.6-acre nature preserve with hike-and-bike trails, Glenwyck Farms' wooded running trail and natural ponds), a 2,500-acre open ranch landscape (Circle T Ranch, not public access), and immediate access to excellent public parks in adjacent cities. Bob Jones Nature Center in Southlake (738 acres, 4+ miles of trails, free, 5 min) is where most Westlake families go for serious outdoor recreation. Bear Creek Park in Keller (splash pad, athletic fields, playgrounds, 8 min) is the nearest park with a splash pad for young children. Southlake Bicentennial Park (farmers market, playgrounds, 5 min) is a good quick-outing option.

  • Southlake has significantly more family infrastructure: a full public parks system, city-run youth athletics, the Southlake YMCA, a robust restaurant and retail district, and an event calendar that runs most weekends of the year. Southlake's Bob Jones Nature Center, Bicentennial Park, Koalaty Park, and dozens of other parks give families genuine park access without a membership or a drive to another city. Westlake has none of that infrastructure internally but has Southlake as a neighbor, so most Westlake families access Southlake's amenities as their practical backyard. The meaningful differences are in school options (Westlake Academy is uniquely compelling for IB-focused families), home price points (Westlake homes start higher and scale to $8M+ in Vaquero), and community scale (Westlake is 1,500 people vs. Southlake's 35,000+). Families who want to be inside a complete, internally serviced suburb will prefer Southlake. Families who want the education access of Westlake Academy or the estate character of Vaquero or Terra Bella choose Westlake specifically.

  • Westlake is known for three things that are unusual in any DFW suburb. First, it is home to the headquarters or major campuses of Charles Schwab, Fidelity Investments, and Deloitte University, three of the largest corporate facilities in North Texas, inside a town of 1,500 people. Second, it is home to Westlake Academy, one of the only public K–12 International Baccalaureate schools in Texas, free and open to residents. Third, it is home to Vaquero, one of the most exclusive private club communities in the country, with a Tom Fazio-designed golf course and equestrian facilities. It is a genuinely unusual place in DFW: enormous in its economic footprint, tiny in its residential scale, and defined by a combination of world-class schools, extreme privacy, and open ranch character that you cannot find in any other suburb in the Metroplex.
