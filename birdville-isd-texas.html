Birdville ISD at a Glance

Birdville Independent School District serves the northeast corner of Tarrant County: primarily Haltom City, Richland Hills, Watauga, and large portions of North Richland Hills. The district operates 30 campuses for roughly 22,000 students and covers communities that sit between the Mid-Cities and downtown Fort Worth, giving families access to DFW employment hubs without the premium of Keller or Southlake.

Birdville ISD earned a B rating (83/100) from the Texas Education Agency in 2024. The district is not among the most-searched options for relocating families in DFW, but its fine arts programs, particularly Birdville High School's theater and music departments, have earned national recognition over decades of competition. Three full-size comprehensive high schools and an Early College High School on the Tarrant County College campus round out a district that offers more academic pathway variety than its size and reputation might suggest.

Who This District Is Best For

Best fit for families who:

  • Are buying in Haltom City, Richland Hills, or Watauga where the housing cost-to-amenity ratio is strong
  • Have a student interested in fine arts, particularly theater, vocal music, or band
  • Want an Early College pathway where a student can earn an associate's degree by graduation
  • Are looking for a CTE program in a mid-size district with vocational and technical pathways
  • Are prioritizing affordability in northeast Tarrant County and can verify their address falls in Birdville (not HEB)

May not be the best fit if:

  • STAAR proficiency rates are the primary screening criterion (Birdville's scores are below the state average in math)
  • You're buying in North Richland Hills and aren't sure which district you'd land in (HEB ISD may be the better fit for academic-priority families in NRH)
  • The district's enrollment decline since 2016 is a concern for long-term facilities investment

TEA Accountability Rating

The Texas Education Agency assigns annual A-F ratings across three domains. Birdville ISD scored 83/100 (B) in 2024.

DomainScoreWhat it measures
Student Achievement84/100STAAR performance, graduation rate, college/career readiness
School Progress81/100Year-over-year growth and relative performance vs. similar districts
Closing the Gaps79/100Equity across student subgroups (ELL, economically disadvantaged, SpEd)

An 83 puts Birdville ISD solidly in the B range, though on the lower end compared to nearby HEB ISD (88) and EMS ISD (87). The Closing the Gaps domain reflects the district's higher share of economically disadvantaged students. Individual campus ratings vary, with some campuses earning higher marks than the district average. Check campus-level accountability at txschools.gov before zeroing in on a specific neighborhood.

Niche.com District Grade (Third-Party)

Niche rates Birdville ISD a B+ overall, placing it among middle-tier Tarrant County districts. Niche's methodology combines parent surveys, test scores, graduation data, and demographic information. A B+ from Niche alongside a B (83) from TEA tells a reasonably consistent story: a serviceable district without the premium profile of Keller or Carroll ISD.

For context, many high-performing DFW districts post math proficiency rates in the 55-65% range. The 46% figure reflects a student population where 62% qualify for free or reduced-price lunch, a demographic factor correlated with test score gaps in districts statewide.

Graduation & College Readiness

The 4-year graduation rate is 92%, a meaningfully strong outcome for a district serving as many economically disadvantaged students as Birdville does. College readiness rates (students meeting TSI benchmarks or completing a college-prep sequence) are available by campus on the TEA website and vary by high school.

Advanced Coursework

Advanced Placement courses are available at all three comprehensive high schools. The Collegiate Academy of Birdville is the most selective academic pathway in the district, admitting students from across Birdville ISD to pursue an associate's degree while completing high school requirements on the Tarrant County College Northeast campus.

Programs & Special Offerings

Fine Arts

Birdville ISD's strongest differentiator. Birdville High School in particular has built a decades-long record in UIL theater and vocal music competitions. The district has produced state championship and nationally recognized performing arts programs, an unusual achievement for a B-rated mid-size suburban district. Families with a student passionate about theater, choir, or band have a genuine reason to consider Birdville HS specifically, separate from the district's overall academic profile.

Collegiate Academy of Birdville (Early College)

The Collegiate Academy operates on the Tarrant County College Northeast campus and accepts students from across Birdville ISD by application. Students take dual credit coursework at TCC beginning in grade 9 and can graduate with both a high school diploma and an associate's degree. Admission is competitive. This program is one of the district's most compelling offerings for college-prep families and rivals programs at higher-rated districts in the region.

Career & Technical Education (CTE)

CTE pathways are available across the three comprehensive high schools in areas including health science, information technology, automotive, and business. Unlike EMS ISD's dedicated Hollenstein campus, Birdville's CTE programs are integrated into each high school rather than centralized at one facility.

Dual Credit

Beyond the Collegiate Academy, standard dual credit coursework through Tarrant County College is available to qualifying students at all three comprehensive high schools. Students must meet TSI assessment benchmarks to enroll in college-level courses.

Advanced Placement

AP courses are offered at Birdville, Haltom, and Richland high schools across core academic subjects. Course availability varies by campus. Review the AP course catalog for your assigned high school directly with the school counselor after move-in.

Gifted & Talented

Birdville ISD operates a district-wide Gifted and Talented program beginning at the elementary level. GT identification involves a formal screening process. Families relocating with a student who holds GT identification in another state should contact the district's Gifted and Talented office before enrolling to discuss the reassessment process.

Special Education

Birdville ISD provides a full continuum of special education services. Families transferring with an existing IEP should contact the receiving campus special education coordinator before enrolling. Texas law requires an ARD meeting within 30 days of enrollment to review, adopt, or revise the existing plan.

Approximately 62% of students qualify for free or reduced-price lunch, reflecting the socioeconomic profile of the communities Birdville ISD serves. English Language Learners represent a significant portion of enrollment, particularly at campuses in Haltom City. These factors inform the district's TEA domain scores and the context for interpreting STAAR proficiency rates.

District enrollment has declined roughly 9% since 2016. This is not unique to Birdville, enrollment contraction is common in older inner-ring suburban districts as families shift toward fast-growth outer suburbs. The practical implication for relocating families: facilities and program investment may follow growth areas more aggressively than stable or declining enrollment zones.

Enrollment Process

Birdville ISD enrolls through the district's online portal at birdvilleschools.net. Required documents include:

  • Two proofs of residency (lease, closing disclosure, or utility bill in your name)
  • Birth certificate
  • Texas-compliant immunization records
  • Most recent school records or transcripts
  • Any existing IEP or 504 documentation

Mid-year enrollment is accepted at any time. Contact the district at (817) 547-5700 for campus-specific enrollment assistance.

North Richland Hills is a split district. Portions of NRH fall in Birdville ISD and others in HEB ISD. The two districts have different TEA scores, Niche grades, and tax rates. HEB ISD's 2024-25 tax rate is $0.97 per $100 assessed value, significantly lower than Birdville's $1.20. This is one of the more consequential address-verification situations in the Dallas-Fort Worth metro. Run your specific address through the Birdville district locator at birdvilleschools.net before purchasing any home in North Richland Hills.

Birdville ISD vs. HEB ISD

This is the most common comparison for families buying in North Richland Hills or the Mid-Cities area. HEB ISD earns an A+ from Niche (vs. Birdville's B+), posts higher STAAR proficiency rates, and carries a notably lower tax rate ($0.97 vs. $1.20). Birdville's case over HEB: the Collegiate Academy early college program, Birdville HS's fine arts reputation, and housing prices that can be slightly lower in Haltom City vs. the HEB district's Bedford or Euless. For families prioritizing academic ratings, HEB ISD's NRH addresses tend to be the stronger choice when available.

Birdville ISD vs. Keller ISD

Keller ISD covers the wealthier northwest Tarrant corridor. Homes in Keller ISD typically cost more than comparable square footage in Birdville ISD communities. Keller earns an A from Niche and a slightly higher TEA score with a lower tax rate ($1.09). Families who can stretch their budget into Keller ISD and prioritize academic ratings usually prefer it. Birdville ISD's advantage: more affordable entry points and a genuine fine arts differentiator for the right student.

Living in the Birdville ISD Community

Birdville ISD's footprint covers some of the most accessible real estate in northeast Tarrant County. Haltom City in particular offers housing well below the DFW median, with reasonable commutes to the Mid-Cities employment corridor, Alliance Airport, downtown Fort Worth, and even north Dallas via SH-121. Families who want a single-family home in a walkable, established neighborhood without committing to a 30-minute drive from a master-planned suburb frequently end up looking at Haltom City or Richland Hills.

Home Price Context

Haltom City and Richland Hills typically range from the low-to-mid $200s for older single-family homes up to the low $400s for updated properties. Watauga and the Birdville-served portions of North Richland Hills tend to price slightly higher. These figures represent meaningful savings compared to Keller ISD or HEB ISD communities where comparable homes often run $50,000-$100,000 more.

Community Character

These are established suburban communities with mature trees, older neighborhoods, and a long history in the DFW metro. They lack the master-planned feel of Frisco or Prosper but offer a different kind of value: less distance to the urban core, more diverse local business districts, and housing stock that was built before price points became stratospheric.
