Things to Do in Frisco, TX with Kids

From Dallas Cowboys training camps to 130+ miles of trails and a full-size T-Rex in the library, Frisco has built the kind of family infrastructure that makes "What should we do this weekend?" the easiest question in town.

What Frisco Families Actually Do Here

Frisco isn't just one of DFW's fastest-growing cities — it's been deliberately engineered for active family life. Men's Journal once named it the "Best Place to Raise an Athlete," and that reputation has only grown. Whether your kids are into baseball, soccer, hiking, gaming, or just catching a live game, Frisco delivers at a scale most suburbs never reach. See the full Frisco relocation guide →

Sports City USA

The Star (Cowboys HQ), Toyota Stadium (FC Dallas), Riders Field (RoughRiders baseball), and Comerica Center (Dallas Stars practice) — Frisco packs more pro sports infrastructure into one suburb than most entire cities have.

Parks Built for Families

60+ parks, splash pads at multiple locations, and a connected trail network running 130+ miles — Frisco's parks infrastructure is designed for families who want to spend time outside every single weekend.

Year-Round Events

From the Dallas Cup (the world's largest international youth soccer tournament) to Christmas in the Square (North Texas' biggest choreographed light show), Frisco's community calendar is one of the most packed in DFW.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Frisco manages 60+ parks totaling over 2,700 acres of parkland, with more than 130 miles of connected hike and bike trails winding through the city. Whether you need a splash pad on a Tuesday or a full trail run with the stroller on Saturday morning, the park system has you covered.

Frisco Commons Park

The heart of Frisco's outdoor scene — a large multi-use park with a splash pad, playground, amphitheater, fishing pond, covered pavilions, and direct trail connections. The splash pad is a summer staple for young families.

Splash Pad Playground Trails Fishing

Warren Sports Complex

Frisco's primary multi-field athletic complex, used heavily by youth soccer, baseball, and softball leagues. Large playground, concession facilities, and ample parking make it a weekend fixture for sports families.

Sports Fields Playground Picnic Areas

Lone Star Ranch Park

A neighborhood park anchoring one of Frisco's most established communities. Excellent playground equipment, walking trails, and open green space make it a daily destination for residents in the area.

Playground Walking Trails Open Green Space

Shawnee Trail Park

A natural corridor park that follows the historic Shawnee Trail route through Frisco. Wooded stretches, creek crossings, and wildlife sightings make it feel like a nature escape without leaving the suburbs.

Hike & Bike Nature Trail Wildlife Viewing

Sunset Valley Sports Complex

One of Frisco's dedicated youth sports complexes with multiple athletic fields, a playground, and open areas for informal play. Used year-round by Frisco ISD and recreational leagues.

Sports Fields Playground Restrooms

Frontier Park

Located near The Star development, Frontier Park serves the western side of Frisco with sports courts, a shaded playground, and trail connections. Popular with families in the Stonebrook and Starwood communities.

Sports Courts Playground Trails

Panther Creek Natural Area

A less-manicured stretch of natural land along Panther Creek, great for families who want a bit more wilderness in their walks. Native plants, creek access, and quiet trails attract birders and nature-loving kids alike.

Natural Area Creek Access Birding

Frisco Athletic Center (Outdoor)

The FAC's outdoor aquatics complex spans 40,000 sq ft with a competition pool, leisure pools, waterslides, and splash zones. Paired with the indoor facility, it's the city's premier aquatics destination for all ages.

Aquatics Waterslides All Ages

Trail System Highlight

Cottonwood Creek Trail Network — The spine of Frisco's trail system, connecting parks from the southern city limits north through Frisco Commons and beyond, with 130+ miles of connected paths citywide. Perfect for stroller walks, family bike rides, and morning runs. The trail regularly earns Tree City USA designation for its tree canopy coverage.

Relocation Tip

Start at Frisco Commons Park — it's the most visited park in the city and gives you a great first read on the community. Go on a weekend morning and you'll meet half your future neighborhood. The splash pad runs May through September and is free with park entry.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

Men's Journal called Frisco the "Best Place to Raise an Athlete" for good reason. The city is home to FC Dallas, the Frisco RoughRiders (Double-A MLB), and the Dallas Cowboys' headquarters — and youth sports programs here operate at a matching level of organization and investment. If your kids are serious about a sport, Frisco will find them a team.

Soccer

League: Frisco Youth Soccer Association (FYSA) / FC Dallas Academy
Ages: 4–18
Season: Fall & Spring
Home of the Dallas Cup, the world's largest international youth soccer tournament. FC Dallas Academy operates elite development pathways for competitive players.

Baseball & Softball

League: Frisco Baseball & Softball Association (FBSA)
Ages: 4–18
Season: Spring (Feb–May) & Fall (Aug–Oct)
Established 1984 — one of the most organized youth baseball programs in Collin County, with divisions from T-ball through travel ball.

Football

League: Frisco Football League (tackle) / Flag Football for Fun (FF4FUN)
Ages: 5–14
Season: Fall (Aug–Nov)
Recreational tackle football for younger kids who want to play before entering Frisco ISD's middle school program. Flag football option for ages 5–8.

Swimming

League: Frisco Athletic Center Swim Programs / Storm Swim Club
Ages: 5–18
Season: Year-round
Frisco has an Olympic-sized natatorium and 18,000 sqft indoor aquatics center at the FAC. Competitive swimming pathways through Frisco ISD and private club programs.

Basketball

League: City of Frisco Recreation Basketball / Frisco ISD
Ages: 6–18
Season: Winter (Nov–Feb)
Recreational leagues through City of Frisco Parks & Recreation, plus YMCA programs and private training academies near Legacy Drive.

Lacrosse

League: Frisco Junior Lacrosse / DFW Lacrosse Club
Ages: 6–18
Season: Spring (Feb–May)
Growing fast in Frisco, driven by the city's sports-forward culture. The Ford Center at The Star has hosted lacrosse events, raising the sport's profile locally.

Tennis

League: City of Frisco Tennis Programs / USTA Junior
Ages: 5–18
Season: Year-round (outdoor spring–fall, indoor year-round)
Multiple courts at city parks plus private tennis clubs in the Frisco/Prosper area. USTA junior development programs run through local clubs.

Golf

League: PGA Junior Golf / Frisco ISD Golf
Ages: 7–18
Season: Year-round
Frisco is now home to PGA of America headquarters and two championship courses at Omni PGA Frisco Resort. Junior golf programs and a short course make it accessible for younger players getting started.

Featured Sports Complex

The Star in Frisco — The Dallas Cowboys' 91-acre world headquarters includes the 12,000-seat Ford Center, practice fields, a state-of-the-art athletic training facility, and a retail/dining district. Frisco ISD high school teams practice and play games here on a rotating basis — there's a reasonable chance your child's football team plays in the same building where the Cowboys train.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Frisco punches well above its weight for a suburb when it comes to family-oriented attractions. Professional sports venues, a world-class gaming museum, and a library with a T-Rex skeleton give you plenty of "let's do something" options without driving into Dallas.

Riders Field

Museum · Minor League Baseball · Baseball fans of all ages love a Frisco RoughRiders game at the 10,316-seat Riders Field. The Double-A affiliate of the Texas Rangers plays here spring through early fall. Friday night fireworks, themed events, and affordable tickets make it a go-to family night out. Named the best new ballpark in the country when it opened.

National Videogame Museum

Museum · Interactive · Located at the Discovery Center near Frisco Square, the National Videogame Museum traces the history of gaming from Pong to PlayStation with hands-on exhibits, playable consoles from every era, and rotating special events. A full-afternoon destination for kids and the adults who grew up with them.

Toyota Stadium & National Soccer Hall of Fame

Sports Venue · Museum · The 20,500-seat stadium is home to FC Dallas and co-located with the National Soccer Hall of Fame. Beyond MLS matches, the venue hosts concerts, college championships (Frisco Bowl), and the annual Dallas Cup. Tour options give soccer fans a behind-the-scenes look.

The Star in Frisco

Sports Complex · Dining · Retail · The Dallas Cowboys' 91-acre headquarters is open to the public. Walk the concourse, visit the Cowboys exhibit at the Ford Center, dine at one of the waterfront restaurants, and watch Frisco ISD teams practice on the same fields as NFL players. Kids who love football won't want to leave.

Frisco Public Library

Library · Family Programs · Opened in 2023 in a converted warehouse building, this is the sixth-largest public library in Texas. The anchor attraction is Rexy — a full-size Tyrannosaurus Rex skeleton on display in the main hall. Robust children's programming, maker spaces, and teen activities run year-round.

Museum of the American Railroad

Museum · Heritage · Based at the Frisco Heritage Museum while a new permanent facility is under construction. Features historic locomotives, railcars, and exhibits on the railroad history that literally put Frisco on the map. A great short-visit option for kids who love trains.

Coming Soon — Universal Kids Resort

Universal Kids Resort Frisco — Universal Destinations & Experiences announced a new family-focused theme park concept to be built in Frisco. Designed specifically for families with young children, this will be a major addition to the city's entertainment landscape. Check universalparks.com for the latest updates on the opening timeline.

Family-Friendly Dining

Frisco's dining scene has grown alongside its population — there's no shortage of options that work for picky eaters and food-obsessed parents alike. The heaviest concentration is along the Dallas North Tollway corridor and Legacy Drive, with The Star development adding a waterfront dining cluster near the Cowboys facility.

Grimaldi's Pizzeria — Coal-fired New York-style pizza with a crispy, charred crust that's become a Frisco staple. Large booths and a welcoming vibe for families. At Stonebriar Centre.
Mi Cocina — The DFW Tex-Mex institution with authentic fajitas, frozen Mambo Taxi margaritas (for adults), and a kids' menu that actually satisfies. Always a good call with children in tow.
Torchy's Tacos — Creative tacos with bold flavors, an energetic vibe, and kids who end up more adventurous eaters than you expected. Multiple Frisco locations make this a regular-rotation dinner spot.
First Watch — The breakfast and brunch chain done right — fresh-squeezed juices, farm-to-table ingredients, and an attentive kids' menu. Several Frisco locations with family-friendly wait times on weekends.
Pinstack at The Star — Bowling, laser tag, and arcade games paired with full bar and kitchen service. A great rainy-day option where parents can actually relax while kids burn energy. Order a pizza and stay awhile.
BJ's Restaurant & Brewhouse — Reliable, spacious, and great for groups. The deep-dish pizookie (pizza-cookie dessert) is a Frisco family tradition. Near Stonebriar Centre.
Pappadeaux Seafood Kitchen — Fresh Cajun seafood in a lively atmosphere. Kids who love shrimp and crawfish are in heaven. Along the DNT corridor — consistently one of the top-rated seafood options in the area.
Kona Grill — Contemporary American with Asian fusions, sushi bar, and a bar program parents appreciate. Generous happy hour and a surprisingly solid kids' menu at Stonebriar.
Velvet Taco — Globally inspired tacos in a cool, casual setting. Kids love the novelty flavors; parents love the cocktails. Reliably packed on weekend evenings in the Legacy corridor.
Yard House — American classics, an enormous menu, and a layout that handles large family groups well. Near the Stonebriar Centre area — great post-game or post-shopping dinner.

Most of these are clustered along the Dallas North Tollway (Legacy Drive to Warren Parkway), with a growing dining cluster at The Star on Gaylord Parkway. Preston Road has solid everyday-dining options throughout the city.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Frisco is one of the better-served suburbs in DFW for retail. You have a full regional mall, several lifestyle centers, a massive IKEA, and The Star's waterfront retail district — plus the Legacy West development just over the Plano border for everything else.

Stonebriar Centre

Regional Mall

One of DFW's premier enclosed malls with 165+ stores including Nordstrom, Macy's, and specialty retailers. Strong food court options, an on-site ice skating rink (seasonal), and a movie theater make it a true full-day destination. For a full family shopping outing, this is your anchor.

The Star at Frisco

Lifestyle Center

The Cowboys headquarters development has evolved into a retail and dining hub with waterfront patios, specialty boutiques, and a growing restaurant scene. Less about shopping, more about the experience — grab dinner, let the kids explore, and watch the sunset from the event lawn.

IKEA Frisco

Big Box

One of the largest IKEA stores in the US at 310,000 sqft. For newly relocated families furnishing a new home, this is a multiple-trip destination. The children's play area (Småland) and the famous meatballs in the restaurant make it oddly kid-friendly for a furniture store.

Frisco Square

Mixed-Use Downtown

Frisco's planned downtown core — Cinemark theater, restaurants, boutique retail, and the city hall. Home to Arts in the Square and Christmas in the Square events. Good for a quick evening walk, dinner, and a movie with the family.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Frisco's event calendar reflects its identity: big, well-organized, and sports-forward. These are the events Frisco families plan their calendars around.

April

Dallas Cup at Toyota Stadium

The world's largest and most prestigious international youth soccer tournament — teams from 60+ countries compete at Toyota Stadium and surrounding venues. Even if your child doesn't play, watching the caliber of soccer is extraordinary.

April–May

Arts in the Square

Frisco Square's annual outdoor arts festival brings 200+ fine art and craft vendors to the city's downtown plaza. Live music, food vendors, children's activities, and a juried art show. Free to attend; a beloved Frisco spring tradition.

June–August

Frisco RoughRiders Baseball Season

Friday Fireworks Nights at Riders Field are a Frisco summer ritual. Minor league baseball at its most affordable and fun — $10 tickets, themed giveaway nights, in-game contests, and post-game fireworks the kids will talk about all week.

June–August

Music in the Square

Free outdoor concerts at Frisco Square, held on summer evenings. Bring a blanket, grab food from the nearby restaurants, and let the kids dance on the lawn. One of those community events that makes you feel instantly at home in Frisco.

July 4th

Frisco Freedom Fest

One of the largest July 4th celebrations in DFW — fireworks, live music, food, and family activities centered around Toyota Stadium. Draws tens of thousands of residents and is a defining Frisco community moment. Arrive early to secure your spot.

November–January

Christmas in the Square

The largest choreographed holiday lights and music show in North Texas, filling Frisco Square with synchronized displays timed to carols. A nightly show that draws families from across DFW. Free, festive, and genuinely magical for young kids.

Year-Round

FC Dallas at Toyota Stadium

MLS regular season runs March through November. FC Dallas home games at Toyota Stadium offer a world-class soccer experience at accessible price points. Family sections, tailgating areas, and Frisco's soccer culture make this a great intro to live professional sports for kids.

Spring–Fall

Frisco Fresh Market

The city's weekly farmers market — local produce, artisan foods, live music, and vendors along the DNT corridor. A Saturday morning tradition for Frisco families who want fresh ingredients and a community gathering point outside the typical big-box experience.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When July hits 100°F and nobody wants to be outside, Frisco delivers. From the national-caliber library to trampoline parks to pro hockey games, the indoor options are genuinely excellent for a suburb its size.

Frisco Public Library

Type: Library + Learning · Ages: All
The sixth-largest public library in Texas — housed in a converted warehouse with Rexy the T-Rex in the main hall. Weekly storytime, STEM programs, teen maker space, summer reading challenges, and an extensive children's wing. Free.

Frisco Athletic Center (Indoor)

Type: Rec Center + Aquatics · Ages: All
18,000 sqft indoor aquatics with lap pools, a leisure pool, and fitness classes. Plus an Olympic-sized natatorium, basketball courts, and group fitness. The definitive year-round rec facility in Frisco.

National Videogame Museum

Type: Museum + Interactive · Ages: 5–adult
Hands-on exhibits covering gaming history from the 1970s to today, with playable consoles from every generation. A multi-hour visit for any family with kids who play games — which is basically every family in Frisco.

Pinstack at The Star

Type: Entertainment Venue · Ages: 4–adult
Bowling, laser tag, arcade games, virtual reality, and a full restaurant and bar — all under one roof at The Star. The rare entertainment venue where parents genuinely enjoy themselves too. Great for birthday parties and rainy evenings.

Main Event Frisco

Type: Entertainment Center · Ages: 6–adult
Bowling, billiards, laser tag, climbing, arcade, and restaurant. Larger than a typical arcade and better for group outings. Popular for post-school Friday afternoons and weekend birthday party packages.

Altitude Trampoline Park

Type: Trampoline Park · Ages: 3–adult
Wall-to-wall trampolines, foam pits, dodgeball courts, and a toddler zone for younger kids. A 90-minute session burns enough energy to guarantee a solid bedtime. Multiple Frisco-area locations.

Comerica Center Events

Type: Sports Arena · Ages: All
Home of the Texas Legends (NBA G League) and Frisco Fighters (Indoor Football League), plus a Dallas Stars practice facility. Pro basketball and football games at suburb ticket prices — a fantastic intro to live arena sports for kids.

Cinemark Frisco Square

Type: Movie Theater · Ages: All
A well-maintained 16-screen theater at Frisco Square with reserved recliner seating and a full concessions menu. Kids' Matinee programs on weekend mornings offer discounted tickets during summer and school breaks.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When the July heat index cracks 108°F, Frisco families default to the Frisco Athletic Center for morning swim sessions, the Frisco Public Library for afternoon reading programs, and Pinstack or Main Event for evening fun. The FAC's outdoor splash pad is also open until 9 PM on summer evenings — locals know it's actually more pleasant at 7 PM than at noon.
