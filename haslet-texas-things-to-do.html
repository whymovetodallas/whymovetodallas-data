What Haslet Families Actually Do Here

Haslet is a smaller, newer suburb in the fast-growing Alliance corridor of northwest Fort Worth, the kind of place families pick for bigger lots, lower density, and Northwest ISD schools. Day-to-day life splits between in-town basics like Haslet Community Park and the Public Library, and the Alliance Town Center strip just minutes south, which carries most of the area's dining, shopping, and rainy-day entertainment. If you know the master-planned growth corridors of Phoenix or Austin, Haslet will feel familiar. See the full Haslet relocation guide →

Alliance Town Center

The Alliance corridor's anchor: dozens of restaurants, a big-box and boutique shopping mix, a Cinemark theater, and entertainment, all a few minutes from home.

Room to Spread Out

Acreage lots and low-density, master-planned neighborhoods like Sendera Ranch, plus Haslet Community Park's disc golf, sand volleyball, and walking trails.

Northwest ISD & Sports

Haslet is served primarily by Northwest ISD (Eaton High feeder) and backed by a deep bench of youth leagues for baseball, football, soccer, and more.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

Haslet keeps its public parks simple but useful: two connected city parks at the heart of town, a baseball complex, and a growing trail network. Master-planned communities add their own pools and amenity centers on top.

Haslet Community Park

The town's main park at 301 1st Street South: an 18-hole disc golf course, sand volleyball, a basketball court, a large covered pavilion, swings, and play structures. Restrooms on site; open daily 6 a.m. to 10:30 p.m.; best for active grade-schoolers.

Playground Disc Golf Sand Volleyball Trails

John Gammill Park

Connected to Community Park at 101 Gammill Street, next to the Public Library. Features a covered pavilion, a fenced all-abilities play area, picnic tables, restrooms, and shade trees. The all-abilities, fenced playground makes it a top pick for toddlers and kids with special needs.

All-Abilities Play Fenced Shade Picnic

Nance Field

The town's baseball home on Schoolhouse Road, with additional fields behind the Haslet Fire Department. This is where the local youth baseball and softball seasons play out. Best for game days; bring shade and water in summer.

Baseball Softball

Sendera Ranch Amenity Centers

One of the largest master-planned communities in the area, shared between Haslet and north Fort Worth, with resident pools and a spray park for little ones. A reminder that in Haslet, a lot of the water and play amenities live inside the neighborhoods themselves.

Resident Pools Spray Park

Wellington Community Green Space

A 615-acre master-planned community north of Fort Worth with lakes, acres of open green space, walking trails, and an amenity center built around a large pool with its own children's area. Another example of Haslet's parkland living inside the neighborhoods.

Lakes Walking Trails Amenity Pool

LeTara Community Green Space

A newer Northwest ISD master-planned community with a pool, walking trails dotted with fitness stations, pocket parks, and an on-site elementary school. A short drive from Alliance Town Center, it bundles play space into the neighborhood itself.

Pocket Parks Fitness Trails Pool

Trail System Highlight

The TAP Trail, a 1.40-mile path funded through the Transportation Alternatives Set-Aside Program, connects into Haslet Community Park's own Lower (0.50 mile) and Upper (0.25 mile) loops. Great for stroller walks, beginner bike rides, and an after-dinner lap close to home.

Relocation Tip

Start at the connected Gammill and Community Park complex on 1st Street. You can let toddlers loose in the fenced all-abilities play area at Gammill, then walk the trail over to the disc golf and pavilion at Community Park. It's the quickest way to get the feel of in-town Haslet. For the full neighborhood breakdown, see the Haslet relocation guide.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

For a town its size, Haslet has an unusually deep set of youth sports options, largely because it sits inside the Northwest ISD and Eaton High feeder area. Several volunteer associations and recreational providers cover everything from t-ball to tackle football.

Baseball & Softball

League: Haslet Sports Association
Ages: Roughly 4–15
Season: Spring and fall, played at Nance Field

Tackle Football & Cheer

League: Northwest Youth Sports Association (NYSA)
Ages: Kindergarten through 6th grade
Season: Fall tackle, flag, and cheer; spring 7-on-7

Multi-Sport (Recreational)

Provider: N Zone Sports of North Fort Worth
Ages: 3–14
Sports: Soccer, flag football, basketball, t-ball, and cheer

Youth Athletics (NISD Community)

League: Eaton Youth Sports Association
Ages: Elementary and up
Where: 1350 Eagle Blvd, serving Haslet and the Northwest ISD community

Swimming

Where: Northwest ISD Aquatic Center (indoor); Northwest YMCA of Fort Worth; Sendera Ranch resident pools
Ages: Lessons from toddler up
Season: Year-round indoors

School Sports (Northwest ISD)

Program: Northwest ISD athletics, Eaton High and feeder schools
Ages: Middle and high school
Sports: Football, soccer, baseball, softball, volleyball, track, swimming, and more

Good to Know

Northwest ISD Aquatic Center gives Haslet families a true indoor pool for swim lessons and team practice year-round, a real advantage in a smaller town. Between the school district, the YMCA, and the volunteer associations, most kids can find a league within a short drive.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Most of Haslet's entertainment lives a short drive south in the Alliance corridor, one of the densest clusters of family attractions in north Tarrant County.

Alliance Town Center

Open-air center · Dozens of restaurants and shops plus services and entertainment, the everyday hub for Haslet families, about 10 min south.

Cinemark Alliance Town Center and XD

Movie theater · Recliner seating, XD, and ScreenX at 9228 Sage Meadow Trail, the go-to family movie stop in the corridor.

Main Event

Family fun center · Bowling, an arcade, laser tag, and pool under one roof in north Fort Worth, an easy birthday-party and rainy-day default.

Urban Air Adventure Park

Trampoline park · Trampolines, a ropes course, climbing walls, and obstacles for burning off energy, a north Fort Worth favorite near the corridor.

Texas Motor Speedway

Motorsports venue · The iconic superspeedway sits right in the Alliance area, hosting NASCAR weekends and special events families plan their year around, about 10 min away.

Tanger Outlets Fort Worth

Outlet shopping · A large outlet center along I-35W with family brands and seasonal events, a few minutes from Haslet.

Day Trip from Haslet

Fort Worth Stockyards (about 25 min south), the historic district with twice-daily longhorn cattle drives, Western shops, and the Cowtown Cattlepen Maze. It's the quintessential Texas day out for kids and visiting grandparents alike.

Family-Friendly Dining

Haslet's everyday dining is concentrated at Alliance Town Center, which carries dozens of restaurants ranging from quick tacos to sit-down brewhouses.

Chuy's — big, colorful Tex-Mex with a kids' menu and a come-as-you-are vibe. Best loud family dinner
Fuzzy's Taco Shop — a Fort Worth favorite for fast, cheap Baja-style tacos. Best quick weeknight
Cheddar's Scratch Kitchen — made-from-scratch comfort food at family prices. Best picky-eater friendly
BJ's Restaurant & Brewhouse — deep-dish pizza, a huge menu, and the famous Pizookie dessert. Best big-group dinner
Delucca Gaucho Pizza & Wine — Brazilian-style endless wood-fired pizza brought to your table. Best hungry-kid feast
Jason's Deli — sandwiches, a salad bar, and free soft-serve, an easy lunch with kids. Best healthy-ish lunch
Hanabi Hibachi and Sushi — teppanyaki tables where the chef cooks at your seat, a hit with kids. Best dinner-and-a-show
The Sicilian Butcher — build-your-own pasta and meatballs in a lively Italian room. Best weekend sit-down
Beacon Café — casual American breakfast and lunch, easy with little ones. Best weekend breakfast

Nearly all of these sit inside Alliance Town Center, about 10 minutes south of Haslet, so a meal usually pairs with a movie or shopping in one trip.

Shopping & Retail Districts

Haslet's retail is all about the Alliance corridor, where a lifestyle center, outlets, and big-box power centers cover everything from a birthday gift to the weekly grocery run.

Alliance Town Center

Lifestyle Center

An open-air mix of national stores, boutiques, dining, and a theater, anchored by Best Buy and JCPenney. Best for: a full Saturday outing combining shopping, a meal, and a movie.

Tanger Outlets Fort Worth

Outlets

Outlet shopping along I-35W with back-to-school and seasonal sales families plan around. Best for: stocking up on kids' clothes and shoes a couple of sizes at a time.

Presidio Towne Crossing

Everyday

A big-box power center near Heritage Trace and I-35W with Target and everyday essentials. Best for: the weekly errands run without fighting the lifestyle-center crowds.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Haslet runs a tight, family-first slate of city events, most of them centered on Haslet Community Park.

Spring

Easter Egg Hunt

A city-sponsored egg hunt and spring gathering for the youngest residents, a long-running Haslet tradition at the community park.

July

Independence Fireworks Celebration

Haslet's Fourth of July event with fireworks and family activities. Check the city events page for this year's lineup and location.

December

Christmas in the Park

The city's marquee holiday event at Haslet Community Park, with seasonal activities, lights, and a community gathering to open the season.

Nov – Dec

Lights of Haslet

A community holiday light display that draws families from across the area to drive or stroll through during the season.

Year-Round

Library Story Times

The Haslet Public Library runs regular story times and children's programming, a free, dependable staple of family life in town.

Year-Round

Parks & Rec Classes

The city offers ongoing community classes such as yoga and tai chi, with recreation programming that grows alongside the town.

Indoor Activities for Kids

When the Texas heat or a storm rolls in, Haslet families rely on the library in town and the cluster of indoor spots a few minutes south in the Alliance corridor.

Haslet Public Library

Type: Library · Ages: Babies through teens
Story times and children's programs right next to John Gammill Park, the free in-town anchor.

Northwest ISD Aquatic Center

Type: Indoor pool · Ages: Lessons from toddler up
The district natatorium gives Haslet a true year-round indoor pool for lessons and team swim.

Urban Air Adventure Park

Type: Trampoline park · Ages: Toddlers to teens
Trampolines, a ropes course, and climbing in north Fort Worth, a top energy-burner.

Main Event

Type: Family fun center · Ages: All ages
Bowling, laser tag, an arcade, and pool, an easy plan when it's too hot to be outside.

Cinemark Alliance Town Center and XD

Type: Movie theater · Ages: All ages
Recliners and big-format screens for a cool-down matinee in the corridor.

Alliance Town Center

Type: Shopping & dining · Ages: All ages
Dozens of stores and restaurants for a low-key, air-conditioned afternoon out.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

When the thermometer climbs past 100, Haslet families head to the Northwest ISD Aquatic Center's indoor pool, the resident pools and spray park in communities like Sendera Ranch, or the air conditioning at Main Event and the Alliance Town Center theater. Save the disc golf and trails for early morning before the heat sets in.
