District Overview

Lake Dallas Independent School District is one of the smallest districts in the Dallas to Fort Worth area: roughly 3,940 students across just five campuses in north Denton County, wrapped along the western shore of Lake Lewisville. If you are reading this, you are most likely a family drawn to lakeside living in Corinth, Lake Dallas, Hickory Creek, or Shady Shores, and you want to know what the schools are actually like before you buy. The honest headline: families choose Lake Dallas ISD for the lifestyle and the tight-knit, small-town school feel, not for the breadth of specialized programs you would find in a giant district like Frisco or Lewisville.

That small scale is the defining fact of this district. There is one comprehensive high school, one middle school, and three elementary schools, so the vast majority of local kids move through the same elementary to middle to high school pipeline together. By the time they reach Lake Dallas High School, many students have known their classmates since kindergarten. For a family that values continuity and a place where teachers and coaches know your child by name, that is a genuine draw. For a family shopping for a wide menu of magnet tracks, an IB Diploma Programme, or multiple high schools to choose from, it is a real limitation worth naming up front.

The district earned a TEA C rating in the most recent published accountability cycle (2024), its high school posts a graduation rate near 99 percent, and it holds a Niche grade of B. Those numbers tell slightly different stories, which is exactly why this page walks through what each one measures and what it means for your family in practice.

Best Fit For Families Who...

  • Want lakeside or lake-adjacent living in Corinth, Lake Dallas, Hickory Creek, or Shady Shores and want their kids zoned locally
  • Prefer a small district where the same group of kids grows up together from elementary through high school
  • Value a personal, know-your-name culture over a large menu of specialized academic tracks
  • Want strong dual-credit access through a local community college to earn college hours before graduation
  • Are weighing a lower home price point in north Denton County against the larger neighboring districts

May Not Be the Best Fit If...

  • Your student needs an IB Diploma Programme or a large, specialized magnet or STEM academy
  • You want multiple high schools to choose from within one district
  • You are optimizing purely for the highest TEA accountability scores in Denton County
  • Your address turns out to sit in Denton ISD or Lewisville ISD despite a Corinth or Lake Dallas mailing address
Boundary Verification

A Corinth, Lake Dallas, Hickory Creek, or Shady Shores mailing address does not guarantee Lake Dallas ISD enrollment. Parts of these same communities are zoned to Denton ISD or Lewisville ISD, and the district covers only about 9.8 square miles. Always verify your specific address with the district and the TEA School District Locator before making an offer.

TEA Accountability Rating

The Texas Education Agency assigns every district and campus an annual A to F accountability rating built on three domains. In the most recent published cycle, the 2024 A to F ratings released in August 2025, Lake Dallas ISD earned an overall C, with a C in each of the three domains.

What the three domains measure:

  • Student Achievement: STAAR performance, graduation, and college and career readiness indicators
  • School Progress: year-over-year academic growth compared with similar campuses
  • Closing the Gaps: how consistently the district serves every student group

Here is the practical translation. A "C" in the TEA system means "acceptable" performance, roughly the 70 to 79 point band, while a "B" begins at 80 and an "A" at 90. A C is not a warning sign of a struggling district, but it does signal that Lake Dallas ISD's STAAR and growth metrics land in the solid middle rather than at the top of the Denton County pack. Because the district has a single high school and a single middle school, the district rating and the campus experience are closely linked here in a way they are not in a 70-campus district, where a strong district average can hide a weak individual school. For families, the takeaway is to weigh this C against the district's very high graduation rate and its small, personal culture, then pull the campus-level reports for your specific schools at txschools.gov. TEA ratings are refreshed annually and can lag the current classroom reality, so treat the letter as one input, not the whole story.

Graduation Rate

Lake Dallas High School reports a four-year graduation rate near 99 percent, well above the Texas average of roughly 90 percent (the Class of 2023 finished at about 99.7 percent). This is one of the district's clearest strengths, and it is a useful counterpoint to the C accountability rating: TEA's overall score leans heavily on STAAR performance and growth, while the graduation rate reflects how reliably the district gets students across the finish line. A small district where staff know every senior tends to do that part well.

Dual Credit and Advanced Coursework

Lake Dallas ISD offers dual credit through North Central Texas College (NCTC), allowing eligible high schoolers to earn college hours while still enrolled, along with Advanced Placement (AP) courses at the high school. For a small district, this dual-credit access is meaningful: a motivated student can accumulate transferable college credit and reduce future tuition, without leaving Lake Dallas High School. Course availability at a single-high-school district is naturally more limited than at a multi-campus district, so families with a specific AP or dual-credit pathway in mind should confirm current offerings directly with the campus counseling office.

STAAR Proficiency

Campus-level STAAR proficiency rates (the share of students performing at "Approaches Grade Level" or above) are published for each Lake Dallas ISD campus at txschools.gov. Because the district is small, we recommend reading the report for your child's specific assigned campus rather than relying on a district average, since with only five schools a single campus moves the district number noticeably.

Special Programs

Lake Dallas ISD offers a focused, standard-curriculum set of programs rather than a large specialty-magnet menu. That is normal and expected for a district this size, and it is one of the honest tradeoffs of choosing a small, community-centered district.

Dual Credit (NCTC)

Through the partnership with North Central Texas College, eligible juniors and seniors can earn college credit while in high school. This is districtwide at Lake Dallas High School. Access typically requires meeting GPA and college-readiness benchmarks, so confirm eligibility with the counseling office.

Advanced Placement (AP)

AP courses are offered at Lake Dallas High School for students seeking college-level rigor and potential college credit through exam scores. Because there is one high school, the AP catalog is more compact than a multi-campus district, so verify the specific AP courses your student wants.

Gifted and Talented (GT)

GT services identify and serve advanced learners beginning in the elementary grades through a multi-criteria process. Newly enrolled students identified as gifted elsewhere should bring documentation for review; GT services follow the student across the district's campuses.

Special Education and 504

The district provides special education services under IDEA and Section 504 accommodations for eligible students. Because program depth varies by student need in a small district, families with an existing IEP should contact the Special Services office directly to confirm the right fit before committing to an address.

Enrollment: New Families

How to Enroll

New-student enrollment is handled through the district at ldisd.net. Expect to provide:

  • Proof of residency: a lease or deed plus a current utility bill (the district may require more than one document)
  • Birth certificate for first-time Texas enrollees
  • Immunization records meeting Texas requirements
  • Previous school records: transcript, IEP or 504 plan if applicable, and any test scores
  • Proof of guardianship or custody if applicable

Mid-Year and Transfer Enrollment

The district enrolls students year-round. Intra-district transfers between the district's own campuses are limited by capacity in a five-campus system and are not guaranteed. Inter-district transfers (students who live outside district boundaries) are subject to the district's current transfer policy and available space; confirm the current-year policy directly with the district office.

Students With IEPs or 504 Plans

Texas law requires the district to either adopt an incoming student's existing IEP or convene an ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior evaluation and eligibility documentation to enrollment, and contact the Special Services office early so services are in place when your child starts.

Boundary Verification: Read Before You Offer

This is the single most important step for a Lake Dallas ISD home search, and it trips up more relocating families here than almost anywhere else in Denton County. The reason is geography: Lake Dallas ISD covers only about 9.8 square miles, and the cities it touches (Corinth, Lake Dallas, Hickory Creek, and Shady Shores) are also served in part by Denton ISD and Lewisville ISD. Two homes on nearby streets, both with a Corinth mailing address, can be zoned to two different districts.

Practical implications for your search:

  • Never assume the city name on the listing equals the school district. Corinth in particular is split between Lake Dallas ISD and Denton ISD.
  • New construction on the edges of these communities can feed to an unexpected district or campus. Confirm the actual zoned schools, not the marketing.
  • Because there is one middle and one high school, if you are inside Lake Dallas ISD your secondary schools are set, the variable is which elementary you feed and whether you are in the district at all.

How to verify: use the district's own address lookup at ldisd.net and cross-check with the TEA School District Locator. Making an offer without confirming your specific address can mean your child attends a different district than you planned, and boundary lines do not always follow common-sense geography.

District Finances

Tax Rate

Lake Dallas ISD's adopted 2025 to 2026 total tax rate is $1.2552 per $100 of assessed value, unchanged from the prior year. That breaks down into a Maintenance and Operations (M&O) rate of $0.7552 and an Interest and Sinking (I&S), or debt service, rate of $0.50. The I&S portion funds voter-approved bond debt for facilities.

What that means in dollars: on a home assessed at $450,000, the district tax before exemptions is about $5,648 per year (roughly $471 per month). Texas homeowners with a primary-residence homestead exemption get a $100,000 reduction in taxable value for school taxes, which lowers the taxable amount to $350,000 and the annual district tax to about $4,393 (roughly $366 per month). County, city, and any special-district taxes are billed separately and vary by location.

How the Rate Compares

At $1.2552, Lake Dallas ISD sits above its larger Denton County neighbors on the school tax line: Lewisville ISD is around $1.12 and Denton ISD around $1.16, while Argyle ISD runs near $1.21. On a $450,000 home, the gap between roughly $1.12 and $1.26 is on the order of $600 per year before exemptions, a real but modest difference that families should weigh alongside home prices and lifestyle. Always confirm the current certified rate with the Denton County tax office and the Texas Comptroller before you budget.

Per-Pupil Funding

Like all Texas districts, Lake Dallas ISD is funded largely on average daily attendance plus state weights for special programs, and its per-pupil expenditure is published through TEA's PEIMS data at txschools.gov. Small districts have less room to spread fixed costs across students, which is part of why a compact district leans on its community and its focused program set rather than a sprawling menu.

Community Feel

Life in Lake Dallas ISD is shaped by two things: the lake and the size. Corinth, Lake Dallas, Hickory Creek, and Shady Shores sit right on Lake Lewisville, so boating, fishing, and lakeside parks are part of the everyday rhythm, and the district's small footprint means school events double as community events. Friday night football, the fine arts programs, and youth sports pull in families who have known each other for years. Parents consistently describe a personal, accessible culture where reaching a teacher or an administrator is straightforward.

Approximately 39 percent of students are identified as economically disadvantaged, per state enrollment data, which places the district in a more economically mixed profile than some of the higher-priced districts nearby. We share that figure factually, not as a judgment; every family's priorities differ, and the data is here so you can draw your own conclusions.

The honest tradeoff of a district this small is program depth. You are trading the sprawling course catalog and multiple-high-school choice of a Lewisville or Frisco for continuity, familiarity, and a lakeside setting. For many relocating families, especially those who value knowing their child's school community personally, that trade is exactly the point. For safety-specific questions about any particular neighborhood, review third-party tools such as NeighborhoodScout, your local police department's public crime map, or City-Data so you can evaluate the data yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Corinth or Lake Dallas address guarantee enrollment in Lake Dallas ISD? ▾
No. The cities of Corinth, Lake Dallas, Hickory Creek, and Shady Shores are split among Lake Dallas ISD, Denton ISD, and Lewisville ISD. The district covers only about 9.8 square miles, so two nearby homes with the same mailing city can be in different districts. Always verify your exact address using the district's lookup at ldisd.net and the TEA School District Locator before you make an offer.
Why is the TEA rating a C when the graduation rate is around 99 percent? ▾
The two measure different things. The TEA A to F rating leans heavily on STAAR test performance and year-over-year academic growth, where Lake Dallas ISD lands in the "acceptable" middle band. The graduation rate measures how reliably students finish, which a small, personal district tends to do very well. Read the C as a signal that test-based metrics are solid but not top-of-county, and weigh it alongside the district's other strengths and your child's specific campus reports at txschools.gov.
Does Lake Dallas ISD offer dual credit or AP courses? ▾
Yes. Lake Dallas High School offers Advanced Placement courses and dual credit through North Central Texas College (NCTC), so a motivated student can earn transferable college hours before graduating. Because there is one high school, the specific catalog is more compact than a multi-campus district; confirm the exact AP and dual-credit courses your student wants with the campus counseling office.
Is there more than one high school in the district? ▾
No. Lake Dallas ISD has a single comprehensive high school (Lake Dallas High School), one middle school, and three elementary schools. If you live within the district, your middle and high school are effectively set; the main variable is which elementary campus your address feeds. Families who want to choose among several high schools within one district should look at larger neighbors like Lewisville or Denton ISD.
How does the district handle a student transferring in with an existing IEP? ▾
Texas law requires the district to either adopt your child's current IEP or hold an ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior evaluation, eligibility, and IEP or 504 documentation to enrollment, and contact the Special Services office early. Because program depth varies by student need in a small district, families with fit-sensitive needs should speak with Special Services before committing to an address.
What is the property tax impact of living in Lake Dallas ISD? ▾
The adopted 2025 to 2026 school tax rate is $1.2552 per $100 of assessed value (M&O $0.7552 plus I&S $0.50). On a $450,000 home, that is about $5,648 per year before exemptions. With the $100,000 homestead exemption for school taxes, the taxable value drops to $350,000 and the annual district tax to about $4,393. County, city, and special-district taxes are billed separately. This school rate runs a bit higher than Lewisville ISD (around $1.12) and Denton ISD (around $1.16).
Why do families choose Lake Dallas ISD over the bigger neighboring districts? ▾
Almost always for two reasons: the lakeside setting on Lake Lewisville and the small, tight-knit school culture where kids grow up together through a single elementary-to-high-school pipeline. The tradeoff is a more compact program menu and one high school rather than several. Families who value continuity, a personal school community, and lake living tend to prefer it; families who want maximum program variety or the highest accountability scores often choose Lewisville, Denton, or Argyle ISD instead.
