District Overview

Joshua Independent School District is a small, growing district in southern Johnson County that quietly outperforms what you might expect from its rural location. It serves roughly 6,000 students across about ten campuses in and around the city of Joshua, plus parts of south Burleson and north Cleburne, spread over about 76 square miles. If you are considering it, you are probably a family looking for an affordable small-town setting with genuinely solid schools, and you are willing to trade urban convenience for that. The honest headline: Joshua ISD earns a TEA B and a Niche A minus, which is a strong showing for a rural district, but it is rural, and the commute to Fort Worth job centers runs about 30 to 45 minutes.

What makes Joshua stand out for its size is the college-credit stack. Between dual credit through Hill College and Tarleton State University, University of Texas OnRamps courses, and an Associate's Degree Program, a motivated Joshua High School student can graduate with a meaningful pile of transferable college hours, an opportunity that is often thinner in small rural districts. The high school has also been a UIL Academic Champion in nine of the last eleven years, which points to a real academic-competition culture, not just a football town.

The tradeoff is exactly what you would expect from a rural-fringe district: this is not a place for families expecting walkability, dense amenities, or a large menu of specialized magnet academies. It is a place for families who want space, affordability, a tight community, and schools that punch above their weight, within driving distance of Fort Worth employment.

Best Fit For Families Who...

  • Want an affordable, small-town, rural setting with more land per dollar than the closer-in suburbs
  • Value a district that earns strong ratings (TEA B, Niche A minus) despite its rural location
  • Have a motivated student who would use dual credit, UT OnRamps, or the Associate's Degree Program to bank college hours
  • Work remotely or have Fort Worth-side employment and can absorb a 30 to 45 minute commute
  • Prefer a close-knit school community with a strong academic-competition and athletics culture

May Not Be the Best Fit If...

  • You need walkability, dense amenities, or an urban lifestyle near your front door
  • Your student needs a large specialized magnet or IB Diploma Programme
  • Your daily commute must be short and you work in central Dallas or the north suburbs
  • You want a choice of several comprehensive high schools within one district

Joshua ISD stretches across roughly 76 square miles and touches several communities, so a Burleson, Cleburne, Crowley, or Godley mailing address does not tell you the district. Parts of these areas fall in Burleson ISD, Cleburne ISD, Crowley ISD, or Godley ISD instead. Always verify your specific address with the district and the TEA School District Locator before making an offer.

TEA Accountability Rating

The Texas Education Agency rates every district and campus A to F across three domains. In the most recent published cycle, the 2024 A to F ratings released in August 2025, Joshua ISD earned an overall B, with a B in each of the three domains.

What the three domains measure:

  • Student Achievement: STAAR performance, graduation, and college and career readiness indicators
  • School Progress: year-over-year academic growth compared with similar campuses
  • Closing the Gaps: how consistently the district serves every student group

Here is the practical translation. A "B" is "recognized" performance, the 80 to 89 point band, one tier below an A (90 and up) and one above a C (70 to 79). For a rural district where roughly 47 percent of students are identified as economically disadvantaged, earning a B across all three domains, including Closing the Gaps, is a meaningful signal that the district serves a broad range of students well, not just its top performers. It is a genuinely strong result for its context. As always, ratings are refreshed annually and can lag the current classroom, so pull the campus-level reports for your specific schools at txschools.gov before you decide.

Graduation Rate

Joshua High School reports a four-year graduation rate of roughly 98 percent, well above the Texas average near 90 percent. Combined with the TEA B, that consistency is a good sign for families: the district both gets students to the finish line and holds up on the test-based and growth metrics that drive the accountability score.

College Credit Pathways

This is Joshua ISD's standout academic feature for its size. The district offers dual credit through Hill College and Tarleton State University, University of Texas OnRamps courses (which put students on a UT Austin transcript with credit that transfers to Texas public universities), and an Associate's Degree Program (ADP) that lets qualifying students work toward an associate degree while still in high school. Add Advanced Placement, with about a 39 percent AP participation rate at Joshua High School, and a focused student has several ways to bank transferable college hours and reduce future tuition. Because eligibility and course lists change year to year, confirm the specific pathways and prerequisites with the high school counseling office.

STAAR Proficiency

Campus-level STAAR proficiency rates are published for each Joshua ISD campus at txschools.gov. As always, read the report for your child's specific assigned campus rather than relying on the district average.

Special Programs

For a rural district, Joshua ISD offers an unusually deep set of college-credit pathways, which is the main reason motivated families shortlist it.

Dual Credit (Hill College & Tarleton)

Joshua High School partners with Hill College and Tarleton State University so eligible juniors and seniors earn high school and college credit at the same time, largely on the JHS campus. Eligibility typically requires meeting GPA and college-readiness benchmarks; confirm with the counseling office.

UT OnRamps

OnRamps places students in a University of Texas at Austin course alongside their high school class, with college credit posted to a UT Austin transcript that transfers to Texas public universities. There is no separate application or TSIA required to enroll, and students stay on their home campus.

Associate's Degree Program (ADP)

Through its college partnerships, Joshua ISD offers a pathway for qualifying students to work toward an associate degree while still in high school, a rare option for a district this size. Because seats and prerequisites are limited, families targeting this pathway should plan the course sequence early with a counselor.

Gifted and Talented (GT) and AP

GT services identify advanced learners beginning in the elementary grades, and Advanced Placement courses at Joshua High School (about 39 percent participation) offer college-level rigor and potential exam credit. Newly enrolled students identified as gifted elsewhere should bring documentation for review.

Enrollment: New Families

How to Enroll

New-student enrollment is handled online through the district at joshuaisd.org. Expect to provide:

  • Proof of residency: a lease or deed plus a current utility bill (the district may require more than one document)
  • Birth certificate for first-time Texas enrollees
  • Immunization records meeting Texas requirements
  • Previous school records: transcript, IEP or 504 plan if applicable, and any test scores
  • Proof of guardianship or custody if applicable

Mid-Year and Transfer Enrollment

The district enrolls students year-round. Intra-district and inter-district transfers are subject to the district's current transfer policy and available capacity. Because the district is growing, confirm the current-year policy and any deadlines directly with the district office.

Students With IEPs or 504 Plans

Texas law requires the district to either adopt an incoming student's existing IEP or convene an ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior evaluation and eligibility documentation to enrollment, and contact the Special Services office early so services are in place when your child starts.

Boundary Verification: Read Before You Offer

Joshua ISD covers roughly 76 square miles of southern Johnson County and shares its territory with several neighboring districts. Because the district name, the city of Joshua, and the mailing addresses in the area do not line up neatly, this is a real trap for relocating buyers.

Practical implications for your search:

  • A Burleson, Cleburne, Crowley, or Godley mailing address may sit in Burleson ISD, Cleburne ISD, Crowley ISD, or Godley ISD rather than Joshua ISD. Do not assume the city equals the district.
  • Rural parcels and new construction on the district's edges can feed to an unexpected campus. Confirm the actual zoned schools, not the listing description.
  • Because there is one comprehensive high school, if you are inside Joshua ISD your high school is set; the variables are your elementary and middle feeder pattern.

How to verify: use the district's tools at joshuaisd.org and cross-check with the TEA School District Locator. Making an offer without confirming your specific address can mean your child attends a different district than you planned, and rural boundary lines rarely follow common-sense geography.

District Finances

Tax Rate

Joshua ISD's 2025 total school tax rate is $1.2575 per $100 of assessed value, per the Central Appraisal District of Johnson County. This combines the Maintenance and Operations (M&O) rate that funds day-to-day operations with the Interest and Sinking (I&S) rate that services voter-approved bond debt; the district business office publishes the current split.

What that means in dollars: on a home assessed at $350,000, which fits Joshua's more affordable price point, the district tax before exemptions is about $4,401 per year (roughly $367 per month). With the $100,000 homestead exemption for school taxes, the taxable value drops to $250,000 and the annual district tax to about $3,144 (roughly $262 per month). County, city, and any special-district taxes are billed separately and vary.

How the Rate Compares

At $1.2575, Joshua ISD sits right in line with its Johnson County neighbors on the school tax line: Burleson ISD is around $1.26 and Crowley ISD around $1.26, while Cleburne ISD runs a bit lower near $1.21 and Godley ISD a bit higher near $1.29. The bigger financial story here is usually the home price, not the rate: Joshua's rural land and housing typically cost less than the closer-in suburbs, so the total tax bill on a comparable-sized home is often lower even at a similar rate. Always confirm the current certified rate with the Johnson County tax office and the Texas Comptroller before you budget.

Per-Pupil Funding

Like all Texas districts, Joshua ISD is funded largely on average daily attendance plus state weights for special programs, and its per-pupil expenditure is published through TEA's PEIMS data at txschools.gov. As a growing district, Joshua has periodically turned to voter-approved bonds to fund facilities, which is reflected in the I&S portion of the rate.

Community Feel

Joshua is rural in the best sense of the word: space, a genuine small-town identity, and a school district that functions as the center of community life. Friday night football, UIL academic competitions, and youth sports pull families together, and the district's growth (enrollment is up about 17 percent over the past decade) reflects families steadily discovering that they can get affordable land and good schools within driving distance of Fort Worth. Parents on public review sites consistently describe a strong sense of investment and belonging, the kind of familiarity that is harder to find in a large metro district.

Approximately 47 percent of students are identified as economically disadvantaged, per state enrollment data. We share that figure factually, not as a judgment; it is part of understanding a district that earns a TEA B across all three domains, including Closing the Gaps, while serving an economically mixed community. Every family's priorities differ, and the data is here so you can draw your own conclusions.

The honest tradeoff is the commute and the amenities. If you work in Fort Worth or remotely, the 30 to 45 minute drive and the quieter setting are usually the point; if you need short commutes to central Dallas or the north suburbs, Joshua is likely too far. For safety-specific questions about any particular neighborhood, review third-party tools such as NeighborhoodScout, your local police department's public crime map, or City-Data so you can evaluate the data yourself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a Joshua, Burleson, or Cleburne address guarantee enrollment in Joshua ISD? ▾
No. Joshua ISD covers about 76 square miles and shares territory with Burleson ISD, Cleburne ISD, Crowley ISD, and Godley ISD. A mailing address in one of those cities may sit in a different district. Always verify your exact address with the district's tools at joshuaisd.org and the TEA School District Locator before you make an offer.
What college credit options does Joshua High School offer? ▾
Quite a lot for a rural district: dual credit through Hill College and Tarleton State University, University of Texas OnRamps courses that post credit to a UT Austin transcript, an Associate's Degree Program for qualifying students, and Advanced Placement (around 39 percent participation). A motivated student can graduate with a substantial number of transferable college hours. Confirm the current course list and eligibility with the high school counseling office, since offerings change year to year.
Is Joshua ISD a good district despite being rural? ▾
By the state's own measures, it holds up well. Joshua ISD earned a TEA B across all three accountability domains in the 2024 ratings and carries a Niche A minus, and Joshua High School graduates roughly 98 percent of its students. Earning a B in Closing the Gaps while serving a community where about 47 percent of students are economically disadvantaged is a strong signal that the district serves a wide range of students, not just its top performers.
How does the ninth grade campus work? ▾
Joshua ISD runs a dedicated Joshua High School Ninth Grade Campus, so freshmen attend their own campus before moving up to the main high school for grades 10 through 12. Many families like this because it eases the transition into high school and keeps ninth graders together during a pivotal year. All freshmen in the district feed into it.
How does the district handle a student transferring in with an existing IEP? ▾
Texas law requires the district to either adopt your child's current IEP or hold an ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 days of enrollment. Bring all prior evaluation, eligibility, and IEP or 504 documentation to enrollment, and contact the Special Services office early so services are in place when your child starts.
What is the property tax impact of living in Joshua ISD? ▾
The 2025 school tax rate is $1.2575 per $100 of assessed value. On a $350,000 home, that is about $4,401 per year before exemptions. With the $100,000 homestead exemption for school taxes, the taxable value drops to $250,000 and the annual district tax to about $3,144. The rate is in line with Burleson ISD and Crowley ISD (around $1.26) and a bit above Cleburne ISD (around $1.21); because Joshua homes often cost less, the total bill on a comparable home can still be lower. County, city, and special-district taxes are billed separately.
How long is the commute from Joshua to Fort Worth? ▾
Plan on roughly 30 to 45 minutes to Fort Worth employment centers, depending on your exact location within the district and traffic. Joshua works best for families with Fort Worth-side jobs or remote work; commuting to central Dallas or the far north suburbs from here is a long daily haul that most families find impractical.
