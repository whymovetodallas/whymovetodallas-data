Things to Do in McKinney, TX with Kids

McKinney offers something no other Collin County suburb can match, a thriving historic downtown that feels nothing like a suburb, paired with one of DFW's best lake recreation parks and a family infrastructure that makes every weekend feel easy.

What McKinney Families Actually Do Here

McKinney families have two entirely different versions of a great Saturday, the kind that starts at Towne Lake with kayaks and a splash pad, and the kind that starts with brunch in Historic Downtown before browsing boutiques and catching a community event on the square. Both happen here, often in the same weekend. See the full McKinney relocation guide →

Historic Downtown Unlike Any Suburb

McKinney's Historic District, on the National Register of Historic Places, hosts independent boutiques, acclaimed restaurants, live music, and year-round community events in a genuine 19th-century town square setting that no other Collin County city can replicate.

Towne Lake, The Outdoor Hub

Towne Lake Recreation Area is McKinney's signature outdoor destination, a 117-acre reservoir with kayak and paddleboat launches, a spray playground, catch-and-release fishing, and trail access that makes it the first stop for families new to town.

Heard Museum, Destination Attraction

The Heard Natural Science Museum & Wildlife Sanctuary spans 289 acres with trails, live animal exhibits, a butterfly garden, planetarium shows, and year-round educational programs, one of DFW's premier family destinations and a reason families drive from all over the metro to visit McKinney.

Parks, Trails & Green Space

McKinney manages more than 70 parks and recreation areas, from major destination parks anchored by lakes and nature preserves to neighborhood greens within walking distance of most subdivisions. The city's trail network connects parks across the entire community.

Towne Lake Recreation Area

McKinney's flagship outdoor park, centered on a 117-acre reservoir. Families use the kayak and paddleboat launches, spray playground (open seasonally per the city's official schedule), catch-and-release fishing docks, and paved trail loop. Shaded pavilions and restrooms make this a full-morning outing for any age. Best for strollers on the paved perimeter path.

Spray Playground Kayak Launch Fishing Trails Pavilions

Heard Natural Science Museum & Wildlife Sanctuary

A 289-acre nature sanctuary with 5 miles of hiking trails through native habitats, a butterfly garden and greenhouse, live native animal exhibits, and a digital planetarium. The Heard's education programs run year-round for school-age children. One of McKinney's defining family attractions, plan at least two hours. Terrain is moderate; strollers handle the main paved paths.

Trails Planetarium Butterfly Garden Live Animals Nature Programs

Craig Ranch Regional Park

A large community park in west McKinney with multi-use sports fields, trail connections into the Craig Ranch greenway network, playgrounds, and ample shade from mature trees. Heavily used on weekends for youth soccer and baseball. Parking is plentiful, a practical choice for families with weekend game schedules.

Sports Fields Trails Playground Picnic Areas

Bonnie Wenk Park

A 63-acre signature park on the west side of McKinney, featuring an 18-hole disc golf course, multi-use athletic fields, nature trails, and a large playground. The disc golf course draws families on weekday afternoons. Wide open green space makes this a reliable low-key option for younger kids who need room to run.

Disc Golf Sports Fields Trails Playground

Gabe Nesbitt Community Park

McKinney's central sports and aquatics park, home to the Apex Centre recreation complex, outdoor pool, synthetic turf fields, and baseball diamonds. The hub for McKinney ISD feeder athletics and youth recreation leagues on weekday evenings. Best for families already enrolled in city leagues, the facility layout keeps practice and pickup in one place.

Outdoor Pool Baseball Diamonds Sports Fields Apex Centre

Erwin Park (Greenbelt)

A 212-acre natural area on McKinney's western edge with rugged mountain biking and hiking trails, primitive camping, and creek crossings, a genuine nature escape that feels remote despite being inside city limits. Best for older kids and teens comfortable on uneven terrain; not suitable for strollers.

Mountain Biking Hiking Trails Primitive Camping Creek Access

Chestnut Square Heritage Village

A living history museum in Historic Downtown McKinney with a collection of restored 19th-century structures, a church, cabins, a one-room schoolhouse, and working gardens. School programs and seasonal events (including old-fashioned holiday celebrations) make this one of the most distinctive educational destinations in any DFW suburb.

Living History Educational Programs Seasonal Events

Trail System Highlight

McKinney Greenway Trail System, McKinney's trail network spans 85+ miles of paved and natural-surface paths connecting parks, neighborhoods, and open space across the city. The core greenway loops are stroller-friendly and well-lit; spur trails into natural areas like Erwin Park offer more rugged options for older kids and cyclists. Great for family bike rides, morning runs, and walking the dog without repeating the same path.

Relocation Tip

Start your first McKinney weekend at Towne Lake Recreation Area on a Saturday morning, then drive five minutes to Historic Downtown for lunch and a walk through the square. In two stops you'll understand exactly what makes McKinney different from every other Collin County suburb, the outdoor infrastructure and the genuine town identity exist together here in a way that's genuinely hard to replicate.

Youth Sports Leagues & Facilities

McKinney ISD fields competitive programs across virtually every sport, and the city's Parks & Recreation department runs a parallel set of recreational leagues for kids who want a lower-stakes introduction. From the youngest age groups through high school, McKinney offers one of the deepest youth sports ecosystems in Collin County. Compare DFW school districts →

Soccer

McKinney Youth Soccer Association (MYSA): Recreational and competitive soccer, ages 3–18; Fall and Spring seasons; games played at fields across McKinney parks. MYSA is the primary youth soccer organization serving McKinney families and McKinney ISD feeder enrollment.

Featured Sports Complex

Apex Centre at Gabe Nesbitt Community Park, McKinney's premier multi-sport recreation facility, with an indoor pool for year-round swim lessons and competitive programs, basketball and volleyball courts, a fitness center, and direct access to the outdoor athletic complex featuring baseball diamonds and multi-use sports fields. Most McKinney youth recreation league games rotate through this complex and the surrounding park facilities on weekends.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

McKinney keeps families entertained without requiring a highway drive to a big city, the Heard Museum alone justifies a visit, and the combination of the Apex Centre, Historic Downtown, and Adriatica Village means there's always something to do regardless of season or age.

Heard Natural Science Museum & Wildlife Sanctuary

Nature Museum & Preserve · A 289-acre working wildlife sanctuary with live native animal habitats, a butterfly garden and greenhouse (open spring and summer), a digital planetarium with regular shows, and 5 miles of walking trails. The Heard's weekend nature programs are particularly popular with elementary-age kids, plan for a full morning or afternoon.

Apex Centre

Community Recreation Center · McKinney's flagship indoor rec facility with a full indoor aquatic center, fitness equipment, basketball courts, group fitness studios, and year-round programming for all ages. The aquatic center runs open swim sessions on weekends, a lifesaver in July when outdoor temperatures make everything else a challenge.

Historic Downtown McKinney

Entertainment District · More than a shopping area, the square hosts street performers, outdoor movie nights, weekly farmers markets, and most of McKinney's signature annual events. Walking its brick streets, boutiques, and restaurants with kids is a genuinely pleasant experience, not a concession to someone else's idea of fun.

Adriatica Village

Lifestyle Village · A European-inspired mixed-use neighborhood in west McKinney, with a village clock tower, fountains, boutique shops, and patio dining. Different in character from Historic Downtown, quieter and more neighborhood-scale, with a relaxed patio atmosphere that families use for weekend brunch and evening walks.

Galaxy Drive-In Theatre

Drive-In Movie Theater · A genuine double-feature drive-in experience just outside McKinney, one of only a handful still operating in North Texas. Bring blankets, tune the car radio, and arrive early for the best spot. Kids love the novelty; parents appreciate the privacy. A well-worn McKinney family tradition on summer weekends.

First Monday Trade Days

Monthly Outdoor Market · One of Texas's oldest and largest flea markets, held the weekend before the first Monday of each month on the McKinney square. Antiques, handmade goods, vintage finds, and food vendors fill the historic district, a genuinely unique outing that brings the whole family and turns a regular weekend into an event.

Day Trip from McKinney

National Videogame Museum, Frisco (20 min south), The only museum in the world dedicated to the history of video games, with more than 10,000 sq ft of playable exhibits spanning every console generation from Pong to the present. An automatic win for families with kids ages 6 and up, and genuinely fascinating for parents who grew up gaming. Pair it with the Frisco Discovery Center next door for a full museum day.

Family-Friendly Dining

McKinney's dining scene is anchored by Historic Downtown, a walkable collection of independent restaurants in restored 19th-century buildings that feel nothing like the national-chain strips found in most suburbs. The variety ranges from old-school BBQ to upscale steakhouses, with enough casual options to handle a post-game Wednesday dinner without ceremony.

Rick's Chophouse Best celebration dinner
McKinney's most storied restaurant, a USDA Prime steakhouse in a Historic Downtown building operating since 1997. Award-winning wine list, tableside service, and the kind of atmosphere that turns a birthday dinner into a real occasion.
Harvest Seasonal Kitchen Best weekend brunch
Farm-to-table American with rotating seasonal menus, located in Historic Downtown. Weekend brunch draws a devoted local crowd; the menu changes enough to keep regulars coming back monthly. Request a patio table if the weather allows.
Sugarbacon Proper Kitchen Best for the whole family · lively atmosphere
Elevated American comfort food with a bourbon-heavy cocktail program and a genuinely kid-friendly menu. One of Historic Downtown's most consistently packed spots, arrive before 6:30 on Friday evenings or expect a wait.
Patina Green Home & Market Best weekend morning · unique McKinney experience
A garden shop, home goods store, and café rolled into one Historic Downtown destination. The lunch menu, soups, sandwiches, seasonal salads, is consistently excellent, but the real draw is the atmosphere: kids wander the garden while parents linger over coffee.
Local Yocal BBQ & Grill Best after a game · picky-eater safe
Texas BBQ from a McKinney institution that also runs a full-service butcher shop. Brisket, ribs, and pulled pork in a casual counter-service setting, no child has ever rejected the mac and cheese. Open lunch and dinner Tuesday–Sunday.
The Celt Irish Pub Best after a Historic Downtown event
A genuine Irish pub in Historic Downtown with live music most evenings, a wide-ranging pub menu, and a patio that stays comfortable through much of the year. Casual enough for families at lunch; lively enough for parents on a Friday night.
Dos Generaciones Best quick family dinner · reliable Tex-Mex
Family-owned Tex-Mex with generous portions, house-made salsas, and a relaxed dining room that handles kids without a second thought. A dependable choice when the family vote lands on tacos and no one wants to debate further.
LOOT Bar + Kitchen Best weeknight dinner for parents
Modern American fare with a craft cocktail program in an approachable Historic Downtown setting. The menu ranges wide enough to satisfy different age groups at the same table, a useful quality on busy school-night dinners out.
Cadillac Pizza Pub Best unanimous family vote · live music weekends
Craft pizza and Texas beer in a casual Historic Downtown location with live music on weekends. The pizza is good enough to become a regular Thursday-night habit, and the atmosphere is low-key enough that kids don't require constant management.

Most of these are concentrated in and around Historic Downtown McKinney, the square and its surrounding blocks form one of DFW's most walkable independent dining corridors, where browsing boutiques and stopping for lunch combine naturally into a full afternoon.

Shopping & Retail Districts

McKinney's shopping landscape is defined by its Historic Downtown, unlike any other DFW suburb, with complementary everyday retail in newer corridors that handle the practical side without a long drive.

Historic Downtown McKinney

Historic District

A walkable square of 100+ independently owned boutiques, antique dealers, galleries, and restaurants in restored Victorian-era buildings, the most distinctive shopping experience in Collin County. Saturdays bring farmers market activity, First Monday brings the flea market crowd, and the lead-up to Christmas turns the square into one of DFW's most photographed seasonal destinations.
Best for: full-day family outing, shops, lunch, events, and evening dining all within a few blocks.

Stonebridge Village & Craig Ranch Marketplace

Everyday

The western McKinney retail corridor anchored by H-E-B, Target, and major grocery options with a full complement of medical, fitness, and service tenants. Handles the practical weekly errands without a trip to Historic Downtown. Convenient to the Craig Ranch, Stonebridge Ranch, and Eldorado Pkwy neighborhoods.
Best for: weekly grocery and errand runs, efficient layout, ample parking, everything in one stop.

Allen Premium Outlets

Outlets

A full-scale outdoor outlet center about 10 minutes south of McKinney with 100+ brand-name stores. The go-to for back-to-school shopping, athletic gear, and anything where paying outlet prices makes sense. The outdoor walkway format works well for families, kids can move around without being cooped up in a traditional enclosed mall.
Best for: back-to-school runs, athletic gear, and brand shopping without full retail markup.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

McKinney's community event calendar is built around Historic Downtown, the square serves as the city's natural gathering place and the backdrop for most signature annual events, giving the city a community identity that newer suburbs can't manufacture.

April

ArtFest McKinney

A juried fine arts festival held annually on the Historic Downtown square, featuring artists from across Texas and the Southwest. Live entertainment, food vendors, and a kids' activity area make it accessible to the whole family. One of the signature spring events in Collin County, check mckinneytexas.org/events for this year's details.

July

4th on the Square

McKinney's Independence Day celebration in Historic Downtown, featuring live entertainment, family activities, food and vendor booths, and a fireworks display over the square. One of the most well-attended events on the McKinney calendar, arrive early and plan to stay after dark for the full experience. Check cityofsouthlake.com's McKinney counterpart for entertainment lineup.

October

Oktoberfest McKinney

An annual fall festival in Historic Downtown with live music, craft beers, German-inspired food, artisan vendors, and family-friendly activities. The fall colors on the historic square make this one of McKinney's most photographed annual events. Held annually in October, check mckinneytexas.org for specific dates.

Monthly

First Monday Trade Days

One of Texas's oldest continuously operating flea markets, held the weekend before the first Monday of each month in and around Historic Downtown McKinney. Antiques, handcrafted goods, vintage clothing, and food vendors fill the square. A genuinely local tradition that draws collectors and casual browsers alike, a different kind of family outing every month.

December

Christmas on the Square

McKinney's annual holiday celebration transforms Historic Downtown into one of DFW's most charming seasonal destinations, tree lighting, holiday illuminations, live entertainment, ice skating (seasonally), and Christmas shopping in the boutiques. Held annually since the 1990s; check mckinneytexas.org for this year's programming schedule.

Ongoing

Heard Museum Seasonal Events

The Heard runs a year-round calendar of family programs, spring Butterfly Festival (one of Texas's largest annual butterfly events), summer nature camps, fall raptor programs, and holiday-themed education events. Many sell out; check heardmuseum.org for the current schedule and advance registration requirements.

Full calendar: McKinney official events page

Indoor Activities for Kids

When Texas summers hit 105°F, McKinney families have no shortage of climate-controlled options, from the Apex Centre's full aquatic complex to the Heard Museum's indoor exhibits and a solid lineup of trampoline parks and entertainment centers.

Altitude Trampoline Park

Type: Trampoline Park · Ages: All ages
Wall-to-wall trampolines, foam pits, slam dunk zones, dodgeball courts, and a dedicated toddler section for younger kids. One of McKinney's most-visited rainy-day destinations, birthday packages available; best to book ahead on weekend afternoons.

Apex Centre Aquatics

Type: Indoor Pool · Ages: All ages
McKinney's primary year-round indoor aquatics facility, with lap lanes, open swim sessions, and a dedicated programming area for swim lessons and water fitness classes. Open swim is available on weekend afternoons, check mckinneytexas.org/parks for current hours and admission.

Heard Museum Indoor Exhibits

Type: Natural Science Museum · Ages: All ages
The Heard's indoor exhibit halls house live native animal displays, hands-on natural science activities, and a digital planetarium with regular scheduled shows. A reliable option on cloudy days or when the summer heat makes the outdoor trails impractical for young children.

McKinney Public Library, Children's Programs

Type: Public Library · Ages: All ages (infant through teen)
The McKinney Public Library's children's department runs story times, STEM workshops, summer reading programs, and school-break activity sessions. Free to attend with a library card. The summer reading program in particular is heavily used by McKinney families as a daily structure during June and July.

Bowlero McKinney

Type: Bowling & Arcade · Ages: All ages
Modern boutique bowling with cosmic lanes, an arcade, and food service, a reliable go-to for group outings and birthday parties. Lane reservations are strongly recommended on weekend evenings; weekday afternoons are significantly less crowded.

Studio Movie Grill McKinney

Type: Dine-in Movie Theater · Ages: All ages
Full-service dine-in theater with in-seat food and beverage ordering, a significantly more relaxed family movie experience than standard theaters. The combination of current releases and table service makes weekend matinees a go-to for families who want a low-effort afternoon out.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

McKinney summers routinely hit 100°F+ from mid-July through August. The practical local answer: Towne Lake's spray playground and Apex Centre open swim handle the water urge, the Heard Museum's indoor exhibits and planetarium give you a substantive outing without the sun, and the McKinney Public Library's summer reading program gives structure to the July weeks when every outdoor option requires 7am timing. Most McKinney families rotate through all three regularly.
