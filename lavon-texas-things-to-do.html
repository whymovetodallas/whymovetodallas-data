Things to Do in Lavon, TX with Kids

Lavon is where the lake is the backyard. A 21,400-acre reservoir sits at the city's edge with 16 parks, five swimming beaches, and 25 miles of trails. The city itself is small, quiet, and still growing. The outdoor access is already there.

Quick answer: Lavon's main draw for families is direct access to Lake Lavon, a 21,400-acre U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir with five swimming beaches (open April through September), 19 boat ramps, 25 miles of trails, and 16 surrounding parks. The city itself is small with two main parks, an annual Easter and Fall Festival, and a Community ISD elementary school on Presidents Boulevard. Families who value outdoor access over commercial entertainment consistently rate it as one of the best-value Collin County options.

What Lavon Families Actually Do Here

Lavon is genuinely small. The city has a few hundred acres of municipal parkland, an annual festival calendar, and a historic City Hall. What it has in abundance is the lake. Lake Lavon starts where the subdivision ends, and for families who moved here for outdoor lifestyle rather than suburban amenities, that's exactly the point. Fishing before school, kayaking on weekends, hiking the Trinity Trail on fall afternoons. The city is adding park acreage near City Hall with 20+ acres in development. See the full Lavon relocation guide.

Lake Lavon

A 21,400-acre reservoir managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers with 16 parks, five designated swimming beaches (open April through September), 19 four-lane boat ramps, fishing, kayaking, camping, and 25 miles of trails. The closest thing to a private lakefront many DFW families will ever have.

City Hall Park

Lavon's main city park near the historic City Hall at 120 School Road. Paved walking and biking trail, shaded pavilion, updated playground, and the site of the city's two signature annual events: Easter Breakfast with the Bunny and the Fall Festival. The pavilion is reservable for private events.

Eagle Pond

At Lincoln Ave and Harrison Lane in the Bear Creek subdivision. A passive nature park with a pond surrounded by native trees, shrubs, and walking trail. Benches along the trail, a pavilion, playground, and a doggie waste station. Quiet and well-maintained, best for kids who want to be near water without the lake scale.

Parks, Trails & Lake Access

Lavon's park system splits into two tiers: the city's own two parks (Eagle Pond and City Hall Park), and the 16 U.S. Army Corps of Engineers parks that ring Lake Lavon. The city parks are small and walkable. The lake parks are a different scale entirely: over 200 picnic sites, five beaches, and a trail system that runs 25 miles.

City Hall Park

120 School Road, next to the historic Lavon City Hall. Shaded trees, paved walking trail, playground, and pavilion available for reservation. The most-used park in the city for casual weekday walks and the home base for annual city events. Stroller and bike accessible on the paved loop.

Paved Trail Playground Pavilion Stroller-Accessible

Eagle Pond

Lincoln Ave and Harrison Lane, Bear Creek subdivision. Nature-passive park built around a pond with native plantings, benches, and a paved walking trail that loops the water. Pavilion and playground on-site. Dog waste stations throughout. Quieter than City Hall Park and a good first-nature outing for toddlers and preschoolers.

Nature Walk Pond Playground Dog-Friendly

Lake Lavon Swimming Beaches

Five designated swimming areas open April through September: Mallard Park, East Fork Park, Ticky Creek Park, Avalon Park, and Pebble Beach Park. All are on the Lake Lavon shoreline managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. No admission charge beyond parking fees at gated parks. Sandy, lakefront, family-appropriate.

Trinity Trail

A 25-mile trail that loops around Lake Lavon, open to hikers and equestrians year-round (weather and ground conditions permitting). Three trailheads provide access from different points around the lake. Unpaved. Good for families with older kids comfortable on mixed terrain. One of the longer trail systems in Collin County.

25-Mile Trail Equestrian Hiking Year-Round

Sister Grove Park Trail

A primitive trail on 75 acres managed by Collin County. Open for hiking, off-road biking, and dog carts. About one mile, unpaved, and branches out to the lake shore. A shorter, wilder option than the Trinity Trail for families who want a quick morning on dirt without committing to a long route.

Primitive Trail Off-Road Biking Dog-Friendly Lakeside

Lake Lavon Camping Parks

16 parks around the lake include 71 tent camping sites with water, 167 sites with electric and water hookups, and 244 picnic tables. Reservations through Recreation.gov (1-877-444-6777). Car camping with lake access is 10 minutes from most Lavon neighborhoods. A practical option for families who want an overnight outdoor experience without a long drive.

Future City Park (20+ Acres)

The City of Lavon has reserved more than 20 acres adjacent to City Hall for future park development. The 2022 Parks and Recreation Master Plan guides what comes next. Families buying now should factor in that the park system is still actively growing, not a finished product.

Lake Lavon Boat Ramps

19 four-lane boat ramps dispersed around the lake, some open seasonally (April 1 through September 30) and others 24 hours year-round. If you own a kayak, canoe, or boat, these ramps are within a few minutes of most Lavon addresses. Two full-service marinas serve the lake.

Relocation Tip

Start at East Fork Park on the southeast shore of Lake Lavon. It has a designated swimming beach (open April through September), boat ramp, picnic sites, and trail access. It's the fastest way to understand why Lavon families say the lake is the main reason they moved here. To obtain an America the Beautiful annual pass, stop at the East Fork Park gatehouse (1901 Skyview Drive, Wylie) for discounted access to all 16 parks.

Youth Sports & Outdoor Recreation

Lavon is a small city without its own municipal sports leagues. Families find organized youth sports through Community ISD school programs, nearby Wylie and Rockwall leagues, and county-level options in Collin County. Lake Lavon itself provides the largest sports infrastructure, covering fishing, kayaking, paddleboarding, and equestrian access.

Fishing: Lake Lavon

Lake Lavon is one of the more productive recreational fishing lakes in North Texas. White bass peak in spring and summer off the dam. Crappie fishing is best in winter and spring. Striped bass, largemouth bass, blue and channel catfish, and sand bass are all present. No fishing license required for children under 17. A Texas fishing license is required for adults.

Kayaking & Canoeing

With 19 boat ramps, launching a kayak or canoe at Lake Lavon is as simple as loading it in the car on a Saturday morning. No permit needed for non-motorized craft at most ramps. The lake's 21,400 acres provide plenty of quiet coves away from motorboat traffic. Paddleboards are also common. A good first water sport for kids 6 and up.

Boating & Water Sports

Lake Lavon allows motorized boats, water skiing, and wave runners. Two full-service marinas serve the lake. The lake receives approximately 1.6 million visitors per year. Summer weekends draw significant boat traffic, particularly near the swimming beaches. Families with kids should target early morning weekday outings for calmer water conditions.

Hiking & Trail Running

The Trinity Trail around Lake Lavon runs 25 miles with three trailheads, open to hikers and equestrians year-round. The 9-mile equestrian and hiking trail between Brockdale Park and East Fork Park is a good intermediate option for families with older kids. Sister Grove Park adds a 1-mile primitive loop. All trails are unpaved and weather-dependent.

Community ISD School Sports

Community Independent School District (communityisd.org) serves Lavon students and runs athletic programs at the middle and high school levels. For younger recreational league options, families typically register with Wylie Parks and Recreation or the Rockwall area leagues, which are each 10 to 20 minutes from Lavon.

Equestrian Access

The Trinity Trail and the 9-mile Brockdale-to-East Fork route are open to equestrian use year-round. Several properties in and around Lavon include acreage suitable for horses. For families with riders, Lavon and its rural surroundings offer proximity to trail access that is unusual for a Collin County suburb at this price point.

Fishing Note

Zebra mussels have been detected in Lake Lavon. Texas law requires you to clean, drain, and dry your boat, trailer, and any live wells or bait buckets before transporting them to another water body. Draining water is required. Possession or transport of zebra mussels is illegal. All events or organized activities on the lake require a Corps of Engineers permit from the Lavon Lake Office at (972) 442-3141.

Family Entertainment & Attractions

Lavon doesn't have commercial entertainment within the city limits. The lake, the city parks, and the school calendar are the anchors. For commercial options (movie theaters, bowling, trampoline parks), families drive 15 to 25 minutes to Wylie, Rockwall, or Garland. Lavon's identity as a family destination is built around outdoor access, not commercial infrastructure.

Lake Lavon Swimming

Summer Swimming · Five lake beaches open April through September: Mallard Park, East Fork Park, Ticky Creek Park, Avalon Park, and Pebble Beach Park. Sandy shoreline, lake access, and no fee beyond park entry at gated locations. The primary summer entertainment anchor for Lavon families.

Lake Camping Overnight

Overnight Camping · 71 tent sites with water and 167 electric hookup sites across 16 lake parks. Reservations through Recreation.gov. Camping within 10 minutes of your home is a genuinely rare suburban perk. For families with young kids who want an introduction to camping, Lavon Lake campgrounds offer a lower-stakes starting point than a remote state park.

City Hall Gymnasium

Indoor Community Space · The gymnasium at Lavon City Hall (120 School Road) has been restored to near-original condition and is used for city-sponsored events, elections, and community gatherings. The adjacent Community Room has kitchen amenities and large windows overlooking City Hall Park. Available for Lavon School reunions and city events.

Easter Breakfast with the Bunny

Spring City Event · City Hall Park hosts this annual spring celebration for families. The Easter Breakfast with the Bunny is one of the two signature community events organized by Lavon Parks and Recreation. Check the city's event calendar at lavontx.gov/event-calendar for this year's date.

Fall Festival

Fall City Event · City Hall Park's fall counterpart to the Easter event. A family-focused community gathering held annually in fall at City Hall Park. Both annual festivals are organized by the city and represent Lavon's community identity as a small town that shows up for itself. Check lavontx.gov for current year dates.

Wylie & Rockwall (15-20 Min)

Nearby Options · Wylie has a historic downtown with restaurants and a city recreation center. Rockwall has the Harbor mall waterfront, a commercial entertainment corridor, and its own lake access. For organized commercial entertainment, Hawaiian Falls Garland (water park) and The Main Event are each under 30 minutes.

Family-Friendly Dining

Lavon is small, and its restaurant scene reflects that. There are a handful of local spots within the city, plus a broader selection in nearby Wylie (15 min west) and Rockwall (25 min southeast). The dining pattern for most Lavon families is quick local stops for weeknight meals, with Wylie for sit-down dinners.

El Rodeo Mexican Restaurant
Best family Mexican night
Authentic Mexican in a colorful, casual setting. Traditional dishes, fresh salsas, and a festive atmosphere that holds up with kids of all ages. One of the best-regarded local spots in Lavon for a full sit-down dinner without driving to Wylie.
Big Taco La Casita
Best post-lake taco stop
Near Lake Lavon with tacos, burritos, shrimp plates, tortas, and carnitas in a casual format. The kind of place families stop at after a morning at the beach or a kayak session. Convenient, unpretentious, and good for hungry kids in wet clothes.
Just E Nuff II
Best Texas BBQ
Texas-style smoked meats with traditional sides. The second location of a local BBQ operation. Family-friendly format with hearty portions and no-fuss ordering. Good for weeknight dinners when the lake or park wore everyone out.
Big Daddy's BBQ
Best smoked meat weekend
Big portions of traditionally smoked meats with savory sides. Another local BBQ option to have in rotation. Worth knowing when you're not in the mood to cook after a full Saturday at the lake.
Burgers & Chile Loco
Best casual family burger
American classics with a spicy edge and a casual dining atmosphere that works well for families. Comfortable for groups and practical for weeknight takeout when you don't need a full restaurant experience.
Ballard Street Cafe (Wylie, 15 min)
Best sit-down weekend brunch
A Wylie institution serving breakfast, lunch, and dinner. Warm community atmosphere, local following, and the kind of menu that holds up for both kids and adults. The short drive to Wylie is worth it for a relaxed family weekend morning out.

Most of Lavon's dining is concentrated near the TX-78 corridor. For broader restaurant options, Wylie (15 minutes west) and Rockwall (25 minutes southeast via FM 548) offer significantly more variety.

Shopping & Retail

Lavon has minimal retail within city limits. Practical shopping is in Wylie (15 min) or along TX-78 toward Princeton. For larger mall-style trips, Rockwall and Garland are each 25 to 30 minutes. Most Lavon families build a weekly grocery run into a Wylie trip and handle everything else from there.

Wylie Corridor (15 min)

Everyday Retail

Wylie along FM 544 and FM 1378 has grocery stores, pharmacy, hardware, and everyday retail that covers most practical household needs. Most Lavon families run errands in Wylie rather than driving farther north or south. About 15 minutes west on FM 78 or TX-78.

Best for: weekly grocery runs, pharmacy, and practical household errands.

The Harbor Rockwall (25 min)

Waterfront Mall

Rockwall's waterfront shopping and dining district on Lake Ray Hubbard. National retailers alongside restaurants with lake views. A weekend outing destination rather than a practical shopping run. About 25 minutes southeast via FM 548. Good for back-to-school shopping or a family dinner with lake scenery.

Best for: weekend outings, birthday dinners, or larger seasonal shopping trips.

Firewheel Town Center (20 min)

Major Retail

Full-scale lifestyle center in Garland with major anchors, specialty stores, restaurants, and a movie theater. About 20 minutes southwest via I-30 or TX-78. The go-to option when Lavon families need broader shopping than Wylie offers, without commuting into Dallas.

Best for: back-to-school shopping, clothing, and major retail purchases.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Lavon's event calendar is intentionally small-town. Two city-organized events anchor the year. Lake Lavon fills in the rest with fishing tournaments, beach season, and outdoor programming through the Corps of Engineers. The city's online calendar at lavontx.gov is the single reliable source for current dates.

Spring

Easter Breakfast with the Bunny

Held annually at City Hall Park (120 School Road). One of Lavon's two signature community events, organized by Parks and Recreation. Check lavontx.gov/event-calendar for this year's date and registration details.

Fall

Fall Festival

Annual fall community gathering at City Hall Park. Family activities, seasonal celebration, and the kind of small-town event that most growing suburbs have already outgrown. Check lavontx.gov/event-calendar for this year's date.

Apr–Sep

Lake Swimming Season

All five Lake Lavon swimming beaches open April through September. No advance registration. Parking fee at gated parks. The most-used seasonal amenity for Lavon families from late April through Labor Day.

Year-Round

Lake Lavon Fishing

Open year-round from dawn to dusk. White bass season peaks spring and summer; crappie peaks winter and spring. No license required for children under 17. Current fishing reports available from Texas Parks and Wildlife at tpwd.texas.gov/lake/lavon.

Year-Round

Camping at Lake Lavon

Reservations through Recreation.gov (1-877-444-6777) for 238 total camping sites across 16 parks. Peak season fills quickly on spring and summer weekends. Book at least a month ahead for holiday weekends. Off-season camping (October through March) is often available with minimal advance notice.

Seasonal

Lake Lavon Tournaments

Fishing tournaments and organized lake events require a Corps of Engineers permit (Lavon Lake Office, 972-442-3141). The lake hosts multiple organized fishing tournaments through the year. Check the Corps Facebook page for scheduled event dates.

City events: lavontx.gov/event-calendar  |  Lake events: U.S. Army Corps of Engineers Lavon Lake

Indoor Activities for Kids

Lavon's indoor options within city limits are limited to the City Hall Gymnasium and Community Room. For rainy days and summer heat, most families drive to Wylie (15 min) or Rockwall (25 min), which have recreation centers, libraries, and commercial indoor entertainment. This is the honest trade-off of choosing Lavon: you get the lake for most of the year, and you make a short drive for the days you need a roof over your head.

City Hall Gymnasium

Type: Community Gymnasium · Ages: All
Restored gymnasium at Lavon City Hall used for city-sponsored events, community gatherings, and elections. Not a commercial recreation center. Check with Lavon City Hall (972-843-4220) for availability during non-event periods.

City Hall Community Room

Type: Event Space · Ages: All
Formerly the historic Lavon school cafeteria, now a multipurpose room with kitchen amenities and large windows overlooking City Hall Park. Available for Lavon School reunions and city events. Reservable for community use.

Wylie Recreation Center (15 min)

Type: Recreation Center · Ages: All
Wylie Parks and Recreation runs a full recreation center with pools, fitness, and youth programs. About 15 minutes west on TX-78. The practical rainy-day and summer heat solution for Lavon families who need more indoor options than the city itself provides.

Wylie Public Library (15 min)

Type: Library Programs · Ages: All
Wylie's public library has youth programs, story times, and reading programs throughout the year. Free and air-conditioned. The closest public library to Lavon families for regular weekday programming during summer or rainy-season weeks.

Hawaiian Falls Garland (25 min)

Type: Indoor/Outdoor Water Park · Ages: All
Full water park about 25 minutes southwest via TX-78. Indoor heated attractions plus outdoor slides and pools during season. Good for birthday parties or a summer day when the lake isn't the answer. Seasonal hours, check before going.

Phyllis NeSmith Elementary (CISD)

Type: Community ISD School · Ages: K–5
801 Presidents Blvd, Lavon. The local Community ISD elementary school. CISD runs school-based programming and extracurriculars through the academic year. For families new to Lavon, the school is often the first community connection, particularly for parents of elementary-age kids.

Summer Heat Reality Check

Lavon's honest answer to hot summer days is the lake. The five beaches open April through September provide real swimming water with no admission charge beyond parking fees. When the lake is too rough or thunderstorms force everyone inside, Wylie and Rockwall are the practical fallback. Families who need daily indoor structured programming within 5 minutes of home should weigh that honestly before choosing Lavon.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions from families considering Lavon.

  • Lavon is a good fit for families who prioritize outdoor access over suburban amenities. The city sits directly adjacent to Lake Lavon, a 21,400-acre reservoir with five swimming beaches, 25 miles of trails, fishing, kayaking, and camping. The city itself is small with two main parks, a Community ISD elementary school on site, and two annual community events. Commercial entertainment, youth sports leagues, and structured programming require a 15 to 25 minute drive to Wylie or Rockwall. Families who came from cities or denser suburbs and want their kids to grow up with lake access and outdoor space regularly report Lavon as exactly what they were looking for.
  • Lavon does not have its own city-run youth sports leagues. Families with kids in organized sports typically register with Wylie Parks and Recreation (about 15 minutes west) or with Rockwall-area leagues (about 25 minutes southeast). Community Independent School District (CISD) runs athletic programs at the middle and high school levels. For elementary-age recreational leagues, Wylie is the most commonly used neighboring city. Lake Lavon itself provides informal fishing, kayaking, and paddleboarding that many families treat as the youth sports equivalent for outdoor-focused kids.
  • Yes. Lake Lavon has five designated swimming areas open April through September: Mallard Park, East Fork Park, Ticky Creek Park, Avalon Park, and Pebble Beach Park. All five are on the lake shoreline managed by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. There is no admission charge beyond parking fees at gated park areas. The water is open-lake swimming without lifeguards, so parents should supervise children closely. Always check current water conditions and weather before swimming.
  • Lavon is served by Community Independent School District (CISD), which covers Lavon, Nevada, Josephine, and Copeville across 89 square miles. CISD has more than 5,200 students across eight schools. Phyllis NeSmith Elementary is located in Lavon at 801 Presidents Boulevard and opened in 2008. For middle and high school, students attend Community ISD campuses in the broader district. You can find current school information at communityisd.org.
  • Lavon and Wylie both sit near Lake Lavon, but they are quite different cities. Wylie is significantly larger with Wylie ISD, a full recreation center, an established downtown, youth sports leagues, and more commercial retail and dining. Lavon is smaller, more rural-feeling, and directly adjacent to the lake in a way that makes outdoor access feel immediate rather than a day trip. Lavon typically offers lower home prices and less infrastructure. Wylie offers more built-out suburban amenities. Families who want more to do within 5 minutes of home tend to choose Wylie. Families who want more land, more quiet, and the lake right there tend to choose Lavon.
  • Lavon is best known for its proximity to Lake Lavon, one of the largest freshwater reservoirs in North Texas at 21,400 acres. It's also known as one of the faster-growing small cities in Collin County, with new residential development around its rural character. The city's historic City Hall building and small-town event calendar attract families who want community identity alongside growth. For buyers coming from larger metros, Lavon represents a specific DFW value proposition: Collin County quality of life at a price point below the more established suburbs to the north and west.
