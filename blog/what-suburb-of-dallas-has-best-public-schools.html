What Suburb of Dallas Has the Best Public Schools? An Honest Answer for Relocating Families

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  June 2026  ·  8 min read

Reviewed and updated June 2026 using the latest available TEA (2024-25) accountability ratings, Niche 2026 grades, and current DFW market medians.

It is the question almost every family asks me before they ask about a single house: which suburb of Dallas actually has the best public schools? In Texas your home address decides your child's public school, so this is not a side detail — it is the decision that shapes the next decade of your family's life. The honest answer is more useful than a single name, because the "best" district on paper is not always the best fit for the children you are actually raising.

Quick answer: By 2024-25 TEA accountability score, Highland Park (96) and Southlake's Carroll ISD (95) post the highest district ratings of the Dallas suburbs, with Allen ISD (91) just behind — but Frisco, Allen, McKinney, and Plano give relocating families top-rated schools at far more accessible prices, so the best suburb depends on your budget, your commute, and the specific campus your address feeds.

How "best schools" actually gets measured

Before naming a winner, it helps to know what the rankings mean — because a moving company's blog post and a relocation specialist's answer are built on very different homework. There are three sources worth trusting, and they measure different things. The Texas Education Agency (TEA) assigns every district an A–F accountability rating on a 0–100 scale, based on standardized student achievement, growth, and how well schools serve every student group. Niche blends state test data, SAT/ACT scores, and tens of thousands of parent and student reviews into a letter grade. GreatSchools rates individual campuses, which matters because a great district can still contain an average campus, and vice versa.

The takeaway: TEA tells you how a district performs on raw academic accountability, Niche captures the lived experience and college-prep strength, and GreatSchools gets you down to the specific school your address feeds. When all three line up, you have real confidence — and across the Dallas suburbs, a handful of districts line up at the very top.

The top scorers: Highland Park and Southlake

If your single priority is the highest possible district rating and budget is not the constraint, two suburbs sit at the top. Highland Park, just north of downtown Dallas, posted the highest 2024-25 TEA accountability score of any Dallas suburb at 96. Highland Park ISD also says it ranked first among 5A/6A public school districts in Texas in the 2025 release — a small, prestigious district with decades of academic consistency. Carroll ISD in Southlake is right on its heels at 95, pairing elite academics with the kind of tight-knit, one-high-school community many families want.

The trade-off is cost. Highland Park's median home price runs into the millions, and Southlake's sits north of $1.3 million — these are the most expensive school suburbs in the metro, and inventory is limited. For families whose budget reaches here, the schools are genuinely exceptional. For everyone else, the good news is that the drop-off below them is very small.

Parents assume the highest-rated district is automatically the right move. Often the smarter play is a district one or two points back — where your dollar stretches into a bigger home, a shorter commute, and a campus that fits your child just as well.

Top academics for the money: Frisco and Allen

This is where most relocating families I work with actually land — and for good reason. Frisco ISD is the largest district in Texas to earn an A in the 2024-25 TEA ratings — 56 of its 75 rated campuses scored an A — carries an A+ Niche grade, and built its reputation on a deliberate small-high-school model that keeps campuses from becoming overwhelming. With a median home price around $679K and a balanced market, Frisco delivers top-tier academics alongside modern amenities, dining, and youth sports at a scale few suburbs match.

Allen ISD may be the best pure value on this list. It earned an A from TEA with a score of 91 — among the strongest of any district its size in Texas — and an A+ Niche grade, yet its median home price of about $516K sits well below Frisco. Allen's single-high-school model produces a powerful sense of community, and its campuses regularly post 9- and 10-out-of-10 GreatSchools ratings. Families who hit Frisco's price ceiling very often find their answer here.

Kristen's Take

If you want the broadest range of homes, amenities, and an A-rated district at scale, Frisco is the safe, well-rounded choice. If you want nearly identical academics for meaningfully less money — and a tighter, one-town feel — Allen is the suburb I point budget-minded families toward first.

Established and affordable: McKinney and Plano

The two suburbs families overlook most are also two of the most practical. McKinney and Plano both carry an A Niche grade, and both come with an important honesty note: at the district level, McKinney ISD earned a TEA B (88) and Plano ISD a TEA B (82) in 2024-25, even though many of their individual campuses are A-rated. The district score blends every campus together, which is exactly why your specific address matters more than the headline grade.

What you get in return is value and maturity. McKinney, with a median around $498K, pairs strong schools with a beloved historic downtown and a high 93-rated value profile. Plano, at roughly $526K, is the established option — mature, tree-lined neighborhoods, more homes under $500,000 than most of Collin County's newer suburbs, and quick access to the Dallas core and major employers like Toyota and JPMorgan Chase. For families who value a settled community and a shorter commute over a perfect district scorecard, both deliver.

How to actually choose your suburb

There is no single "best" — there is the best fit for your family. After helping hundreds of families relocate to DFW, I find the decision almost always comes down to three honest questions: your budget, your commute, and what your child specifically needs. Work through these in order and the field narrows quickly.

Choose by budget
  • Under $550K and want a top-rated district? Start with Allen, McKinney, or Plano
  • $550K–$900K with the widest range of homes and amenities? Frisco fits most families
  • $1.3M+ and prioritizing the highest district rating? Southlake (Carroll ISD)
  • Buying at the luxury tier near central Dallas? Highland Park
Choose by commute and priorities
  • Working downtown or in central Dallas? Plano and Highland Park keep you closest
  • Employed along the Tollway, Legacy West, or The Star? Frisco, Plano, and Allen are ideal
  • Want a tight, one-high-school community feel? Southlake and Allen deliver it best
  • Need the most new construction and family amenities? Frisco and McKinney lead

A note on safety and "nicest" suburbs

Families often ask which suburb is the "safest" or "nicest" alongside the schools question. As a Realtor, I am not able to rank or compare neighborhoods by safety — that is both a Fair Housing matter and simply not something a sales professional should steer. For objective, independent safety research, use third-party resources such as NeighborhoodScout, your local police department's online crime map, and City-Data, and draw your own conclusions.

Why boundaries do not follow city lines

One mistake I see constantly: families assume a city name guarantees a school. It does not. District and individual campus boundaries do not always match city limits, and in fast-growing suburbs like Frisco, McKinney, and Plano, rezoning happens as new campuses open. A home on one street may feed a sought-after elementary while a home a few blocks away feeds a different one entirely — and parts of some suburbs even sit in a neighboring district. Before you fall in love with a specific house, confirm the exact campus that address feeds for the grade levels your children will attend. Most DFW districts publish an online attendance-zone or school-finder lookup on their website — run your prospective address through it, and I am glad to confirm the feeder pattern for any home you are considering. That single verification step prevents the most expensive surprise in the whole relocation.

What this guide does not cover

This is a public-school-district comparison. It does not weigh private and parochial schools, magnet or choice programs, charter options, or special-program fit — gifted and talented, dual-language, or special education — any of which can change the right answer for your child. If those matter to your family, tell me up front and we will build them into the search.

Frequently asked questions

What suburb of Dallas has the best public schools?

By 2024-25 TEA accountability score, Highland Park (96) and Southlake's Carroll ISD (95) lead the Dallas suburbs, with Allen ISD (91) close behind. But the best suburb for your family depends on budget, commute, and the specific campus your address feeds — Frisco and Allen pair top academics with far more accessible prices.

Is Frisco or Plano better for schools?

Frisco ISD is the largest A-rated district in Texas and carries an A+ Niche grade, while Plano ISD earned a district-level TEA B (82) in 2024-25 despite an A Niche grade and many A-rated campuses. Frisco edges ahead on district-wide ratings; Plano offers an established community at a lower entry price.

Which Dallas suburb has the best schools for the money?

Allen and McKinney deliver the strongest academics relative to price. Allen ISD earned an A (TEA 91) with a median home price well below Frisco, and McKinney ISD (TEA B, 88) is similarly affordable. Both let families on a tighter budget land in a highly rated district near Dallas.

Does Highland Park have the best schools in Dallas?

Highland Park ISD earned the top 2024-25 TEA accountability score among the Dallas suburbs (96) and ranked first among all 5A/6A districts in Texas. It is academically outstanding, but it is also the most expensive option, with median home prices several times higher than the North Dallas suburbs.

Do school district boundaries follow city lines in DFW?

No. School district and individual campus boundaries do not always match city limits. A home in one part of a suburb may feed a different campus than a home a few streets away, so always verify the exact school zone for a specific address before you buy, not just the city or district name.

The right district is the one that fits your family

So which suburb of Dallas has the best public schools? On the scorecard, Highland Park and Southlake — but for most relocating families, Frisco and Allen win on the combination of excellent schools, the right home, and a price that works, with McKinney and Plano close behind for those who want value and a settled community. The real "best" is the district whose budget, commute, and campus fit line up with your family. That is the part a ranking can never tell you — and the part I help families get right every week.
