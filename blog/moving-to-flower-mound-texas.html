Moving to Flower Mound, Texas: Newer House or Bigger Yard?

A current sales-data guide to one of the most important Flower Mound choices: newer construction with a tighter lot, or an established home with more outdoor space.

Kristen CarpentierLicensed TX Realtor® · September 23, 2026 · 12 min read

If you are moving to Flower Mound, Texas, you will probably face a choice that does not show up in a simple list of homes. Do you put more of the budget toward a newer house with fewer near-term projects, or toward an established home with a larger yard? Flower Mound offers both, but the current sales data shows that they often sit in different parts of the market.

Quick answer: At roughly the mid $600,000s in the 12 months ending September 15, 2026, Flower Mound buyers generally found either a home built in the late 2010s on about 0.14 acre or a late 1990s home on about 0.25 acre. Getting both newer construction and a substantially larger lot usually required a much higher price band.

What the current Flower Mound market shows

I reviewed NTREIS single-family sales with a Flower Mound city field that closed from September 15, 2025, through September 15, 2026. Of 863 sales in that set, 742 were built before 2016. That is about 86 percent. The result does not make Flower Mound an old-home market. It does mean that a search restricted to homes built in 2016 or later starts with a much smaller pool.

How the choice changes by price band

The tradeoff is not identical at every budget. Around the middle of the market, year built and lot size can pull in opposite directions. At higher bands, buyers gain more ways to combine newer construction and outdoor space, but the price jump can be material. That last limitation matters. Furst Ranch sits in Flower Mound, but some real estate feeds can surface its homes under Argyle and ZIP code 76226. The Town of Flower Mound calls it Furst Ranch, also known as Flower Mound Ranch. A search that uses only the obvious city name may miss relevant inventory. Search by map, community name, builder, postal city, and legal address before concluding that a category has no homes.

Flower Mound also has a formal Cross Timbers Conservation Development District. Its standards help explain why some western areas do not follow the same development pattern as a typical compact subdivision. That does not make every western property large or every eastern property small. It is a reminder that location, utilities, roads, and the parcel itself matter alongside the age of the house.

A practical decision path

Start with the thing you would resent giving up after the novelty wears off. If the yard is central to how you use the home, treat lot size as a first filter and decide what condition work you can reasonably absorb. If fewer projects and newer systems matter more, start with year built and let the acceptable lot range widen only when the budget supports it.

If both newer construction and a larger lot are firm requirements, expect a narrower pool, a higher price band, or a wider geographic search.

Here is a fictional example, not a client story. Imagine a household with a $700,000 target that wants room for a play structure and a dog but does not want a long renovation list. The sales data suggests that a strict 2016-or-later filter may protect the condition preference while undermining the yard goal. A better search could include well-maintained late 1990s and 2000s homes, then use age and inspection findings to price the real project load.

The reverse can also happen. A buyer may say that a large lot matters, then realize that weekend maintenance, pool responsibility, irrigation, and landscaping compete with the time they hoped to protect. The goal is not to choose the bigger number. It is to choose the property burden and daily use that match the life you are planning.

The survey check

Acreage does not tell you how much yard is usable. Review the survey for easements, setbacks, encroachments, pool placement, and the shape of the lot. Walk the drainage path after rain if possible. A wide side easement or steep grade can change what the headline acreage means.

What to verify for either home

An older home is not automatically a repair problem, and a newer home is not automatically low maintenance. A 1998 home may have updated systems and careful ownership. A recent home may still need warranty follow-up, drainage correction, or a close look at the builder documents. Condition belongs to the individual property.

Run these checks before comparing two Flower Mound homes
  • Request the seller's disclosure, inspection history when available, and permits for material work.
  • Record actual ages and service history for the roof, HVAC units, water heaters, windows, and major appliances.
  • Review the survey, drainage, easements, setbacks, retaining walls, and pool or accessory structure placement.
  • Price current property taxes from the exact parcel and ask about HOA dues, assessments, and any district charges.
  • Obtain an insurance quote for the exact address, roof profile, claims inputs, and coverage choices.
  • Verify the assigned public schools for the exact address and the relevant school year with the district.

For the financial side, compare the complete monthly obligation rather than sale price alone. Property tax, insurance, HOA charges, utilities, maintenance, and likely near-term work can erase an apparent advantage. The Dallas cost of living guide explains the major categories, and the Dallas home buying guide gives you a wider due diligence checklist.

School information deserves the same address-level discipline. Attendance boundaries and assignments can change. Confirm the exact property and school year through the responsible district before relying on a listing, map, or neighborhood label. For a broader relocation plan, see moving to DFW with kids.

How to search without hiding the tradeoff

Do not begin with one giant search that mixes every age, lot, and price. Build two or three saved searches that reveal the decision. One can hold the budget and minimum size steady while prioritizing homes built in 2016 or later. Another can hold the budget steady while setting a real minimum lot. A third can test the combination at the upper end of the range.

Then compare actual homes in pairs. Put one newer, tighter-lot property next to one established, larger-lot property. Record the monthly cost, confirmed system ages, likely work, yard use, drive pattern, and address-specific school check. The comparison gets much clearer when every home answers the same questions.

Frequently asked questions

Is it hard to find newer homes in Flower Mound?

Newer homes exist, but they were a minority of the reviewed sales. In the NTREIS Flower Mound city set for the 12 months ending September 15, 2026, 121 of 863 single-family sales were built in 2016 or later. Search by map and community name too, since city labels can vary.

Do older Flower Mound homes usually have bigger yards?

The reviewed sales show a clear group-level pattern, but not a rule for every property. Near the mid $600,000s, the established cohort had a 0.247-acre median lot versus 0.138 acre for the newer cohort. Confirm actual lot size, shape, easements, drainage, and usable area for each home.

Can I get a newer Flower Mound home on a large lot?

Yes, but the combination was limited and expensive in the reviewed period. Twelve sales built in 2016 or later on 0.35 to 0.49 acre had a $1.582 million median sale price. That small sample provides market context, not a forecast or a promise that a similar home is available now.

What should I inspect in an older Flower Mound home?

Start with the specific home's roof, HVAC, water heaters, windows, foundation history, drainage, electrical system, plumbing, pool equipment, and prior improvements. Age alone does not establish condition. Review disclosures and permits, hire appropriate inspectors, and use actual findings to build a repair and replacement reserve.

What should I verify in a newer Flower Mound community?

Confirm the exact base price and included features, lot premium, design selections, completion terms, warranties, HOA documents, taxes, assessments, utilities, construction conditions, and what amenities are open today. Treat future roads, retail, schools, parks, and community features as plans until the controlling documents confirm otherwise.

How should I compare school information for two homes?

Verify each exact address through the responsible school district for the school year that matters to you. Do not assume that the community name, listing text, or a nearby campus determines assignment. Boundaries and programs can change, so contact the district when the answer is important to the purchase decision.

The strongest Flower Mound search is not the one with the most filters. It is the one that makes your tradeoff visible. Decide whether condition or outdoor space gets priority, compare current homes with the same worksheet, and verify the exact property before you let a category label make the choice for you.
