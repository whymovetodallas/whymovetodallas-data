Texas Property Tax for Out-of-State Buyers: What DFW Families Should Budget

There is no shortcut around the property-tax line. There is a smarter way to read it.

Kristen CarpentierLicensed TX Realtor® · September 1, 2026 · 9 min read

Quick answer: Texas property tax can be higher than what many California, New York, and Illinois buyers expect, but the bill depends on the exact address, exemptions, and taxing districts. Compare an address-level estimate with your household income and total housing costs before deciding whether a DFW move works for you.

Property tax is the question that comes up after a family sees a North Texas home price that looks manageable. It should. Texas does not levy a state property tax. Local taxing units do, and the stack can include a city, county, school district, college, hospital district, and sometimes a special district. That makes a headline rate useful only as a starting point.

What a Texas Property Tax Rate Includes

The Texas Comptroller explains that local taxing units set their own rates. That means a city name is not enough to price a home. For a planning reference, the Tax Foundation lists 2023 owner-occupied effective rates of 0.71 percent in California, 1.60 percent in New York, 2.07 percent in Illinois, and 1.58 percent in Texas. Those are state averages, not quotes for a particular house. Start with the Comptroller's property-tax guidance, then confirm the address through the county appraisal district.

A newer community can have an additional assessment that changes the answer materially, so never use a citywide figure as a final payment estimate.

The Tax Math Needs the Income-Tax Line Too

A higher property-tax bill is real. It is not the entire relocation comparison. Texas has no individual state income tax. California, New York, and Illinois all do. The benefit varies by taxable income, filing status, deductions, credits, and local tax exposure, so this is a CPA question rather than a universal savings claim. The right exercise is to put your existing state income-tax line, expected Texas property tax, insurance, tolls, and utilities on the same page.

Kristen's practical rule: If a spreadsheet shows only home price and a state average, it is not ready to guide a move. Use it to make a shortlist, then price the specific homes you would actually buy.

The Homestead Exemption Is a Homeowner Step

For a qualifying primary residence, Texas requires school districts to offer a $140,000 residence-homestead exemption. The exemption applies to the school-district portion of the tax calculation, not automatically to every part of the combined rate. Cities, counties, and special districts may offer additional local exemptions. The Comptroller's January 2026 property-tax guide is the source to review, along with your appraisal district's application process.

Do not assume the exemption is handled at closing. A buyer needs to qualify and apply with the appropriate appraisal district. Ask the title company and your lender what they are using in the projected payment, then make the application part of your after-closing checklist. This article is educational, not tax advice.

Three Costs That Belong Beside the Tax Bill

A tax rate is only one address-level input. MUD and PID charges can apply in growth areas, including parts of Prosper, Celina, and north Frisco. Homeowners insurance can change quickly in North Texas because wind and hail deductibles matter. And a northern-suburb commute may make tolls a recurring budget item. None is a reason to reject a community. Each is a reason to compare the whole address rather than a marketing headline.

A Five-Step Address Comparison

  1. Get the current assessed value, taxing units, and exemption status for each address.
  2. Ask whether a MUD, PID, or other special-district assessment applies.
  3. Use a lender's estimate with your intended purchase price and down payment.
  4. Get a homeowners-insurance quote that states the deductible.
  5. Review the complete monthly cost with your lender and a CPA before making a financial decision.

There is no universally better tax state or universally better suburb. A family that wants to live near a particular employer, needs a particular home layout, or has a different income profile will reach a different answer. That is exactly why the comparison should start with your addresses and your numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Texas property tax higher than California property tax?

At the statewide level, the Tax Foundation's 2023 owner-occupied effective-rate table shows Texas above California. That does not predict an individual bill. California purchase history, local assessments, a Texas homestead exemption, and the exact DFW taxing units all matter. Compare a specific California bill with a specific Texas address.

What is the Texas homestead exemption?

For a qualifying primary residence, Texas school districts must offer a $140,000 residence-homestead exemption. It reduces the value used for the school-district tax calculation. Local taxing units may offer additional exemptions. Confirm eligibility, timing, and application requirements with the county appraisal district after purchase.

Do MUD and PID charges count as property tax?

They are address-level assessments that can appear alongside the ordinary local tax stack, so they need to be included in a buyer's budget. Their amount and duration vary by community. Ask for the current levy, the issuing district, and the expected payoff or expiration schedule before relying on a payment estimate.

Does no state income tax offset higher Texas property tax?

It can, but the answer is personal. It depends on taxable income, filing status, deductions, the home price, local taxes, insurance, and other recurring costs. A lender can model the housing payment, while a CPA can help compare state tax treatment. Avoid a blanket savings claim.
