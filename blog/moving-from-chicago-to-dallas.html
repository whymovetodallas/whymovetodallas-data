Moving from Chicago to Dallas: A Family Relocation Guide

By Kristen Carpentier, Licensed Texas Realtor, TREC #760457 · July 28, 2026 · 12 minute read

Quick answer: Moving from Chicago to Dallas can remove Illinois state income tax from the household equation and offer a wider range of newer single family housing. The practical tradeoffs are car dependence, intense summer heat, address specific property taxes, and a metroplex where commute direction matters. Choose the job corridor first, build an all in ownership budget, and verify schools by exact address.

Chicago families usually understand the idea of a metro area, but Dallas-Fort Worth operates on a much larger geographic canvas. Dallas is only one employment center. Plano, Frisco, Las Colinas, Richardson, Fort Worth, and the airport corridor all pull commuters in different directions. That changes how you should search for a home.

The goal is not to find a Texas replica of Lincoln Park, Naperville, Evanston, or Oak Park. It is to identify the routines behind your preference and test DFW neighborhoods against them. Use this guide with the DFW Family Relocation Guide and the site's Suburb Matcher.

Chicago vs. Dallas: Four Family Tradeoffs That Change the Move

The useful comparison is not which city “wins.” It is how a move changes the routines that shape your family’s budget, time, and home search. Before choosing a DFW community, test the four tradeoffs below against your actual work, school, and household needs.

What may feel familiar in ChicagoWhat to test in DFW
A transit routine and a known commuteUse the exact office address, arrival time, and in-office schedule to test the drive, tolls, parking, and whether one or two vehicles are realistic.
Illinois income tax in the monthly pictureCompare the full ownership and household budget: Texas property taxes, insurance, electricity, transportation, tolls, maintenance, and HOA dues can all change the math.
Dense housing and established city or suburban routinesDecide how much home, lot, maintenance, walkability, and access to everyday destinations your family actually wants before comparing addresses.
Winter preparation and snow-related costsPlan for sustained summer heat, air-conditioning use, drainage, roof condition, wind and hail deductibles, and short freeze or ice disruptions.
Use this as a decision tool: If the new routine works for the job corridor, budget, school process, and home type your family needs, the move is on firmer ground. If it does not, a lower list price or a familiar suburb name will not solve the mismatch.

Build the Replacement Budget

Illinois withholds individual income tax at 4.95 percent. Texas does not impose an individual state income tax. That difference is meaningful for many households, but it is only one line in the comparison. Property taxes, homeowners insurance, electricity, transportation, tolls, maintenance, childcare, and HOA dues can change too.

Source: Illinois Department of Revenue withholding tax rates. Consult a qualified tax adviser for your filing situation.

Costs that may decrease

  • State individual income tax
  • Price per square foot in many comparisons
  • Winter heating and snow related expenses
  • Some city parking expenses

Costs that may increase

  • Vehicle ownership and fuel
  • Summer electricity
  • Home maintenance on a larger property
  • Property tax or insurance, depending on the address

Use the same monthly categories on both sides. If the Dallas home is larger, include realistic reserves for HVAC service, roof replacement, landscaping, pest control, and repairs. Compare the life you plan to live, not only the list price.

A good budget test: Keep the home price constant, then stress test the monthly result with a higher insurance quote, a realistic summer electric bill, and two vehicles. If the plan still feels comfortable, the shortlist is on firmer ground.

Translate the Chicago Commute Before Choosing a Suburb

A familiar suburb name is not a commute strategy. In DFW, ten miles can feel easy in one direction and difficult in another. Ask the employer for the exact office address, expected in office days, arrival window, and likelihood that the team will change locations.

Downtown or UptownCompare Richardson, selected Plano areas, Lake Highlands, and closer Dallas neighborhoods based on route and housing needs.
Plano or FriscoCompare Plano, Frisco, Allen, and McKinney before assuming a southern commute will work.
Las Colinas or DFW AirportCompare Coppell, Irving, Grapevine, Colleyville, and nearby corridors.

Rail can be helpful in selected locations, but the regional transit network is not a direct replacement for the CTA or Metra. DART rail serves particular corridors, and many suburban trips still require a car. If your household wants one vehicle, test the actual address for walking access, school transportation, groceries, medical care, and the workplace.

Housing, Property Taxes, and the Offer Budget

DFW often gives Chicago movers more choices among newer homes, attached garages, larger lots, planned amenities, and recently built schools. Older close in neighborhoods may offer mature trees, distinctive architecture, and shorter routes to Dallas employment centers. Neither is automatically better. They carry different maintenance patterns and price structures.

Texas property taxes are local and vary by overlapping taxing entities. Eligible owners may apply for a residence homestead exemption. Texas school districts provide a $140,000 general residence homestead exemption for an eligible primary residence; other local exemptions and the final tax bill vary by address and eligibility. Review the Texas Comptroller homestead exemption FAQ.

For every property under serious consideration, obtain the current tax record, current exemptions, estimated taxes based on the expected purchase price, an insurance quote, HOA documents, and a careful inspection. Texas inspections deserve attention to foundation observations, drainage, roof condition, HVAC performance, windows, and insurability. A seller's current tax bill is not a reliable estimate of your future bill.

Plan for the Climate Change

Trading a Chicago winter for a North Texas summer changes the household maintenance calendar. Dallas winters are generally milder, but freezes and ice events still occur. Summer heat is sustained, and severe thunderstorms, hail, high winds, and tornado risk are part of regional planning.

SpringService the air conditioning, check drainage, review the roof, and prepare a severe weather plan.
SummerExpect high electric use, protect pets and children from heat, and schedule outdoor activity carefully.
FallClean gutters, review trees near the roof, service heating equipment, and check weather seals.
WinterKnow how to protect pipes, monitor freeze forecasts, and keep supplies for short ice related disruptions.

Ask an insurance professional about wind and hail deductibles, roof payment provisions, water backup, and replacement cost. Do not compare premiums without comparing coverage.

Verify Schools by Address, Not City Name

School district boundaries cross city lines, and a mailing address may not match the district name. Some cities are split among multiple districts. Assigned campuses, programs, transportation, and boundaries can change.

  1. Choose a property address, not only a subdivision.
  2. Use the district's official boundary lookup.
  3. Confirm the assigned campuses directly with the district.
  4. Ask about enrollment timing, transportation, special education services, language programs, and extracurricular priorities.
  5. Repeat the check before signing a contract or lease.

Ratings are a starting point, not a substitute for direct research. Use the site’s DFW school districts guide to compare objective district information, then confirm the assigned campus and programs directly with the district. Your agent can provide objective sources and property information, while your family defines the educational criteria that matter.

Decide whether to rent or buy without forcing the timeline

A lease is not a failed relocation plan. It can be a deliberate research period when a household has not experienced the commute, does not yet know the long term office location, or needs time to understand school services. A six or twelve month rental may also separate a Chicago sale from a Texas purchase and reduce the pressure to accept a house that only partly works.

Buying sooner can be reasonable when the household has a stable job corridor, a long enough ownership horizon, a comfortable cash reserve, and confidence in the target area. The purchase decision should include closing costs, inspection findings, insurance terms, likely maintenance, and the possibility that the first year brings several setup expenses. Keep funds available after closing rather than using every available dollar for the down payment.

If you rent, verify the assigned schools by the rental address, review pet and vehicle rules, and confirm commute conditions. If you buy, test at least two alternate communities so that the choice reflects a comparison rather than the first attractive listing. Neither path removes the need to understand the region.

Use a decision date: Set a date by which the family will choose renting or buying, and list the facts required for that choice. This prevents an open ended search while allowing time for real evidence.

Tour DFW as a resident, not a visitor

An orientation trip should include school arrival traffic, an evening drive from the workplace, a grocery stop, a park or activity your family would actually use, and a look at the neighborhood at another time of day. Spend less time on landmarks and more time rehearsing Tuesday. Ask for objective property and market information, then record the household's reaction to noise, street layout, home condition, and driving time while it is fresh.

A Practical Moving Timeline

Three to six months out

Confirm work arrangements, estimate the all in budget, decide whether to rent or buy, and identify the first three geographic zones to research. Families selling in Illinois should coordinate the sale and Texas purchase conservatively.

Six to ten weeks out

Visit DFW for an orientation tour. Drive commute routes at realistic hours, tour both older and newer housing, verify school procedures, and compare routine destinations. If purchasing, secure lender documentation and begin property specific insurance conversations early.

Two to four weeks out

Finalize movers, utilities, travel, school records, vehicle plans, and closing or lease details. Keep critical documents, medications, valuables, and several days of essentials with you. Build margin into the schedule for weather and delivery changes.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Dallas cheaper than Chicago?

It can be, but the answer depends on your current location, target DFW community, housing payment, property taxes, vehicles, insurance, utilities, and routines. Start with the total monthly budget, then compare two or three realistic addresses instead of assuming a lower list price will produce a lower cost of living.

Does Texas have an individual state income tax?

Texas does not impose an individual state income tax, while Illinois uses a flat individual income tax rate. That difference is only one part of the move budget. Compare it with expected property taxes, insurance, transportation, utilities, and any change in household income before estimating your overall savings.

Can we live in DFW with one car?

Some households can, particularly in selected walkable or rail-connected locations, but many suburban routines are easier with two vehicles. Test the exact address for school transportation, groceries, medical care, work trips, and evening activities. A map can suggest convenience, but a trial drive at the times you will actually travel is more useful.

Which DFW suburb is most like a Chicago suburb?

There is no reliable one-to-one match because DFW job corridors, school boundaries, housing ages, lot sizes, and transportation patterns differ. Start with objective features such as commute, housing type, walkability, exact school assignment, and airport access. Then compare several communities rather than trying to recreate one Chicago neighborhood.

Should we rent before buying?

Renting can lower decision pressure if the office location, school plan, or preferred corridor is uncertain. It can also give your family time to test routines before committing. Buying sooner may fit a stable job corridor and a longer ownership horizon, provided the full budget and property-specific due diligence are comfortable.
