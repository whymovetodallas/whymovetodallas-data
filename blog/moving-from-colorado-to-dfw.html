Moving from Colorado to DFW: An Honest Family Relocation Guide

The house search is only the start. The climate, school process, and cost stack decide the move.

Kristen CarpentierLicensed TX Realtor® · September 3, 2026 · 9 min read

Quick answer: Moving from Colorado to DFW can change the home options and remove Colorado's 4.4 percent state income-tax line, but it also means higher property-tax planning, a much hotter summer, and a metro where work location determines the best suburb. Compare your two addresses, not two state averages.

Colorado is the largest uncovered origin state in the site's current audience data, with 134 clean users in the last 90 days. Denver and Colorado Springs are the concentration. That does not mean every Colorado family should make this move. It means this guide should be candid about the practical questions that show up after the job offer or the first Zillow search.

The Money Conversation Is More Than No Income Tax

Colorado's Department of Revenue states that its individual income tax rate is 4.40 percent. Texas has no individual state income tax. That is a meaningful difference for many households, but it is not a blank check. The repository's Tax Foundation source lists 2023 owner-occupied effective property-tax rates of 0.49 percent in Colorado and 1.58 percent in Texas. Your purchase price, income, and local districts decide what the exchange means.

These are reference figures, not a personalized forecast. Texas property tax is local, not statewide. A DFW estimate can include school, city, county, college, hospital, and special districts. In growing communities, a MUD or PID can be another line item. Use the Texas property-tax guide as the next step, then verify each address with the appraisal district and lender.

Choose a Home by the Life You Need

Colorado families often begin by comparing square footage. That is useful, but the daily map is more important. In DFW, a home that looks ideal can put a parent on a toll road every weekday or create a school pickup routine that does not work. Start with work location, then decide which home type, lot, and school options fit the family you have now.

That approach also avoids making a neighborhood recommendation based on subjective labels. School information, house options, distance, and tax records are objective comparison points. For any safety question, use third-party tools such as NeighborhoodScout, City-Data, and the appropriate local police department's public crime map to make your own call.

Approach Schools at the Address Level

Texas school districts are independent local systems, and city boundaries can cross district lines. A district's published accountability information and an address-specific boundary check are more useful than a citywide generalization. Start with the DFW school-district hub, then ask the district to confirm attendance for the homes on your shortlist before you write an offer.

For families looking north, Prosper and Argyle are relevant starting points because Colorado is a top feeder for both in the current audience analysis. That is an audience signal, not a promise about fit. Prosper is a larger growth setting with Prosper ISD and some Frisco ISD coverage. Argyle has Argyle ISD and some Denton ISD coverage. Confirm the precise address, not just the listing headline.

The Climate Adjustment Is a Daily-Life Change

A Colorado family already understands weather preparation. North Texas changes the kind of preparation. Summer heat affects outdoor routines, electricity use, car comfort, and the time of day families use parks. Hail risk affects insurance deductibles. Winter is generally shorter, but ice events can disrupt a region that is built around driving. The adjustment is not good or bad on its own. It is real, and a scouting trip should include a look at the ordinary weekday rhythm, not just model homes.

Community-research note: resident discussions repeatedly make the same practical point: do not trade a Colorado outdoor routine for a DFW address without visiting the parks, errands, and school route in the conditions you will actually live with. Treat that as a scouting question, not a lifestyle promise.

A Starting Shortlist, Not a Ranking

Use a shortlist as a way to schedule research, not as a declaration that one suburb is best. Prosper can suit a family that wants newer housing options and accepts a growing-community cost stack. Argyle can be worth exploring for a smaller-town scale and different home or lot options. Frisco and McKinney offer other North DFW starting points. Each has different work-location, tax, and address-level school questions.

After you name the four levers that matter most, use the matcher to turn a broad DFW map into a research list.

A Better Colorado to DFW Relocation Sequence

  1. Anchor the search to the office, airport, or daily work location.
  2. Set a total monthly housing number that includes tax, insurance, tolls, and summer electricity.
  3. Use district sources to check the exact addresses you are considering.
  4. Visit at least two areas on an ordinary weekday, including the routes you will use.
  5. Ask for an address-level tax and insurance review before the option period ends.

The useful outcome is not a generic verdict on Texas. It is a short list you can defend with your family's actual priorities. The moving to DFW with kids guide can help organize the wider school and relocation timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Texas cheaper than Colorado for a family?

It can be, but it depends on the two addresses and the household's income. Texas has no individual state income tax, while Colorado's stated rate is 4.40 percent. Texas property tax, insurance, electricity, tolls, and any special-district charges can offset some of that difference. Compare a complete monthly budget.

What should Colorado buyers know about Texas property tax?

Texas property tax is set by local taxing units rather than the state. A DFW property can have school, city, county, college, hospital, and special-district lines. A qualifying homestead exemption can reduce the school-district taxable value. Verify the current address-level estimate with the appraisal district and lender.

Which DFW suburbs should a Colorado family explore?

Start with the places that work for the job location, budget, desired home type, and school-boundary needs. Prosper, Argyle, Frisco, and McKinney are useful North DFW research stops, but none fits every household. Compare exact addresses and district boundaries before treating a city name as a decision.

How should we research schools before moving to DFW?

Use published district accountability information and confirm attendance boundaries for each exact address. DFW city and district lines do not always match. A suburb page can help frame a visit, but the district itself is the right source for enrollment and address-specific attendance questions.
