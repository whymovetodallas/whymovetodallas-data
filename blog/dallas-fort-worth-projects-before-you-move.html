10 Dallas-Fort Worth Projects to Know Before You Move

Ten parks, venues, transit hubs and new neighborhoods taking shape around DFW, what is open, what is being built and what is still a plan, and how to use them on a scouting trip.

Kristen CarpentierLicensed TX Realtor®  ·  November 18, 2026  ·  14 min read

If you're thinking about moving to Dallas-Fort Worth with your family, it helps to picture an ordinary Saturday here, not just a house. Where would you take the kids? Where would you eat? How easy is it to fly back and see the people you're leaving behind? Ten projects around the region are about to change some of those answers, and a few of them may help you decide which part of DFW to look at first.

I'm a Realtor who has lived and raised four kids here, so I'll be straight with you about one thing: some of these are open or under construction, and some are still plans. How much weight I'd give each one depends on when you're moving. I'll show you what the official sources say, separate what's built from what's proposed, and point you to what you can actually see on a visit today.

Quick answer: The ten projects are South Sunset in Flower Mound, Grand Park in Frisco, the Perot Museum's outdoor expansion in Dallas, the Sunset Amphitheater in McKinney, Terminal F at DFW Airport, Landmark in Denton, Haggard Farm in Plano, Addison Junction, the new Children's Health and UT Southwestern pediatric campus in Dallas, and The Marco in Prosper. Use them to plan where you spend a scouting trip, and treat anything not yet built as a plan that can still change.

Where the Ten Projects Are

These projects are spread across the whole region, from Lake Grapevine and the airport in the middle, to Denton in the northwest, Frisco, Prosper and McKinney to the north, and Plano, Addison and Dallas toward the center. That spread is useful: it means you can tie a project to the part of DFW you're already curious about rather than driving across the whole Metroplex to see everything.

1. South Sunset, Flower Mound

Start at Lake Grapevine, on the edge of Grapevine and Flower Mound. It's a U.S. Army Corps of Engineers reservoir between Dallas and Fort Worth with marinas, boat ramps, fishing, camping and trails, and it's already there for you to enjoy. On its shore in Flower Mound, Lakeside Village plans South Sunset, a seven-acre waterfront dining and entertainment district. The developer's plans show lake-view restaurants, a public plaza, a wedding chapel, walking trails and an amphitheater overlooking the lake, with underground parking to keep cars out of sight.

The Lakeside Village site describes South Sunset's amphitheater and a planned Bluff Trail, alongside the existing Northshore Trail, which runs more than 21 miles along the lake. The developer lists South Sunset as "Coming Soon" without a date, so I'd treat the timing as open.

What you can see on a visit today

Walk Lakeside Village's existing restaurants and shops, and stop at the Sunset Point overlook for the view. That tells you more about living nearby than any rendering will. My Flower Mound page covers the rest of the town.

2. Grand Park, Frisco

Frisco broke ground on the first phase of Grand Park on April 27, 2026. The City of Frisco says the park will span 1,011 acres when complete, from the Dallas North Tollway west to Lake Lewisville near Lewisville. For a sense of scale, New York's Central Park is about 843 acres.

The first phase, called the Civic Room, covers about 58 acres. The city lists an amphitheater, shade structures, restrooms, an orchard and Arrowhead Pond, a pond of about three acres planned for kayaking and paddleboarding. At the April groundbreaking, the city anticipated Phase I opening in October 2027, and its project page lists construction completion in fall 2027. Both are projections.

If you're picturing a family that spends weekends outside, Frisco is worth a close look for reasons beyond this park. My Frisco page walks through neighborhoods, schools and commutes.

3. Perot Museum Outdoor Expansion, Dallas

The Perot Museum of Nature and Science in downtown Dallas has been one of my family's favorites for years. In October 2026 it announced an outdoor expansion. CultureMap Dallas reported that the approximately four-acre project will double the museum's exhibition space, with groundbreaking planned for early 2027 and a public opening targeted for late 2028.

The headline exhibit is a fire tornado demonstrating how wildfires behave, which KERA News describes as 30 feet tall. The museum's renderings also show a water vortex, geysers, gardens and a lightning and thunder pavilion. You can see them on the museum's expansion page.

4. Sunset Amphitheater, McKinney

McKinney's Sunset Amphitheater sits on a 46-acre site at the northeast corner of U.S. 75 and State Highway 121. The City of McKinney says it will seat up to 20,000 at full capacity and about 5,000 in a multi-season configuration, and lists completion in summer 2027. Community Impact describes a covered canopy, fire pit suites, heating for cooler months and a members-only club in partnership with Troy Aikman.

Bisnow reports that the opening, first planned for 2026, moved to the first quarter of 2027 as the project grew; VENU's own announcement covers the land deal. No date for the first concert has been announced. If live music matters to your family, it's a reason to look at the northern suburbs along U.S. 75. McKinney's historic downtown is worth a walk on its own, and my McKinney page covers what it's like to live there.

5. Terminal F, DFW Airport

If most of your family is staying where you live now and you'll fly back a few times a year, the airport may matter more than you expect. Terminal F will be DFW's sixth terminal, served entirely by American Airlines.

The DFW Airport newsroom says the first phase is anticipated to open 15 gates in 2027, and the estimated $4 billion terminal is planned to deliver a total of 31 gates. A new Skylink station is being built from prefabricated modules lifted into place, which lets construction move while the airport keeps running. The airport has not dated the later phases.

6. Landmark, Denton

Landmark is a 3,200-acre master-planned community in southwest Denton from Hillwood, the Perot family's development company. Hillwood says that at full build-out it will have 6,000 single-family homes and 3,000 apartments, plus parks, trails and preserved open space around Pilot Knob, a natural rock formation. Community Impact reports an H-E-B under construction. The Landmark FAQ says the H-E-B is currently planned to open in early 2027.

For families, the part I'd look at closely is schools. Landmark plans three Denton ISD school sites, two elementary and one middle school, and Denton ISD has secured the first one, a 12-acre elementary site. Planned schools aren't open yet, so confirm with Denton ISD which campuses serve a specific address before you buy. My Denton page explains why Denton feels more like its own city than a suburb.

7. Haggard Farm, Plano

Haggard Farm is a mixed-use development on nearly 130 acres near Spring Creek Parkway and Parkwood Boulevard, just east of the Dallas North Tollway. The developer, Stillwater Capital, says the Haggard family has owned the land since 1856.

The plans center on a walkable retail and restaurant village. Community Impact describes a restaurant and bar called The Almanac 1856 and an event barn called Haggard Hall. Apartments and townhomes are part of the first construction, and a boutique hotel, office space and green space are planned for later phases. The project site lists the retail village opening in fall 2027. My Plano page covers the neighborhoods around it.

8. Addison Junction

Addison Junction is planned around the Addison Silver Line station, next to Addison Circle Park. The Town of Addison describes a $240 million mixed-use project, with the town investing $40 million in infrastructure and two public garages. Dallas Innovates reported that plans include The Hangar, a 12,000-square-foot aviation-inspired event venue, a 155,550-square-foot mass timber office building, a 140-room boutique hotel, about 30,000 square feet of entertainment space and a beer garden. The town projects more than 1,600 permanent jobs at build-out.

The location is the point. DART's Silver Line connects Addison by commuter rail with Plano, Richardson, Carrollton and DFW Airport, and the district ties into Addison Circle Park and the Cotton Belt Trail. My Addison page covers living there.

9. The New Pediatric Campus, Dallas

Children's Health and UT Southwestern are building a new pediatric campus in the Southwestern Medical District, and in September 2026 they placed the final beam on the hospital frame. UT Southwestern describes a $5 billion campus of more than 5 million square feet, nearly double the size of AT&T Stadium, centered on the 12-story Moody Children's Hospital. Children's Health lists 552 inpatient beds across three towers, and the campus is projected to open in 2031.

Children's Health's expansion page also lists two helicopter pads, a Level I trauma center and a new fetal care center (see also Building Design and Construction).

10. The Marco, Prosper

The Marco is a proposed 143-acre, roughly $2 billion development at the northeast corner of the Dallas North Tollway and U.S. 380, modeled on an Italian waterfront village. Community Impact describes a 15.5-acre lagoon with canals and up to 4,400 residential units, figures also reported by Texas Construction Hub. The Town of Prosper says the approved master plan includes more than 1 million square feet of Class A office space, up to two full-service hotels, a market hall and entertainment venues.

The developer, Jeffory Blackard, is also behind Adriatica Village in McKinney, so you can visit Adriatica today to get a feel for the style. Prosper approved the zoning in October 2026, and the approved plan calls for phases over several years. Prosper's economic development office calls the Tollway and U.S. 380 "one of the region's premier growth corridors." My Prosper page covers what the town is like now.

Turning the List Into a Scouting Trip

A list of projects is only useful if it helps you decide where to spend your time. Here's how I'd use it with a family visiting for the first time:

  1. Start with your work location. Pick the part of DFW your commute points to, then look at the projects in that area. A park you'd only reach on a weekend road trip won't shape your routine the way one near home will. Test the drive at the times you'd make it on Rush Hour Planner.
  2. Visit what's open today. Two pieces of these projects are already open: Landmark's model homes in Denton and DART's Silver Line through Addison. Nearby, Lakeside Village and Lake Grapevine, Frisco's existing parks, the current Perot Museum, downtown McKinney, Addison Circle Park and Adriatica Village are all there now. Leave the construction sites themselves off the itinerary.
  3. Match the timing to your move. If you're moving within a year, give more weight to what's open or close to opening. If you're several years out, the projects still in planning are worth watching, not counting on.
  4. Check schools at the address, not the city. School district and campus assignments follow the address. Confirm them with the district before you make an offer. My DFW school district guide is a good place to start.

Questions Families Ask

Which of these projects can I visit on a trip right now?

Landmark's model homes in Denton and DART's Silver Line through Addison are open now. Nearby, Lake Grapevine and Lakeside Village in Flower Mound, the current Perot Museum in Dallas, downtown McKinney, Addison Circle Park and Adriatica Village in McKinney are open too. The rest of the projects are under construction or still in planning as of November 15, 2026.

When will Grand Park in Frisco open?

The City of Frisco projects the first phase, the Civic Room, to finish construction in fall 2027, and anticipated an October 2027 opening when it broke ground. That phase covers about 58 acres with an amphitheater, an orchard and Arrowhead Pond. The full park will span about 1,011 acres and is being built in phases, so check the city's Grand Park page before you plan a visit.

When does Terminal F at DFW Airport open?

DFW Airport anticipates the first 15 gates opening in 2027. The terminal is planned to reach 31 gates in total and will be served entirely by American Airlines. The airport has not dated the later phases, so check its newsroom for the current schedule.

Will Landmark in Denton have its own schools?

Landmark plans three Denton ISD school sites, two elementary and one middle school, and Denton ISD has secured the first elementary site. None is open yet, so confirm which campuses serve a specific address with Denton ISD before you buy.

Is The Marco in Prosper approved?

Prosper approved the zoning in October 2026. The approved plan calls for phases over several years, so the lagoon, homes, offices and hotels would not all arrive at once. If you're buying nearby, check the Town of Prosper's updates for the current timeline before you make an offer.
