Moving from Atlanta to Dallas: What Georgia Families Should Know

Kristen Carpentier · Licensed Texas Realtor®October 12, 2026 · 10 min read

Quick answer: Moving from Atlanta to Dallas-Fort Worth does not guarantee lower housing costs or a tax saving. Texas currently has no individual income tax, while property-tax bills depend on local taxing units, exemptions, and the property. Compare current housing data on the same basis, confirm school assignment by address, and model the full monthly cost for specific homes before deciding.

Watch Kristen’s 8-minute Dallas Relocation Guide, then use the DFW Family Relocation Guide for the next research steps.

The latest audience snapshot, dated September 28, 2026 and covering June 29 through September 27, records 97 clean users from Georgia, including 31 from Atlanta. Georgia is a meaningful reader source, and Atlanta is its largest listed city group in that snapshot; the figures do not establish a growth trend or majority. The comparison may resonate: both are large Sun Belt metros with similar climates, broad footprints, and corporate relocation activity. Still, moving from metro Atlanta changes the cost equation in ways that a generic relocation story can miss.

A housing discount alone may not make the case for a move from metro Atlanta. Compare the household income-tax picture with property tax, housing, school assignment, and commute at the addresses under consideration.

One geography note before the numbers. "Dallas" means the city of Dallas. "Dallas-Fort Worth," or DFW, means the whole region, including Fort Worth, Celina, and other suburbs, each with its own tax rate and school district. Most relocating families consider suburbs. The figures below come from different source snapshots; each table and source note identifies the measure where available, so check the cited geography and date before comparing them.

The Income Tax Line

Georgia has been steadily cutting its individual income tax. The state moved to a flat structure and has stepped the rate down in successive years. For tax year 2026, House Bill 463 reduced the flat rate from 5.19 percent to 4.99 percent, signed May 11, 2026 and retroactive to January 1, 2026. The same legislation raised the standard deduction to $30,000 for married filing jointly and $15,000 for single and head of household filers, and increased the dependent deduction to $5,000. The Georgia Department of Revenue tax updates page carries the official figures.

Texas currently has no individual state income tax. A simple gross-rate illustration at $200,000 of Georgia taxable income would be about $9,980 at 4.99 percent before credits or other adjustments; that is not a household tax estimate. Filing status, deductions, credits, income composition, residency dates, and property-tax changes affect the result.

The honest version: the income-tax difference can matter, but it should be weighed against local property-tax costs. Before you treat the income tax saving as free money, subtract the property tax increase on the specific house you plan to buy.

What happens to Georgia taxes in the year you move?

Do not assume the Texas move erases Georgia filing duties for the year. Georgia says a part-year resident who must file a federal return files Georgia Form 500 with Schedule 3 and reports Georgia taxable income for the residency period and any applicable Georgia-source income. Keep the move date, lease or closing records, payroll dates, and work-location records; ask a tax professional how remote work and income timing affect your return. The Georgia DOR residency and filing page is the state’s starting point.

Property Tax: Where Texas Takes It Back

Texas funds schools, cities, counties, and emergency services largely through local property tax, and there is no statewide rate. Your bill is the sum of the rates set by every taxing entity covering your parcel, applied to appraised value after exemptions.

As a historical statewide benchmark, Tax Foundation reported effective property-tax rates on owner-occupied housing for calendar year 2023 of about 0.81 percent in Georgia and 1.58 percent in Texas. This dated comparison is not a current rate or a forecast for a particular home.

The $140,000 school-district homestead exemption was set by voter-approved Proposition 13 in 2025. It is subject to eligibility and application rules; confirm the current amount and your eligibility with the county appraisal district.

Georgia’s HB 581 created a statewide floating homestead exemption tied to inflation, with local opt-outs in 2026. Enacted SB 33 removes the opt-outs and makes the statewide base-year exemption mandatory beginning January 1, 2027, using the 2026 base year. Check the official 2026 opt-out list for the transition year and the signed SB 33 for the new rule. Local tax rates and other charges still apply; verify the rule for the relevant county and property. Texas has a different rule: under Tax Code Section 23.23 as explained by the Comptroller, the 10 percent appraisal limitation generally takes effect January 1 of the tax year following the first year the owner qualifies the home for a residence-homestead exemption. Qualifying new improvements are excluded. The limitation applies to appraised value used for taxation, not the tax bill itself; verify eligibility and timing with the county appraisal district.

Illustrative 2025 primary-jurisdiction tax-rate stacks in selected suburbs were roughly these amounts per $100 of taxable value: Plano about 1.71, Frisco about 1.68, McKinney about 1.75, Allen about 1.77, Wylie about 1.95, and Melissa about 1.91. The combined figures depend on the jurisdictions serving the example location and may differ within the same city. They are not a citywide rate or a 2026 quote. Check the actual parcel and each applicable taxing unit using the county appraisal district and the Texas Comptroller’s rate and levy information.

A municipal utility district (MUD) may levy an ad valorem property-tax rate, while a public improvement district (PID) can impose parcel-specific assessments or fees. These are different charges, and neither has one blanket rate for a suburb. For a home under consideration, review the parcel tax record, district disclosures, and any PID assessment documents before making an offer. See the Texas Comptroller guides to local appraisal and tax information and property-tax rates.

Housing: The Honest Comparison

Georgia MLS reported a $390,000 median sale price for its 29-county Atlanta metropolitan statistical area in August 2026. MetroTex’s August 2026 report also put the DFW regional single-family median at $390,000 for that month. The same headline number does not make these equivalent measures: GAMLS labels its Atlanta figure all residential sales across a 29-county MSA, while MetroTex reports regional single-family sales under its North Texas MLS geography. Neither is a neighborhood match or a promise about what a particular Atlanta home will buy in DFW. The individual-suburb figures below are a third measure: rolling 12-month single-family profile data, last updated September 7, 2026. See Georgia MLS statistics and the MetroTex August 2026 DFW report for the current releases. How Texas School Ratings Actually Work

Georgia has 180 local school systems: 159 county systems and 21 city systems, according to the Georgia Department of Audits and Accounts 2024 report. Metro Atlanta examples include county systems as well as Atlanta Public Schools and City Schools of Decatur. Texas has a different independent-district map, and city name, postal address, and school attendance zone can diverge. So a family moving from a county-based Georgia system should treat the Texas district and campus as parcel-level facts, not as a suburb feature.

Texas school districts are independent taxing entities, not departments of the county, which is why they are called independent school districts, or ISDs. District boundaries do not follow city limits. One city can contain territory from several districts, and one district can cover several cities. A Frisco mailing address does not guarantee Frisco ISD, and this is the single most common assumption I have to correct.

The Texas Education Agency releases annual A through F accountability ratings for districts and campuses; the 2026 ratings were released August 14, 2026 and are available at TXschools.gov. Niche publishes a separate letter grade built from a different mix of inputs including academics, test data, survey responses, and college outcomes. They frequently disagree at the margin, and reading both gives you a fuller picture than either alone.

The step that protects you is simple. Before you write an offer, verify the assigned elementary, middle, and high school campus for that exact address using the district's own attendance boundary tool. Start with our DFW school district guide to compare districts, then verify by address.

Ask the district to confirm all three assigned campuses for the exact street address, whether the attendance zone is changing, transfer/program eligibility, enrollment documents, and transportation. The TEA locator FAQ explains that its district locator is not a campus attendance-zone guarantee.

Work Location Is the First Filter

DFW does not have one downtown that everything orbits. It has several separate employment cores, and the suburb that makes sense for one is often impractical for another. Starting a suburb search before you know the work location can add avoidable commute and housing trade-offs.

Examples of major employment locations include Toyota North America and JPMorgan Chase in Plano, Charles Schwab in Westlake, State Farm in Richardson, and American Airlines in Fort Worth. Confirm the assigned worksite with the employer: the company’s regional presence does not identify an employee’s building or required office days. For any specific offer, verify the worksite and expected schedule. A job at the Plano or Richardson end pulls Allen, McKinney, Wylie, and Frisco into range. A job on the Fort Worth side pulls an entirely different list.

Toyota lists its North American headquarters in Plano; JPMorganChase announced an additional Frisco office for planned 2028 occupancy; State Farm lists its Richardson CityLine campus; Charles Schwab identifies its corporate headquarters in Westlake; American Airlines lists its Fort Worth headquarters. These are examples of separate job corridors, not commute recommendations. I do not publish a single commute-time claim because the route and office schedule change it. Check the actual building and test your departure time, including tolls.

What Will Insurance and Utilities Add Each Month?

Get the address-specific homeowner quote before treating a DFW list price as affordable. Texas Department of Insurance data says wind and hail have accounted for an average 62% of homeowner losses since 2019; that statewide claims mix is not a premium quote for a particular house. Ask the insurer to show the wind/hail deductible, roof settlement method, roof age requirements, water backup, and exclusions in dollars. TDI’s county premium and loss overview and county loss data provide context; the insurer’s written quote controls your household budget.

For utilities, request the property’s last 12 monthly electricity-use readings (kWh), HVAC age/service records, and any known insulation or window upgrades. Then compare current retail plans for that service address and include summer cooling months separately. Use your Atlanta bills as the baseline for the same household size and comfort settings; a generic city average will hide differences in home size and thermostat use. Add water, trash, internet, HOA, tolls, and both adults’ school/activity routes to the monthly worksheet.

For a budget screen, use the Dallas cost calculator with each candidate address, but replace estimates with the lender’s payment worksheet, county tax estimate, insurer quote, and actual utility history before you set a ceiling.

A Realistic Atlanta to DFW Sequence

  1. Before leaving Georgia, collect the current tax bill, homestead status, pay records, utility history, insurance declarations, and exact move/last-work dates. Georgia’s 2026 one-time homestead grant is an assessed-value reduction of $18,000 for eligible homestead property with the required timely filing; check the state notice and local bill rather than assuming it applies to your account. Read the Georgia DOR 2026 grant rules.
  2. Keep the Georgia filing trail. A year-of-move Georgia return may still be required; use DOR’s part-year residency instructions and ask a tax preparer about remote work and income source.
  3. Fix the work address or corridor first. Everything downstream depends on it.
  4. Set a monthly ceiling, not a purchase price. Principal and interest, the full local tax rate, any MUD or PID, homeowners insurance, and HOA.
  5. Get a real insurance quote on a real DFW address. Wind and hail are material statewide homeowner-loss categories: the Texas Department of Insurance reports they averaged 62% of statewide homeowner losses since 2019. Ask for an address-specific quote and check whether the wind and hail deductible is separate.
  6. Subtract the property tax increase from the income tax saving before you decide how much house the move buys you.
  7. Shortlist three or four suburbs, not ten. The Suburb Matcher is built for this step.
  8. Verify school zoning by address for every home you seriously consider.
  9. File your homestead exemption in your first year of ownership. April 30 is the general filing deadline; confirm eligibility and county procedures with your county appraisal district.
  10. After arrival, transfer Texas identification and vehicle registration on the state’s current deadlines, then file the Texas residence-homestead application with the county appraisal district if eligible. Use Texas.gov’s move checklist and verify forms/deadlines with the relevant agency.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Dallas cheaper than Atlanta?

Not automatically. Georgia MLS reported a $390,000 median for the 29-county Atlanta MSA in August 2026. The DFW suburb table uses rolling 12-month single-family figures, so it is not a direct comparison. Texas has no individual income tax, while property-tax costs can be higher; compare the full costs for two specific addresses.

What is the Georgia income tax rate compared to Texas?

Georgia’s flat individual income-tax rate is 4.99 percent for tax year 2026; the standard deduction is $15,000 for single and head-of-household filers and $30,000 for married filing jointly, according to the Georgia Department of Revenue. Texas currently has no individual income tax, as summarized by the Texas Comptroller. The effect on a household depends on its filing status, income, credits, residency dates, and property-tax costs.

Is property tax higher in Texas or Georgia?

The dated Tax Foundation 2023 effective-rate benchmark was about 0.81 percent in Georgia and 1.58 percent in Texas for owner-occupied housing; it is not a current address-level comparison. Eligible Texas homeowners may receive a $140,000 school-district homestead exemption. The 10 percent appraisal limitation generally begins January 1 of the tax year after the first qualifying homestead year and excludes qualifying new improvements.

Does Texas have anything like Georgia's floating homestead exemption?

Texas generally limits a qualifying homestead’s appraised-value growth to 10 percent beginning January 1 of the tax year after the first qualifying homestead year; qualifying new improvements are excluded. Georgia’s 2026 HB 581 exemption had local opt-outs; SB 33 makes the statewide base-year exemption mandatory in 2027. A separate 2026 grant may reduce eligible Georgia homestead assessed value by $18,000. Confirm year, eligibility, and parcel with the county.

Which DFW suburbs should a family from Atlanta start with?

Start with the job location, not a list of suburb names. DFW has several separate employment cores, and the suburbs that work for a Plano job are not the ones that work for a Fort Worth job. Fix the work address, then narrow by school district, then by price band.

How do Texas school ratings compare to Georgia's?

Texas districts are independent taxing entities rather than county-run systems, and boundaries do not follow city limits. The Texas Education Agency publishes an annual A through F accountability rating, and Niche publishes a separate letter grade. Verify the assigned campus for a specific address on the district's own boundary tool before making an offer.

The Bottom Line

Whether Atlanta to Dallas-Fort Worth fits a family depends on its work location, housing needs, and address-level costs. The housing figures in this article use different geographies and periods, so they do not establish a like-for-like price advantage. Texas currently has no individual income tax; local property taxes, insurance, school assignment, and any district charges can materially change the household result. Use current figures for two specific addresses and confirm the school, taxing units, and insurance quote before deciding.
