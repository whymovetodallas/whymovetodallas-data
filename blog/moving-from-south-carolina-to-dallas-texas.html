Moving to DFW

Moving from South Carolina to Dallas: A Family Relocation Guide

Kristen Carpentier · Licensed Texas Realtor®October 15, 2026 · 12 min read

Quick answer: Moving from South Carolina to Dallas-Fort Worth can reduce your state individual income tax to zero, but that does not establish a lower total household cost. The answer depends on the exact DFW home's local property taxes, insurance, utilities, tolls, financing, and purchase price. Start with the work address, a complete monthly housing ceiling, and the school assignment for each specific home.

That decision is more useful than asking whether Texas or South Carolina is cheaper in the abstract. A family moving from North Charleston faces a different housing baseline than one elsewhere in the state, and a job in Plano creates a different DFW search map than a job in Fort Worth. This guide uses Charleston-area context because North Charleston accounts for most of this site's current South Carolina audience.

A geography note: Dallas is one city. Dallas-Fort Worth is a large region that includes Fort Worth, Irving, and many other independent suburbs, counties, school districts, and taxing units. Any metro figure below is labeled as a regional benchmark. It is not a quote for a particular home.

For the region-wide orientation, open the DFW Family Relocation Guide. After you know the actual office corridor and complete monthly ceiling, use the DFW Suburb Matcher to turn those constraints into a shorter research list.

What Really Changes in Taxes After the 2026 Reform?

South Carolina changed its individual income tax formula for tax year 2026. According to the South Carolina Department of Revenue's H.4216 summary, taxable income under $30,000 uses a 1.99% rate. Taxable income of $30,000 or more uses 5.21% minus $966. Federal adjusted gross income is the starting point, and the new South Carolina Income Adjusted Deduction applies. The deduction varies by filing status and may be reduced by income, so a headline rate is not a household tax estimate.

Texas has no state individual income tax. It also has no state property tax. As the Texas Comptroller explains, local school districts, cities, counties, and special districts set and collect property taxes. A qualifying Texas principal residence receives a mandatory $140,000 school district homestead exemption under the Comptroller's 2026 property tax guide.

South Carolina calculates an owner-occupied legal residence using a 4% assessment ratio, then applies local millage and available relief. The South Carolina Department of Revenue and the state's Revenue and Fiscal Affairs office describe that process. Because the two states use different bases, exemptions, and local units, statewide effective-rate arithmetic does not reproduce either home's bill.

Use the Dallas Cost of Living Calculator to place the Texas costs that decrease and those that increase into one household budget. Treat the result as a planning model, then replace every estimate with a lender, insurer, utility, toll, and tax quote tied to a real address.

Which North Charleston Bills Should You Use as the Baseline?

North Charleston is not one county-level tax or school geography. The city's official comprehensive plan states that its municipal boundaries span Berkeley, Charleston, and Dorchester counties. That makes the current street address essential before any Texas comparison. Record the county, school district, assessed value, legal-residence status, real property tax, and vehicle property tax from the documents attached to your home, rather than using a North Charleston average.

For a Charleston County owner-occupant, the county's official tax FAQ says a qualifying legal residence must be approved for the 4% assessment ratio before receiving property-tax relief from school operating millage. The same FAQ explains that motor vehicle property taxes are billed in advance. Those are separate lines in the current South Carolina budget, and both belong in the move comparison.

How Should You Compare Insurance and Utilities?

A coastal South Carolina household may already separate homeowners, wind, and flood decisions. The South Carolina Department of Insurance says a homeowners policy typically does not cover flood damage, including water from street flooding, overflowing waterways, or storm surge. Its disaster guidance also says coastal owners may need more than one policy for homeowners, wind and hail, and flood coverage.

For a DFW address, the Texas Department of Insurance says non-coastal homeowners probably have wind and hail coverage inside the homeowners policy, although it may carry a different deductible. TDI's March 2026 roof guidance warns that the wind and hail deductible is often higher than other deductibles and that roof age, material, and settlement terms affect what a policy pays.

Bring from South CarolinaDeclarations pages for homeowners, wind or hail, and flood coverage; each deductible; coverage limits; roof age; and the latest premiums. Mark any coverage that will end when the property sells.
Quote for the DFW finalistThe same dwelling and personal-property limits, replacement-cost treatment, wind and hail deductible in dollars, roof settlement terms, optional flood coverage, and insurer assumptions about roof age and material.

Do not compare one South Carolina premium with one Texas premium unless the coverage and deductibles are equivalent. The decision is the annual price plus the out-of-pocket exposure after the type of loss each region can produce.

What should you do about electricity before choosing a DFW home?

Bring 12 complete months of electric and gas bills from the current North Charleston home, with the home's size and major fuel sources. For a DFW finalist, request prior electricity usage and identify the service territory before choosing a plan. In areas with retail competition, the Public Utility Commission of Texas Power to Choose site lets consumers compare plans. The PUCT advises reading each Electricity Facts Label, fees, contract term, and usage assumptions rather than comparing only an advertised cents-per-kilowatt-hour headline.

What Five Inputs Should You Collect Before Comparing Suburbs?

What belongs in your DFW decision file?

  1. The exact office or regular destination. Confirm the street address, expected in-office days, and arrival time.
  2. An all-in monthly housing ceiling. Include principal, interest, local taxes, insurance, HOA, and district charges.
  3. Your home product. Define the size, age, condition, lot, and whether new construction is a preference or a requirement.
  4. The exact-address school assignment. Record the assigned elementary, middle, and high school for every serious property.
  5. Your acceptable toll and commute pattern. Test the real route at the times you expect to travel.

These inputs turn a large metro into a manageable search. They also prevent a low listing price from hiding a long commute, an extra district assessment, a different school boundary, or an insurance cost that changes the monthly total.

How Do You Verify the Exact School Assignment?

Texas and South Carolina publish accountability information through different systems, so their labels are not direct equivalents. Review South Carolina schools through the state's official report cards. For Texas, use the Texas Education Agency's 2026 accountability resources and campus reports at TXschools.gov.

A city name does not guarantee one district or one campus. District boundaries and attendance zones can cross municipal lines, and assignments can change. Use the DFW school district guide for orientation, then verify the elementary, middle, and high school with the district for the exact address before relying on an assignment in a purchase decision.

What Must You Do Before and After Arrival?

  1. Before leaving South Carolina: save the current property tax bill, vehicle tax receipt, insurance declarations, school records, and sale or lease documents. These records make the post-move comparison and any vehicle tax refund request easier.
  2. Before choosing a home: confirm the exact worksite, build the all-in budget, collect address-specific insurance quotes, verify mover licensing, and check school assignments by exact address.
  3. Before closing or signing a lease: review the taxing units, exemptions, HOA, any MUD or PID, utility providers, toll route, and financing terms for that address.
  4. Within 30 days of arriving: register each out-of-state vehicle in Texas. The state posts this deadline and links the registration steps on Texas.gov's moving page.
  5. After Texas vehicle registration: if you paid a Charleston County vehicle property tax bill covering future months, return the South Carolina plate and proof of the Texas registration to the Charleston County Auditor and ask whether you qualify for a refund for unused months. Berkeley or Dorchester County residents should ask their own county office because North Charleston crosses county lines.
  6. Within 90 days of arriving: obtain a Texas driver license. Texas.gov says a valid, unexpired out-of-state license can be used for up to 90 days after the move.

Texas.gov also links the state's mover-license check and the Texas Education Agency school locator. Use those official tools instead of assuming a mover is licensed or a mailing address maps to a specific campus.

Property tax cash flow: Both states administer property tax separately from paycheck income-tax withholding. A Texas local bill is generally annual, while a homeowner with a mortgage may fund it monthly through escrow. Confirm the lender's escrow estimate against the exact local bill and exemptions rather than treating the escrow payment as a separate tax.

What Else Do South Carolina Households Ask?

Is Dallas cheaper than South Carolina for a family?

Not automatically. August 2026 regional median closed sale prices were $431,500 in the Charleston Trident MLS and $410,000 in Dallas-Plano-Irving, but those aggregates do not describe the same home. Compare exact purchase price, financing, local taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA, district charges, tolls, and commute costs before deciding.

Does Texas really have no state income tax?

Texas has no state individual income tax. That does not mean Texas has no taxes, and it does not make a move cheaper by itself. Local governments levy property tax, while sales and other state taxes also support public revenue. Compare your South Carolina income-tax result with the complete Texas household cost.

Which Dallas-Fort Worth suburb should a South Carolina family choose?

There is no universal best suburb. Start with the exact office or regular destination, then set an all-in housing ceiling, define the home age and type, verify school assignment by address, and test the toll and commute pattern. Those five inputs usually narrow the metro to three or four workable candidates.

How should I compare property taxes between the states?

Use two real addresses. South Carolina applies a 4% assessment ratio to a qualifying legal residence, then local millage and relief. Texas has no state property tax; local units levy it, and a qualifying principal residence receives a $140,000 school district exemption. Confirm both calculations with the responsible local offices.

Data and legal note: Housing figures are dated regional market statistics, not appraisals, quotes, or forecasts. Tax information is general education, not tax or legal advice. School assignments, tax bills, insurance, district assessments, financing, and commute conditions must be confirmed for the exact address and household.

Is the Move Worth Deeper Planning?

A South Carolina to Dallas-Fort Worth move changes the structure of the household budget. State individual income tax falls to zero, while the Texas home brings an address-specific stack of local property tax, insurance, utilities, possible district charges, and transportation costs. The matched August market data provides context, but the exact worksite and home determine the decision.

Collect the five inputs, narrow the map by office corridor, and compare real addresses with the same assumptions. That process can show whether the move deserves deeper planning without pretending that either state is the universal lower-cost choice.
