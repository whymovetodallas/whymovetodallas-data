Moving to McKinney, Texas: What Families Need to Know

By Kristen Carpentier, Licensed Texas Realtor®  ·  June 26, 2026  ·  11 min read

McKinney keeps showing up on relocation shortlists — and for good reason. A Niche A+ for families, McKinney ISD earning an A overall, 3,000 acres of parks, and one of the most distinctive historic downtowns in North Texas. For a lot of relocating families, it checks every box on paper.

But there are some things about McKinney that catch people off guard — and I want to make sure you're not one of them. The ISD boundary situation is more complicated than most people realize. The property tax picture includes MUD districts in certain communities that change your monthly number significantly. And the price you see in a new construction brochure is almost never the price you'll pay at closing.

This guide is the longer companion to my McKinney neighborhood tour video — it goes deeper into the mechanics, the data, and the stuff that matters most when you're actually making the decision.

Quick Answer: Is McKinney a Good Fit for Relocating Families?

McKinney is usually a strong fit for families who want historic downtown character, master-planned community options, a solid school district, and a North Collin County lifestyle at a lower price point than Frisco or Prosper. But before you make an offer, verify these things by address:

✓ School district — not every McKinney address feeds McKinney ISD
✓ MUD/PID districts — some communities carry additional tax surcharges
✓ Commute — US-75 to downtown Dallas during rush hour is real time
✓ Builder pricing — base price and move-in price are different numbers
✓ HOA dues — vary significantly by community and section

The Neighborhoods Families Actually Land In

McKinney covers a lot of ground — roughly 65 square miles — and the experience varies significantly depending on where you land. Here are the four areas that come up most consistently in my conversations with relocating families.

Painted Tree

Painted Tree is the biggest conversation I have with families moving to McKinney. It's an 1,100-acre master-planned community on the west side of the city with 25 miles of pathways, 200 acres of parks and open space, a 20-acre lake, resort-style pools, and multiple builders across a wide range of price points. Community-wide pricing spans from the $300s into the $1M+ range, depending on builder, section, lot, and timing — verify current availability before anchoring on any number.

One important note for Painted Tree specifically: some sections sit in McKinney's municipal limits but feed Frisco ISD or Prosper ISD rather than McKinney ISD. This is exactly the kind of thing you need to verify by address before falling in love with a specific lot.

Trinity Falls

Trinity Falls sits on McKinney's northwest edge and is one of the master-planned communities that comes up most consistently with relocating buyers. It has a resort club, fitness center, pools, a dog park, and — this is a notable detail for families with young kids — Harold and Ruth Frazier Elementary School is on-site inside the neighborhood. For buyers where an on-campus elementary is a priority, that's a meaningful convenience factor — but still verify the assigned campus by address before assuming it applies to a specific lot.

Trinity Falls is located within McKinney's MUD district area. MUD surcharges — where applicable to a specific parcel — add to the total property tax bill. Verify the exact taxing entities for any address you're considering through Collin County Appraisal District before making budget assumptions. More detail in the cost section below.

Stonebridge Ranch

For families who want something more established — mature trees, a community that's been around a while, and amenities that are fully built out — Stonebridge Ranch is consistently at the top of the list. It's a golf course community with beach and tennis clubs, well-maintained streets, and it feeds into McKinney ISD. Resale inventory here can offer more negotiating room than new construction, depending on market conditions when you're looking.

Historic Downtown McKinney

The older neighborhoods near McKinney's historic downtown square are a different experience entirely. The downtown has over 120 unique shops, more than two dozen restaurants, a seasonal farmers market, art festivals, and weekly community events. It feels like a small-town main street inside a city of 200,000. Families who move here often say it was the downtown that sealed the decision — and it's genuinely one of the most underrated assets in North DFW.

McKinney ISD — And the Boundary Detail You Must Verify

McKinney ISD earns an A overall from Niche, covering academics, family reviews, college prep, and campus quality. The district runs three high schools — McKinney High, McKinney Boyd, and McKinney North — and reports graduation rates in the mid-90% range. Elementary campuses like Glen Oaks and Wolford are ones families often ask about. Jack Cockrill Middle is one families often ask about — check SchoolDigger for current ratings before relying on any specific ranking.

That's all good news. But here's what surprises people: not every address in McKinney feeds McKinney ISD.

Because the city is large and growing fast, some neighborhoods — particularly on the western edge near Frisco and Prosper — fall inside McKinney's city limits but are served by Frisco ISD or Prosper ISD instead. And here's the detail that really catches buyers: a McKinney mailing address does not automatically mean McKinney ISD. The two are not the same thing.

How to Verify Your School Boundary

Before you make an offer on any home in McKinney, look up the specific address — not the zip code, not the neighborhood name — using the district's enrollment tools:

McKinney ISD: mckinneyisd.net → Enrollment → Find Your School
Frisco ISD: friscoisd.org → Departments → Attendance Zones
Prosper ISD: prosper-isd.net → Enrollment → Attendance Zones
Third-party cross-check: greatschools.org — enter the full address

This takes five minutes and can save you from a very unpleasant mid-contract surprise. I always verify this before my clients fall in love with a specific house.

I never tell families which district is "better." That's your call, based on what matters for your kids specifically. What I do is make sure you have accurate information before you're under contract.

Commute Reality

McKinney's main highway is US-75, running north-south through the city. If you're headed to Frisco or the Toyota campus in Plano, you're looking at roughly 20–25 minutes on a normal day. To the JPMorgan and Capital One campuses in Plano, 20–35 minutes depending on where you're going and what time you leave.

Downtown Dallas is where expectations need to be set honestly. Off-peak, you can make it in 30–40 minutes. During the 4:30–6:30 PM window heading south on 75, 45 minutes to over an hour on a bad day is real. That's the tradeoff for being this far north.

DFW is a driving city. DART rail can be accessed from McKinney, but connections are limited and most families here rely on cars for daily life. The Census Bureau puts McKinney's median commute at around 29 minutes — close to what you'd expect for the northern Collin County corridor. If both adults in your household are working in different directions, run the commute math for both before you commit to a specific area of town.

Property Taxes

Texas has no state income tax, which matters a lot for families coming from California, Illinois, or New York. But property taxes are higher than most states, and in McKinney, what you pay depends on the specific address, the school district serving that address, and whether any special taxing districts apply.

As one reference point, the City of McKinney's published 2025 combined rate — stacking the city, McKinney ISD, Collin County, and Collin College — runs approximately 1.75% per $100 of assessed value before exemptions. But never use a combined rate from one source as a blanket number. Verify the actual taxing entities for the specific address you're considering through the Collin County Appraisal District parcel search.

On a $500,000 home at ~1.75%, you're looking at roughly $8,750 per year in property taxes before exemptions. Texas's residence homestead exemption currently reduces the school district portion of a qualifying primary residence's taxable value by $140,000 (TX Tax Code §11.13(b)), which meaningfully reduces that number once you establish the home as your primary residence. Always verify the current exemption amount with the Texas Comptroller or Collin CAD before making tax decisions.

MUD Districts: The Line Item Most People Miss

Important — MUD Districts in McKinney

Some McKinney communities — including Trinity Falls — are located within Municipal Utility Districts (MUD). A MUD is a political subdivision of Texas authorized to fund water, sewage, drainage, and road infrastructure. It has its own tax rate layered on top of your standard property tax stack.

Trinity Falls is in a McKinney MUD district area. Where a MUD applies to a specific parcel, it adds its own tax rate on top of the standard city, county, school, and college stack — covering water, sewer, drainage, and road infrastructure costs. Before making an offer in any master-planned community, ask specifically: "Is this property in a MUD or PID district, and what is the current rate?" Then verify through Collin CAD and run the math on your all-in monthly payment.

You can verify MUD and PID district assignments through the Collin County Appraisal District parcel search at collincad.org.

The list price and the monthly payment are two different conversations. Before comparing homes, compare the total monthly picture: principal, interest, property taxes (including MUD/PID if applicable), HOA dues, insurance, and utilities. A home that looks $30K cheaper may cost the same or more per month once the full tax stack is in.

New Construction: What to Expect Beyond the Brochure

McKinney has active new construction options across communities like Painted Tree, Trinity Falls, and newer sections of the city's western edge. If you're considering new construction — which a lot of relocating families are — here's what the buying process actually looks like, and where the surprises usually happen.

Base Price vs. Move-In Price

The price you see advertised at a builder's model home or on their website is almost always the base price — the lowest possible configuration of that floor plan on a standard lot. The actual move-in price is typically higher, sometimes significantly, once you add:

  • Lot premiums — corner lots, cul-de-sac lots, greenbelt-backing lots all carry a premium, often $10,000–$50,000+
  • Structural options — an added bedroom, extended patio, media room, covered porch
  • Design center upgrades — flooring, countertops, cabinetry, fixtures (this is where a lot of money goes)
  • Builder incentives (temporary) — interest rate buydowns or closing cost credits offered at a given time may not be available later

The New Construction Process in Texas

Texas new construction contracts differ from resale contracts in important ways. You're typically signing the builder's contract — not the standard TREC form — and the buyer protections are different. A few things to understand before you walk into a model home:

  • You can (and should) have a buyer's agent represent you at no additional cost — the builder pays the commission
  • You can have an independent home inspection at multiple stages: pre-drywall and at final walkthrough
  • Builder warranties vary significantly — understand what's covered and for how long before signing
  • Build timelines are estimates. Weather, supply chain, and trade availability affect actual completion dates
  • Verify the school boundary for the specific lot before signing — not just the neighborhood or zip code
Kristen's Take

I've walked many families through new construction in McKinney, and the families who have the smoothest experience are the ones who come in knowing what questions to ask before the sales rep starts their presentation. The builder's rep works for the builder — having your own agent means you have someone in your corner.

Family Lifestyle

McKinney's lifestyle assets are genuinely competitive. The City of McKinney maintains nearly 3,000 acres of parks and 80 miles of hike and bike trails. The parks and recreation program is one of the more comprehensive in Collin County — soccer fields, baseball and softball complexes, an aquatic center, and extensive programming for kids.

Youth sports culture here is real. Baseball, softball, soccer, lacrosse, flag football, wrestling, track — the infrastructure for organized youth sports is well-developed, and families who move here frequently cite it as one of the things that makes the daily lifestyle work.

And then there's the downtown square. More than 120 shops, over two dozen restaurants, a seasonal farmers market, art festivals, and holiday events throughout the year. The combination of small-town community feel inside a city of 200,000 is genuinely rare in DFW — and it's one of the first things families mention when they tell me why McKinney sealed the deal for them.

Is McKinney Right for Your Family?

McKinney is likely a strong fit if...
  • You want a combination of good schools, serious park and trail access, and a real community downtown
  • Your commute is to Frisco, Plano, or the McKinney area (and you're not going downtown daily)
  • New construction is on your list — McKinney has strong builder variety across price tiers
  • Budget flexibility matters: $400K–$700K covers a lot of McKinney, whereas comparable Frisco homes run $180K higher on median
  • You want an established community feel alongside growth (Stonebridge Ranch) or the option to buy into a still-developing master plan (Painted Tree, Trinity Falls)
McKinney may not be the right fit if...
  • You need to commute downtown Dallas daily and want that drive to be short and predictable
  • Walkability is a priority — McKinney scores 65 vs. Plano's 92
  • You want a very small, quiet community — McKinney is a city of 200,000+ and growing
  • The A+ ISD grade matters more than price — Allen ISD and Frisco ISD both carry that rating

Frequently Asked Questions

What school district is McKinney, Texas in?
Most of McKinney is served by McKinney ISD, which earns an A overall from Niche. However, the city is large enough that some neighborhoods — particularly on the western edge — fall into Frisco ISD or Prosper ISD instead. A McKinney mailing address does not guarantee McKinney ISD. Always verify the specific address using the district's enrollment tools before making an offer.
How much do homes cost in McKinney, Texas?
The median single-family home price in McKinney is approximately $498,000. The range is wide: new construction in master-planned communities like Painted Tree spans the $300s to $1M+, while established resale neighborhoods like Stonebridge Ranch offer a range of resale options. Always compare total monthly cost — not just list price — since property taxes, HOA, and MUD district fees vary significantly by neighborhood.
What is a MUD district in McKinney, and how does it affect my taxes?
A Municipal Utility District (MUD) is a state-authorized taxing district that funds infrastructure — water, sewer, drainage, roads — in areas where city services weren't already in place. Some communities in McKinney, including areas around Trinity Falls, are in MUD district territory — but which specific parcels fall within a MUD and at what rate can only be confirmed by address. Check whether a specific address has a MUD or PID (Public Improvement District) attachment through the Collin County Appraisal District parcel search before committing. If one applies, it adds its own tax rate on top of the standard city, county, school, and college stack.
Is McKinney a good place to raise a family?
McKinney earns an A+ for families from Niche, which factors in schools, safety, amenities, and family reviews. With nearly 3,000 acres of parks, 80 miles of trails, strong youth sports infrastructure, and a distinctive historic downtown, it consistently ranks among the most family-friendly cities in North Texas. The biggest consideration is the commute — families who work in Frisco, Plano, or McKinney itself find the daily drive very manageable; families commuting downtown Dallas five days a week should factor in the longer drive time.
How does McKinney compare to Frisco for families?
McKinney and Frisco are the two most common comparison cities I see from relocating families. Frisco has a slightly higher ISD grade (A+ vs. A) and more corporate-area amenities, but its median home price runs about $180K higher. McKinney offers more of a historic community feel — the downtown square is distinctive — and covers a wider range of price tiers. Neither is objectively better; the right answer depends on your school priorities, commute, and budget.
