Moving to Colleyville, Texas: Homes, School Districts, and the Tax Bill

Kristen Carpentier · Licensed Texas Realtor®August 22, 2026 · 14 min read

Two houses. Same street in Colleyville, Texas. Roughly the same price. One of them sits in Grapevine-Colleyville ISD and the other one does not, and that single invisible line decides which school your kids report to in August and what your property tax bill looks like every year you own the place.

Quick answer: Colleyville is an affluent, largely built out suburb of about 25,906 people in northeast Tarrant County, known for half acre to acre plus lots and mature trees rather than a walkable town center. The thing most buyers get wrong is that the city is not one school district. Five districts hold territory inside the Colleyville city limits, and because the school district portion is the largest single line on a Texas tax bill, the district your exact address sits in can change your annual taxes by thousands of dollars on the same street.

I am not exaggerating the "same street" part. Later in this guide I walk through three named Colleyville neighborhoods, verified parcel by parcel against Tarrant Appraisal District records. One of them is split down the middle between two districts. One of them is entirely outside GCISD, which surprises almost everyone. Only one of the three is wholly GCISD.

This guide is the written companion to my Colleyville video, and it covers the things a relocating family actually has to decide: whether Colleyville belongs on your shortlist at all, and if it does, which part of Colleyville you should be searching in. Those are two different questions, and most people only ask the first one.

What Colleyville Actually Is

The fastest way to understand Colleyville is to hold it up next to its more famous neighbor. Southlake is built around a walkable town center, so the social life there has an address you can drive to. Colleyville does not have that. Here the land is the amenity: big lots, many of them half an acre to an acre or more, mature trees, winding roads, and homes set well back off the street.

That physical character is not a style choice. It is a consequence of how the city grew. Colleyville did not develop as one master planned community with one school district drawn neatly around it. It filled in as a patchwork of subdivisions over decades, which is exactly why the city limits ended up overlapping several districts instead of one. The lots and the district problem come from the same history.

Those numbers matter more than they look. Median household income runs around $203,566, homeownership sits near 95.6%, and the median age is about 46.3. High ownership and an older median age mean low turnover, and low turnover means very few homes come to market in any given year. That is a structural feature of Colleyville, not a phase the market is passing through.

The other expectation to set early: there is very little new construction here. Colleyville is essentially built out, so "new" almost always means a custom build or a teardown and rebuild on an existing lot, not a new subdivision with a model home park. If a brand new tract home is what you are picturing, I would rather tell you in the first two minutes than the last two. Look at new construction across DFW instead, or read our guide on where to live in DFW based on where you work to widen the map first.

Grapevine-Colleyville ISD and iUniversity Prep

Start with the name, because the name is the first clue. GCISD stands for Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, so the top rated district here is literally shared with the neighboring city of Grapevine. Colleyville Heritage High School and Grapevine High School are the district's two comprehensive high schools, sibling campuses under one administration.

GCISD carries an A+ overall grade and an A+ for academics on Niche, which currently ranks it #2 among Tarrant County districts and #4 in Texas. The Texas Education Agency assigned the district a B in its 2024 to 2025 accountability ratings, which uses a different methodology built around state test performance and college and career readiness. Both are worth looking at, and neither one tells you anything reliable about an individual campus.

The genuinely differentiating piece, and the one most competing guides never mention, is iUniversity Prep. It is GCISD's online public high school, serving grades 5 through 12, and Niche has ranked it the top online public high school in Texas and #2 in the country. If you have a competitive athlete who travels, a kid in performing arts, or a student who simply needs a different schedule, that is a real option most suburbs cannot offer at all. It is a public school inside the district, not a private program.

You can compare GCISD against the other North Texas districts on our DFW school districts hub. What I will not do, here or in person, is tell you which district is best for your family. That is your call, and it depends on your kid. My job is to make sure you know exactly which district a specific house is in before you write an offer, because in Colleyville that answer sets your tax bill too.

One City, Five School Districts

Here is the part that catches people. Colleyville is served primarily by GCISD, and GCISD does cover the large majority of the city. But the city limits overlap five school districts, not one.

That number is worth explaining, because you will see different counts in different places, including on the city's own materials. We verified it from boundary geometry rather than from any website: NCES EDGE school district boundaries, TIGER/Line 2025, school year 2024 to 2025, clipped against the U.S. Census TIGERweb polygon for the Colleyville city limits. The computed city area came out at 13.17 square miles against the 13.12 square miles the city publishes, and only 0.021% of the city landed outside any district, so the two independent sources agree almost exactly.

Where the Split Is Real, Parcel by Parcel

Percentages of a city are abstract. What actually matters is whether the specific neighborhood you are looking at is split, and this is where Colleyville gets genuinely counterintuitive.

Families relocating here consistently ask me about the same handful of named pockets: Whittier Heights, the established sections like Montclair Parc and Remington Park, and the custom and estate corridors along roads like Glade Road and Hall-Johnson Road, where you find the acre plus lots and the semi rural feel.

Read that again, because it is the whole point of this guide. Of the three neighborhoods buyers ask about by name, one is split roughly in half, one is entirely outside GCISD, and only one is wholly inside it. A family assuming "Colleyville means Grapevine-Colleyville ISD" would be wrong about two of the three.

None of this makes any of these neighborhoods better or worse. Keller ISD is a strong district in its own right, and plenty of families choose it deliberately. The point is only that the assumption is unreliable here in a way it simply is not in most DFW suburbs, and the correction is free if you make it before you fall in love with a house.

What the District Does to Your Tax Bill

In Texas, the school district is usually the largest single line on your property tax bill. So when the district changes by address, the bill changes with it.

Every Colleyville property pays the same set of non school taxing entities. On top of that shared base sits the school district rate, and that is where the spread comes from.

Rates are reset each September, and actual effective rates vary by parcel. Verify the current combined rate for any specific property with the Tarrant Appraisal District before you close.

Now put that spread onto the Remington Park situation, where the split is real and two comparable homes can genuinely sit on the same road in different districts. Texas gives homeowners a $140,000 residence homestead exemption off the school district portion of appraised value under Texas Tax Code §11.13(b), so here is the honest math after that exemption is applied.

Two things soften the picture. Colleyville offers additional exemptions for homeowners who are 65 or older and for disabled homeowners, including a city tax freeze, so an eligible household's bill can look very different from the table above. And Texas has no state income tax at all, which still changes the overall math for families arriving from California, New York, or Illinois. The Two Minute Address Check

This is the part I promised, and it genuinely takes about two minutes. Do it before you write an offer, not after.

  1. Start with the exact address. Not the neighborhood name, not the subdivision, not the street. The specific property.
  2. Do not trust the subdivision. Remington Park is the proof: one recorded subdivision, two districts.
  3. Do not trust the street either. Two homes on the same road can land differently.
  4. Verify with the district itself. Use the district's own official address lookup, then confirm it with the district. Not a listing, not a third party portal, not the MLS remarks.
  5. Pull the combined rate for that parcel from the Tarrant Appraisal District, so you are budgeting the real number rather than a city average.

Step four matters more than it sounds. Listing data is entered by people, and a district field on a listing is not an authority. The district is.

What Colleyville Texas Homes Actually Cost

The median single family sale price in Colleyville runs about $1.0M, at roughly $277/sq ft overall. But the median is not the entry point, and those are very different numbers. Plenty of families who assume Colleyville is out of reach have never looked at the bands underneath that headline.

So the honest read: typical homes land in the $600s, move up homes in the $800s, and the luxury tier runs well past a million and up over $1.3M for the big custom estates. Colleyville is one of the pricier suburbs I cover. If budget is your single hardest constraint, you will get more house for the money a little further out, and I will tell you that plainly rather than let you find out on a scouting trip.

The bigger constraint is not price, it is selection. About 335 single family homes sold here across the last 12 months, against roughly 69 active listings and 2.4 mo. of supply, in what the data currently reads as a seller's market, with homes going under contract in about 19 days. Now narrow that pool to your price band and your preferred district and your lot preference, and at any given moment you may be choosing between two or three houses. That is structural, and planning around it is most of the job.

The Running Cost of an Acre

Here is the line item people relocating from a quarter acre lot never budget for. An acre is a lot of yard. More mowing, more irrigation, more water, more trees that need real maintenance from someone with a bucket truck. It is not a reason to avoid Colleyville, but it is a monthly cost, and it surprises people.

If that gives you pause, there is an answer most buyers never discover: Colleyville has lower maintenance patio and villa communities. Same city, same district rules apply, without the acre to mow. A lot of empty nesters and busy two career households take that route specifically. It is worth asking about before you conclude that the upkeep rules the city out.

Two more Texas costs worth budgeting while you are here. North Texas sits in a hail prone region, so homeowners policies commonly carry a wind and hail deductible of 1% to 2% of the insured value, which on a $1M home is $10,000 to $20,000 out of pocket before insurance pays anything. And summer electricity runs high from June through September when air conditioning runs nearly continuously, which is a very different bill shape from the annual average most calculators show.

Where Colleyville Sits, and What the Drive Takes

Colleyville's real structural advantage is position. It sits in the middle of the metro, which makes it one of the few suburbs genuinely reachable from both downtowns and the airport. For a two income household that means one of you can work toward Dallas and the other toward Fort Worth from the same house, which is a harder thing to pull off than it sounds in a metro this spread out.

Drive times get quoted carelessly in real estate, usually as a single optimistic number captured at 10 in the morning. Here are the real spreads, off peak to rush hour, captured from a fixed origin pin.

Traffic is heaviest on all four routes roughly 5pm to 7pm. Your own commute depends on your exact address, your office, and the hours you actually drive.

Notice the shape of that. The airport is genuinely close, and the two downtowns are reachable but not trivial at rush hour. Downtown Dallas doubles. If someone quotes you thirty minutes to downtown Dallas from Colleyville, they are quoting you the 11am number.

On employment, the airport itself is a major employer, Grapevine anchors like GameStop and Kubota's North American headquarters are minutes away, and the mid cities and Las Colinas job base sits within that range above. The honest caveat: this is a car first suburb, with a Walk Score of 64 and no rail service. Great central access, but you are driving, not catching a train. Plan on two cars.

How to Actually Search Colleyville

Put all of that together and your search here looks different from a normal suburb search. You are not searching "Colleyville." You are searching a district and a lot size inside Colleyville.

Decide the acre question first, because the big lot market and the patio villa market are almost two different markets with different buyers, different price behavior, and different inventory. Then filter by district, or even by a specific school, rather than by city. Then confirm the district on every single address before you get attached to a house.

And because inventory is structurally thin rather than temporarily tight, have your financing, your district decision, and your lot preference settled before the right house appears. Not because the market is frantic, but because there are only ever a handful of homes that match both your district and your lot preference at the same time, and they do not wait for you to finish deciding.

Who Colleyville Is Not For

Let me talk you out of it, because that is more useful than another list of pros.

Colleyville is not for a first time or budget constrained buyer. Typical homes start in the $600s and the median sale is near a million, and that prices a lot of families out. It is not for someone set on brand new construction, for the built out reasons above. And it is not for someone who wants a walkable, buzzy downtown at their doorstep, because this is a quiet town where a big night out means driving to Southlake, Grapevine, or Dallas.

It is also not the right fit if you want low maintenance living and do not know the patio and villa options exist, because the default here is a big lot and real upkeep.

One factual thing to go check for yourself: part of the city sits near the DFW Airport corridor, so some areas experience flight path noise. That is not a knock, it is just true, and it varies street by street. There is no published noise contour that places Colleyville inside a formal noise corridor, so do not take a number from anyone, including me. Go stand in the specific yard at the time of day you would actually be home.

On lifestyle, what is genuinely here: the Colleyville Nature Center is a real preserve with trails and ponds right in town, plus a solid parks system. The commercial core is Town Center Colleyville, and there is a Whole Foods anchored center on Colleyville Boulevard. Convenient, not a town square. And Grapevine shares a border with Colleyville, which puts historic Main Street, Lake Grapevine, the Gaylord Texan, and Great Wolf Lodge in your family entertainment backyard without really leaving the area.

And the one I would hate for you to learn the expensive way: do not assume the whole city is GCISD. Verify.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many school districts does Colleyville have?

Five districts hold territory inside the Colleyville city limits: Grapevine-Colleyville ISD at about 85%, Keller ISD at 9.75%, Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD at 4.60%, Carroll ISD at 0.55%, and Birdville ISD at 0.08%. That is measured from NCES EDGE boundary data clipped against the Census city polygon, not from any website.

Is all of Colleyville in Grapevine-Colleyville ISD?

No. GCISD covers roughly 85% of the city, which is most of it but not all of it. Parts of Colleyville feed Keller ISD, Hurst-Euless-Bedford ISD, Carroll ISD, and Birdville ISD. Whittier Heights, for example, is entirely Keller ISD despite a Colleyville address. Always verify the specific property.

How much do Colleyville Texas homes cost?

The median single family sale runs about $1.02 million at roughly $277 per square foot, but the bands underneath matter more. Typical homes sell around $603,000, move up homes around $851,000, and the luxury tier around $1.35 million. About 55% of sales happen above a million.

Why is the property tax different on the same street in Colleyville?

Because the school district portion is the largest line on a Texas tax bill, and the district changes by address here. Tax year 2025 combined rates range from $1.644011 per $100 with GCISD to $1.961951 with Birdville ISD. On a $1 million home that spread is over $3,000 a year before exemptions.

How do I check which school district a Colleyville address is in?

Use the exact address, never the subdivision or the street. Run it through the district's own official address lookup, then confirm it directly with the district rather than relying on a listing. Finally, pull the combined tax rate for that specific parcel from the Tarrant Appraisal District.

Is there new construction in Colleyville?

Very little. Colleyville is essentially built out, so new construction almost always means a custom build or a teardown and rebuild on an existing lot rather than a new subdivision. Families who want a brand new tract home generally do better looking at other DFW suburbs with active master planned communities.

How far is Colleyville from downtown Dallas and DFW Airport?

DFW International Airport is 8.8 miles and runs 12 to 24 minutes depending on traffic. Downtown Dallas is 27.5 miles and runs 30 to 61 minutes. Downtown Fort Worth is 22 to 43 minutes and Las Colinas is 19 to 35. All four peak roughly 5pm to 7pm.

What is the difference between Colleyville and Southlake?

Same tier of schools and affluence, close to opposite build. Southlake is organized around a walkable town center, so its social life has an address. Colleyville has no equivalent core, and the land is the amenity instead: bigger lots, mature trees, and homes set well back from the street.

Why does the video say four school districts and this guide says five?

The video was filmed before the boundary geometry was verified, using a count published by the city. The verified data shows five, including Carroll ISD, which is about six times larger inside the city than the Birdville strip the video does name. A video cannot be corrected after publishing, so the accurate count lives here.

The Bottom Line

Put Colleyville on your shortlist if you want top tier schools and real space, mature trees, big lots, and privacy, with central access to both downtowns and the airport, and you are comfortable in a market where typical homes start in the $600s. Leave it off if you need new construction, a walkable downtown, or a lower entry price.

And whichever way you go, verify the school district on the exact address. In most DFW suburbs that is a formality. In Colleyville it decides your schools and your taxes both, and it is invisible from the curb.
