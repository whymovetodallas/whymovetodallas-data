What to Know Before Moving from Phoenix to Dallas

Phoenix and Dallas are more alike than most people expect. Both are big, fast growing Sun Belt metros with strong job markets and plenty of new construction. Phoenix runs a little cheaper day to day, roughly 6% lower on overall cost of living by some cost-of-living index estimates, though these indexes vary by methodology and should be treated as a general signal rather than an exact figure. The clearest financial difference is the income tax: Texas has no individual state income tax, while Arizona currently applies a flat 2.5% rate. Home prices do not follow the same simple pattern. At the city level, Dallas has actually run somewhat higher than Phoenix recently, so the honest comparison has to happen suburb by suburb, not city to city. The move is rarely decided by the cost of groceries. It usually comes down to housing costs, taxes, and schools.

Read that home price line carefully, because it cuts against the common assumption. At the city level, Dallas has run somewhat higher than Phoenix, not lower. That does not mean DFW is automatically more expensive for a relocating family. It means the comparison has to happen at the suburb level, between the specific Phoenix-area suburb you are leaving and the specific DFW suburb you are considering, using homes with a similar size and age. Rent is close to a wash between the two metros. Phoenix's higher median income reflects its tech and semiconductor wage base. The one difference that holds regardless of which suburb you choose is the income tax: Texas has no individual state income tax, while Arizona applies a flat 2.5% rate, and on a six-figure household income that adds up over years.

One thing not to expect a discount on is property tax. Texas property taxes run noticeably higher than Arizona's, because Texas funds itself through property rather than income. Budget for it as part of your monthly payment, not as an afterthought. For some households the income-tax savings offset part or all of the higher property-tax cost; for others it does not fully close the gap. The outcome depends on your taxable income, the home's value, your exemptions, and the local tax rate, so run the numbers on a specific home before deciding. A $140,000 homestead exemption on the school district portion of appraised value (TX Tax Code §11.13(b)) helps once you file, and since a 2022 change in Texas law, buyers can typically claim a prorated exemption the same year they move in rather than waiting until the following January.

Kristen's Take

The Arizona families I work with rarely decide based on the grocery bill. The real comparison is the total monthly cost of a specific home: mortgage, property tax, insurance, HOA if any, and summer utility bills, weighed against the income-tax savings. Run those numbers on the actual home and suburb you're considering; the metro-level averages above are a starting point, not a verdict.

The Heat Trade Off (It Is Real, Just Different)

You are leaving the land of 110-degree summers, so let's be straight about what changes. Dallas summers are hot too, but they are humid rather than dry. DFW trades Phoenix's dry heat for muggy summers, real spring storms, and short, mild winters. Most Arizona transplants adjust within a season, though the adjustment period is different for every family. One practical change: the landscape is greener here, but that comes with regular mowing, irrigation management, and seasonal lawn care, not desert landscaping.

The bigger due-diligence item is severe weather and what it does to homeownership costs. DFW sees hail, straight-line wind, and occasional tornado watches, especially in spring. That translates into real considerations: roof age and condition matter more here than in Phoenix, homeowners insurance premiums and deductibles are often affected by hail and wind exposure, and it is worth asking specifically about a home's roof history and insurance claims before you buy. Freeze preparation (insulating exposed pipes, knowing where your shutoff valve is) is a new seasonal task, though DFW winters are short and generally mild.

Utility costs follow a similar logic in reverse. Phoenix runs air conditioning nearly year round; DFW's electric bills spike hardest from June through September, often $300 to $450 a month for a larger home, then drop substantially in the cooler months. Ask any Dallas-area homeowner about their August bill before assuming the shift from Phoenix will feel like a break.

Jobs, Commutes, and the Remote Worker Angle

Dallas-Fort Worth has one of the deepest, most diversified job markets in the country, anchored by corporate headquarters and finance. Major employers include Toyota North America, JPMorgan Chase, Charles Schwab, State Farm, and American Airlines, many clustered in the northern suburbs (DFW reports 4.3M+ nonfarm payroll jobs as of April 2026, per BLS). Phoenix, by comparison, is strong in semiconductors, tech manufacturing, and healthcare.

"Dallas" is not one commute. A job in Las Colinas, downtown Dallas, Plano, Frisco, Fort Worth, near DFW Airport, or Arlington can point to a completely different set of suburbs. Before you fall in love with a suburb, identify the employment node you'll actually be driving to (or the airport you'll fly out of most, DFW versus Love Field) and work outward from there. For remote and hybrid workers who are not tied to a specific office, the decision usually comes down to school-district boundaries, housing budget, and neighborhood layout instead of commute. DFW's no-income-tax math works the same regardless of which employer's payroll a remote worker is on.

A Buyer Due-Diligence Checklist

Before you commit to a DFW suburb, verify these directly rather than relying on averages:

  • Drive the commute during actual rush-hour conditions, not a map estimate
  • Confirm the exact school assignment by street address with the district, not just the city name
  • Pull the total property tax rate for that specific address, including any MUD or PID assessments
  • Get a homeowners insurance quote before writing an offer, since hail and wind exposure vary by area and roof age
  • Ask about the roof's age and any prior insurance claims on the home
  • Estimate summer utility costs for the home's size, not a metro-wide average
  • Check HOA dues and rules if the community has one

Planning the Move: Timing and Logistics

The drive from Phoenix to Dallas is roughly 15 to 17 hours depending on route, which most families split over two days whether driving their own vehicles or using an auto-transport service. A few practical planning points: start school and neighborhood research at least 60 to 90 days before your move if your timeline allows it, since Texas school enrollment requires proof of residency and immunization records. Consider a scouting trip focused on driving actual commutes and touring specific school zones rather than just browsing listings online. If your closing date and move-out date do not align, ask your agent about short-term or temporary housing options in your target suburb. Gather pay stubs, tax returns, and pre-approval paperwork before you start touring, since competitive DFW listings can move quickly in the northern suburbs.

Is Moving from Phoenix to Dallas Worth It?

It can be worthwhile for households whose income, housing needs, and school priorities line up with what a specific DFW suburb offers, particularly once the no-state-income-tax difference is weighed against that suburb's property tax rate and insurance costs. For a renter on a tight budget, the math is closer, since Phoenix runs somewhat cheaper on overall cost of living. The honest answer depends on the specific suburb and the complete monthly ownership cost, not a blanket city-to-city comparison. Frequently Asked Questions

Is Dallas cheaper than Phoenix?

On overall cost of living, Phoenix runs somewhat cheaper by index estimates. At the city level, Dallas home prices currently run higher than Phoenix's (Redfin, May 2026). Texas has no state income tax, which can offset the difference for families who are buying and earning a solid income, but the full comparison depends on the specific suburb and home.

Does Texas have lower taxes than Arizona?

Texas has no individual state income tax, while Arizona charges a flat 2.5% rate. Texas property taxes run higher than Arizona's. For some households the income-tax savings offset part or all of the higher property-tax cost; for others they do not fully close the gap, so run the numbers on a specific home before deciding.

Is Dallas as hot as Phoenix?

Dallas summers are hot but humid rather than dry, and it rarely reaches Phoenix's 110-plus extremes. In exchange you get spring storms, hail, and occasional tornado watches, plus short, mild winters. Most Arizona transplants adjust within a season, though it varies by household.

What are the best DFW suburbs for families moving from Phoenix?

Families relocating from the East Valley often compare Frisco, Prosper, Argyle, Northlake, Flower Mound, Highland Village, McKinney, and Allen for their newer construction and rated schools (Niche 2026). Choose by commute and budget first, then verify school zoning by address before buying.

How is the job market in Dallas compared to Phoenix?

Both are strong, diversified Sun Belt economies. DFW (4.3M+ nonfarm payroll jobs, BLS, April 2026) leads in corporate headquarters, finance, tech, and logistics. Phoenix is strong in semiconductors, tech manufacturing, and healthcare. Remote workers typically decide based on schools and housing budget rather than local job market.
