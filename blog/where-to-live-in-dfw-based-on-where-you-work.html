Moving to DFW

Where to Live in DFW Based on Where You Work

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  June 20, 2026  ·  9 min read

Your spouse just accepted a job at a Dallas-Fort Worth company. You have about 60 days to choose a suburb, find a home, and get the kids enrolled before the first day. The problem: you have never lived in Texas, and DFW is not one city. It is dozens of suburbs spread across a metro that runs nearly 100 miles end to end, and the wrong choice could mean a 50-minute commute every morning for the next decade.

Most relocation guides start with suburb personalities: this one feels upscale, this one is family-friendly, this one has charm. That is the wrong place to begin when a job is driving the move. The right starting point is the office. Once you anchor on the campus, the map shrinks fast, and a handful of suburbs rise to the top for commute, schools, and home prices that fit a family.

Quick answer: The best place to live in DFW depends on your office. Families at the Plano and Legacy West campuses (Toyota, JPMorgan Chase) cluster in Frisco, Plano, and Allen. Westlake employees (Charles Schwab, Fidelity) favor Southlake and Trophy Club. American Airlines families near DFW Airport choose Grapevine, Coppell, and Flower Mound.

How to Use This Guide

The logic here is built around a commute window. In DFW, a commute under 30 minutes is ideal: it leaves room for school drop-off and a real evening at home. A 30 to 45 minute drive is acceptable, especially if a toll road keeps it predictable. Anything in the 45 to 60 minute range only makes sense when a suburb offers a school or community advantage strong enough to justify the time. Beyond an hour, you are trading away your family's daily life, and I would steer you back toward the closer options.

Every commute estimate below is a realistic rush-hour range, not an empty-road number. Actual times shift with weather, construction, and which side of a suburb you settle on, so always test your specific route at the hour you will drive it before you commit. School grades come from Niche, and home prices update live from current DFW market data. Toll road notes matter here: the Dallas North Tollway, the President George Bush Turnpike, SH 114, and the Sam Rayburn Tollway shape nearly every North DFW commute.

Where to Live Near Toyota & JPMorgan Chase in Plano (Legacy West)

Legacy West in Plano is the single biggest gravity well for relocating corporate families in DFW. Toyota North America consolidated roughly 4,000 employees here at its campus at 6565 Headquarters Drive, and JPMorgan Chase runs a major Legacy West campus that remains one of the biggest corporate draws for relocating families in North DFW. Liberty Mutual and several other large employers sit in the same corridor. If your offer letter says Legacy West, you are in the best-connected part of the entire metro.

The good news for families: the strongest school districts in DFW sit within a 30-minute drive. The three that come up most often with Toyota and Chase families are Frisco, Plano, and Allen, with McKinney and Prosper close behind for families who want newer construction and more land.

Frisco, about 15 to 25 minutes to Legacy West

Frisco is the default answer for a reason. Living in Frisco, Texas puts you in Frisco ISD, which earns a A+ from Niche, and the drive to Legacy West runs about 15 to 25 minutes at rush hour down the Dallas North Tollway. That is a toll route, so budget for it, but it is fast and predictable. The median home price sits around $679K, and the trade-off for that premium is the strongest combination of schools, sports, dining, and entertainment in North DFW.

Plano, about 5 to 15 minutes to Legacy West

If you want to be on top of the office, Plano is the play. West Plano neighborhoods put you 5 to 15 minutes from Legacy West with no toll required, and Plano ISD holds a A Niche grade. At a median around $526K, Plano is also the most affordable of the close-in options, with more established trees, larger lots, and a shorter commute than the newer suburbs to the north.

Allen and McKinney, about 20 to 35 minutes

Head up US-75 (a free highway, no toll) and you reach Allen in roughly 20 to 30 minutes and McKinney in about 25 to 35. Allen ISD carries a A+ grade and a famously tight-knit sports culture, while McKinney offers a historic downtown and a median near $498K. For families who want newer construction farther out, Prosper is the choice, though the commute via the tollway stretches to 25 to 35 minutes.

Choose the Legacy West corridor if...
  • Your offer is at Toyota, JPMorgan Chase, Liberty Mutual, or anywhere in Legacy West
  • Top-rated schools are non-negotiable and you want several A-rated districts to choose from
  • You are comfortable in the $500K to $850K range depending on how new you want the home
  • You do not mind a toll road on the Frisco or Prosper commute

Best Suburbs Near Charles Schwab & Fidelity in Westlake

Westlake is a small town with two very large financial campuses. Charles Schwab made Westlake its official corporate headquarters at 3000 Schwab Way, and Fidelity Investments runs a 332-acre regional campus nearby at 1 Destiny Way with more than 6,000 employees. The whole cluster sits along SH 114, which is a free highway, so commuters here generally avoid tolls entirely.

This corner of DFW skews more expensive and more low-key than the Plano corridor. The suburbs that surround it, Southlake, Trophy Club, Colleyville, Keller, and Flower Mound, are some of the most sought-after family communities in the metro.

Where to Live Near State Farm at CityLine in Richardson

State Farm built a campus of more than 12,000 employees at CityLine in Richardson, centered around 1251 State Street. CityLine is a walkable, amenity-rich development sitting right on US-75 with a DART rail station at its doorstep, which makes this one of the few DFW campuses where a car-free commute is genuinely possible.

For families, the closest strong-school options run up the US-75 corridor. Richardson ISD earns a A- and offers the shortest commute, with a median near $463K, the most affordable entry point of any cluster in this guide. Plano and Allen are both reachable in 10 to 25 minutes for families who want to step up to an A or A+ district.

Kristen's Take

State Farm families have an advantage most transferees do not: the DART Red Line runs right to CityLine. If you settle in Richardson or Plano near a rail station, one parent can skip the drive entirely. That single fact reshapes the whole housing search, so factor transit access in before you rule a neighborhood out on price alone.

Best Places to Live Near American Airlines HQ (Fort Worth & DFW Airport)

American Airlines runs its global headquarters, the Skyview campus, at 1 Skyview Drive in Fort Worth, immediately next to DFW International Airport. With eight buildings and a workforce in the thousands, it anchors a different part of the metro than the Plano and Westlake campuses, and the best family suburbs sit just north and west of the airport along SH 114 and SH 360, mostly toll-free.

Grapevine is the closest fit, with Coppell and Southlake right behind, and Flower Mound and Colleyville a short step further for families who want more square footage. Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, which serves Grapevine and Colleyville, earns a A+ from Niche.

Where to Live Near GameStop in Grapevine

GameStop keeps its corporate headquarters at 625 Westport Parkway in Grapevine, in the same airport-adjacent zone as American Airlines. If you are heading to GameStop, the suburb math looks a lot like the airline math: Grapevine itself is the shortest commute at 5 to 10 minutes, and the surrounding family suburbs are all within easy reach on free highways.

Grapevine and Colleyville share Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, an A+ district, so a family can keep the commute under 15 minutes without compromising on schools. Southlake and Coppell round out the options for buyers who want a different price point or community feel, each about 10 to 20 minutes out.

Why This Matters

The airport corridor is the one part of DFW where two unrelated employers, GameStop and American Airlines, point to the exact same shortlist of suburbs. If your household has one person at each, or you simply want flexibility to change jobs without changing homes, this cluster gives you the most room to do it.

Where to Live Near AT&T in Downtown Dallas

AT&T is still headquartered today at Whitacre Tower, 208 South Akard Street, in the heart of downtown Dallas. A downtown campus changes the calculus: the close-in family suburbs sit to the north, and the commute leans on US-75 or the Dallas North Tollway, both of which carry real rush-hour congestion heading into the city core.

Realistically, plan on 25 to 45 minutes from the northern family suburbs. Coppell and Plano are common choices, with Richardson offering the shortest of the strong-school options up US-75. One note for AT&T families planning long-term: the company has publicly indicated plans to shift its headquarters footprint to a new Plano campus, with occupancy beginning around late 2028. If you are buying for the long haul, weighting your search toward the Plano side of the metro is worth keeping in mind and may save you a second move later.

When a job is downtown but the family needs are in the suburbs, I tell clients to be honest about the drive before they fall for a house. A beautiful home with a 45-minute one-way commute can wear a family down. Test the actual route at 8 a.m. on a Tuesday, not on a quiet weekend.

What Corporate Transferees Always Ask

Is it better to live close to the office or in a suburb with better schools?

For most relocating families, the school district wins. In DFW you can usually find a top-rated district within a 30 to 45 minute commute of every major campus, so you rarely have to choose. Pick the district first, then confirm the commute is one you can live with daily.

How bad is DFW traffic really?

Manageable compared to coastal metros, but real. Suburb-to-suburb drives stay under 30 minutes; the pain shows up heading into central Dallas at peak hours. The free highways like US-75 and SH 114 carry most commuters, and toll roads buy back time when you need it.

Can I use a VA loan for new construction in DFW?

Yes. A VA loan works for new construction in DFW as long as the builder and home meet VA requirements and the appraisal supports the price. Many North DFW builders in Frisco, Prosper, and Celina regularly close VA buyers. Get your certificate of eligibility early so financing keeps pace with the build.

Should I rent first or buy immediately when relocating?

If your 60-day window is tight and you already know your district, buying first avoids paying twice to move. If you are unsure how the commute feels or which neighborhood fits, a short lease near your campus lets you test the drive before committing. Both are valid; it depends on your certainty. Our guide on whether to buy or rent in Dallas walks through the math.

What is the commute like from Frisco to Plano?

Short. Frisco to the Plano Legacy West campuses runs about 15 to 25 minutes at rush hour using the Dallas North Tollway. It is one of the easiest commutes in North DFW, which is exactly why so many Toyota and JPMorgan Chase families settle in Frisco.

Are toll roads worth it in DFW?

For daily commuters, usually yes. The Dallas North Tollway and Sam Rayburn Tollway can cut 10 to 20 minutes off a peak drive. Regular users budget roughly $80 to $160 a month. If your route stays on free highways like US-75 or SH 114, you can skip them entirely.

What suburbs are closest to DFW Airport?

Grapevine, Coppell, and Southlake sit closest to DFW International Airport, each within about 10 to 20 minutes. They are the natural choices for American Airlines crews and frequent flyers, with Flower Mound and Colleyville a short step further out for more space.

Don't See Your Employer?

This guide covers the largest relocation drivers, but DFW has hundreds of major employers, from Texas Instruments and Raytheon to McKesson, USAA, and the growing roster at The Star and Cypress Waters. The commute logic is the same no matter where you work: anchor on the campus, find the strong school districts inside a 30 to 45 minute drive, then weigh price and toll costs.
