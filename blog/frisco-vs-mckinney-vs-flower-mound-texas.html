Frisco vs McKinney vs Flower Mound, Texas: Which Suburb Is Right for Your Family?

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  June 12, 2026  ·  10 min read

Watch: Frisco vs McKinney vs Flower Mound — schools, commute, and cost of living compared side by side.

Three suburbs. Three completely different personalities. If you're relocating to the Dallas–Fort Worth area with school-age kids and your shortlist has landed on Frisco, McKinney, and Flower Mound — you're in excellent company. These are consistently three of the most searched suburbs in North DFW for a reason. But choosing between them is harder than it looks from the outside.

I've lived in Flower Mound for years, sold homes across all three suburbs, and I hear the same question from nearly every family I work with: "They all look good online — what's the real difference?" This post breaks it down honestly, with current data on home prices, school ratings, commute times, and lifestyle so you can stop second-guessing and start narrowing your search.

Quick answer: Frisco leads in school ratings and entertainment infrastructure, McKinney offers the best value per dollar and the most new construction options, and Flower Mound delivers a quieter, established community feel with the shortest drive to DFW Airport — often under 15 minutes.

At a Glance: Three Suburbs, Three Personalities

Before diving into the data, here's the honest shorthand. Frisco is the largest and most built-out of the three — a true city in its own right, with 200,000+ residents, a packed entertainment calendar, and a school district ranked #11 in Texas and #8 in DFW by Niche for 2026. McKinney sits just east of Frisco with a historic downtown, a more affordable price point, and so much new construction activity that whole neighborhoods are still being built. Flower Mound, where I live, is smaller, quieter, and more established — the suburb you pick when you want great schools, an incredible location near the airport, and a neighborhood where the trees are actually mature.

Location & Commute

Where you work should weigh heavily on this decision — these three suburbs sit in different corners of the North DFW map, and your daily drive will feel completely different depending on which one you choose.

Frisco is positioned along the Dallas North Tollway corridor, about 25 miles north of downtown Dallas. If your employer is anywhere from Legacy West in Plano to the Uptown corridor — or if you work remotely and just need a good airport run occasionally — Frisco's DNT access is hard to beat. Expect 30–40 minutes to downtown in typical conditions, though rush-hour DNT traffic can extend that. The good news: Frisco is increasingly self-sufficient, meaning many families rarely need to go downtown at all.

McKinney sits northeast of Frisco along US-75 (Central Expressway). It's also about 30 miles from downtown Dallas, but the commute dynamics are different. US-75 can be punishing during rush hour, particularly through Allen and Plano. If your office is in the Richardson/Plano tech corridor, McKinney is actually well-positioned. If you're headed downtown daily, build in 40–50 minutes.

Flower Mound is the outlier here in the best possible way. It sits northwest of the metro, right along the SH-121 corridor — which means DFW Airport is typically 10–15 minutes away. For corporate transferees flying constantly or families with relatives spread across the country, that proximity is genuinely life-changing. Downtown Dallas runs 35–40 minutes via 121 and I-35E. And because Flower Mound is in Denton County rather than Collin County, it sits away from the heaviest Frisco/McKinney growth congestion.

Schools & Education

For most families landing in North DFW, schools are the deciding factor — and all three suburbs rank well above the national average. But there are real differences worth knowing about. If you want a deeper look at how these districts stack up regionally, I wrote a full breakdown in the best school districts in DFW guide.

Frisco ISD earns an A+ from Niche and is ranked #11 in Texas and #8 in DFW by Niche for 2026. It's large — covering most of Frisco and extending into parts of McKinney and Prosper — with dozens of campuses, a well-funded athletics and fine arts pipeline, and a growing slate of career and technical education (CTE) programs. One thing to know: Frisco ISD is a split district, meaning your home's location within Frisco determines which specific high school feeds your neighborhood. Some areas flow to Liberty, Frisco, Lone Star, or Heritage. Check your specific address before assuming which campus your kids will attend.

McKinney ISD earns an A from Niche and is expanding rapidly to keep pace with the city's growth. Newer master-planned communities in McKinney often sit in Frisco ISD rather than McKinney ISD — again, check your specific address. McKinney ISD high schools have strong athletics programs and solid college-prep pathways. Families who've come from high-pressure competitive school environments sometimes find McKinney ISD a breath of fresh air — strong academics without the intensity pressure some associate with Frisco ISD culture.

Lewisville ISD — which serves Flower Mound — earns an A from Niche and includes Flower Mound High School, Marcus High School, and Ranchview High School as the primary high school feeders for Flower Mound residents. Lewisville ISD is one of the larger districts in North Texas and tends to fly under the radar compared to Frisco ISD, which means less competition noise and a less pressure-driven culture. Marcus High School in particular has a strong reputation for fine arts and athletics.

Kristen's Take on Schools

Every family I work with asks me to pick a winner here. Honestly? All three of these districts are excellent — The rating gap does not always tell the full story of day-to-day campus fit. What I tell clients: figure out your target price range and location first, then look at the specific campus your kids would attend. A strong neighborhood school in McKinney ISD will serve your kids beautifully. Don't sacrifice location and budget chasing a half-grade difference on a ranking website.

Housing Costs & What Your Budget Buys

This is where the three suburbs diverge the most — and where McKinney's value proposition becomes impossible to ignore.

Frisco carries a median single-family home price around $676K. That's the entry fee for what is arguably the most amenity-rich suburb in North DFW. You'll find new construction in master-planned communities like The Fields and Westridge, established neighborhoods with mature trees and larger lots closer to the DNT, and a wide range of price points from the mid-$500Ks up into the $2M+ custom home tier. Homes are averaging around 38 days on market — a balanced condition that gives buyers reasonable time to make thoughtful decisions.

McKinney's median is around $499K — roughly $180K less than Frisco's median, for a city that's almost the same size. That gap buys a lot: a larger lot, a newer home in a still-developing community, or simply the financial flexibility to put more toward the down payment, your kids' college fund, or keeping the mortgage comfortable. McKinney has an extraordinary value score of 92/100 — the highest of the three — and its new construction pipeline is among the most active in North Texas. Communities like Painted Tree, Trinity Falls, and Erwin Farms are drawing buyers who want brand-new construction at prices Frisco can no longer offer.

Flower Mound's median sits around $624K — and the market moves faster than either of the other two. With a seller's market condition and homes averaging just 24 days on market, inventory is tight. This isn't a coincidence — Flower Mound is geographically constrained by Lake Grapevine to the south and northwest, which limits new development and keeps resale demand high. If you're buying in Flower Mound, come prepared with a strong offer and a flexible timeline.

Lifestyle & Community Vibe

Data tells you what to expect from the market. Vibe tells you whether you'll love living there. These three are genuinely different in ways that matter to families with kids.

Frisco is the entertainment capital of the group. The Star — the Dallas Cowboys' global headquarters and training facility — anchors a live-work-play district with hotels, restaurants, and a Nike flagship. PGA Frisco (two championship courses) opened in 2022. Universal Kids Resort, a major theme park, is scheduled to open July 1, 2026, near the PGA site. Frisco is the suburb for families who want access to major-league amenities, a packed youth sports league calendar, and a downtown-adjacent restaurant scene. It has the energy of a city. If that's what you're after, Frisco delivers.

McKinney offers something increasingly rare in fast-growing DFW: a genuine historic downtown. The McKinney Historic District — locally called "Uptown McKinney" — is a walkable square of 19th-century buildings with independently owned restaurants, boutiques, and a weekend farmers market. Families who left a city with a real downtown and still want some of that character without paying Austin prices tend to land in McKinney and feel instantly at home. The growth on McKinney's western and northern edges is dramatic, but the core of the city has a soul that Frisco, built entirely new over the last 30 years, doesn't quite replicate.

Flower Mound has Lake Grapevine — more than 7,000 acres of public lake with marinas, hiking trails, mountain bike paths, and boat launches — essentially as a backyard amenity. The community feel is quieter and more established. Flower Mound doesn't have a flashy downtown or a Cowboys facility, but it has the kind of neighborhood warmth that comes from a community that's been growing in the same place for 40 years rather than 10. It often appeals to buyers who want a quieter, more established community feel. Given the proximity to DFW Airport, Flower Mound also attracts a higher-than-average share of corporate transferees — families who travel frequently and need that 12-minute airport drive as part of their lifestyle infrastructure.

Frisco is the suburb that looks the most impressive on paper. McKinney is the suburb that surprises you once you walk downtown. Flower Mound is the suburb you don't want to leave once you're settled. All three are genuinely excellent — the question is which version of "excellent" fits your family right now.

Property Taxes: What to Expect

Texas has no state income tax, which relocating families from California, Illinois, New York, or Washington consistently celebrate. But Texas funds schools and local services through property taxes, and the effective rates in North DFW are real numbers that belong in your monthly budget.

Suburb County Typical Effective Rate On a $600K Home
Frisco Collin / Denton ~1.8%–2.1% ~$10,800–$12,600/yr
McKinney Collin ~1.8%–2.3% ~$10,800–$13,800/yr
Flower Mound Denton ~1.9%–2.1% ~$11,400–$12,600/yr

Rates are blended estimates for illustrative comparison. Actual effective rates vary by ISD, county, city, and any Municipal Utility District (MUD) or Public Improvement District (PID) overlays — which can add 0.25%–0.75% in newer master-planned communities. Verify with the county appraisal district before closing. Texas homestead exemption reduces the school-district taxable value by $140,000 (TX Tax Code §11.13(b), effective 2023+).

One note specifically for McKinney buyers: many of the newer master-planned communities on McKinney's western fringe — particularly near FM 1461 and SH-380 — carry Municipal Utility District overlays that add 0.3%–0.6% to the base ISD rate. A community that lists a headline rate of 2.1% might have an effective rate closer to 2.5%–2.7% once MUD bonds are factored in. Always ask for the full tax rate sheet before committing to a community, not just the ISD rate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Frisco or McKinney better for families?

Both rank among the top family suburbs in North Texas. Frisco ISD earns an A+ vs. McKinney ISD's A, and Frisco has more entertainment infrastructure. McKinney wins on value — the median home price is roughly $180K lower — and its historic downtown offers a community character Frisco lacks. The best choice depends on your budget and commute priorities.

Is Flower Mound a good place to raise kids?

Flower Mound consistently earns one of the highest family scores in North DFW, with Lewisville ISD earning an A rating, strong parks access, Lake Grapevine amenities, and a close-knit community feel. Its smaller size — about 78,000 residents — means kids often attend the same schools K–12, which families moving from smaller towns tend to appreciate. For safety research, review local police maps, NeighborhoodScout, or City-Data directly.

Which suburb is closest to DFW Airport?

Flower Mound, by a significant margin. Sitting along the SH-121 corridor in Denton County, most Flower Mound addresses are 10–15 minutes from DFW Airport terminals. Frisco is approximately 35 minutes away, and McKinney is 45–50 minutes depending on traffic on US-75.

Is McKinney cheaper than Frisco?

Yes — meaningfully so. McKinney's median single-family home price is currently around $498K compared to Frisco's roughly $679K. That $180K gap represents significant differences in monthly mortgage payment, down payment size, and overall financial flexibility. McKinney ISD earns an A from Niche, so families aren't giving up school quality to capture that savings.

What are property taxes like in these three suburbs?

All three carry effective rates roughly in the 1.8%–2.2% range, depending on specific location and whether MUD (Municipal Utility District) overlays apply. Texas has no state income tax, which offsets these rates significantly for families moving from California, Illinois, or New York. Always verify the specific rate for the community you're buying in before closing.

How is the new construction market in McKinney vs Frisco?

McKinney currently has a much more active new construction pipeline. Communities like Painted Tree, Trinity Falls, and Erwin Farms are delivering new homes at price points that no longer exist in Frisco. Frisco's remaining buildable land is mostly in higher-priced master-planned communities like The Fields. If new construction is a priority and budget matters, McKinney is the stronger option right now.

All three of these suburbs are excellent choices for families relocating to DFW. The question isn't whether they're good — it's which one fits your specific situation.
