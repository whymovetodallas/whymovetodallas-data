Moving from California to DFW: What Your Money Actually Buys

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  June 16, 2026  ·  7 min read

More of the families I help relocate come from California than anywhere else, and the conversation almost always starts the same way: "We love a lot about California, but we cannot make the math work for our kids anymore." If that sounds familiar, this guide is for you. I am not here to talk you out of California or sell you a fantasy about Texas. I am here to show you, in real numbers, what your California budget actually buys in Dallas-Fort Worth, where the savings come from, and the trade-offs you should understand before you list your house.

Quick answer: For a family earning $150,000, moving from California to DFW commonly produces $25,000–$40,000 in annual savings from no state income tax and lower housing costs — with the four-bedroom you are stretching to afford in California often becoming a newer, larger home in a top-ranked DFW school district.

Why California Families Keep Landing in DFW

The overall cost of living in Texas runs roughly 30 to 40 percent lower than California, driven mostly by dramatically cheaper housing and the absence of a state income tax. For a family, that gap usually shows up in three places: the mortgage payment, the paycheck after taxes, and the size and newness of the home. Dallas-Fort Worth in particular draws California families because it pairs that affordability with a deep set of highly rated suburban school districts to the north — which is exactly what parents are trying to protect when they leave the coast.

The Housing Math: What Your Money Buys

This is the number that makes families sit up. Median Dallas-area family homes run roughly $380,000 to $500,000, while the equivalent in Los Angeles often lands at $850,000 to $950,000 or more. In practice, that means the four-bedroom you are stretching to afford in California is often a newer, larger five-bedroom with a yard and a two-car garage in a strong DFW school district — at a meaningfully lower monthly payment.

No State Income Tax: What It Means for Your Paycheck

Texas has no state income tax — none. For households earning above roughly $100,000, the combined savings from zero state income tax, no California SDI charge, and lower housing typically run $15,000 to $32,000 or more per year. For a family earning around $150,000, the move often translates to roughly $25,000 to $40,000 in annual savings depending on which California metro you leave and which DFW suburb you choose. That is real money you can redirect to college funds, activities, or simply breathing room.

California's marginal income tax rate is 9.3%+ for most professional households — roughly $7,900–$9,200 annually on a $120,000 income. In Texas that number is $0. Every single year.

Here is how the income tax difference looks at a $120,000 household income:

  • California (9.3% marginal): roughly $7,900–$9,200 in state income tax annually
  • Texas: $0
  • Monthly difference: $660–$770 more take-home pay every month

The Honest Trade-Off: Property Taxes

Texas funds its budget partly through higher property taxes, so the rate is meaningfully higher than California's. Average property tax rates run about 0.75 percent in California versus roughly 1.8 percent in Texas. On a $400,000 home, that is about $3,000 a year in California versus about $7,200 in Texas — a difference of around $4,200. The good news is that the much lower purchase price usually more than offsets it, and the no-income-tax benefit compounds every year.

The Rule of Thumb

If you are buying under about $500,000 in DFW, you almost always come out clearly ahead on the full tax picture. If you are buying above $700,000, sit down with a CPA before you list your California home so there are no surprises. Also note: Texas offers a $140,000 homestead exemption off the school district portion of your appraised value (TX Tax Code §11.13(b)) — on a $500K home, this meaningfully reduces the effective school-district tax you actually pay.

Where California Families Tend to Land in DFW

Bay Area tech families often gravitate to Frisco and Plano, drawn by the school districts and the corporate corridor that includes Toyota, Frito-Lay, and JPMorgan. SoCal families looking for space and safer suburbs frequently look at Prosper, Flower Mound, and McKinney, where newer homes and larger lots make the change from California feel like a genuine upgrade. Families who want a smaller-town feel with a top district look at Southlake or Argyle.

Top DFW Suburbs for California Families
  • Frisco — Bay Area tech families, A+ Frisco ISD, The Star, new construction
  • Plano — established, Plano ISD, Legacy West job corridor, urban feel
  • Flower Mound — space, trails, excellent Lewisville ISD feeder campuses
  • McKinney — value, A-rated McKinney ISD, growing restaurant and arts scene
  • Prosper — newer, larger lots, top Prosper ISD, quieter pace
  • Southlake — Carroll ISD (#1 in Texas), luxury feel, central Metroplex location

What to Expect in the Adjustment

Most California families adjust quickly, but a few things are worth naming honestly. Summers are hot — though the lower humidity compared to the Gulf Coast helps significantly. You will trade ocean weekends for lakes, parks, and a calendar full of youth sports. Day-to-day life is more car-centric and more suburban. And the dollar simply goes further, which changes the emotional texture of family decisions in a way most parents tell me they did not fully appreciate until they were here.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money will my family actually save moving from California to Dallas?

It depends on income and home price, but for a family earning around $150,000, the combination of no state income tax and lower housing commonly produces about $25,000 to $40,000 in annual savings. Higher earners save more from the tax side; the housing savings are largest when you buy under $500,000 in DFW.

Are Texas property taxes going to wipe out my savings?

Usually not. Texas property tax rates are higher (roughly 1.8 percent versus about 0.75 percent in California), but because DFW home prices are so much lower and there is no state income tax, most families still come out clearly ahead, especially when buying under $500,000. Buyers above $700,000 should run the numbers with a CPA first.

What does a California family home budget buy in DFW?

A budget that buys a stretched four-bedroom in much of California often buys a newer, larger home with a yard in a strong DFW school district, at a lower monthly payment. Median DFW family homes run roughly $380,000 to $500,000 compared with $850,000-plus for many California equivalents.

Which DFW suburbs are best for families moving from California?

Frisco and Plano are popular with Bay Area tech families for the schools and job corridor; Prosper, Flower Mound, and McKinney suit families wanting space and value; Southlake and Argyle appeal to those wanting a smaller-town feel with a top district. The right one depends on your budget, commute, and your kids' needs.
