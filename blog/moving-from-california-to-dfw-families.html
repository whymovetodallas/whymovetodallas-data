Moving from California to DFW: What Your Money Actually Buys

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  October 4, 2026  ·  15 min read

Moving from California to Dallas-Fort Worth is a household decision, not a statewide savings formula. Your current home, equity, filing status, work address, school needs, and the specific DFW property all change the result. This guide helps parents compare those inputs and plan the move without assuming that one suburb or one budget fits every California family.

Quick answer: A move may improve your household budget if the DFW home, financing, taxes, insurance, commute, and school fit work for your family. Texas has no individual net-income tax, but there is no reliable universal savings figure; compare your own California situation with a specific DFW address.

Why California Families Keep Landing in DFW

The latest 2024 state Regional Price Parities from the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis put California's all-item index at 110.7 and Texas at 97.057, a statewide difference of about 12.3% relative to California. That index combines many goods and services; it is not a matched home-price comparison or a forecast for a particular family. Housing should be compared by origin market, home type, condition, and date.

Start with the actual work arrangement and address. A remote household, a family commuting to Legacy in Plano, and one working near DFW Airport may have different viable shortlists. Then compare the current California home and school needs with specific DFW options. No State Income Tax: What It Means for Your Paycheck

Texas has no individual net-income tax under Article VIII, Section 24-a of the Texas Constitution. California has a progressive income tax, so a household's liability depends on filing status, taxable income, deductions, credits, and tax year. Gross salary alone is not enough to calculate take-home pay.

For a concrete reference, the California Franchise Tax Board's 2025 tax booklet sets the 9.3% married-filing-jointly marginal bracket above $145,448 of taxable income. That is a marginal rate on income within the bracket, not on all income.

The structural difference is clear, but the household effect requires a like-for-like calculation:

  • California: calculate from current FTB schedules using the household's filing status and taxable income.
  • Texas: no individual net-income tax.
  • Comparison: include deductions, credits, residency dates, and any California-source income.

The California EDD lists a 1.3% SDI contribution rate for 2026 and no taxable wage ceiling. This payroll item is separate from personal income-tax liability. For stock compensation, a home sale, residency timing, or part-year filing, ask a tax professional to compare your own circumstances.

The Honest Trade-Off: Property Taxes by Suburb

Texas property taxes are set by overlapping local taxing units, so a statewide or suburb-wide percentage cannot give you a reliable bill. California's assessment rules also differ, so a headline-rate comparison does not predict your household's cost. Check the specific parcel's current value, exemptions, and adopted rates before comparing.

Rates vary by parcel and its overlapping taxing units, so a suburb-level table would imply precision it cannot provide. For a candidate address, check:

Cost input What to check
School district Current adopted rate and residence-homestead eligibility
City and county Each taxing unit listed for the specific parcel
Special districts Any MUD, PID, or other special-district rate on the parcel
Value and exemptions Current appraised value, exemptions, and tax year
Insurance Quote and deductible for the actual property
The Texas Homestead Exemption

Texas requires a $140,000 residence-homestead exemption for school-district taxes for a qualifying principal residence. It reduces the taxable value for school-district purposes, not for every taxing unit. The Comptroller's guidance says an owner who acquires a home after January 1 may receive the applicable portion of that tax year if the prior owner did not receive the same exemption for that year. Confirm eligibility and filing with the county appraisal district.

Do not assume a home-price threshold predicts the full tax result. Compare the actual parcel and household with a tax professional when a sale, stock compensation, or residency change affects your California taxes.

What California Families Usually Miss

I believe in going into this move clear-eyed. The families who regret relocating to Texas are almost always the ones who were not honest with themselves about what they were leaving behind. So here is my honest list of what California genuinely has that DFW does not — and how families tend to adapt:

  • Ocean access. Galveston is the nearest Gulf Coast beach — about 4.5 hours from most DFW suburbs. It is not the Pacific. Families who love a weekly beach day have to recalibrate. Most adapt by leaning into DFW's lake lifestyle: Grapevine Lake, Lake Lewisville, Lake Ray Hubbard, and Possum Kingdom are all within reach.
  • Mountains and skiing. The nearest skiing from DFW is Ruidoso, New Mexico — about 6 hours. Taos is 8. Families who ski regularly tend to plan one or two annual ski trips instead of weekend runs.
  • Mild summer weather. This is the one most California families underestimate. July and August in DFW regularly hit 100°F or higher. Outdoor comfort in summer requires real planning — morning activities, indoor facilities, and pools. The flip side: DFW winters are shorter and milder than most of the country, and spring and fall are beautiful.
  • Walkability. DFW is car country. Most suburbs have a Walk Score under 30. If you come from a walkable California neighborhood and value being able to walk to coffee or the farmers market, you will feel this shift. Some walkable pockets exist — downtown McKinney, Southlake Town Square, Legacy West in Plano — but they are the exception.
  • Building a new routine. Moving changes school, activities, childcare, and social connections. Ask each district about enrollment, student services, and transfer of records, then look at activities near the actual home you are considering.
  • Cultural and political environment. Texas leans conservative, and DFW suburbs lean more so. Families coming from coastal California metros often feel this cultural shift in daily life. This is something to think about honestly, not something to dismiss or overstate.

These trade-offs may matter differently to each household. For a candid look at adjustment challenges, read our separate post on why some Californians regret moving to Texas.

DFW Cost Surprises California Families Don't Expect

These costs vary too much by address, household, plan, and usage for one estimate to fit everyone. Add them to the same worksheet as housing and taxes:

  • Home insurance. Request a quote for the actual property and inspect roof age, wind and hail coverage, and deductibles. A regional description cannot predict a policy premium or claim cost.
  • Parcel taxes. Check each taxing unit, exemption, and any MUD, PID, or other special district for the address. Ask for the current tax bill and review appraisal-district records.
  • Toll roads. Use the NTTA trip planner and rate map for the exact route, vehicle, and payment method. Monthly cost depends on how often and where you travel.
  • Electricity. Compare current plans and the home's usage history through Power to Choose. Square footage alone does not determine a bill.
  • Maintenance and activities. Get property-specific service quotes and compare the activities your children actually use. These are household choices, not fixed costs of living in DFW.

California to DFW Family Moving Checklist

A family move has several deadlines that affect the home search. Use this sequence to coordinate work, housing, school enrollment, and the long-distance move; confirm each deadline with the responsible agency or provider.

6–12 Months Before You Move

  • Research DFW school districts and the specific elementary, middle, and high school feeder pattern for each candidate address.
  • Set a monthly housing ceiling and map each adult's actual worksite and schedule before narrowing suburbs.
  • Ask a lender to estimate payments with the candidate parcel's current taxing units, expected purchase price, insurance, and any HOA or special-district costs.
  • Plan a scouting trip that includes ordinary weekday conditions. Drive the actual work routes, arrange campus visits with the districts, and walk the neighborhoods you are considering.
  • Compare your available California equity and financing plan with current DFW listings in multiple areas, using homes with similar size and condition.

2–6 Months Before You Move

  • Get at least three written moving quotes using the same inventory, dates, services, and storage assumptions.
  • Compare renting first with buying using actual lease, purchase, moving, and school-enrollment timing. A confirmed campus and worksite can make buying feasible; uncertainty may make a rental more useful.
  • Reserve temporary housing if the California and DFW transaction dates do not overlap cleanly.
  • Check current Texas vehicle-registration and driver-license requirements with the relevant state agencies after establishing residency.

First 90 Days After You Arrive

  • Check residence-homestead eligibility and filing timing with the county appraisal district. A qualifying owner who buys after January 1 may receive the applicable portion of that tax year if the previous owner did not receive the same exemption; see the Texas Comptroller guidance.
  • Check the current Texas driver-license deadline with the Department of Public Safety and complete the application.
  • Check the current Texas vehicle-registration deadline with TxDMV and register the vehicle.
  • Review homeowners and auto coverage with a licensed agent, including wind and hail deductibles for the specific home.
  • Set up utilities and ask the provider whether a budget-billing plan fits the household's expected usage.
  • Ask the receiving district for its enrollment documents and current immunization requirements; bring the student's records.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much money will my family actually save moving from California to Dallas?

There is no dependable universal figure. Compare your filing status and taxable income, current home equity and mortgage, a specific DFW home's financing and parcel taxes, insurance, utilities, commute, and moving costs. The cost calculator can help organize assumptions, but it is not a quote or tax calculation.

How much does it cost to move from California to DFW?

Prices depend on shipment volume, origin, destination, dates, packing, storage, access, and coverage. Ask several providers for written estimates based on the same inventory and service assumptions. Include temporary housing and travel only if your plan requires them.

Are Texas property taxes going to wipe out my savings?

You cannot answer that from a citywide rate. Review the exact parcel, every taxing unit, current appraised value, insurance, and your household tax situation. The $140,000 Texas residence-homestead exemption applies to school-district taxable value for an eligible principal residence; it does not apply across every taxing unit. Confirm qualification and timing with the appraisal district.

What does a California family home budget buy in DFW?

It depends on the California market and the exact DFW home. Compare home type, size, age, condition, monthly financing, parcel taxes, insurance, and the assigned campus. A DFW listing search can show current options, but a statewide California comparison cannot identify an equivalent home.

Which DFW suburbs are best for families moving from California?

Start with the actual work address and schedule, then compare homes within your monthly budget and verify the assigned campus and needed programs at each address. Frisco, Plano, Allen, Flower Mound, McKinney, Prosper, Southlake, and other areas differ in housing, commute, and school boundaries; no district-wide reputation guarantees a particular campus experience.

Do California families regret moving to Texas?

Experiences vary. Before deciding, test the climate, driving, school, work, and community trade-offs that matter to your family. Our guide to common adjustment challenges covers that separate question.

Is DFW better for families than Austin?

Compare the actual job location, desired daily routine, housing budget, and school needs in each metro. Avoid treating whole-metro home prices or school quality as directly comparable without matching property type, date, and school assignment.

What should California parents know about DFW school districts?

The assigned campus depends on the address, and district-level ratings do not tell you the school's programs or fit for your child. TEA released 2026 A-F ratings on August 14, 2026; review the official rating release, then confirm the campus and enrollment requirements with the district. Our DFW school districts guide is a starting point for comparisons.

Should we rent first or buy when we move to DFW?

Renting first can give you time to learn the area; buying may be appropriate when the address, financing, school assignment, and timing are already clear. Neither choice is right for every household. Compare lease and transaction costs, school enrollment timing, and the flexibility your family needs before deciding.

What salary do you need to live comfortably in DFW with kids?

There is no single salary threshold. Household size, debt, home choice, childcare, insurance, school and activity needs, and commute all affect the budget. Build a monthly plan with actual housing and recurring costs rather than relying on a statewide income rule.
