Is Fort Worth, Texas Safe for Families? Here's How to Actually Check

This is one of the most common questions I get from relocating parents. Here is the honest, useful answer: not a citywide grade, but exactly how to check any address yourself.

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  July 13, 2026  ·  6 min read

Every parent researching a move asks some version of this question, and Fort Worth is no exception. It is a fair thing to want to know. But a whole-city answer, safe or not safe, is not actually a useful one for a specific family looking at a specific street. Like any major American city, Fort Worth is large and varied, and the honest way to answer this question is to show you how to check it yourself, then point you toward the research that actually changes outcomes for relocating families.

Quick answer: There is no single yes-or-no answer to whether Fort Worth is safe, because conditions vary by specific address in every large U.S. city. Use NeighborhoodScout, City-Data, and the Fort Worth Police Department's public crime map to evaluate any address directly, and separately confirm which school district that address is zoned to before you buy.

How I answer this for my own clients: Safety is an important factor for every family, and every family's priorities are different. I recommend reviewing NeighborhoodScout, the Fort Worth Police Department's Crime Data Center, or City-Data to evaluate specific addresses directly. These tools let you see the data yourself and make the call that is right for your family, rather than relying on a secondhand opinion, mine included.

Is Fort Worth safe for families? Why the answer depends on the address

Fort Worth covers roughly 350 square miles and is now home to more than a million people, according to Census Bureau estimates. A city that size cannot be usefully described with one word. That is true of Dallas, Houston, Austin, or any metro of comparable size, not something specific to Fort Worth. The useful question is never "is this city safe," it is "how do I evaluate the specific home I am considering," and that is a research process anyone can do in about twenty minutes with the right tools.

The right question is not "is Fort Worth safe." It is "how do I check this specific address," and that answer is the same for every city in the country.

How to check any Fort Worth address yourself

Before you write an offer on a home, or even before you plan a weekend of house hunting, run this quick research pass on any address you are seriously considering:

A five-step check for any address: pull the NeighborhoodScout and City-Data reports for the exact address, review the Fort Worth Police Department's public crime map for that patrol area, drive the neighborhood at two different times of day (a weekday morning and a weekend evening), ask about HOA rules and any PID or MUD tax overlays, and confirm the assigned public schools directly with the district's boundary lookup tool rather than assuming from the mailing address.

That last point deserves its own section, because in my experience helping families relocate to Fort Worth, it is the piece almost everyone skips, and it matters more than most people expect.

The question families actually miss: which school district

Here is what surprises most relocating parents about Fort Worth: the city is not served by one school district. Depending on the exact address, a Fort Worth home could be zoned to Fort Worth ISD, Keller ISD, Northwest ISD, Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD, Crowley ISD, Birdville ISD, or one of a couple of smaller neighboring districts. The mailing address alone does not reliably tell you which one you will get.

According to current Niche ratings, Fort Worth ISD (which covers most of the central city) carries an overall grade of B-, while nearby Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD and Northwest ISD each earn an A-, Birdville ISD earns a B+, Crowley ISD earns a B-, and Keller ISD earns an A. None of these grades is a reason to avoid or favor a neighborhood on its own. They are simply a reason to verify the exact zoning for any address before you fall in love with the house. If you would rather do less district-by-district checking

Some families would rather not think about ISD boundaries at all, and that is a completely reasonable preference. Southlake sits entirely within Carroll ISD, so it removes that particular verification step. Keller and Colleyville are each anchored by one primary district, Keller ISD and Grapevine-Colleyville ISD, but both still include smaller areas zoned to a neighboring district, so the address still matters, just less often than in Fort Worth. That relative simplicity comes at a higher price point than much of Fort Worth. Neither approach is right or wrong. It is a trade-off between cost and simplicity, and the honest answer depends on what matters most to your family this year. Frequently asked questions

Is Fort Worth, Texas safe for families?

There is no single answer for a city this size. Safety varies by specific address, as it does in any large U.S. city. Review NeighborhoodScout, City-Data, and the Fort Worth Police Department's public crime map for the exact address you are considering, and visit at different times of day.

How can I research safety for a specific Fort Worth neighborhood or address?

We do not rank or compare neighborhoods by safety. Instead, use NeighborhoodScout, City-Data, or the Fort Worth Police Department's Crime Data Center to independently research any specific address you are considering, so you can make the call based on current data rather than a secondhand list.

What school district will my Fort Worth address be zoned to?

It depends on the exact address. Fort Worth spans Fort Worth ISD, Keller ISD, Northwest ISD, Eagle Mountain-Saginaw ISD, Birdville ISD, Crowley ISD, and a couple of smaller neighboring districts. Always confirm zoning directly with the district's boundary lookup tool before making an offer, since assignments can differ between nearby addresses and can change when boundaries are updated.

Should I choose Fort Worth or a nearby suburb if schools are my top priority?

If you would rather not verify school zoning address by address, Southlake sits entirely within Carroll ISD. Keller and Colleyville are each anchored by one primary district but still include smaller zoned exceptions. Fort Worth generally offers more home for the price, so the right choice depends on your budget and how much verification you want to do.

Are Fort Worth schools good?

It depends on the district your address falls into. Niche currently rates Fort Worth ISD B-, Eagle Mountain-Saginaw and Northwest ISD A-, Birdville B+, Crowley B-, and Keller ISD A. Always verify the specific campus assignment for any home you are considering.

Fort Worth rewards families who do the research instead of relying on a one-word verdict about the whole city. Check the specific address with the tools above, confirm the school zoning directly, and you will have a far more useful answer than any citywide label could give you. That research is exactly what I help families work through every week.
