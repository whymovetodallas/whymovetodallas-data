Why Argyle, Texas Is One of DFW's Best-Kept Secrets for Families

Kristen Carpentier Licensed TX Realtor®  ·  June 18, 2026  ·  7 min read

When families ask me for a DFW suburb that still feels like a small town but has a top-ranked school district and easy reach to everything North Texas offers, Argyle is the name I keep coming back to. It does not get the headlines that Frisco or Southlake do, and honestly that is part of the appeal. Tucked into southern Denton County, Argyle gives families room to spread out, a genuinely rural feel, and an ISD that consistently ranks at the very top of the county. If you are weighing a move and want a community that prioritizes space, schools, and a slower pace without giving up access, here is an honest look at what living in Argyle, Texas is really like.

Quick answer: Argyle, TX is ideal for families who want the #1-ranked school district in Denton County (Argyle ISD, 97/100 TEA score), spacious lots, and a tight-knit rural feel — entry prices start in the mid $400Ks in the 76226 ZIP, with median prices around $720K in Argyle proper.

Where Argyle Is and Who It Suits

Argyle sits in southern Denton County, roughly between Denton and Flower Mound, with quick access to I-35W and Highway 377. That location is a big part of the value: you get a rural, open feel while staying within a reasonable drive of DFW's job corridors, the airport, and the shopping and dining of Flower Mound and Highland Village.

Argyle tends to suit families who want acreage or larger lots, value a tight-knit community, and put schools near the top of their list. It is less suited to buyers who want walkable urban density or the newest big-box retail at their doorstep — though both are a short drive away.

Argyle ISD: The Headline Reason Families Move Here

The school district is what puts Argyle on most families' radar. Argyle ISD is consistently ranked the number one school district in Denton County by Niche, and it carries a 97 out of 100 TEA accountability score. For a district this small, that level of performance is remarkable — and it is exactly the kind of consistency I tell parents to look for.

Because Argyle ISD is compact, families often feel a real sense of community around the schools — with the kind of involvement that is harder to find in a district serving 50,000+ students. If schools are your number-one filter, Argyle deserves a spot on your short list. Compare it against the metro's other top districts in our best DFW school districts guide.

One Important Reminder

Always confirm the exact Argyle ISD campus zone for any specific address before falling in love with a house. Not every property in the 76226 ZIP code feeds into Argyle ISD — some areas fall into other districts. I always verify this for my buyers before they get attached to a listing.

The Housing Picture

Argyle is not a budget suburb, and it is fair to set expectations. The median sale price in Argyle proper runs around $720K (with Zillow and Movoto tracking the range at roughly $670,000–$765,000 in 2026). That said, the broader 76226 ZIP gives families more entry points than the headline number suggests, with townhomes and smaller-lot products starting in the mid $400,000s.

Safety and Community Feel

One of the quiet selling points families notice is safety. Argyle's violent crime rate runs well below the Texas average, and the overall vibe is one of neighbors who know each other. Most residents own their homes, the pace is unhurried, and community life tends to revolve around the schools, local churches, and small-town events.

For parents who grew up in a place where kids could roam a bit — and who want some of that for their own children — Argyle delivers a version of it that is increasingly rare this close to a major metro. The Harvest community in Argyle is one of the most popular master-planned options in the area, offering the amenities of a planned neighborhood with the larger lots Argyle is known for.

The Trade-Offs to Know Before You Move

No suburb is perfect, and Argyle's strengths come with honest trade-offs worth knowing before you fall in love with a listing.

What to Factor In Before Moving to Argyle
  • Fewer in-town dining and retail options — you will drive to Flower Mound, Highland Village, or Denton for bigger shopping trips and most restaurant choices
  • Higher entry prices than many nearby suburbs — the school district premium is real and reflected in every listing
  • Septic and well systems on some larger-lot properties — always verify utilities for any specific home before making an offer
  • ISD zone verification — not every address in 76226 feeds Argyle ISD; confirm the campus zone early in your search
  • More rural infrastructure — roads, utilities, and services are less built-out than in more established suburbs like Plano or Allen

How Argyle Compares to Nearby Suburbs

If you like the idea of Argyle, it is worth comparing it to its neighbors. Flower Mound offers more retail, dining, and amenities with a still-strong school district, at a wider range of prices. Northlake and the newer communities nearby give you more new construction and sometimes lower entry points. Denton brings a college-town energy and more affordability. Argyle's distinct lane is the combination of a number-one county school district, a rural feel, and larger lots — which is exactly why it appeals to a specific kind of family.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Argyle, Texas a good place to raise a family?

Yes, for the right family. Argyle pairs the number-one-rated school district in Denton County (Argyle ISD, 97/100 TEA) with a rural, spacious feel, low crime, and a tight-knit community. It suits families who want space and top schools and are comfortable driving a bit for major shopping and dining.

How much do homes cost in Argyle, TX?

The median sale price in Argyle proper runs roughly $720K, with the broader 76226 ZIP offering entry points such as townhomes and smaller-lot homes from the mid $400,000s. Argyle skews toward larger custom and semi-custom homes on generous lots.

How good are Argyle schools?

Very good. Argyle ISD is consistently ranked the number one district in Denton County by Niche and holds a 97 out of 100 TEA accountability score. Its small size also gives families a strong sense of community around the schools that larger districts cannot replicate.

What are the downsides of living in Argyle?

The main trade-offs are fewer in-town retail and dining options (you drive to nearby Flower Mound or Denton), higher entry home prices than some neighboring suburbs, and the fact that some larger-lot properties use septic and well systems — so it is important to verify utilities for any specific home before making an offer.

Is Argyle the Right Suburb for Your Family?

Argyle is one of those communities that is either exactly right for a family or just slightly off — depending on what you value — and the only way to know is to weigh it against your budget, your commute, and your kids' needs.
