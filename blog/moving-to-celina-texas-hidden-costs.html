Moving to Celina, Texas? Build the Address File First

The list price is only the cover page. Compare every tax unit, any PID or MUD, the current school assignment, and the route your family will actually drive.

Kristen CarpentierLicensed TX Realtor®  ·  October 2, 2026  ·  11 min read

If you are moving to Celina, Texas, a beautiful model home and an attractive list price can make two properties look easy to compare. The difficult part is everything the price card does not settle: the hidden costs and daily-life differences behind each address. Two homes a few minutes apart can connect to different taxing units, different special-district records, different school assignments, and very different weekday routes.

Quick answer: Before comparing two Celina homes, build the same four-page file for each exact address: every property-tax unit, any PID or MUD, the current school assignment, and the real route in both directions.

The four-page address file

This is not a warning against living in Celina, Texas. Celina can be a strong fit for households that want newer housing choices and access to North DFW. The point is to make the comparison honest. Build the same file from the same source types for every home, then decide whether the complete ownership picture and daily routine fit your family.

Your address file
1Tax unitsEvery local unit listed on the exact property's appraisal record.
2PID or MUDThe current plan, assessment, unit, and rate that apply.
3SchoolThe district and campuses confirmed for the address.
4RouteThe trips your household will make in both directions.

Same questions, same source types, same assumptions for every home you compare.

The file also keeps one good-looking feature from carrying too much weight. A lower advertised payment may omit a PID installment, an HOA line, or the insurance quote for the actual property. A nearby campus may not be assigned to the home. A route that works at noon may feel different at the hour you leave for work. The file turns each of those into a question with a source.

Page one: every property-tax unit

Texas does not have one state property-tax rate. According to the Texas Comptroller's property-tax basics, counties, cities, school districts, colleges, and special districts can be separate local taxing units. The list attached to the address matters more than one headline rate.

The City of Celina adopted a 2026 rate of $0.571677 per $100 of taxable value in Ordinance 2026-087 on September 8, 2026. That figure is the City line only. It is not an all-in rate, a tax-bill estimate, or a monthly-payment promise.

Celina crosses from Collin County into Denton County. Start with the exact property's record in the Collin Central Appraisal District property search or the Denton Central Appraisal District property search to confirm its recorded value, exemptions, and listed taxing entities. Then choose those same units in the Collin County Tax Estimator or Denton County Tax Estimator, using the same value and exemption assumptions for every home. Check the tax year displayed because an estimator may still show the prior year's adopted rates. Save the date, and treat the result as a planning estimate rather than a final bill or school-assignment tool.

New construction needs one extra check

An early tax record can reflect land or a partially completed improvement rather than the finished home. The Texas Comptroller explains that property is generally valued as of January 1 and new improvements can change appraised value. Ask what completed-home value assumption sits behind any payment illustration.

If Texas property taxes are new to you, my Texas property-tax guide for out-of-state buyers explains taxable value, exemptions, and why address-level verification matters. Then use the DFW Cost Calculator for a suburb-level budget estimate that includes Texas property tax and insurance, and add the address-specific lines from your file separately.

Page two: separate PID, MUD, and HOA records

PID, MUD, and HOA are not three names for the same charge. A Public Improvement District can levy a special assessment on property inside that district. A Municipal Utility District can be a local taxing unit. HOA dues and transfer charges come from association documents. Put them on separate lines so one does not disappear inside another.

The City of Celina's PID directory explains that assessments are community-specific, governed by each district's service and assessment plan, collected annually, and separate from property taxes. A PID installment may be collected alongside the tax bill, but it remains its own obligation. The useful question is not whether Celina has PIDs. It is whether the exact lot is inside one and what the current plan and assessment roll say about that property.

Ask for the documents behind the monthly number
  • For a PID, request the required notice and current annual service and assessment plan for the exact lot.
  • Confirm the current annual installment, scheduled term, and whether the controlling plan describes prepayment.
  • For a MUD or other special district, confirm the taxing unit and current rate in the official parcel record.
  • For the HOA, request current dues, transfer charges, buyer fees, rules, and any separate assessments in writing.
  • Ask whether the payment illustration includes taxes, the PID installment, HOA dues, insurance, and likely toll use.

Do not carry one community's answer to another community, or even from one improvement area or lot type to another. Some Celina addresses will have a PID. Some may connect to a MUD or another special district. Some will not. The exact property decides the homework.

Page three: verify the school assignment

City limits and school-district boundaries are different maps. A geometry check processed for the companion video on September 21, 2026 used NCES EDGE 2024-25 school-district boundaries and U.S. Census TIGERweb incorporated-place boundaries. It found that Celina's city limits overlap Celina ISD, Prosper ISD, and Pilot Point ISD at or above roughly one-half of one percent. Gunter ISD and Denton ISD also touch the city at under 0.1 percent each. Those shares provide city-level context only. They do not assign a home to a district or a child to a campus.

Start with the exact address. Confirm the district, then use the Celina ISD boundary-verification guide to reach the applicable official lookup and confirm the elementary, middle, and high school assigned for the school year that matters to you. New campuses and changing feeder patterns can make an old screenshot or listing description stale.

I am not going to rank districts or tell you which campus is right for your child. That is your family's decision. The job here is to connect the house you are considering to the district and campuses you are actually evaluating. The DFW school-district hub can help with the research, but the responsible district remains the source for assignment.

School verification also belongs in the cost file because the school district is one of the property's taxing units. Pages one and three should agree. If the district assignment changes, revisit the tax estimate rather than leaving the old assumption in place.

Page four: test the route both ways

Do not ask only, "What is the commute from Celina?" A citywide answer cannot account for the exact home, exact destination, time of day, toll route, return trip, hybrid schedule, or a second adult's routine. Start with the trip that controls your week, then test the actual hours you expect to drive.

For the published video, I used a public-place example from 142 North Ohio Street in Celina to Toyota's North American headquarters at 6565 Headquarters Drive in Plano. A Rush Hour Planner forecast captured September 21, 2026 showed 25 to 36 minutes southbound and 26 to 35 minutes on the return, with overnight hours excluded. That is one endpoint pair and one dated planning example, not a promise for every Celina home.

The forecast is the beginning of the check. Drive the route if it can materially affect your decision. Include school pickup, airport trips, medical appointments, sports, and the route each parent uses. My DFW commute and employer-location guide explains why matching the suburb to the real job corridor is more useful than measuring everything from downtown Dallas.

Compare two homes with the same assumptions

Once both files are complete, make the homes answer the same questions. Use the same taxable-value assumption and exemptions. Convert annual tax, PID, and HOA items into monthly planning numbers. Use property-specific insurance quotes. Use the routes you just tested to estimate likely toll exposure. Keep future plans separate from what is documented today.

Imagine a fictional family transferring to the Toyota campus with two school-age children. Both homes fit the purchase budget. Home A may have the lower list price, but Home B could have the route, school assignment, or district record that better matches their priorities. The address file does not choose for them. It stops the wrong comparison from choosing for them.

Celina may fit if newer housing options and North DFW access carry real weight in your decision and you are willing to verify the property details. It may be a weaker fit if you need a short, predictable drive to a central job every day, if one exact school assignment is nonnegotiable and not yet verified, or if you do not want a special assessment or additional district in the ownership picture.

Frequently asked questions

What costs should I check before buying a home in Celina?

Check every property-tax unit, any PID assessment, any MUD or other special-district tax, HOA dues and buyer fees, a property-specific insurance quote, utilities, likely toll use, and near-term maintenance. Use the same value, exemption, and timing assumptions when comparing two homes.

Does every Celina home have a PID or MUD?

No. Some addresses have a PID, some may connect to a MUD or another special district, and some will not. Verify the exact lot through the City PID directory, the current service and assessment plan, and the responsible county's parcel and tax records.

Is Celina's City tax rate the total property-tax rate?

No. The 2026 City rate of $0.571677 per $100 is one local taxing-unit line, not the complete bill. The full property-tax picture depends on the exact parcel, taxable value, exemptions, school district, county, college district, and any other applicable special district.

How do I verify schools for a Celina address?

Use the exact address to confirm the responsible district and the assigned elementary, middle, and high school for the applicable year. Check the official district tool or contact the district directly. Do not rely only on a listing, community website, nearby campus, or old map.

How long is the drive from Celina to Plano?

There is no single citywide time. One public-place forecast captured September 21, 2026 showed 25 to 36 minutes from 142 North Ohio Street in downtown Celina to Toyota headquarters and 26 to 35 minutes back. Test your exact endpoints, schedule, route, and toll exposure.

What should I compare between two Celina homes?

Compare the full monthly ownership picture and weekly routine, not list price alone. Put tax units, PID or MUD records, HOA charges, insurance, utilities, school assignment, both route directions, tolls, and property condition into the same worksheet using current address-specific sources.

The best Celina comparison is not the one with the prettiest finishes or the smallest advertised payment. It is the one where both addresses have a complete file and every time-sensitive answer has a date. Build the file first, then compare the homes.
