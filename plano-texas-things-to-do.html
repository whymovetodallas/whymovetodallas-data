Why Plano Is a Great City for Families

Plano doesn't need to convince anyone — its reputation as one of the best family cities in Texas is built on decades of consistent delivery. Plano ISD produces exceptional academic outcomes at scale, serving one of the most diverse student populations in Collin County. The park system is genuinely world-class for a suburb, anchored by Oak Point Park's 800+ acres. And Legacy West gives Plano a walkable, upscale town center that most DFW suburbs spend years trying to build. The city is large enough to offer urban amenities without the urban tradeoffs — and its trail network means much of it is accessible without a car.

Oak Point Park & Nature Preserve

At 800+ acres, Oak Point is one of the largest urban nature parks in North Texas — a rare combination of lake access, native prairie, upland forest, and creek habitat all within Plano city limits. The trail network through Oak Point connects to Plano's broader 85+-mile system, making it the backbone of outdoor recreation for families in every part of the city. The scale means you can spend a full day here and genuinely feel like you've left the suburbs.

Legacy West

Legacy West is Plano's anchor mixed-use development — 250+ stores and restaurants in a walkable outdoor design along the Dallas North Tollway, developed alongside the Toyota North America headquarters and a cluster of major corporate campuses. The dining variety alone (from Haywire to North Italia to Flower Child) rivals anything in DFW's northern suburbs. It's the city center Plano never had, and it's become the defining family destination for weekend dining and shopping for much of Collin County.

Plano Balloon Festival

The Plano Balloon Festival (held each September at Oak Point Park) is one of the largest hot air balloon events in the Southwest — mass ascensions of 50+ balloons, a balloon glow at dusk, live music, carnival rides, and family activities across a full September weekend. It's one of those events that immediately becomes a family tradition from the first year in Plano. Morning ascension timing means early arrival is essential; pack breakfast and come prepared for a genuinely spectacular show.

Parks & Outdoor Spaces

Plano's park system is one of its most consistent competitive advantages — 80+ parks, 85+ miles of trails, and three major nature preserves that deliver genuine outdoor recreation within the city limits. For families choosing between Plano and other Collin County suburbs, the scale of Oak Point Park and Arbor Hills alone is often the deciding factor. The trail network is the glue — connecting neighborhoods to parks, schools, and recreation areas in a way that makes car-optional outdoor access realistic for many Plano families.

Oak Point Park & Nature Preserve

Size: 800+ acres · Best for: All ages, nature, trails
Plano's crown jewel — a massive urban nature preserve encompassing Rowlett Creek and its tributaries, wetland areas, native prairie, upland forest, and lake access. The trail system within Oak Point connects to Plano's city-wide trail network and is used by cyclists, runners, hikers, and families with strollers. Fishing is available along the creek corridor. The scale means Oak Point functions less like a park and more like a genuine wilderness accessible from a suburban address — a major quality-of-life differentiator for families choosing Plano over neighboring cities.

Arbor Hills Nature Preserve

Size: 200 acres · Best for: Hiking, cycling, nature
Arbor Hills is Plano's second major nature preserve — a 200-acre upland forested park with a paved and unpaved trail system, open meadows, and native North Texas tree canopy. The moderate terrain (Plano's hills are modest by Texas standards, but real) makes it more interesting than flat suburban trails. The observation tower offers rare elevated views across North Texas prairie. A popular early-morning destination for families with dogs and for weekend cyclists; arrive before 9am on fall and spring weekends to avoid crowds.

Bob Woodruff Park

Size: 360 acres · Best for: All ages, sports, trails, equestrian
One of Plano's largest developed parks — featuring an equestrian area (one of the few in DFW's suburban parks), athletic fields, a disc golf course, mountain biking trails, a boat ramp on Lake Lavon channel, and an extensive playground system. Bob Woodruff is particularly popular with families who have school-age children involved in multiple activities, since the sheer variety of facilities means multiple family members can pursue different activities simultaneously. The equestrian trails are the most distinctive feature and draw visitors from across Collin County.

Haggard Park

Size: 27 acres · Best for: All ages, community events
Old Town Plano's community green — a central park in the heart of Plano's historic district with a playground, a splash pad (seasonal), a performance amphitheater, and open lawn space for community events. Haggard Park hosts the majority of Old Town Plano's outdoor events — farmers markets, concerts, holiday celebrations, and seasonal programming. For families who settle in Plano's central or older neighborhoods, Haggard Park functions as the local town square equivalent. The adjacent Old Town Plano district adds dining and shopping within walking distance.

Jack Carter Community Park

Size: 60+ acres · Best for: All ages, youth sports, family recreation
A major multi-use community park in northeast Plano with athletic fields, tennis courts, a recreation center, and playground facilities. Jack Carter is one of the primary venues for Plano's organized youth sports programs in its part of the city and sees consistent family use year-round. The adjacent Jack Carter Recreation Center provides indoor programming on days when outdoor use is limited.

Carpenter Park

Size: 150+ acres · Best for: Sports, all ages
A large northwest Plano park with athletic fields, walking trails, a playground, and lake access. Carpenter Park is one of the primary community parks for northwest Plano neighborhoods and a key venue for youth sports leagues organized by Plano Parks & Recreation. The park's size makes it practical for multiple simultaneous family activities — a common pattern on Saturday mornings in Plano sports season.

Russell Creek Park

Size: 90+ acres · Best for: Youth sports, walking, family recreation
One of central Plano's larger community parks, with youth sports fields, trails, a playground, and creek-corridor greenspace. Russell Creek connects to Plano's trail system and serves as a primary recreation corridor for surrounding neighborhoods. A consistently busy park during youth sports seasons — particularly fall and spring — and a calmer option during summer weekday mornings for families seeking a less crowded outdoor experience.

Plano's 85+ Mile Trail Network

Plano maintains over 85 miles of hike and bike trails — one of the most extensive systems in North Texas. The trail network connects Oak Point Park, Arbor Hills, Bob Woodruff, and dozens of neighborhood parks in a system that makes car-optional outdoor recreation realistic for families across the city. For current trail maps, conditions, and park hours, visit plano.gov/parks.

Youth Sports & Recreation Leagues

Plano's youth sports infrastructure matches its size — one of the largest organized youth sports ecosystems in Collin County, with Plano Soccer Association (one of the biggest AYSO affiliates in North Texas), Plano Sports Authority, and Plano ISD's highly competitive athletic programs across all sports. The breadth of options means children at every commitment level — from recreational Saturday leagues to elite travel programs — have well-supported options in Plano.

Soccer — Plano Soccer Association (PSA)

Ages: 4–18 · Seasons: Fall & Spring
The Plano Soccer Association is one of the largest youth soccer organizations in North Texas — serving thousands of players each season across recreational, select, and EXTRA (travel) divisions. Fall is the primary season; Spring offers additional league play and development programming. PSA's scale means virtually every age group and skill level is well-served, from first-year T-shirt players to competitive travel teams. Registration typically opens in June for fall and October for spring.

Baseball & Softball — Plano Sports Authority

Ages: 4–18 · Seasons: Spring & Fall
Plano Sports Authority (PSA) is the primary organization for youth baseball and softball in Plano, with T-ball through high school age divisions and both recreational and competitive tracks. Spring is the main season; fall development leagues give younger players additional at-bats. Plano ISD fields highly competitive high school baseball and softball programs — multiple campuses (Plano Senior, Plano West, Plano East) have strong regional track records.

Football — Plano Youth Football

Ages: 5–14 · Season: Fall
Youth football in Plano operates through the Plano Youth Football Association and several school-feeder programs, with flag and tackle divisions. Plano ISD's football programs across three high schools (Plano Senior, Plano West, Plano East) maintain a long and competitive history in Texas high school football — providing strong downstream development motivation for youth programs. Fall season runs August through November.

Basketball — Plano Recreation Centers

Ages: 5–18 · Season: Winter
The City of Plano Parks & Recreation Department runs youth basketball leagues through its network of recreation centers (including the IH-635/Plano Recreation Center and the Jack Carter Recreation Center) in Winter. Plano ISD fields competitive varsity and JV basketball programs at all three high school campuses. Private organizations and club basketball programs also operate year-round for competitive players.

Swimming — Plano Aquatics

Ages: All ages · Season: Year-round
Plano maintains multiple aquatics facilities including the Plano Aquatic Center and recreation center pools, with learn-to-swim programs for infants through adults, recreational swim, and competitive club programs. The Plano Aquatic Club (PAC) is one of the most established competitive swim clubs in North Texas. Outdoor spray parks operate seasonally at several Plano parks — for current locations and hours, check plano.gov/parks.

Tennis — Plano Tennis Center

Ages: All ages · Season: Year-round
The Plano Tennis Center is a dedicated municipal tennis facility with multiple indoor and outdoor courts, USTA-affiliated junior development programs, adult leagues, and private lesson options. Plano ISD's high school tennis programs are consistently competitive at the regional level. For families with tennis-inclined children, Plano's dedicated facility is a significant upgrade over parks with a few public courts.

Lacrosse — Plano Lacrosse & Club Programs

Ages: 6–18 · Season: Spring (primary)
Youth lacrosse in Plano operates through local clubs and DFW-area travel organizations. Plano ISD has added lacrosse at multiple high school campuses, reflecting the sport's continued growth in Collin County. Spring season is primary; fall development and conditioning programs supplement year-round for competitive players.

Gymnastics & Martial Arts

Ages: 2–18 · Season: Year-round
Plano's size supports multiple dedicated gymnastics academies and martial arts studios, providing year-round programming from recreational tumbling for toddlers through competitive gymnastics and black-belt programs for teens. Several nationally recognized gymnastics programs operate in Plano — the city's strong youth sports culture generates consistent demand for these options. A natural complement to team sports for families looking for individual athletic development.

Plano Recreation Centers

The City of Plano operates multiple full-service recreation centers — including the IH-635 Recreation Center, Jack Carter Recreation Center, and the Plano Tennis Center — providing indoor programming year-round. Each center offers leagues, fitness programming, youth enrichment, and drop-in recreation. Plano's recreation center system is one of the most developed in Collin County. Visit plano.gov/recreation for current membership options and program schedules.

Entertainment & Family Attractions

Plano's entertainment landscape reflects its size — a range of options that goes well beyond what most DFW suburbs offer, anchored by Legacy West's dining and boutique scene, the Interurban Railway Museum, the ArtCentre of Plano, and the consistent draw of Old Town Plano. For families seeking more specialized experiences, the US-75 corridor puts Frisco's stadium and entertainment complex within 15 minutes north.

Legacy West

Type: Mixed-use Outdoor District · Ages: All ages
Legacy West is the defining entertainment and dining destination for Plano — 250+ stores and restaurants in a walkable outdoor design along the Dallas North Tollway. Weekend evenings at Legacy West have the energy of an urban district without the parking frustration of downtown Dallas. The restaurant variety alone justifies regular trips: Haywire, North Italia, Flower Child, Legacy Food Hall, and a dozen more quality options within a five-minute walk. Family-friendly events are programmed seasonally, and the outdoor layout makes it workable for families with young children even on busy evenings.

Interurban Railway Museum

Type: Transportation Museum · Ages: All ages (train enthusiasts especially)
The Interurban Railway Museum in Old Town Plano preserves an original 1908 Texas Electric Railway interurban car and documents the history of the Dallas-to-Denison electric railway that passed through early Plano. The museum occupies the original 1909 depot building — one of Plano's oldest surviving structures. A niche but genuinely interesting outing for families with train-obsessed children, and a good pairing with Haggard Park and Old Town dining.

ArtCentre of Plano

Type: Community Arts Center · Ages: All ages
The ArtCentre of Plano is the city's primary visual arts venue — featuring rotating gallery exhibitions, an art school with classes for children through adults, and community events throughout the year. The children's art classes are well-regarded and fill quickly; summer camps are a popular enrichment option for families. A useful complement to the sports-heavy summer programming that dominates most Plano families' calendars.

Cinemark Legacy & XD

Type: Movie Theater · Ages: All ages
Plano's primary multiplex — a full-service Cinemark with XD format, conveniently located near Legacy West. The Legacy location benefits from the surrounding dining options, making a movie + dinner combination genuinely easy. Parking is ample compared to more urban theater options. Matinee pricing on weekdays makes it practical for midweek family outings during school breaks.

Old Town Plano

Type: Historic District · Ages: All ages
Plano's original downtown district — a compact historic area around 15th Street and K Avenue with independently owned shops, restaurants, galleries, and Haggard Park at its center. Old Town Plano has a genuine small-town character that contrasts pleasantly with the newer Legacy West development. The area hosts a rotating calendar of community events — farmers markets, art walks, holiday celebrations, and seasonal programming — and is particularly appealing for families who settled in Plano's older neighborhoods.

Day Trip: Toyota Stadium & FC Dallas (15 min)

Distance: ~15 min north via US-75 · Ages: All ages
Frisco's Toyota Stadium — home of FC Dallas (MLS) — is just 15 minutes north on US-75, making MLS soccer games a practical family outing for Plano residents. The stadium campus also connects to The Star (Dallas Cowboys practice facility), a full entertainment and dining complex. Plano's US-75 location means Frisco's major attractions are effectively extensions of Plano's own entertainment options, a distinct geographic advantage over western Collin County suburbs.

Family-Friendly Restaurants

Plano has one of the strongest restaurant scenes in Collin County — anchored by Legacy West's world-class dining corridor and complemented by Old Town Plano's independent restaurant culture. The range spans genuinely excellent upscale options to reliable family weeknight spots. The Legacy West concentration means families can walk between multiple restaurants when the first choice has a wait.

Haywire

Best Special Occasion Dinner

Haywire at Legacy West is Plano's standout special-occasion restaurant — Texas-inspired cuisine in an elevated but comfortable setting, with a cocktail program, dry-aged steaks, and an atmosphere that feels genuinely celebratory without being stiff. Closing-day dinners, school milestone celebrations, and out-of-town guest dinners all work here. Reservations are essential on weekends; the patio is one of Legacy West's best outdoor dining spots in spring and fall.

Whiskey Cake Kitchen & Bar

Best Weekend Brunch

Plano's Whiskey Cake is one of the city's most consistently popular brunch destinations — a farm-to-table concept with inventive cocktails, substantive brunch dishes, and an atmosphere that works for couples and families equally. The outdoor patio is a strong spring and fall option. Weekend waits can be significant; arrive before 10am or plan for a 45+ minute wait on Saturday and Sunday mornings.

Legacy Food Hall

Best for the Whole Family

Legacy Food Hall solves the "everyone wants something different" family dinner problem — multiple vendors in a single space covering burgers, tacos, Asian cuisine, pizza, desserts, and more, all under one roof at Legacy West. Everyone chooses their own option, there's no shared-menu negotiation, and the communal seating makes it relaxed and low-pressure. Consistently the easiest family dinner decision in Plano when agreement is impossible. Popular for both weeknight convenience and weekend outings.

North Italia

Best Family Italian Night

North Italia at Legacy West delivers elevated Italian in a family-friendly format — pastas, wood-fired dishes, and a full cocktail program in a lively, comfortable space. A strong choice for family dinners where adults want something genuinely good and kids are satisfied with pasta. The energy level works well for families; it's never too quiet to feel awkward with children. Reservations strongly recommended on weekend evenings.

Flower Child

Best Healthy Family Lunch

Flower Child at Legacy West is Plano's best healthy fast-casual option — bowls, salads, wraps, and protein plates with genuinely fresh ingredients and allergen-friendly options across most of the menu. A practical choice for health-conscious families and for post-Arbor Hills hike recovery lunches. The fast-casual format means it works for families without the pacing stress of a full-service restaurant.

Ida Claire

Best Saturday Dinner Vibe

Ida Claire delivers Southern comfort food in a bright, warm atmosphere that genuinely accommodates families — the menu has enough variety for adults to feel excited and enough familiar comfort-food options (fried chicken, biscuits, mac and cheese) to satisfy kids. The Plano location is one of the DFW area's most popular for good reason: the quality is consistent, the atmosphere is relaxed, and the portions are substantial. A reliable Saturday dinner option that rarely disappoints.

Torchy's Tacos

Best Quick Family Weeknight

Plano's Torchy's is the default "everyone eats and nobody complains" weeknight dinner — fast, reliably good, and kid-friendly in a way that doesn't feel like settling. Multiple Plano locations mean one is near most neighborhoods. The Trailer Park taco is the crowd-pleaser across all ages; the breakfast taco options at weekend morning hours are genuinely one of the best quick breakfasts in Plano.

Bob's Steak & Chop House

Best Celebration Steakhouse

Bob's in Plano is a Texas steakhouse institution — generous cuts, consistent quality, and the famous giant carrot. A go-to for major celebrations: first days in a new Plano home, school milestone dinners, or any occasion where the family wants to mark something properly. The atmosphere is classic steakhouse without being stiff; children are comfortable here. Reservations are essential and should be made well in advance for weekend evenings.

HTeaO

Best After-School Treat Stop

HTeaO — founded in Plano and headquartered here — operates several Plano locations offering 100+ iced tea flavors in a drive-through format. It's the after-sports, after-school, and after-park default for a significant portion of Plano families. The range of flavors and sweetness options covers both adults and kids with different preferences. A uniquely Plano institution that quickly becomes a family ritual after moving to the city.

Plano's primary dining corridors are Legacy West (Dallas North Tollway / Legacy Dr), the US-75 / Spring Creek corridor, and Old Town Plano. For the most current restaurant directory, visit plano.gov or exploredtx.com.

Shopping for Families

Plano's retail landscape is anchored at the upscale end by Legacy West and The Shops at Legacy, and at the everyday end by one of the densest concentrations of Target, H-E-B, Costco, and major retail in Collin County. For outlet shopping, Allen Premium Outlets is 15 minutes east on US-75. Plano's size means virtually every retail scenario can be resolved within 20 minutes of any neighborhood in the city.

Legacy West

Parent Use Case: Upscale retail, boutique shopping, and the best dining-and-shopping combination in Collin County — Plano's answer to a premium town center.
250+ stores and restaurants anchored by Restoration Hardware, Pottery Barn, lululemon, and a dense concentration of both national and boutique retail. The pedestrian-friendly outdoor design makes it genuinely pleasant for families — wide sidewalks, outdoor seating areas, and an energetic weekend atmosphere. Spring and fall evenings bring the strongest crowds; weekday mornings are significantly calmer for families with younger children who need more space.

The Shops at Legacy

Parent Use Case: Weekend lifestyle shopping adjacent to Legacy West — boutiques, fitness studios, specialty retailers, and dining in a walkable setting.
Adjacent to Legacy West, The Shops at Legacy offers an additional retail and dining layer — boutique clothing, specialty fitness, specialty food shops, and restaurants that complement Legacy West's national brands. The combined Legacy district gives Plano one of the most walkable and complete upscale shopping destinations in North Texas. Weekend evenings here rival any suburban town center in DFW.

Spring Creek / US-75 Everyday Corridor

Parent Use Case: All daily errands in one corridor — H-E-B, Target, Costco, Home Depot, and every major chain in a single stretch.
Plano's primary everyday retail corridor runs along Spring Creek Pkwy, 15th Street, and the US-75 feeder, concentrating a full range of daily retail: multiple H-E-B and Tom Thumb locations, Target, Costco, Home Depot, and virtually every service business a family needs. Plano's density means most daily errands can be completed without leaving the city. A distinct practical advantage for busy families over smaller suburbs with less concentrated retail infrastructure.

Annual Events & Community Calendar

Plano runs one of the most active community events calendars in Collin County — anchored by the nationally recognized Plano Balloon Festival each September. The city's size means the events calendar spans multiple venues and audiences: Old Town Plano's historic district character, Legacy West's upscale outdoor programming, Oak Point Park's outdoor event space, and Plano ISD's robust performing arts season together give Plano families a year-round event landscape that most suburbs can't match.

Plano Balloon Festival

When: September (annual) · Where: Oak Point Park
The Plano Balloon Festival is one of the signature outdoor events in the Southwest — 50+ hot air balloons launching from Oak Point Park's open meadows, with morning mass ascensions, evening balloon glows, live music stages, carnival rides, food vendors, and a full family festival atmosphere across the entire weekend. It consistently draws 100,000+ visitors over the event weekend and immediately becomes a non-negotiable annual tradition for Plano families. Arrive early for morning ascensions; parking fills quickly. Check planoballoonfest.org for the current year's schedule.

Plano Multicultural Festival

When: Fall (annual) · Where: Haggard Park / Old Town Plano
Plano's Multicultural Festival reflects the city's genuinely diverse population — cultural performances, food from dozens of cuisines, artisan vendors, and programming that showcases the international character of one of Texas's most diverse cities. Plano consistently ranks among the most culturally diverse cities in the US, and this festival is the best annual expression of that identity. A particularly valuable experience for families with children who benefit from early exposure to global cultures.

Plano Fourth of July Celebration

When: July 4th · Where: Oak Point Park / various venues
Plano's Fourth of July programming includes a professional fireworks show at Oak Point Park, live music, and family activities — one of Collin County's major July 4th events. Oak Point's open meadow space is one of the best natural settings for fireworks viewing in the area, and the park's size means the crowds are more spread out than smaller-venue events. For families who want the Allen USA Celebration's scale, Allen is 15 minutes east; for a more local option, Oak Point delivers.

Old Town Plano Events

When: Year-round · Where: Haggard Park and Old Town district
Old Town Plano hosts a rotating calendar of community events throughout the year — weekend art walks, seasonal farmers markets at Haggard Park, holiday celebrations, food truck events, and summer concert series. The historic district character gives these events a genuinely distinct feel from the larger-scale Legacy West programming. Particularly popular with families who live in Plano's central and older neighborhoods. Check discoverotp.com for the current Old Town Plano events calendar.

Plano ISD Performing Arts Season

When: Fall–Spring · Where: PISD schools and performing arts centers
Plano ISD's performing arts programs — orchestras, theater productions, choral concerts, and dance — are among the strongest in Texas at the public school level. Each school year brings a dense schedule of performances at multiple campuses, and the quality is routinely at a level that draws community audiences beyond the immediate school families. For families with children in music, theater, or dance, PISD's programs are a genuine draw — the performing arts culture in Plano's schools is self-reinforcing and produces consistently high-quality work.

Plano Holiday Events & Christmas Lighting

When: November–December · Where: Old Town, Legacy West, various parks
December in Plano means a multi-venue holiday season — Old Town Plano's tree lighting and holiday market, Legacy West's seasonal decorations and events, Plano ISD holiday concerts across all campuses, and city park lighting programs. The combination of Old Town Plano's intimate holiday atmosphere with Legacy West's larger-scale programming gives families multiple distinct holiday experience options without leaving the city. A consistent highlight of the Plano family calendar year.

For the official Plano events calendar, visit plano.gov/events. Balloon Festival: planoballoonfest.org.

Indoor Activities for Kids

Plano's size gives it a broader indoor activity inventory than most DFW suburbs — from Dave & Buster's and multiple entertainment centers to the ArtCentre of Plano and a network of recreation centers with year-round programming. When July heat makes outdoor recreation impractical, Plano families have genuine variety in their indoor rotation rather than a single fallback venue.

Dave & Buster's

Type: Entertainment Center · Ages: All ages
Dave & Buster's is headquartered in Plano — and there's a full flagship location here to prove it. One of the largest arcade and entertainment complexes in the area, with a massive arcade floor, full sports bar, restaurant, and regular family-friendly promotions. D&B is the go-to for milestone birthdays, reward outings, and any occasion where the brief is "the kids get to do whatever they want." Weekday afternoons are significantly less crowded than weekend evenings.

Plano Recreation Centers

Type: Recreation Centers · Ages: All ages
Plano operates multiple full-service recreation centers — the IH-635 Recreation Center, Jack Carter Recreation Center, and others — each offering gymnasium courts, fitness equipment, multipurpose programming rooms, and a year-round schedule of youth enrichment activities. The network of facilities means most Plano neighborhoods are within 10 minutes of a recreation center. Summer youth programs at recreation centers are heavily used as structured childcare alternatives during the July weeks when school is out.

Plano Aquatic Center

Type: Indoor Aquatics · Ages: All ages
Plano's dedicated indoor aquatics facility — offering recreational swim sessions, year-round swim lessons from infant through adult, and competitive team programming through the Plano Aquatic Club. One of the primary indoor water-activity options for families during summer months when outdoor heat makes the spray parks less comfortable. Open swim session availability varies; check plano.gov/aquatics for current schedules.

ArtCentre of Plano

Type: Arts Center · Ages: All ages
Plano's primary visual arts venue offers rotating gallery exhibitions and a well-developed arts school with classes for children of all ages. Youth art classes and summer camps are popular enrichment options, particularly for families who want non-athletic programming to balance their children's schedules. The gallery exhibitions change regularly and are free to attend — a reliable low-effort cultural outing on a slow weekend.

Plano Public Library System

Type: Public Library Network · Ages: All ages
Plano operates multiple library branches — the central Harrington Library and several neighborhood branches — each with children's departments running year-round story times, STEM activities, summer reading programs, and school-break enrichment events. The summer reading program across Plano's library system is one of the most heavily used free family resources in the city, providing weekly structured programming for children from June through August. All programs are free with a library card.

Cinemark Legacy & XD

Type: Movie Theater · Ages: All ages
The Cinemark Legacy is Plano's primary multiplex — conveniently located adjacent to Legacy West and its dining options, making a movie-plus-dinner combination genuinely simple. The XD format delivers premium presentations for blockbuster season, and matinee pricing on weekdays makes it practical for school-break outings. The combination of the theater, Legacy Food Hall, and Legacy West's surrounding retail makes this corner of Plano a reliable half-day family destination any time of year.

Summer Heat Pro Tip

Plano summers peak from mid-July through August with temperatures regularly exceeding 100°F. The indoor rotation most Plano families rely on: Dave & Buster's for high-energy entertainment days, Plano Aquatic Center for water time, recreation center programming for structured activity, and the Plano Public Library for summer reading weeks. The ArtCentre summer camps are a popular enrichment option for families who want to give their children something structured and non-athletic during the hottest weeks of the year.
